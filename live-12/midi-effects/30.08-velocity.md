# Velocity (manual 30.8)

**Family:** note transformation
**Note status:** dump checked, partly measured (MIDI recordings, [Measured](#measured))

## Summary

Velocity reshapes the velocity of the notes passing through it. A window of incoming velocities
(Lowest, Range) is stretched onto a window of outgoing velocities (Out Low, Out Hi) along a curve
that Drive and Compand bend, Random adds scatter, and a mode decides what happens to notes whose
velocity lies outside the window: clip them to its edge, drop them, or send a fixed velocity. The
Operation control chooses whether it acts on note-on velocity, on release velocity, or on both
[manual]. Typical uses: keeping a quiet player audible, evening out or exaggerating dynamics,
adding variation to repeated notes, splitting a keyboard by how hard it is played, and inverting
dynamics [manual; source: 6, 7].

History: the Live 11 manual has the same controls and the same three modes [source: 2]. In Live 12
the only listed change is keyboard navigation (Tab goes down each column, then across), in 12.0.5
[source: 3]. The Velocity Shaper of Live 12 is a Max for Live MIDI Tool in the clip view, a
different thing [source: 3].

## MIDI flow

Shared parts: [common.md](common.md) (title bar, note-off and MPE handling, racks, latency).
The device has no Use Current Scale toggle, no clock and no latency: every output note starts and ends where its input note did [measured 12.4.6].

```text
note-on velocity v ─┐   Operation = Velocity or Both
                    ├─► Mode: Fixed ─► Out Hi, whatever v is (no window, no curve)
note-off release v ─┘     Clip / Gate:
  Operation = Rel. Vel. or Both
  (the other message passes unchanged)
   window  t = clamp((v − Lowest) / Range, 0, 1);  Gate drops v outside (Lowest, Lowest + Range]
   ─► w = 1 + 126·t  ─► Drive ─► Compand (both on the 1 … 127 scale) ─► map 1 … 127 onto Out Low … Out Hi
   ─► + Random (uniform ± Random) ─► clamp ≤ 127 ─► out        [measured 12.4.6]
```

- Pitch, channel, timing, CCs, pitch bend and aftertouch are not touched [inferred].
- Operation Velocity changes note-on velocities only, Rel. Vel. release velocities only, Both
  changes both [manual; measured 12.4.6]. The same settings (window, curve, Out, mode) are used for
  both, and Random is drawn separately for each [measured 12.4.6].
- Random is the only random part: one draw per note and one for its release (per note of a chord,
  as a tutorial reports [source: 6]). The draws differ between two identical instances and are not
  repeated by the same device [measured 12.4.6].
- **Velocities are not integers.** The recorded notes carry fractional velocities (an input of 64
  with Out Low 40 and Out Hi 100 came out as 70, one of 2 with Drive +0.5 as 19.2922, and the
  floor of the scale as 1.99213), and the device does not round them [measured 12.4.6]. The
  rounding to a MIDI value happens, if at all, at the instrument.

The manual states that Lowest and Out Low at 0 and Range and Out Hi at 127 leave the velocities
essentially unchanged [manual]. The loaded device differs a little: Lowest 1, Range 127, Out Low
1, Out Hi 127, Drive, Compand and Random 0, and Operation Both [dump 12.4.6]. For note-on
velocities (1 – 127) both settings are the identity, except that velocity 1 comes out as 1.99213
(the lowest value the curve gives, see Behaviour); a release velocity of 0 becomes 1.99213 as
well, with Lowest 1 [measured 12.4.6]. Out Hi can be set below Out Low, and then the response is
inverted [measured 12.4.6; source: 6, 7].

## Behaviour

### Order of operations

Everything below is [measured 12.4.6] and reproduces the recorded cases (Drive, Compand, window,
Out, Gate, Fixed, release velocity) to within 0.04 velocity steps, except where it says otherwise.
For a velocity `v`, with `lo = Lowest` and `rng = min(Range, 127 − Lowest)`:

1. **Window position.** `t = clamp((v − lo) / rng, 0, 1)`. With `rng = 0` the velocity `v = lo`
   gives `t` = 0.496 (63/127) and a larger one gives 1, a smaller one 0. Range is the size of the window above
   Lowest, capped so that Lowest + Range is at most 127 (Lowest 80 with Range 127 behaves as
   Range 47).
2. **Velocity scale.** `w = 1 + 126·t`, so the window fills the velocities 1 … 127.
3. **Drive.** `w = 1 + 127·((w − 1) / 127)^e`, with `e = 1 / (1 + 3·Drive)` for Drive above 0 and
   `e = 1 + 3·|Drive|` below 0.
4. **Compand** (after Drive; the two do not commute). `u = (w − 64.5) / 63.5`,
   `w = 64.5 + 63.5 · sign(u) · |u|^e`, with the same law for `e` from Compand.
5. **Floor and cap.** `w` is held to `[1.99213, 127]`, where 1.99213 is 253/127: the lowest output the
   curve gives, so the velocity at the bottom of the window comes out at 1.99213 (with Out Low 1 and
   Out Hi 127) and not at 1.
6. **Output window.** `out = OutLow + (w − 1)/126 × (OutHi − OutLow)`. The curve is therefore
   applied on the velocity scale 1 … 127 first and rescaled after; with OutHi < OutLow the result
   runs downwards. Out Low 0 gives `out = 1` for a velocity at the bottom of the window.
7. **Random.** A uniform random amount in `[−Random, +Random]` velocity steps is added, the
   result limited at 127 (not at Out Hi, and not at Out Low: Out Low 60 with Random 50 gave
   velocities down to 41.6).

Check values, Out Low 1, Out Hi 127, Lowest 1: Drive +0.5 takes 8 to 40.84, 32 to 73.25 and
64 to 96.94; Drive −0.5 takes 64 to 23.01 and 120 to 108.9; Compand +0.5 takes 64 to 55.38 and
72 to 91.52; Compand −0.5 takes 32 to 52.60 and 72 to 64.80 [measured 12.4.6].

Modes for velocities outside the window [measured 12.4.6]:

- **Clip:** `t` is held at 0 or 1, so a velocity at or below Lowest comes out as the bottom of the
  output window (1.99213 on the default scale) and one above Lowest + Range as Out Hi.
  Random is applied to those too.
- **Gate:** the note is not passed on, with its note-off; the other notes are as in Clip. A velocity passes when
  `Lowest < v ≤ Lowest + Range`: Lowest 40 with Range 40 passed 41 – 80 and dropped 40; with Range 0 only
  `v = Lowest` passed; with Range 1 only 41 did. The exception recorded is velocity 127 with
  Lowest 80 and Range 127, which was dropped (the window 81 – 126 passed 46 notes). A dropped
  note has no note-off either (a held-note table is needed).
- **Fixed:** every note gets velocity Out Hi, whatever its own velocity and the window (Lowest 40
  with Range 40 sent all 127 input velocities to Out Hi), so there is no curve and no gating. Random
  is still added: with Out Hi 127 and Random 50 the outputs ran from 77.8 to 127.

Two Velocity devices with Gate and complementary windows split notes by velocity [source: 7].
Because the lower edge is excluded and the upper included, a low chain with Lowest 0 and
Range = the split point passes 1 … split point, and a high chain with Lowest = the split point and
Range 127 passes split point + 1 … 126; velocity 127 does not pass the second one (the rule above),
so a split by two Gate devices loses velocity 127 [measured 12.4.6; the rack itself was not run].

### Operation, release velocity

With Rel. Vel., the incoming value is the note's release velocity and it goes through the same
window, curve, Out and Random [measured 12.4.6]. The note-on velocity passes unchanged. Clips
store a release velocity per note, 64 by default, and in the recordings Both with a note
of velocity v and release velocity v gave the same output for both values. The edge cases with
Lowest 40, Range 40 and release velocities 0, 1, 64, 127, 40, 80 and 81 [measured 12.4.6]:

- **Clip:** 0, 1 and 40 gave 1.99213, 64 gave 76.6, and 80, 81 and 127 gave 127.
- **Gate:** the note itself is never dropped (Operation Rel. Vel. changes no note-on), and the
  release velocity becomes 0 for those outside the window (0, 1, 40, 81 and 127) and is the
  mapped value inside it (64 gives 76.6, 80 gives 127). So a gated release velocity is sent as 0,
  not removed.
- **Fixed:** every release velocity became Out Hi (90 in the run).

An Ableton staff comment in a 2010 forum thread says note-off velocity matters mainly for samplers
that react on the note-off [source: 9], so a wrong release velocity is mostly inaudible with
ordinary instruments.

### Drive and Compand: the curve

Both stages use one law, an exponent `e` on a normalised value, for a control `c` in −1 … 1:
`e = 1 / (1 + 3c)` for `c > 0` and `e = 1 + 3|c|` for `c ≤ 0`, so `e` runs from 4 (c = −1)
through 1 to 0.25 (c = +1) [measured 12.4.6; the values fit within 0.04 velocity steps for
c = ±0.25, ±0.5, ±0.75 and ±1].

- **Drive** raises the position along the scale: `v' = 1 + 127·((v − 1)/127)^e`. Positive Drive
  lifts the curve above the diagonal (loud), negative Drive pushes it below (soft). At Drive +1,
  2 comes out at 38.8, 8 at 62.5 and 64 at 107.6; at Drive −1, 64 comes out at 8.69, 96 at 40.8
  and 127 at 124.1; velocity 1 comes out at the floor, 1.99213, for every Drive.
- **Compand** is the same law on the distance from the middle, symmetric about 64.5. Positive
  values push the velocities away from the middle (64 goes to 45.6 and 72 to 101.7 at +1,
  so the curve is steep in the middle), negative values pull them towards 64.5 (at −1, all of 56 – 80
  come out between 64.48 and 64.72 and 32 comes out at 60.1).
- **Order:** Drive first, then Compand: the recorded Drive +0.5 with Compand ±0.5 equals Compand
  applied to the Drive output, and not the reverse.

The µ-law form in the notes' earlier candidate list [source: 5] is not what Live does; the power
form above reproduces every recording.

### The display

The display plots the output velocity against the input velocity for the current settings. Its
horizontal axis is the incoming velocity, its vertical axis the outgoing velocity [manual]. At the
default settings it is the diagonal [source: 8]. The Range/Lowest window and the Out Hi/Out Low
window are drawn on the axes [manual]. With positive Random a gray band around the curve shows the
spread [manual]. What is drawn outside the window in Clip and Gate modes, whether the curve is
drawn over the full 0 … 127 input axis or only over the window, and how the band is scaled
(±Random, or only within the output window) are [measure] by looking at the display (screen
only, not MIDI). The display is not needed for the DSP; the curve function is.

## Edge cases

- **Velocity 1 and 127:** at the default settings 127 is unchanged and 1 comes out as 1.99213
  [measured 12.4.6].
- **Lowest = 127, Range 0:** only velocity 127 is in the window: it comes out at 63.5 (Clip and
  Gate), all others at the floor in Clip and are dropped in Gate [measured 12.4.6].
- **Out Hi = Out Low:** a flat curve: with both 64 every note came out 64 [measured 12.4.6].
- **Out Hi < Out Low:** inversion; with Out Low 100 and Out Hi 20, 64 came out at 60, and with Drive
  +0.5 the curve is inverted after the Drive curve (64 at 39.09). Random is added after
  [measured 12.4.6].
- **Random with an output range of one value:** not measured; the scatter is not limited by the
  output window.
- **Rounding:** none; the output velocities are fractional [measured 12.4.6].
- **Gate and the note-off:** a gated note-on takes its note-off with it; with Rel. Vel. the
  note-on always passes and the release velocity becomes 0 (see Operation) [measured 12.4.6].
- **A change of a parameter while notes are held:** the note-on velocity is set at the note-on and
  the release velocity with the settings at the note-off. A note held over a step of Out Hi from 127
  to 40 kept its note-on velocity 100, and all three release velocities, including those of the
  two notes that started earlier, came out at 31.64 [measured 12.4.6].
- **Device off with notes held:** the release velocity of a note ending while the device is off
  passes unchanged (100 stayed 100), a note starting while it is off passes unchanged, and a note
  ending after it is back on has its release velocity mapped (55.71 with Out 40 – 60) [measured
  12.4.6]. Transport stop; panic; sustain: [common.md](common.md).
- **MPE:** per-note expression is not touched [inferred].

## Measurement plan

Done: items 1 – 7 and 9 were recorded (see [Measured](#measured)); item 8 (display) is not a MIDI measurement.
Output must be read as MIDI: route the track to a second MIDI track and record (needs Live's
window; AGENTS.md). Audio rendering through an instrument is not suitable because velocity-to-level
responses are not linear. Ranges, defaults, step lists and display text come from the dump or
`get_device_parameters`, stored values from a saved `.als`.

**Input clip V (velocity sweep):** the same pitch (C3), 127 notes in a row, one per 1/16, length
1/32, velocities 1, 2, …, 127; and a copy with the note-off (release) velocity of each note set to
the same number (the Remote Script's note commands should accept a release velocity; if not, edit
in Live's window). Write both as one clip each.

1. **Curve, Drive.** Defaults except Drive at −1, −0.75, −0.5, −0.25, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1 (the
   display's range [dump 12.4.6]): record clip V, tabulate output against input. Fit the family:
   power, Bézier, exponential, piecewise. Also Out Low 0, Out Hi 127 first, then Out Low 40,
   Out Hi 100 to check that the curve is scaled onto the output window.
2. **Curve, Compand and both.** Compand at the same list, then Drive 0.5 with Compand 0.5, and
   Drive 0.5 with Compand −0.5: do they commute (compare with applying each alone to the other's
   output)?
3. **Window and modes.** Lowest 40, Range 40 (and Lowest 0, Range 80; Lowest 80, Range 127;
   Range 1 and 0) in Clip, Gate and Fixed: output of clip V (which velocities pass; are `lo` and
   `hi` inclusive; what do the outside velocities become). Two Gate devices in a Rack with
   complementary windows at the same boundary: does every note pass exactly once?
4. **Random.** Out Low 60, Out Hi 127, Random 50 (the manual's example), 10, 64 (the top [dump 12.4.6]): a clip of 2000
   notes at constant velocity 100 and one at 60; histogram of the outputs (uniform? width ±Random?
   clamped or reflected at 60 and 127?), repeatability across playbacks and after a
   transport restart. Random with Fixed and with Gate.
5. **Operation.** Velocity, Rel. Vel., Both with clip V and its release-velocity copy: which
   messages change; the same settings applied to both?
6. **Release velocity details.** Notes with release velocity 0, 1, 127, and live-keyboard-style
   note-off at 64; Gate with Rel. Vel.: do the note-ons pass and note-offs vanish (stuck notes)
   or are both dropped?
7. **Extremes.** Out Hi < Out Low (inversion, including with Random and Drive), Out Hi = Out Low,
   Lowest 127, Range 0.
8. **Display.** Screenshots only for the user's own reading: curve and gray band at a few
   settings, to confirm the axes and what the band shows (not part of the MIDI output).
9. **Timing.** Output against input at the tick; device off; parameter changes during held notes.

## Measured

Recorded from Live 12.4.6 (`measurements/30.08-velocity/live/*.json`; runs `velocity-drive`,
`-compand`, `-window`, `-random`, `-operation`, `-release`, `-extremes`, `-held`; clip V of 127
notes with velocities 1 – 127, 512 notes of constant velocity for Random). The laws are in
Behaviour; the points to know [measured 12.4.6]:

- Velocities leave the device as fractions; the lowest the curve gives is 1.99213.
- Drive and Compand are two power laws on one exponent `e(c)`; Drive first.
- Window: `t = (v − Lowest) / min(Range, 127 − Lowest)`, Gate keeps `Lowest < v ≤ Lowest + Range`,
  Fixed sends Out Hi and ignores the window.
- Out maps the curve's 1 … 127 onto Out Low … Out Hi, inverted if Out Hi is lower.
- Random is uniform ±Random velocity steps, added after the mapping, capped at 127.
- Operation, release velocity, held notes and switching off: see the sections above.

Not measured: the display (item 8), two Gate devices in a Rack, and repeatability across
playbacks; the two identical instances drew different values.

## Implementation notes

- **Structure:** a per-block pass over note events. A note-on or note-off event gets its velocity
  from the formulas in Behaviour (a 128-entry table `T[v]` can hold steps 1 – 6 and be rebuilt when a
  parameter changes, but the values are fractional), then Random, then the cap. The velocity is a
  float throughout. Gate removes the event; for Gate, a small held-note table (channel, pitch) so
  the note-off of a dropped note is dropped too. No scheduler, no latency.
- **Random:** a generator seeded per instance; draw once per event and release, uniform, in
  `[−Random, +Random]`. Live's repeatability across playbacks was not measured.
- **Curve:** the formulas are exact (within 0.04), so the display can plot the same function;
  keep Drive and Compand as separate stages in the measured order (Drive, then Compand).
- **Parameters to ship:** Lowest, Range, Out Low, Out Hi (integers 0 – 127), Drive, Compand
  (−1 – 1), Random (0 – 64), Operation (3), Mode (3) [dump 12.4.6].
- **`.als` import.** The device element is `MidiVelocity` [als 12.4.6]. Parameter elements, in
  stored order, all as the API shows them [als 12.4.6]: `On`, `Drive` (−1 – 1), `Compand`
  (−1 – 1), `Random` (0 – 64), `Mode` (index 0 – 2: Clip, Gate, Fixed), `Operation` (index 0 – 2:
  Velocity, Rel. Vel., Both), `MaxOut` (Out Hi, 0 – 127), `MinOut` (Out Low, 0 – 127), `Range`
  (0 – 127), `Lowest` (0 – 127). No other state is stored.
