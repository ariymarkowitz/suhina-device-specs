# Redux (manual 29.30)

**Family:** distortion
**Note status:** dump checked, partly measured (top100 and remaining runs, [Measured](#measured))

## Summary

Redux degrades the signal in two independent ways: downsampling (a lower sample rate, with an
optional jitter on its clock, and optional filters before and after) and bit reduction (a lower
bit depth through a quantizer whose curve can be bent, with an optional DC offset) [manual]. It has
a Dry/Wet control and a Hi-Quality option. Used for lo-fi, aliasing, 8-bit and harsh digital
distortion.

## Signal flow

```text
input ─► [Pre filter] ─► downsampler (Rate, Jitter) ─► [Post filter (Post-Filter Octave)] ─► [DC Shift] ─► quantizer (Bits, Shape) ─► Dry/Wet ─► out
          [manual]        [manual]                       [manual]                            [manual]       [manual]                   [manual]
 └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────► dry ─────────────────┘
```

- The downsampling section is on the left of the panel, the bit reduction section on the right [manual].
- The Pre filter precedes the downsampler and the Post filter follows it [manual]; measured: Pre leaves the tone's images alone and Post removes them, so the downsampler sits between them (Measured › Filters) [measured 12.4.6]. Downsampling comes before bit reduction in the order the manual presents; the real order of the two sections is still [measure]: no run combined low Bits with a low Rate (SOS's test shows both applied on a sine without stating order [source: 3]).
- DC Shift is applied "before the quantization process" [manual].
- The Max wrapper has `rate`, `jitter`, `prefilter`, `post_filter`, `post_filter_octave`, `bitdepth`, `shape`, `quantizer_dc_shift`, `mix` and `resamplingquality` [source: 2].

## Behaviour

**Downsampling.** The input is sampled every `fs/Rate` samples (zero-order hold) so the output is held steps; frequencies above `Rate/2` fold back (aliasing) and spectral images appear [manual], [inferred]. Measured: the Rate is the true rate in Hz, every image sits at m x Rate +/- f and has the level of a zero-order hold, |sinc(f_m / Rate)|, to within 0.1 dB; there are no extra lines at non-integer ratios (Rate 30 kHz and 15 kHz), so the hold times are fractional rather than rounded to whole samples; images above the host's Nyquist frequency are reduced by about 21 dB (23 kHz folding to 21.1 kHz), so the stage is band-limited to the host rate (Measured › Rate) [measured 12.4.6]. Which quality mode these renders used is unknown (Hi-Quality is not a parameter) [measure]; the eco-versus-high difference [source: 2] was not measured.

- **Jitter:** the sample instants are randomised, so the held values come at irregular times; this converts the aliasing into noise [manual]. The jitter's size relative to the period (0 – 100 %) and distribution are [measure].
- **Stereo effect of Jitter:** each channel has its own jitter noise, so the channels decorrelate and the image widens [manual]. The Pre filter, by limiting the bandwidth, reduces this [manual].
- **Pre filter:** a low-pass at a bit below half the downsampling rate, to avoid imaging/aliasing [source: 2]. Measured at Rate 8 kHz: -3 dB near 3.85 kHz (96 % of half the Rate), -8 dB at 4.0 kHz, and -14 to -17 dB from 4.3 kHz (where the estimate reaches its floor); the slope is not resolved and the corner at other Rates was not rendered (Measured › Filters) [measured 12.4.6] [measure].
- **Post filter:** a low-pass after the downsampler at `Rate/2 · 2^octave` [manual]: the displayed number is octaves above or below half the Rate [manual]. Measured: -3 dB exactly there, then a very steep fall (about 96 dB/octave, like a Butterworth of order 16: -6 dB at 1.03 times the corner, -33 dB at 1.25, -60 dB at 1.5, -90 dB at 2) (Measured › Filters) [measured 12.4.6].

**Bit reduction.** `y = Q(x + offset)` where Q rounds to `2^Bits` levels across the full scale [manual]. At 4 bits the waveform has 16 levels [source: 3]. At 1 bit the output is only positive or negative full scale ("reduced to full positive or negative" [source: 3]). Measured (Shape 0, DC Shift off): Q rounds to the nearest multiple of `2^(1 - Bits)`, with levels from -1.0 to `1 - step` (so 4 bits: -1.0 to +0.875, 16 levels) and zero as a level; at 1 bit the output is only 0 or -0.5 (a positive input never changes it), and the output of 1 and 2 bits is scaled by 0.5 and 0.707 (Measured › Bits). Contradicts the "full positive or negative" wording [measured 12.4.6].

- **DC blocker:** the output has a one-pole high-pass at about 5 Hz (time constant 32 ms) that takes out the DC of the asymmetric quantizer (Measured › Bits) [measured 12.4.6].
- **Shape:** morphs the quantizer from linear to an A-law companded quantizer (compress → uniform quantize → expand), so small amplitudes get finer steps [source: 2], [manual]. The A-law constant and the morph law are [measure].
- **DC Shift:** adds an offset before quantization so the quantizer is symmetric about zero in the sense of having a level at zero or not: for a mid-tread quantizer zero is a level; a mid-rise quantizer (DC-shifted) has no level at zero, which makes small signals jump between ±half a step and so louder with a crunchier sound at low Bits [manual], [inferred]. The Max reference says it produces "symmetrical output" [source: 2].
- **Dither:** none mentioned; none expected [inferred].

**Dry/Wet.** Linear [inferred]. In Live 11.3.20 a fix made the Dry/Wet in Eco mode work "as expected" (a phased sound had occurred), which tells that the dry path must be aligned with the wet path's latency in both quality modes [source: 6].

**Stereo.** Both channels processed independently; Jitter adds decorrelation [manual].

**CPU.** In 12.2 Redux uses less CPU when silent [source: 7].

## Edge cases

- **Rate at its maximum (40 kHz on the panel [dump 12.4.6]) with a 44.1 or 48 kHz project:** nearly no reduction, but the hold is still there: -0.6 dB at 8 kHz and -2.5 dB at 16 kHz at 44.1 kHz [measured 12.4.6]; at higher project rates the Rate no longer exceeds the Nyquist (the Rate range may be relative to the sample rate: [measure]).
- **Rate at its minimum (20 Hz on the panel [dump 12.4.6]; 40 Hz in the Max object [source: 2]):** about 1 sample per 50 ms (25 ms at 40 Hz): very coarse stepping. The lowest rate rendered is 400 Hz (a 1 kHz tone aliases to 200 Hz; an impulse can be missed completely) [measured 12.4.6].
- **Bits 16:** near transparent (1.0 % THD at a -60 dBFS tone [measured 12.4.6]); 1: a pulse train between 0 and -0.5 with the DC taken out, not a symmetric square [measured 12.4.6].
- **DC Shift on with Bits high:** little effect [manual].
- **Silence in:** zero out; DC Shift offset may leave DC at low Bits [inferred].
- **Jitter 0:** a deterministic hold [manual].
- **Pre and Post both off:** raw aliasing and imaging [manual].
- **Tempo/transport:** none.
- **Sample rate of project:** Rate is in Hz, independent of the project rate [source: 2].
- **Eco versus high quality:** differences in aliasing and the sound of the resampler [source: 2].
- **Latency:** [measure] (a resampler or filter may add some).

## Measured

[measured 12.4.6] Behaviour runs at 44.1 kHz in Live 12.4.6 (`devices/29.30-redux/`: `redux-rate`, `redux-bits`, `redux-filters` in the top100 set; `redux-shape-dc`, `redux-jitter`, `redux-drywet` in the remaining set; `redux-pre-corner`, `redux-rate-hq`, `redux-section-order`, `redux-bits-shape-ramp` in the third set, rendered by Live 12.4.7: Measured › Third set), Rate 40 kHz (except where the case sets it), Jitter 0, Bits 16, Shape 0, DC Shift off, Pre and Post off, Post-Filter Octave 0.00, Dry/Wet 100 %. The Rate run is read from the 8 s steady 1 kHz tone at -12 dBFS (spectrum with 0.12 Hz bins) and the noise; the Bits run from the 1 kHz staircase (levels of the output samples); the Filters run (Rate 8 kHz) from the cross-spectrum of input and output noise (a 16384-point FFT) divided by that of the case without filters, and from the tone's images. These are observations, not a model.

### Things that affect how to read the renders

- `redux-filters default` is the `redux-rate rate-8k` render and `redux-bits default` is `redux-rate default` (shared tracks).
- A single-sample impulse is a poor test of a sampling device, because whether the sampling clock catches it decides the output: at Rate 400 Hz the -6 dBFS impulse is missed completely (silence), at 8 kHz it comes out at 0.12 of its height, at 3 kHz and 1.5 kHz at 0.5. The impulse peak, "rt60" and tap figures of this run mean nothing; the tone and the noise are used instead [measured 12.4.6].
- The noise response (`response.noise_db`) is the part of the output that is coherent with the input, so it shows the hold's sinc droop and the filters, not the aliasing power, which is incoherent: at Rate 8 kHz it falls to -28 dB at 8 kHz (the hold's null) although the output carries noise there [measured 12.4.6] [inferred].
- Pre on: the 1 kHz tone and its images are identical to the no-filter case, because the Pre filter acts only above its corner; Post +4 (a corner far above the audio band): tone and images equal the no-filter case to 0.1 dB, but the waveform is not bit-identical (the 1 kHz tone is 2.6 degrees later, a -16 dB difference signal), so the filter stays in the signal path [measured 12.4.6].

### Rate

Strongest components of the steady 1 kHz tone (frequency in Hz, level in dB relative to the strongest), no filters, 16 bits [measured 12.4.6]:

| Rate | Components |
|---|---|
| 30 kHz | 1000 only (nothing within 60 dB) |
| 22.05 kHz | 1000 (0); 21 050 (-27.6) |
| 15 kHz | 1000 (0); 14 000 (-22.9); 16 000 (-24.1) |
| 8 kHz | 1000 (0); 7000 (-16.9); 9000 (-19.1); 15 000 (-23.5); 17 000 (-24.6); 21 100 (-48.7) |
| 3 kHz | 1000 (0); 2000 (-6.0); 4000 (-12.0); 5000 (-14.0); 7000 (-16.9); 8000 (-18.1); 10 000 (-20.0) ... |
| 1.5 kHz | 500 (0); 1000 (-6.0); 2000 (-12.0); 2500 (-14.0); 3500 (-16.9); 4000 (-18.1) ... |
| 400 Hz | 200 (0); 600 (-9.5); 1000 (-14.0); 1400 (-16.9); 1800 (-19.1); 2200 (-20.8) ... |

- **The Rate value is the true sampling rate in Hz.** The components sit at m x Rate +/- f to within 0.01 Hz, for every setting: 1 kHz at 1.5 kHz becomes 500 Hz (the alias), at 400 Hz it becomes 200 Hz (1000 = 2.5 x 400) [measured 12.4.6].
- **It is a zero-order hold.** The level of every component follows |sinc(f_m / Rate)|, relative to the strongest, to within 0.1 dB for all 40 or so components of the 15 kHz, 8 kHz, 3 kHz, 1.5 kHz and 400 Hz cases (for example at 400 Hz the 600 Hz line is at -9.5 dB, the ratio of sinc(1.5) to sinc(0.5), which is 1/3). The noise response is the same droop: -0.6 dB at 8 kHz and -2.5 dB at 16 kHz at the default Rate 40 kHz, -4.8 dB at 16 kHz at 30 kHz, -3.2 and -3.7 dB at 4 kHz and -28 dB at 8 kHz at Rate 8 kHz. So even the default Rate 40 kHz is not transparent: it droops 2.5 dB at 16 kHz [measured 12.4.6].
- **The clock is not tied to the host's samples.** At non-integer ratios (Rate 30 kHz is 1.47 host samples per hold, 15 kHz is 2.94) there are no spurs other than the hold's images within 60 dB, so the hold times are not rounded to whole samples; with a plain integer hold the pattern would add a set of inharmonic lines [measured 12.4.6] [inferred]. The hold lasts 44100 / Rate samples (the impulse at 3 kHz is held for 16 samples against 14.7, at 1.5 kHz for 31 against 29.4, including the edges) [measured 12.4.6].
- **Content above the host's Nyquist frequency is mostly removed.** At Rate 8 kHz the image of 1 kHz at 23 kHz would be -27.2 dB; it appears folded to 21.1 kHz at -48.7 dB, 21.5 dB lower. At Rate 30 kHz the images at 29 and 31 kHz (about -29 and -30 dB for a plain hold, folded to 15.1 and 13.1 kHz) are not visible within 60 dB. The impulse shows a short pre-ring of about 4 to 8 samples at 2 to 6 % of its height before the hold starts, as a band-limiting filter would [measured 12.4.6] [inferred].
- Rate 22.05 kHz: the image at 21 050 Hz is -27.6 dB, 1.1 dB below the -26.5 dB of a plain hold [measured 12.4.6].

### Bits

Staircase of the 1 kHz sine, Rate 40 kHz, Shape 0, DC Shift off. The sample values of the output's flat parts are the quantizer's levels [measured 12.4.6]:

| Bits | Level spacing | Levels | Smallest staircase level that gives output | Half a step |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.5 | 0 and -0.5 | -6 dBFS (a few peaks) | -6.0 dBFS |
| 2 | 0.354 (2^-1.5) | -0.707, -0.354, 0, +0.354 | -12 dBFS (a few peaks) | -12.0 dBFS |
| 4 | 0.125 | -1.0 to +0.875 in 16 steps | -24 dBFS (a few peaks) | -24.1 dBFS |
| 8 | 0.0078 | -1.0 to +0.992, 256 levels | -48 dBFS (a few peaks) | -48.2 dBFS |
| 12 | 0.00049 | not resolved | below the staircase's -60 dBFS | -72.2 dBFS |

- **The quantizer is mid-tread with a step of 2^(1 - Bits)** (4 bits: 0.125 = 2/16; 8 bits: 0.0078): zero is a level, the output is silent while the input stays under half a step, and the levels are k x step for k from -2^(Bits - 1) to 2^(Bits - 1) - 1. Positive full scale therefore rounds to 1 - step (0.875 at 4 bits, 0.992 at 8) while negative full scale is exactly -1.0 [measured 12.4.6].
- **At 1 and 2 bits the output is scaled down**: the thresholds are those of a step of 1.0 and 0.5 (silent at -12 dBFS and -18 dBFS), but the levels are spaced 0.5 and 0.354 apart, which is the quantizer's levels times 0.5 (-6 dB) and 0.707 (-3 dB). At 4 and 8 bits the scale is 1; 3 bits was not rendered [measured 12.4.6] [inferred]. A 1-bit signal is only {0, -0.5}, not "full positive or negative": a positive input never produces a level other than zero [measured 12.4.6].
- **The output has a DC blocker.** The mean of the output is zero in every step, although the asymmetric quantizer's own mean is not (the 4-bit levels sit +0.012 above k x 0.125, the 2-bit levels +0.075 at 0 dBFS, the 1-bit square at +0.16 and -0.34 at 0 dBFS). After a step to 0 dBFS the offset decays exponentially with a time constant of 32 ms (a one-pole high-pass at about 5 Hz): 2-bit mean -0.0756 at 2 ms, -0.0167 at 50 ms, -0.0035 at 100 ms. The default case's sweep, -0.1 dB at 31.5 Hz, agrees with a 5 Hz corner [measured 12.4.6] [inferred for the position after the quantizer].
- Accuracy at a 1 kHz tone (gain of the fundamental / THD): 4 bits +0.17 dB / 4.0 % at -6 dBFS and +0.34 dB / 8.6 % at -12; 8 bits -0.02 dB / 0.4 % at -6 and +0.25 dB / 8.6 % at -36; 12 bits +0.17 dB / 8.6 % at -60; 16 bits -0.02 dB / 1.0 % at -60 dBFS. At 2 bits: -1.95 dB / 25 % at -6 dBFS and -4.37 dB / 23 % at 0; at 1 bit: -14.1 dB / 288 % at -6 and -9.7 dB / 65 % at 0 dBFS [measured 12.4.6].

### Filters

Rate 8 kHz, 16 bits, Shape 0 [measured 12.4.6]:

- **Pre** (response with it relative to without): flat to 3.6 kHz (within the estimate's scatter of about 1.5 dB), -1.5 dB at 3.7 and 3.8 kHz, -4.6 at 3.9, -8.1 at 4.0, -11 at 4.1 and -14 to -17 dB from 4.3 kHz on, where the estimate reaches its floor. The -3 dB point is near 3.85 kHz, 96 % of half the Rate; the slope below the floor is not resolved. Pre leaves the tone and its images unchanged.
- **Post**: -3 dB at Rate / 2 x 2^octave. Set to octave 0 it is -3.0 dB at 4001 Hz; octave +2 gives 15 997 Hz (16 000 expected), octave -2 gives 1000.1 Hz and octave -4 gives 247 Hz (250 expected). The displayed number is therefore octaves above or below half the Rate. Octave +4 would be 64 kHz; it has no effect on the tone or its images.
- The Post filter is very steep and the same shape at every setting: relative to the corner f_c it reads -6 dB at 1.03 f_c, -20 dB at 1.15, -33 dB at 1.25, -57 to -61 dB at 1.5 and -90 to -97 dB at 2 (corner 4000 Hz: 4130 Hz -6, 4578 Hz -20, 5000 Hz -33, 6000 Hz -61, 8000 Hz -90; corner 1000 Hz: 1500 Hz -56.6, 2000 Hz -97). That is a Butterworth response of order 16, -10 log10(1 + (f / f_c)^32), to within 5 dB (2 dB up to 1.25 f_c), about 96 dB/octave. Close to the host's Nyquist it is steeper: at corner 16 kHz the 17 kHz image is 28 dB down (-24.9 to -52.8 dB), a Butterworth of order 16 would give 9 dB [measured 12.4.6] [inferred for the order].
- Post removes the tone's images (7, 9, 15 and 17 kHz, -17 to -25 dB without it) below -100 dB and does not change the 1 kHz tone (-0.2 dB); Pre does not touch them. So the downsampler sits between the two: Pre before it, Post after it [measured 12.4.6] [inferred].
- Pre and Post together: the same as Pre alone below 4 kHz and the Post's steep fall above (-51 dB at 5 kHz, -83 at 6 kHz) [measured 12.4.6].

### Shape and DC Shift

Rate 40 kHz, 4 bits unless the case says otherwise, the 1 kHz staircase (`redux-shape-dc`); "gain" is the output level minus the input level of the step. The renders are filtered after the quantizer, so the levels themselves were not read off the samples; the thresholds and the distortion come from the staircase [measured 12.4.6].

| Case | first staircase step with output | gain / THD at -30 dBFS | at -12 dBFS | at 0 dBFS |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 bits, Shape 0 | -24 dBFS | silent | +0.3 dB / 8.6 % | -0.1 dB / 6.1 % |
| 4 bits, Shape 50 % | -30 dBFS | +3.1 dB / 36.7 % | +0.2 dB / 11.6 % | -0.8 dB / 14.0 % |
| 4 bits, Shape 100 % | -48 dBFS | +0.6 dB / 8.8 % | +0.4 dB / 8.5 % | -1.3 dB / 22.1 % |
| 2 bits, Shape 50 % | -18 dBFS | silent | -3.4 dB / 24.2 % | -5.3 dB / 39.4 % |
| 4 bits, DC Shift | every step, constant -21.2 dBFS up to -24 dBFS in | +8.8 dB / 42.9 % | -0.7 dB / 14.7 % | -0.1 dB / 3.8 % |
| 2 bits, DC Shift | every step, constant -12.1 dBFS up to -12 dBFS in | +17.9 dB / 42.9 % | -0.1 dB / 42.9 % | -4.1 dB / 14.7 % |
| 8 bits, DC Shift | every step, constant -45.2 dBFS up to -48 dBFS in | -0.1 dB / 6.0 % | 0.0 dB / 0.7 % | 0.0 dB / 0.2 % |
| 4 bits, DC Shift, Shape 100 % | every step, constant -45.2 dBFS | -1.2 dB / 15.2 % | -1.4 dB / 15.1 % | -1.5 dB / 15.1 % |

- **Shape moves the quantizer's threshold, which is half its smallest step, down**: the first step with output sits at -24 dBFS for 4 bits (half a step of 0.125), -30 dBFS at Shape 50 % (an inner step of 0.063, half as large) and -48 dBFS at Shape 100 % (an inner step of 0.0078, the step of 8 bits, one sixteenth of the 4-bit step). At 2 bits Shape 50 % moves the threshold from -12 to -18 dBFS, again halving the inner step. So the smallest step is the Shape-0 step times 1/2 at 50 % and 1/16 at 100 %, which fits 2^(-4 x Shape^2) with Shape the fraction, but with two points that is a guess [measured 12.4.6], [inferred] for the law between them.
- **At Shape 100 % the quantizer is close to logarithmic**: from -42 to -6 dBFS the gain is +0.9 to +0.4 dB and the THD stays at 8.5 to 9.7 %, so the relative error does not depend on level, as a companded quantizer's does not. At Shape 0 the same 4 bits give a THD that rises as the level falls: 8.6 % at -12, 25 % at -18 and 190 % at -24 dBFS. At 0 dBFS the gain falls (-1.3 dB) and the THD rises to 22 % at Shape 100 % [measured 12.4.6]. The curve is therefore a compander in front of a uniform quantizer [measured 12.4.6], [inferred]; the constants were not fitted.
- **DC Shift makes the quantizer mid-rise**: with it on, a tiny signal never falls in a dead zone. Any input from -60 dBFS up to the threshold produces the same output, a 1 kHz square wave with a constant level (-21.2 dBFS at 4 bits, -12.1 at 2, -45.2 at 8; THD 42.9 %); its rms is half a step (2 bits: a step of 0.354 gives 0.177 x sqrt 2 = -12.0 dBFS; 4 bits: 0.0625 x sqrt 2 = -21.1; 8 bits: -45.1), so the offset is half a step and the output toggles between the two levels either side of zero. At 4 bits the output level is unchanged from -12 dBFS up (-0.7 dB at -12, -0.2 at -6, -0.1 at 0) and the THD at 0 dBFS drops from 6.1 to 3.8 % [measured 12.4.6]. With Shape 100 % the same toggle is at -45.2 dBFS, the 8-bit size, as the smallest step predicts [measured 12.4.6].
- The DC blocker stays on with DC Shift: the output of the toggling square wave has no mean, so the offset does not appear at the output [measured 12.4.6], [inferred] from the constant rms.

- **Jitter turns the tone's aliasing into a broadband noise floor that does not grow with the setting past 25 %.** The noise is flat to about 4 kHz and falls by about 6 dB at 4 to 8 kHz and by 12 to 15 dB above 8 kHz (-58 to -73 dB per bin at 25 %, -57 to -69 at 100 %), 80 dB above the noise with Jitter 0. Its total is -13 dB re the 1 kHz line at 25 % and -9.4 dB at 100 %: the first quarter of the control does nearly all the work, and the 25 to 100 % range changes the total by only 3.5 dB. The panel's square-law taper (25 % at control 0.5) makes the middle of the knob the point where the noise is already complete [measured 12.4.6], [inferred]; what randomised clock gives this floor is [measure].
- The hold's images are smeared by the jitter: they fall from -13.9 dB to -24 dB re the line at 100 %; the 1 kHz line itself loses 0.2 to 0.3 dB [measured 12.4.6].
- **The channels decorrelate from the first step**: the L/R envelope correlation of the tone is 1.00 at Jitter 0 and 0.01 at 25, 50 and 100 %, so each channel has its own random clock, even though the input is identical in both [measured 12.4.6].
- The coherent response falls less deeply at the hold's null with Jitter: at 8 kHz it rises from -28.4 dB (Jitter 0) to -17.0 dB at 100 %, because the random timing fills in the incoherent part [measured 12.4.6]. With **Pre on** it is down 7 dB more at 4 kHz, 13 dB more at 8 kHz and 15 dB more at 16 kHz, but the noise per bin in the audio band is the same (-57 dB re the line): the Pre filter does not reduce the jitter noise on a tone at 1 kHz, only the broadband input above its corner. The manual's remark that Pre reduces the stereo effect of Jitter is therefore not seen with this tone; the channels stay decorrelated (0.01) [measured 12.4.6].
- `analyse.py`'s modulation figures (FM of 200 to 3100 cents) show only the noise and are not a rate or a depth.

### Dry/Wet

Rate 8 kHz, 8 bits (`redux-drywet`) [measured 12.4.6]:

- **Dry/Wet is a linear crossfade with the dry exactly aligned to the wet**: the 50 % render is 0.500 x the 0 % render plus 0.500 x the 100 % render, to -152 dB. At 0 % the output is the input bit for bit; at 50 % the 1 kHz distortion is half the 100 % value (9.0 % against 18.1 % THD) and the small-signal gain is -0.2 dB. The first impulse tap is 0.56 = 0.5 + 0.5 x 0.12 [measured 12.4.6].
- Latency: the wet and dry paths mix with no offset in the export, but the export is delay-compensated, so a device latency cannot be read from it [measured 12.4.6].

### Third set (Live 12.4.7)

[measured 12.4.7] Behaviour runs at 44.1 kHz in Live 12.4.7 (`redux-pre-corner`, `redux-rate-hq`, `redux-section-order`, `redux-bits-shape-ramp`). Unless a case says otherwise: Rate 40 kHz, Jitter 0, Bits 16, Shape 0, DC Shift off, both filters off, Dry/Wet 100 %. "Eco" is the stored `EcoProcessing` element. These are observations, not a model.

**Pre filter at other Rates** (`redux-pre-corner`: the probe's noise and sweep, Pre on, Post off, Rate 2 kHz and 16 kHz; the hold's droop |sinc(f / Rate)| taken off). The two Rates give the same response against f / Rate to 0.1 dB: flat to 0.4 x Rate, residual -2.9 dB at 0.51 x Rate in the sweep and -6.8 dB in the noise's coherent part, and -15 dB (the floor of the estimate) from 0.64 x Rate. The corner therefore scales with the Rate (near 0.48 x Rate, as at Rate 8 kHz, Measured › Filters) [measured 12.4.7].

**Rate with Eco and the lowest Rate** (`redux-rate-hq`: the 10 kHz and 15 kHz sines at -6 dBFS):

- **Eco is a clock locked to whole host samples.** At Rate 12 kHz (3.675 host samples per hold) Eco adds lines 100 or 200 Hz from the hold's images (1900, 2200, 5900, 6100, 10 100, 14 200, 17 900 and 18 100 Hz, at -22 to -31 dB under the strongest line), a pattern that repeats every 441 samples (10 ms, 120 holds), which is what hold edges rounded to the sample grid would give. The high-quality mode puts the hold edges between host samples and has only the hold's own images [measured 12.4.7; the rounding is inferred]. The strongest line is 1.5 dB lower with Eco (-7.9 against -6.4 dB) [measured 12.4.7].
- The hold's images in both modes follow the sinc of 12.4.6: at Rate 25 kHz the 15 kHz line of a 10 kHz sine is -3.5 dB, which is sinc(0.6) / sinc(0.4) = 0.667 [measured 12.4.7].
- **The lowest Rate renders as a 20 Hz hold** (Rate set to 20 Hz): the output of the 1 kHz ramp changes every 2205 samples (50 ms = 44 100 / 20), the 100 Hz staircase gives one constant value per step (its 5 cycles per hold return the same phase at every sample) and the -6 dBFS impulse is missed (silence), as at 400 Hz in 12.4.6 [measured 12.4.7].

**Section order** (`redux-section-order`: a 3 kHz sine at -12 dBFS, Rate 8 kHz, Bits 3, Pre-Filter on or Post-Filter on or neither):

So the quantiser acts on the held samples: the signal is downsampled first and quantised after [measured 12.4.7; the order is inferred: with the quantiser at the host rate in front of the downsampler the offline simulation puts spurs at +-100 Hz at -16 to -18 dB, which are not in the render].
- Pre on gives the same lines at the same levels as Pre off: the 3 kHz sine is under its corner of 3.85 kHz at this Rate, and the products of the quantiser appear after it [measured 12.4.7]. Post on removes every line above its corner (4 kHz at octave 0) and leaves 1 kHz (the alias of the quantiser's 9th harmonic) and 3 kHz; the 5 kHz line falls from -4.4 to -37.7 dB, and THD from 13.6 % to 0.0 %, so Post sits behind the quantiser [measured 12.4.7].

At Shape 0 the same 8 bits give 8.6 % at -36 dBFS (12.4.6) [measured 12.4.7].

## Measurement plan

1. **Rate behaviour.** A 1 kHz … 10 kHz sine sweep with Bits 16, Shape 0, Jitter 0, filters off, Dry/Wet 100 %: read the alias frequencies to get the true effective rate for control positions (min, 25, 50, 75, max). Range, default, unit and taper of the display are settled by the dump [dump 12.4.6]. Check non-integer ratios (stepped hold times).
   Partly: the 1 kHz tone's alias lines at Rate 400 Hz to 30 kHz (seven settings, including the non-integer ratios 30 kHz and 15 kHz) give the effective rate: it equals the Rate value, and the lines follow a zero-order hold's sinc (Measured › Rate). Not done: the 10 kHz sine sweep through the rate, the panel extremes (20 Hz and 40 kHz as a stepped hold), and Rates at other project sample rates.
   More: 10 and 15 kHz sines at Rate 12 and 25 kHz, and the lowest Rate (20 Hz) as a 20 Hz hold (Measured › Third set).
2. **Jitter.** Same, at Jitter 0, 25, 50, 100 %: noise floor spectrum and the timing deviation distribution (sample-time jitter in fractions of a period); stereo correlation between the channels with mono input.
   Done at Rate 8 kHz, Jitter 25, 50 and 100 % and with Pre on: a noise floor of -13 to -9 dB re the tone that barely grows after 25 %, independent clocks per channel (correlation 0.01), the hold's images smeared; the timing distribution itself was not read (Measured › Jitter).
3. **Filters.** Impulse and sine sweep with Pre on only, Post on only, and both: filter corner against Rate and against Post-Filter Octave (the display values are −4.00 to 4.00 [dump 12.4.6]; confirm the mapping to octaves), slopes, whether the Post filter is a low-pass of what order.
   Partly: Pre only, Post only and both at Rate 8 kHz, and Post at octave -4, -2, +2 and +4: the Post corner is Rate / 2 x 2^octave (the display is octaves), about 96 dB/octave; the Pre corner is near 3.85 kHz at Rate 8 kHz (Measured › Filters). Read from noise and the tone's images, not from an impulse and a sweep. Not done: the Pre corner at other Rates, the Pre slope (the estimate reaches its floor), the Post filter's phase.
   More: the Pre corner scales with the Rate (near 0.48 x Rate at 2 kHz, 8 kHz and 16 kHz) (Measured › Third set).
4. **Bits and Shape.** A -60 … 0 dBFS slow ramp or a slow sine, Bits at 1, 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, Shape at 0, 25, 50, 75, 100 %: read the staircase levels (transfer function) to get the quantizer curves; fit the A-law constant.
   Partly: Bits 1, 2, 4, 8 and 12 at Shape 0: step 2^(1 - Bits), mid-tread, levels -1.0 to 1 - step, outputs of 1 and 2 bits scaled by 0.5 and 0.707, a 5 Hz DC blocker on the output (Measured › Bits). Not answered here: Shape 25 to 100 % and the A-law constant (`redux-shape-dc` is not part of these runs); Bits 3, 6, 16 were not rendered.
   Shape done at 4 bits (0, 50, 100 %) and 2 bits (50 %): the smallest step is the Shape-0 step times 1/2 at 50 % and 1/16 at 100 %, near-logarithmic at 100 % (Measured › Shape and DC Shift). Still open: 25 and 75 %, the compander constants, Bits 8 and 12 with Shape.
   More: Bits 3 and 6 at Shape 0, Bits 4 at Shape 25 and 75 %, Bits 8 and 12 at Shape 100 % (Measured › Third set): the smallest step against Shape is 1, 0.71, 0.5, 0.16, 0.0625 of the Shape-0 step; the quantiser's levels themselves are still unread.
5. **DC Shift.** Same with DC Shift on and off at Bits 2, 4, 8: levels at the origin; the offset size.
   Done at 2, 4 and 8 bits and with Shape 100 %: DC Shift is an offset of half a step, giving a mid-rise quantizer whose smallest output is a square wave of half a step (Measured › Shape and DC Shift).
6. **Section order.** A signal where order matters (a 3 kHz sine with Rate 8 kHz and Bits 3 with the post filter on): compare against processing the two stages offline in both orders.
   Not answered: the probe has no 3 kHz sine and no run combined low Rate and low Bits, so the order of downsampler and quantizer cannot be read; only Pre (before) and Post (after the downsampler) follow from the filter run (Measured › Filters).
   Read: the 3 kHz sine at Rate 8 kHz and Bits 3: downsampler first, quantiser after, Post behind both (Measured › Third set).
7. **Dry/Wet and latency.** Impulse at 0 %, 50 %, 100 %, Eco and high quality: delay and mix law; sine at 50 %.
   Dry/Wet done at 0 and 50 %: a linear crossfade of 0.5 dry + 0.5 wet exact to -152 dB, no offset (Measured › Dry/Wet). Eco versus high quality was rendered on the tone only (Measured › Third set).
8. **Hi-Quality.** Compare eco and high on a 10 kHz sine at Rate 12 kHz: the aliasing difference.
   Done at Rate 12 kHz: Eco puts lines 100 Hz from the hold's images (a clock locked to host samples) which high quality does not have (Measured › Third set).

## Implementation notes

- **Building blocks:** a biquad low-pass for the Pre and Post filter (higher orders as cascaded sections), parameter smoothing for Dry/Wet and filter changes, a seeded random generator for the clock jitter. The Post filter needs about 96 dB/octave (a 16th-order Butterworth, eight cascaded sections, fits within 5 dB) [measured 12.4.6] [inferred].
- **Missing:** a downsampler with a fractional hold (a phase accumulator that triggers sample captures; the renders show hold times that are not whole samples and a band limit at the host's Nyquist frequency, so the hold is probably done at a higher internal rate [inferred]), a jitter generator per channel, a quantizer with an A-law companding morph and an offset, a latency-aligned dry path. The same downsampler is the basis of Roar's Resampling filter and the Bit Crusher curve.
- **Hard to match:** the A-law morph (not measured here), the Eco and High modes, the Pre filter's slope, and the 1 and 2 bit output scaling. Settled by the runs: the Rate is the true rate in Hz, the Post-Filter Octave scale is octaves around Rate / 2, the quantizer is mid-tread with step 2^(1 - Bits) and levels -1 to 1 - step, and the output has a 5 Hz DC blocker [measured 12.4.6].
- **`.als` element:** `Redux2` [dump 12.4.6] (the summary header). Older sets may use `Redux` [inferred]. Parameter elements [als 12.4.6]: `On`, `SampleRate` (Hz, 40000), `Jitter` (0), `BitDepth` (16), `QuantizerShape` (0), `QuantizerDcShift`, `EnablePreFilter`, `EnablePostFilter`, `PostFilterValue` (0 = displayed 0.00), `DryWet` (1 = 100 %). Hi-Quality is not among the dump's parameters.
