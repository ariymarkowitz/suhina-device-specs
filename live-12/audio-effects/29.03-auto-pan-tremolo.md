# Auto Pan-Tremolo (manual 29.3)

**Family:** modulation and pitch
**Note status:** dump checked, partly measured (top100 and remaining runs, [Measured](#measured))

## Summary

Auto Pan-Tremolo moves the input around with an LFO. In Panning mode two LFOs (one per side)
change the left and right levels, so the sound travels across the stereo field; in Tremolo mode
one LFO changes the overall level, giving tremolo, gating and pumping effects [manual]. The
panning half is the old Auto Pan; the Tremolo mode, the extra time modes, the attack ramp and the
level-driven rate modulation arrived in Live 12.3, when the device was renamed and became
available in every edition [source: 2], [source: 3]. It has no side-chain and no delay lines.

## Signal flow

```text
                     ┌─ Panning: LFO L, LFO R (stereo offset: Phase or Spin) ─► gain L, gain R ─┐
input ─► (Tremolo: Harmonic? split at 600 Hz) ─► ...                                           ├─► out
                     └─ Tremolo: one LFO ─► one gain (Vintage? non-linear curve) ───────────────┘
        level detector ─► onset ─► Attack Time ramp scales the modulation depth      [manual]
        level detector ─► Frequency Modulation ─► scales the LFO rate                [manual]
```

- No dry/wet and no output gain exist on the panel; the effect depth is Amount alone [manual].
- Stage order inside Tremolo mode (Harmonic split, then LFO gain, then Vintage curve, or the curve first) is [measure].
- The level that drives the Attack ramp and Frequency Modulation is taken from the device input (pre-effect) [inferred].
- The LFO modulates gain only; there is no delay or filter in Panning mode [inferred, from the manual describing panning as a gain change between the channels].

## Behaviour

**Panning gain law.** Each channel is multiplied by its own LFO-derived gain; the left and right
LFOs are `Phase` apart [manual]. Measured: with the LFO as a value p from 0 to 1 (a sine LFO gives
p = 0.5 + 0.5 sin exactly), the full-depth gain of a channel is sqrt(2p), from 0 up to sqrt(2)
(+3 dB) with 1 at the centre, so at Amount 100 % and Phase 180° L² + R² = 2 at every moment: a
constant-power pan law with unity at the centre [measured 12.4.6]. Amount crossfades that gain with
1 linearly: gain = (1 - Amount) + Amount x sqrt(2p), so at 25 % and 50 % the gains run 0.75 to
1.104 and 0.50 to 1.207, and L² + R² is no longer constant (1.71 to 2.0 at 50 %) [measured 12.4.6]
(Measured). The old device is described as modulating the amplitude of the left and right
channels [source: 4].

**Tremolo.** One LFO, one gain for both channels, with the same law as one panning channel:
gain = (1 - Amount) + Amount x sqrt(2p); at 100 % it swings from silence to +3 dB and keeps the
average power, so a steady tone's RMS level is unchanged (-12.00 dB in, -12.00 dB out) [measured
12.4.6]. Where Shape and Invert enter is [measure]. Equivalent to Panning with Phase 0°; a review
says the same [source: 5].

**Shape.** Panning: a waveshaper that steepens the LFO (towards square), clipping at the extremes,
which is what shortens the overlap [manual], [inferred]. Tremolo: both signs — a "peak rounding"
(positive) and "peak sharpening" (negative) curve; the manual does not say whether it is a power
law, a blend between triangle and sine, or a clipped, scaled copy [measure].

**Waveforms.** Sine, Triangle, Saw Up, Saw Down and Square are conventional. Shark Tooth is a
Max/Ableton LFO shape of that name [source: 7]; its form is [measure] (likely a triangle whose
two flanks are curved, like a shark's fin [inferred]). Random and S&H are two different random
generators (a stepped random value per cycle for S&H; Random possibly interpolated or noise
[inferred]); Wander ramps between random levels [source: 7, whose "Wander" modulator is described
that way] [inferred that the same generator is used here]. How each Random-type waveform behaves
with Phase Offset (stereo width) is only known as "0 = identical L/R" for Shifter's similar LFO
[manual 29.35]; Auto Pan-Tremolo's scale [measure].

**Spin.** The right-channel LFO runs at a different speed than the left; the per-channel phase
then changes continuously. Phase and Spin are mutually exclusive [manual]. Wrapper semantics
(right frequency = (1 + spin) × left frequency, stereo phase disabled while spin is on) are the
Max LFO's, not necessarily this device's [source: 7].

**Time modes.** Rate: free Hz. Time: free period in ms, 100 ms – 200 s. Synced, Dotted, Triplet
and 16th: tied to the song tempo; the exact list of divisions per mode is [measure]. "16th" is
probably a count of sixteenth notes per cycle [inferred, from the name]. Synced LFOs follow the
song position, so they land on the same phase at the same beat however playback got there
[inferred].

**Attack Time.** An onset detector on the input restarts a ramp that takes the modulation depth
from 0 to its set value over Attack Time [manual]. Whether the ramp restarts after every onset
or only after a silence, the detector's threshold and how fast it releases are [measure].

**Dynamic Frequency Modulation.** The rate is multiplied by a factor that depends on the
smoothed input level and the slider (louder: faster for positive) [manual]. The mapping (linear
in rate or exponential in octaves), the level smoothing and the maximum range are [measure].

**Harmonic.** Fixed crossover at 600 Hz [manual]; two bands whose modulation runs in opposite
directions, as in a classic harmonic tremolo: low band gain 1 − A·u, high band gain 1 − A·(1 − u)
[inferred]. The crossover slope (second or fourth order) and whether the bands add back to flat
at Amount 0 are [measure]; a Linkwitz-Riley split sums flat [inferred].

**Vintage.** A memoryless non-linear shaping of the level modulation (or the signal) that adds
colour [manual]. Whether it distorts the audio (adds harmonics) or only warps the gain curve
(an optical tremolo's slow response) is [measure]. "Warmth and grit" suggests audio-rate
distortion [manual], [inferred].

**Display.** The live marker shows the instantaneous stereo position or level; it is a
visualization of the LFO, not a meter [manual].

## Edge cases

- Amount 0: no change in either mode [inferred].
- Phase 0° in Panning: both channels move together, which is Tremolo [source: 5].
- Mode switch while playing: Amount and rate persist [manual]; whether the LFO phase resets and
  whether the change is faded in or clicks is [measure].
- Random, Wander, S&H: Spin is unavailable; if Spin is selected and one of these is chosen the
  device falls back to Phase [manual], [inferred].
- Time mode at 200 s: a single cycle outlasts most clips; with the transport stopped the LFO
  probably keeps running free [inferred].
- Transport start/stop and tempo changes in the beat modes: phase is derived from the song
  position or free-running; [measure] which.
- Mono input: Panning makes the sound move; Tremolo is unchanged [inferred].
- Very high Frequency Modulation with loud input: rate limits are [measure].
- Denormals: gains stay between 0 and sqrt(2) [measured 12.4.6], so no special handling [inferred].
- Square and ramp shapes with Shape at its extremes switch gain abruptly; whether the gain is
  smoothed to avoid clicks (a one-pole of a few ms is the usual choice) is [measure].

### Shape and Invert

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shape 0 (Sine) | 0.33 | 0.73 | 1.07 | 1.30 | 1.41 | 1.37 | 1.21 | 0.92 | 0.54 | 0.12 |
| Shape +50 % | 0.03 | 0.17 | 1.30 | 1.41 | 1.41 | 1.41 | 1.40 | 0.51 | 0.08 | 0.01 |
| Shape +100 % | 0.00 | 0.03 | 1.38 | 1.41 | 1.41 | 1.41 | 1.41 | 0.18 | 0.01 | 0.00 |
| Shape -50 % | 0.39 | 0.17 | 1.40 | 1.39 | 1.33 | 1.23 | 1.11 | 0.96 | 0.79 | 0.59 |
| Shape -100 % | 0.00 | 0.00 | 1.38 | 1.13 | 0.76 | 0.46 | 0.24 | 0.11 | 0.04 | 0.01 |
| Panning, Panning Shape 50 % | 1.41 | 1.41 | 1.41 | 1.41 | 1.41 | 0.10 | 0.01 | 0.00 | 0.03 | 0.37 |
| Panning, Panning Shape 100 % | 1.41 | 1.41 | 1.41 | 1.41 | 1.41 | 0.01 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.10 |

- **Positive Shape steepens the sine's flanks through its midpoint**: the sine takes 0.5 s between its trough and its peak; at +50 % the rise takes about 0.1 s and at +100 % about 0.05 s (a square wave), always crossing at the same two instants (0.45 and 1.45 s here, where the sine's gain passes sqrt(1)). The plateaus are the full +3 dB (1.41) and silence [measured 12.4.6].
- **Negative Shape makes a falling ramp**: at -100 % the gain jumps from silence to 1.38 at the same instant and then falls smoothly over the whole cycle (to 0.76 at half a cycle, 0.11 at three quarters), gating with a decay; at -50 % it falls from 1.40 to 0.06 almost linearly in gain over the cycle, jumping up again at the same instant. A falling-ramp ("saw down") shape is reached with Tremolo Shape alone [measured 12.4.6].
- **Panning Shape** works on both channels the same way, mirrored: the plateaus are +3 dB on one side and silence on the other, switching at the sine's crossing, so at 100 % the pan jumps between hard left and hard right, with no overlap; at 50 % each switch takes about 0.1 s [measured 12.4.6].
- **Invert** with Sine is the sine shifted by half a cycle: the gain starts at its peak (1.41) where the plain sine starts near its trough; no other change [measured 12.4.6].

### Phase and Spin

Panning, Sine, 1 Hz, Amount 100 %; rate and phase of each channel fitted from the steady tone's gain (`autopan-stereo-offset`) [measured 12.4.6]:

| Setting | Left LFO | Right LFO | Right minus left phase |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phase 0° | 0.999 Hz | 0.999 Hz | 0° |

### Attack Time

Tremolo 4 Hz, Amount 100 %, the 8 s steady tone starting after silence; depth is the spread between the largest and smallest gain in each 250 ms (`autopan-attack`) [measured 12.4.6]:

| Attack Time | depth in the first 250 ms | depth rises by | full depth (1.35) reached after |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50 ms | 1.35 (full) | | immediately |
| 0.5 s | 0.40, then 1.14 | | 0.5 s |
| 2 s | 0.27, 0.28, 0.47, 0.65 ... | 0.18 per 250 ms | 2 s |

### Dyn Mod

Tremolo 2 Hz, Amount 100 %. LFO rate fitted from the steady tone (-12 dBFS) and from each burst of the level bursts (-30, -6, -30 dBFS) (`autopan-dynmod`) [measured 12.4.6]:

| Dyn Mod | -12 dBFS tone | -30 dBFS burst | -6 dBFS burst |
|---|---|---|---|
| -100 % | 0.51 Hz | 1.68 Hz | below 0.5 Hz |
| -50 % | 1.01 Hz | 1.83 Hz | 0.51 Hz |
| 0 % | 2.00 Hz | 2.00 Hz | 2.00 Hz |
| +50 % | 3.96 Hz | 2.18 Hz | 7.83 Hz |
| +100 % | 7.86 Hz | 2.38 Hz | above 12 Hz |

- **The rate is multiplied by 2^(8 x Dyn Mod x peak level)**, the level being the input's linear peak: at -12 dBFS (0.25) it is 2^(2 Dyn Mod), a factor of 4 up or down at +/-100 % and 2 at +/-50 %; at -30 dBFS (0.032) 2^(0.25 Dyn Mod), 1.19 at +100 % and 0.84 at -100 %; at -6 dBFS (0.5) 2^(4 Dyn Mod), 4 at +50 % and 0.25 at -50 %. The exponent is linear in the slider and in the level over a factor of 16 in level. Octaves of shift are thus in proportion to the level, the same way as the Auto Filter's envelope amount (about 7.7 octaves per unit of peak level) [measured 12.4.6; the follower's time constants not measured].

### Harmonic and Vintage

Tremolo, Sine, 1 Hz (`autopan-harmonic-vintage`) [measured 12.4.6]:

| Case | Response | Gain of the 1 kHz tone over a cycle |
|---|---|---|
| Harmonic, Amount 0 | flat at 0.0 dB from 20 Hz to 20 kHz, no THD at any level | 1.00 throughout |
| Harmonic, Amount 100 % | varies with the LFO; the noise response averages -0.8 to -1.0 dB above 400 Hz | 0.26 to 1.26, in antiphase to the plain Sine (minimum where the Sine has its maximum) |
| Vintage, Amount 0 | flat at **-3.1 dB** at every frequency and level (-3.1 dB at -60, -12 and 0 dBFS), no THD | 0.70 throughout |
| Vintage, Amount 100 % | the noise response averages -2.6 dB (the Sine: -0.7 dB) | 0.07 to 1.36, a narrower peak and an almost linear rise (about 0.14 per 50 ms) |
| Plain Sine, Amount 100 % | | 0.11 to 1.41 |

- Harmonic at Amount 0 is a transparent split in magnitude: the two bands add back to a flat 0.0 dB, but the impulse comes back as a tap of 0.886 (-1.1 dB) at 0 ms with a tail of a few ms, as the sum of an all-pass-like crossover would give (so the phase is not flat) [measured 12.4.6].
- A 1 kHz tone, above the 600 Hz crossover, is modulated in antiphase to Tremolo's when Harmonic is on, and with a smaller depth (0.26 to 1.26, against 0.11 to 1.41), as a high band that gets only part of the modulation would give [measured 12.4.6; the crossover frequency itself not measured].
- **Vintage costs 3.1 dB at any setting** (it is on or off, with Amount 0 included) and reshapes the gain curve rather than adding harmonics at the levels seen (0 % THD at Amount 0 up to 0 dBFS); at 100 % the staircase shows 8.2 % THD at -6 dBFS only where the gain is moving through the step [measured 12.4.6].

6. **Time modes:** read the full list of divisions in each of Synced, Dotted, Triplet, 16th;
   render at 120 BPM with a click and measure the period of each entry; confirm Time mode's
   limits by typing 100 ms and 200 s; compare Freq's minimum and maximum.
   Partly: one entry in each mode at 120 BPM; Triplet is 2/3 of the period, Dotted 3/2 (Measured › Time modes). The full division lists and the limits are open.
7. **Phase Offset in beat modes:** Synced 1/4, Offset steps; compare the LFO peak position with the
   click track; check transport-stop behaviour and a tempo ramp.
8. **Attack Time:** a tone burst train (50 ms on, 450 ms off, 1 kHz), Amount 100 %, Attack Time 0
   and at several values; measure the ramp: time to 90 % of full depth and its shape.
   Repeat with a continuous tone with a step in level to learn the onset detector's
   sensitivity and release.
   Partly: the ramp is linear in depth over Attack Time (Measured › Attack Time); the onset detector is open.
9. **Frequency Modulation:** a 1 Hz LFO, input tone stepped through −40, −20, −6, 0 dBFS with the
   slider at +100 %, −100 % and 50 %; measure the LFO frequency at each level and fit.
   Done at three levels: the rate shifts by 2^(8 x Dyn Mod x peak level) (Measured › Dyn Mod); the detector's smoothing is open.
10. **Harmonic:** a two-tone input (200 Hz + 2 kHz), Tremolo, Sine, Amount 100 %: show that the
    two tones' gains are in opposition; sweep a sine to find the crossover (−3 dB / −6 dB point)
    and its slope; check flatness of the sum at Amount 0 and 100 % in a static test (LFO stopped
    via Time = 200 s and a known phase).
    Partly: flat at Amount 0, and the 1 kHz tone is modulated in antiphase (Measured › Harmonic and Vintage). The crossover and its slope are open.
11. **Vintage:** the same tone at several levels: measure harmonic distortion (FFT of a 1 kHz tone
    at Amount 0 vs. 100 %) and the gain curve against the LFO value.
    Partly: -3.1 dB at Amount 0, no harmonics there; the curve at 100 % (Measured › Harmonic and Vintage). Harmonics at 100 % are open.
12. **Latency:** an impulse through the device in each mode; find any delay (none expected, but Harmonic's crossover and Vintage's non-linearity may add some).
    Harmonic at Amount 0: the main tap at 0 ms (-1.1 dB), so no delay beyond the tail; Vintage a plain 0.70 at 0 ms (Measured › Harmonic and Vintage); Panning and Tremolo without these are open.

## Implementation notes

- **What the device needs:**
  - Two modes, Panning and Tremolo, sharing the LFO; Tremolo-mode Shape (rounded vs. sharp peaks)
    as well as the panning Shape, which is a bipolar curve.
  - Waveforms Sine, Triangle, Saw up and down, Square, Shark Tooth, Wander, S&H and an
    interpolated Random; Wander and S&H differ from a random value held per cycle [inferred].
  - Phase and Phase Offset in degrees; in Random-type shapes Live turns the offset into a width.
  - The Spin stereo mode: a second LFO at a different rate.
  - Rate, Time, Synced, Dotted, Triplet and 16th modes; the synced list of divisions comes from
    step 6 of the plan.
  - Attack Time and Dynamic Frequency Modulation, both driven by an input level detector.
  - Harmonic (a split at 600 Hz, the two bands modulated in opposition) and Vintage (a soft
    saturation on the gain, oversampled).
- **Building blocks:** an LFO with the wave set above, parameter smoothing (a few ms on the gain
  against clicks), a Linkwitz-Riley crossover for the Harmonic split (sum the two bands with
  opposing gains), a static curve with oversampling for Vintage; an onset detector and ramp (envelope follower with fast attack/slow
  release); level-to-rate modulation of a phase accumulator (a synced LFO derived from the song
  position cannot be rate-modulated without drift; use an accumulator and re-lock to the song
  position when Frequency Modulation is 0).
- **Import:** Live 12.3+ saves the new device; older Auto Pan is "Auto Pan Legacy" with the old
  parameter set [source: 2]. The old device's `.als` element is `AutoPan` (unverified); the
  new device is `AutoPan2` [als 12.4.6]. Parameter elements [als 12.4.6]: `On`, `Mode` (0 = Panning,
  1 = Tremolo), `Modulation_Amount` (stored 0.5 = 50 %, a fraction), `Modulation_Waveform` (0 to 8),
  `Modulation_Invert`, `Modulation_TimeMode` (0 to 5), `Modulation_Frequency` (Hz, stored 1),
  `Modulation_Time` (seconds, stored 1), `Modulation_SyncedRate` (stored 6), `Modulation_Sixteenth`
  (stored 16), `Modulation_Phase` (degrees, stored 180), `Modulation_PhaseOffset` (degrees),
  `Modulation_StereoMode` (0 = Phase, 1 = Spin), `Modulation_Spin`, `PanningWaveformShape`,
  `TremoloWaveformShape`, `AttackTime`, `DynamicFrequencyModulation`, `HarmonicMode` (boolean),
  `VintageMode` (boolean). Units of the unit-less ones (Spin, shapes, Attack Time, Dynamic Frequency
  Modulation) are 0 in the saved set, so their stored scale is not visible there.
