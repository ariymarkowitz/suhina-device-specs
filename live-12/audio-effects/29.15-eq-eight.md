# EQ Eight (manual 29.15)

**Family:** EQ and filter
**Note status:** dump checked, partly measured (top100 and remaining runs, [Measured](#measured))

## Summary

An eight-band parametric equaliser. Each band is one of eight filter shapes (two steepnesses of
low cut and high cut, low shelf, peak, notch, high shelf) with its own frequency, gain and Q, and
can be switched off. It runs in Stereo (one curve for both channels), L/R (a curve per channel)
or M/S (a curve for mid and one for side). A spectrum analyser sits behind the curve, an Audition
mode solos a single band, Adaptive Q makes the bands narrower as they boost or cut more, and a
Scale control moves all gains at once [manual].

## Signal flow

Shared parts: [common.md](common.md) (title bar only; no side-chain, no dry/wet).

```text
input ─► [M/S encode if Mode = M/S] ─► band 1 ─► band 2 ─► … ─► band 8 ─► [M/S decode] ─► Gain ─► output
          [manual]                      (each on/off, own type/Freq/Gain/Q; Scale multiplies gains)  [manual]
          Stereo: one curve (A) for both channels; L/R: curve A for left, B for right; M/S: A for mid, B for side
 analyser: spectrum of the device's output, drawn behind the curves while Analyze is on [manual]
```

- In L/R and M/S mode each channel has its own copy of every band parameter; the Edit switch
  chooses which copy the display and dials show. In Stereo mode the Edit switch shows A, and B is
  inactive [source: 2].
- Order of bands: the eight filters are most likely a series cascade, so the response is the
  product of the bands (gains in dB add) [inferred]; the manual's advice to give two bands the same
  settings for "really drastic filtering" fits a series cascade [manual].
- Gain after the bands is the global Gain slider [manual]. Whether Scale acts before it is
  [inferred]: Scale changes band gains, Gain is a plain output level.
- Whether the analyser reads the output after the Gain slider is [measure].

## Behaviour

**Filter family.** The 2012 redesign of the filters was by Cytomic, "an analog model of a linear
SVF circuit" with low noise and smooth modulation [source: 8]. EQ Eight is thus a state-variable
(topology-preserving) filter with the response of each type mixed from its outputs [inferred].
The same filter is the Auto Filter's old "Clean" circuit [source: 9]. Measured: every 12 dB type
and every bell, shelf and notch follows the Audio EQ Cookbook (RBJ) biquad formulas to within
0.003 dB at 44.1 kHz, including the response near Nyquist, so the design is that biquad's transfer
function whatever the circuit that implements it [measured 12.4.6] (Measured › Filter types).

**Types** [manual unless tagged]:

- Low cut and high cut: 12 or 48 dB per octave. 12 dB is one 2nd-order section; 48 dB is four
  2nd-order sections in series [inferred]. Q sets the resonance at the corner; the default Q for
  cut filters is 0.71 (Butterworth) [source: 6]. Gain has no effect. Measured at Q 0.71: the 12 dB
  cut is a 2nd-order Butterworth (-3.01 dB at Freq, -12.3 dB an octave out), the 48 dB cut an
  8th-order Butterworth (-3.01 dB at Freq, -48.3 dB an octave out), built as four biquads with the
  Butterworth section Qs 0.51, 0.60, 0.90 and 2.56 [measured 12.4.6] (Measured › Filter types).
- Low shelf and high shelf: boost or cut below or above Freq; Q sets the shelf's steepness and any
  overshoot [inferred]. A tutorial notes that lowering Q makes shelves and bells less steep [source: 10].
- Peak: bell with gain and Q.
- Notch: sharp cut around Freq, Q sets width; no gain.
- Peak or notch Q 18 and shelf Q 4.12 added no gain on a Live 8 build [source: 6]; this suggests
  the unity-gain form of each filter depends on Q. In 12.4.6 the bell's gain at Freq is exactly the
  set gain at every Q from 0.1 to 18 (+12.0 dB, and -12.0 dB for a cut), so the Q does not change
  the gain at the centre [measured 12.4.6] (Measured › Bell Q). The shelf at Q 4.12 was not read.

**Resonance gain with Q.** Raising Q on a cut filter adds a resonant peak at the corner [source: 6].
The peak level for a given Q follows the filter's design (a 2nd-order low-pass at Q has peak gain
about Q/sqrt(1 - 1/(4Q²)) for large Q) [inferred]. Measured: the 12 dB low-pass at Q 2, 6 and 18
peaks at +6.3, +15.6 and +25.1 dB, the high-pass mirrors it, and the response equals the cookbook
biquad at that Q. The 48 dB cut peaks at +6.9 dB for Q 2 and +13.5 dB for Q 6
[measured 12.4.6] (Measured › Q on the cut filters). The curves fit four cookbook
biquads with the Butterworth section Qs all multiplied by one factor (1.27 at Q 2, 1.56 at Q 6), to
0.02 dB [inferred from the measurement].

**Adaptive Q.** When on, the effective Q rises with the amount of boost or cut so that the output
level stays more consistent, modelled on classic analogue EQs [manual]. The Q knob's displayed
value does not change with gain; the adaptation is applied behind it [source: 11]. Measured on a
bell at 1 kHz, Q 0.71: the effective Q is the set Q times 1.12 raised to (|gain in dB| - 6), which
is x0.71 at 3 dB, x1.0 at 6 dB and x1.97 at 12 dB, the same for a cut as for a boost; so a small
boost or cut gets a wider bell than the Q set, not a narrower one. A +12 dB high shelf is
bit-identical with Adaptive Q on or off [measured 12.4.6] (Measured › Adaptive Q). The factor 1.12
is the `AdaptiveQFactor` the saved set holds, and the unaffected shelf matches
`AdaptiveQAffectsShelves` = false [inferred from the measurement].

**Scale.** Multiplies the gain of every gain-capable band by the Scale percentage; 100 % leaves them
as set, 0 % flattens, a negative value inverts boosts to cuts [manual] [source: 3].

**Modes.** Stereo: the same filters process left and right. L/R: left and right have their own
curves. M/S: the input is encoded to mid and side, filtered with separate curves and decoded again;
the normalisation of the encoding is [measure] (the unity-gain form M = (L + R) / 2, S = (L - R) / 2
and decode L = M + S, R = M - S is [inferred]). Switching modes while playing changes which copy
of the parameters is used; ramping of that change is [measure].

**Audition.** With Audition on, clicking and holding a dot lets you hear that band alone, as the
band's own contribution to the output [manual]. What is played (the input through that filter alone,
the difference between the filtered and unfiltered signal for peaks and shelves, or a band-pass at
the filter's frequency) is [measure].

**Analyser.** A spectrum of the output behind the curves; Block default 8192 samples, Avg sets the
averaging [source: 3]. A Live 11 beta fixed a 10 dB scaling mismatch between the stand-alone Spectrum
device and EQ Eight's display [source: 4]. The curve drawn is the sum of the bands' responses in dB
[inferred].

**Oversampling.** Two times the sample rate; an anti-image and anti-alias filter pair before and
after the bands, which adds latency (amount [measure]); without it the bands are plain IIR and
add none [inferred]. A tutorial says the changes are computed at the doubled rate [source: 12].

**Smoothing.** Cytomic's description says the cutoff can be swept smoothly with automation without
the artefacts of common EQ algorithms [source: 8]; parameter smoothing times are [measure].

## Edge cases

- **All bands off:** the output equals the input times Gain [inferred].
- **Identical bands:** two bands with the same settings multiply their responses (twice the dB gain,
  and steeper cuts) [manual].
- **Gain on cut/notch types:** vertical drag edits Q instead; the Gain control is disabled [manual].
- **Scale at 0 %:** shelf and peak bands become flat; cuts and notches stay [inferred].
- **Frequency near Nyquist:** the response above the top of the range; at 44.1 kHz the 22 kHz upper
  limit is Nyquist [source: 12]; how the device behaves with Freq above Nyquist/2 at lower rates
  is [measure].
- **Very high Q:** noise floor rise at low-Q cut filters and one-channel gain differences at
  high Q reported on old builds [source: 6]; whether this still holds is [measure]. In stereo mode
  the left and right impulse responses are identical at Q 18 (bell, 12 dB low-pass) and Q 6 (48 dB
  low-pass), and the bell and 12 dB filters are cookbook-exact up to Q 18 [measured 12.4.6].
- **Mode change in L/R or M/S:** the curve not being edited stays active [manual].
- **Moving the device across tracks:** a 12.1 fix for EQ Eight going silent, no more [source: 5].
- **Sample rate change:** coefficients are recomputed; frequency stays in Hz [inferred].

## Measured

[measured 12.4.6] Behaviour runs at 44.1 kHz in Live 12.4.6 (`devices/29.15-eq-eight/`:
`eq-eight-types`, `eq-eight-bell-q`, `eq-eight-cut-q`, `eq-eight-adaptive-q` in the top100 set; `eq-eight-shelf-q`, `-freq`, `-scale` in the remaining set; `eq-eight-freq-gain`, `-freq-glide`, `-oversampling`, `-modes` in the third set, rendered by Live 12.4.7: Measured › Third set), Stereo mode, band 3 (copy A)
the only band on at Freq 1 kHz, Gain 0 dB unless stated, Adaptive Q off unless stated. Responses from
the -6 dBFS impulse (65536-point FFT) and compared with the Audio EQ Cookbook (RBJ) biquads at
44.1 kHz; the sweep agrees. Not covered by these runs: Freq 100 Hz and 10 kHz, Gain +-15 dB, the
phase, the shelves' Q (another run), modes, oversampling. These are observations, not a model.

| 2, gain -12 dB | the mirror of +12 dB: +1.04 dB, a 1 dB overshoot above 0 | 1.065 (0.02 dB) |

- **The shelves are the cookbook shelves, but the Q control is compressed**: Q 0.1, 2 and 6 behave as cookbook Q of 0.30, 1.07 and 1.50 (to 0.02 dB over 20 Hz to 20 kHz); the cookbook with the Q as written would overshoot by 4.6 dB at Q 2 and 13 dB at Q 6, where Live's overshoots 1.0 and 2.8 dB. The mapping from the Q value to the cookbook's: 0.1 -> 0.30, 0.71 -> 0.71, 2 -> 1.065, 6 -> 1.502 (a power law of exponent 0.44 at the low end falling to 0.31 at the high end) [measured 12.4.6]. The Q at 18 was not run.
- **Low and high shelves are exact mirrors** (same peak overshoots and the same deviation numbers for the same Q), and a cut is the boost's mirror in gain: the Q mapping does not depend on the sign of the gain [measured 12.4.6].

## Implementation notes

- **Building blocks:** biquad peak, low shelf, high shelf, low-pass and high-pass (RBJ
  cookbook), oversampling, parameter smoothing; Linkwitz-Riley filters are not a fit (the cut
  filters are not crossovers). An FFT library (for example `realfft`, MIT/Apache-2.0) for the
  analyser.
- **Also needed:** a notch, a TPT state-variable form (smooth modulation, Cytomic style) with the
  Q and gain relationships, a 48 dB cascade with the right per-section Qs, Adaptive Q mapping, M/S
  encode/decode, the A/B parameter pair with Edit, Scale, the analyser (FFT, windowing,
  averaging, peak hold), the filter-dot display with Audition and the display-in-main-window
  layout.
- **Hard to match:** the exact response of the Q-controlled cut filters, Adaptive Q, Audition's signal.
- **`.als` element:** `Eq8` [als 12.4.6] (`Bands.2/ParameterA/Gain/Manual` holds band 3's gain,
  +3 dB stored as `3`), with `ParameterA` and `ParameterB`
  per band [source: 7]. Elements in the set saved from Live 12.4.6 [als 12.4.6], each holding a
  `Manual` value: `On`, `GlobalGain` (dB, 0), `Scale` (stored as a fraction, 1 = 100 %),
  `AdaptiveQ` (bool), `SpectrumAnalyzer/On`, and per band (`Bands.0` – `Bands.7`) and per copy
  (`ParameterA`, `ParameterB`): `IsOn` (bool), `Mode` (0 – 7, the step order above), `Freq` (Hz),
  `Gain` (dB) and `Q` (0.7071… by default). Frequency and gain are therefore stored in their display
  units, not as the 0..1 internal value. Not parameters but plain values on the device element [als 12.4.6]: `Mode` (0 = Stereo),
  `EditMode` (false), `Precision` (0, probably Oversampling off), `AuditionOnOff` (false), `SelectedBand` (2),
  `AdaptiveQFactor` (1.12, a constant with no control: probably how strongly Adaptive Q raises the Q [inferred]),
  `AdaptiveQAffectsShelves` (false, matching 12.1's change for shelves [source: 5]), `Live8ShelfScaleLegacyMode`
  (false), and under `SpectrumAnalyzer` the analyser's settings, the same values as Spectrum's (29.38). The
  meanings of the numbers are [inferred].
