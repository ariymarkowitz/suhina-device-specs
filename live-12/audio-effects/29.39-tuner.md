# Tuner (manual 29.39)

**Family:** analysis and utility
**Note status:** dump checked

## Summary

A measurement device that finds the pitch of a monophonic input and shows the nearest note and
how many cents sharp or flat it is, in a large guitar-tuner style display. It has a classic view
(a ball on a curve, or a rotating strobe band) and a histogram view of pitch over time, a Hertz/Cents
switch, a choice of note spellings and a reference-pitch slider (410 – 480 Hz, 440 Hz by default).
It never alters the audio [manual].

## Signal flow

Shared parts: [common.md](common.md) (title bar only).

```text
input ──────────────────────────────────────────────► output (unchanged) [manual]
  └─► pitch detector (monophonic) ─► frequency ─► nearest note vs Reference ─► note, cents, Hz ─► display
        [manual]                                      [manual]
```

- The detector is for monophonic signals; polyphonic, noisy or harmonically rich input may give
  wrong results [manual].
- Since 12.1 the reference pitch comes from the selected tuning system when one is active
  [source: 2], see Behaviour.

### Context menu and hidden options

- **Note spellings** (right-click anywhere in the view): Sharps (C#), Flats (D♭), Sharps and Flats
  (C#/D♭) [manual].
- **Tuning systems** (12.1): the Tuner follows the Set's tuning system; "reference pitch is
  determined by the selected tuning" [source: 2]. The extracted manual text has nothing on it
  and how the Reference slider behaves with a non-12-tone tuning is [measure].

## Behaviour

- **Detection.** Monophonic fundamental frequency. A time-domain method (autocorrelation, YIN or
  similar) or a spectral peak-picking method are both likely [inferred]; the algorithm, the window
  length, the lowest and highest detectable frequency and the update rate are [measure]. A forum poster
  rates its accuracy better than their own Max for Live detector [source: 3], an opinion not a
  measurement.
- **Note and cents.** frequency f gives the note n = 12 · log2(f / Reference) semitones from
  the reference A, nearest integer n, cents = 100 · (n_exact − n) [inferred]. With the reference at
  440 Hz, A4 is 440 Hz [manual]. In a tuning system other than 12-tone equal temperament, the
  notes are the system's own [source: 2].
- **Displays.** Classic: ball position follows cents (the range along the curve, probably ±50 cents)
  [measure]; strobe speed follows the offset, direction the sign [manual]. Histogram: pitch over time
  with an auto-centring option [manual]. Colour changes from red to green near zero cents; the
  thresholds are [measure]. Hertz mode shows the absolute frequency [manual].
- **Audio.** Not altered [manual]. Hold behaviour when the signal stops (the display holding the last
  note or going blank) is [measure].
- **Read-only:** the tuner's note and cents are a graphic display, not parameters, so they cannot be
  read with the Live Object Model or automated [source: 3].

## Edge cases

- **No signal or silence:** display shows no note [inferred]; hold behaviour [measure].
- **Polyphonic or noisy input:** unreliable [manual].
- **Reference at 410 and 480 Hz:** the range ends [manual]; steps and typing precision [measure].
- **Very low and very high pitches:** detection limits [measure].
- **Vibrato:** display follows within the update rate [inferred].
- **Tuning system active:** reference from the tuning [source: 2].
- **Mono vs stereo input:** how the two channels are combined is [measure] (the sum is likely
  [inferred]).
- **Sample rate:** accuracy at 44.1 kHz against 96 kHz [measure].

## Measurement plan

1. **Reference range and default.** The default (440 Hz) is stored in the set as `TuningFreq` [als 12.4.6]; the dump lists no panel parameters beyond Device On [dump 12.4.6]. The default view, mode, Hertz/Cents state, Auto and spelling come from plain values in the saved set [als 12.4.6] (see Controls). Still to read from the display: Reference's range and step size, and which spelling `AccidentalSpelling` = 0 is.
2. **Accuracy.** Generate sines from 30 Hz to 5 kHz in 1/6-tone steps and with ±5, ±20, ±50 cents offsets,
   at -40, -20, -6 dBFS: displayed note and cents against the true value; Hertz readout resolution.
3. **Detection limits.** The lowest and highest frequency detected; a square, saw and a piano-like
   harmonic tone with a weak fundamental (octave errors).
4. **Response time.** Switching a tone between two pitches: the time until the display settles;
   histogram's time axis scale.
5. **Strobe and ball.** Cents at which the ball reaches the ends of the curve; strobe rotation speed against
   cents (rotations per second for 10, 20, 50 cents).
6. **Colour thresholds.** The cents at which the colour turns green.
7. **Silence/hold.** Stop the tone; observe the display.
8. **Spellings.** Right-click menu items and the accidental shown for each pitch class.
9. **Tuning systems.** Load a just-intonation .ascl tuning and check what the Tuner shows for the
   relevant pitches and the effect of the Reference slider.

## Implementation notes

- **Needs:** a monophonic pitch detector (YIN, McLeod or autocorrelation) on an analysis tap
  outside the audio callback (a lock-free queue and a non-real-time analyser thread), a
  note/cents mapper with a reference and tuning systems, and the UI (ball, strobe, histogram).
  The audio path is a plain pass-through.
- **`.als` element:** `Tuner` [dump 12.4.6] (the summary header). Parameter elements [als 12.4.6]: `On` and `TuningFreq` (Hz, 440), the Reference slider; no other stored parameter was listed. The other settings are plain values on the device element [als 12.4.6]: `ViewMode` (0 = Classic), `StrobeMode` (false = Target), `DisplayPitchInHz` (false = Cents), `HistogramFollowsPitch` (Auto, true), `HistogramCenterNote` (0) and `HistogramNoteRange` (3.5, the Histogram's scroll and zoom), `AccidentalSpelling` (0, probably the first menu item, Sharps [inferred]) and `IgnoreTuningSystem` (false, so the Tuner follows the Set's tuning system by default). The meanings of the numbers are [inferred].
