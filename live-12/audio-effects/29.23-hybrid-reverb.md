# Hybrid Reverb (manual 29.23)

**Family:** reverb and resonance
**Note status:** dump checked

## Summary

Two reverb engines in one device: a convolution engine that plays back an impulse response (IR)
and an algorithmic engine built only from delay lines, joined by a routing chooser (serial,
parallel or either engine alone) and a Blend knob. Around them sit a send gain, a predelay with
its own feedback, a four-band EQ that can sit before the algorithmic engine, and an output stage
with stereo width, a "vintage" bit and sample-rate degrader, bass mono and Dry/Wet. It is used
for ordinary rooms, halls and plates, for echo-like and drone textures (Freeze, Shimmer), and
for turning any audio file into a reverb [manual, source: 2].

## Signal flow

```text
                                 +--------------------- convolution engine ------------+
                                 |  IR (category + file) -> Attack/Decay/Size shaping   |
in -> Send -> Predelay (time or  |                                                      |
      (dry copy    beats, with   +--------------------------+---------------------------+
       bypasses)   feedback) --->                           |  Routing: Serial / Parallel / Algorithm / Convolution
                                 +--------------------------v---------------------------+
                                 |  algorithmic engine: Delay (extra ms) -> one of       |
                                 |  Dark Hall / Quartz / Shimmer / Tides / Prism         |
                                 |  with Decay, Size, Freeze, Freeze In                  |
                                 +--------------------------+---------------------------+
                                                            | Blend (Serial, Parallel)
                          EQ (4 bands; default here, or before the algorithmic engine: Pre Algo)
                                                            |
                                  Stereo -> Vintage -> Bass Mono (below 180 Hz) -> Dry/Wet -> out
dry ------------------------------------------------------------------------------^
```

- Order of the stages from left to right follows the manual: input section, engines, routing,
  EQ, output [manual 29.23.1].
- The dry signal is not affected by Send and always passes through; Dry/Wet mixes it with the
  wet path [manual].
- Serial: convolution output feeds the algorithmic engine; Blend sets how much convolution goes
  into the algorithm, 100/0 is pure convolution and 0/100 is pure algorithm fed by the
  convolution output [manual]. Parallel: both engines get the (predelayed) input; Blend is the
  level balance, 100/0 pure convolution, 0/100 pure algorithm [manual]. Algorithm and
  Convolution use one engine and Blend does nothing [manual].
- Pre Algo moves the EQ in front of the algorithmic engine "regardless of routing" [manual]. In
  Serial that means between the two engines; in Parallel and Algorithm only, in front of the
  algorithmic engine alone (the convolution branch is then unequalised) [inferred]. This is
  what "applied selectively to the two engines" in the intro means [inferred].
- The order of Stereo, Vintage and Bass Mono inside the output section is not stated; the order
  above is [inferred] and must be measured.
- The default routing was changed to Parallel in Live 11.3.20 [source: 3].

## Behaviour

- **Convolution engine.** Plays an IR through a partitioned or direct convolution; the IR is
  scaled in time by Size, and multiplied by an attack/decay envelope [manual, source: 5].
  Stereo IRs and mono IRs: how mono files are handled (duplicated to both ears) is
  [inferred]; true-stereo (4-channel) convolution is not mentioned, so assume two channels in,
  two out, with each IR channel convolved with its own input channel [measure].
- **Envelope shape.** Attack ramps the IR in from silence; Decay shapes a fall after the attack;
  the curve (linear, exponential) and whether Decay is a time constant or an end time are
  [measure]. An earlier bug made the IR envelope not decay correctly and was fixed in 12.2
  [source: 3].
- **Algorithms.** Every algorithm is a feedback delay network of delay lines with filters and
  modulation [manual, inferred]; five different structures:
  - Dark Hall: a smooth hall with a damping filter, a two-band decay (Bass X splits the tail
    and Bass Mult scales the low decay), modulation, and a Shape control that moves between
    resonant small and diffuse large [manual, source: 2].
  - Quartz: audible echoes in the tail and clear early reflections of the input; Diffusion
    changes density, Distance changes reflection spacing [manual].
  - Shimmer: dense diffuse delays with a pitch shifter in the feedback; at low Diffusion it
    is a pure delay [manual]. A pitch shifter in the loop is probably a short-grain or
    phase-vocoder shifter [inferred].
  - Tides: a smooth reverb whose output spectrum is modulated by a multiband filter whose
    modulator morphs noise, sine, square; separate phase on L and R [manual].
  - Prism: a reverb built from velvet noise (sparse random impulses with flat spectrum),
    two-band decay with a crossover [manual].
- **Decay.** T60 from 0.1 s to 60 s in the wrapper's attribute (the device's range may be
  wider or narrower) [source: 2].
- **Freeze.** Sets feedback to unity gain (infinite decay) and, unless Freeze In is on, mutes the
  input to the engine; the tail then loops. Freeze is sampled on the switch edge [manual,
  source: 2]. Fading and crossfade behaviour at the switch: [measure].
- **Predelay and feedback.** A delay line with a feedback path in front of both engines; the
  feedback is a plain gain [inferred]. Feedback near 100 % with a sync value gives rhythmic
  echoes into the reverb [manual].
- **Vintage.** Sample-rate reduction and bit-depth reduction (quantisation) of the wet signal;
  the strength slider and the four presets move both, but the actual rates and bit counts at
  each position are [measure]. Whether it adds dither or noise is [measure].
- **Bass Mono.** A fixed 180 Hz crossover; the low band is summed to mono, the upper band keeps
  its stereo. Filter type and slope are [measure] (a linear-phase or Linkwitz-Riley crossover
  is most likely [inferred]).
- **Stereo.** Mid/side width on the wet signal [inferred]; at 0 % the output is mono [manual].
- **Latency.** Convolution reverbs have latency; Ableton advises raising the buffer size or
  shortening IR Decay when the CPU spikes on small buffers or large IRs [source: 6]. The
  reported latency (if any) and whether the dry path is compensated is [measure].
- **State on bypass.** Live 12.3 fixed a bug where the old reverb tail was still audible after
  turning the device off and on again, so switching the device off must clear all state
  [source: 3].
- **Loading hostile IRs.** Live 11.0.10 fixed a crash when a one-sample-long file was loaded
  and Size was changed [source: 3]; very short, silent, long and non-finite IRs must be handled.

## Edge cases

- Routing Algorithm or Convolution: Blend has no effect and the unused engine is not run
  [manual, inferred for the CPU saving].
- Serial with Blend 100/0: the algorithm receives nothing from the convolution, so only
  convolution is heard [manual]. At 0/100 the algorithm is "fed by the convolution reverb's output" and
  the convolution is not heard directly [manual].
- Freeze with Freeze In off and no prior tail: silence [inferred].
- Size 0 %, long Decay in Dark Hall: metallic, gong-like resonance, so the delay lengths at
  Size 0 must not be so short that the loop becomes unstable [manual, inferred].
- Shimmer with Pitch at 0: no pitch shift, just a delay-based reverb [inferred].
- Tides Rate with tempo sync: tempo changes retune the rate [inferred]; transport stop: whether
  the modulator phase resets is [measure].
- IR with a different sample rate than the project: resampled on load [inferred].
- Empty User category: the file menu lists nothing and the display is blank [measure].
- Dry/Wet at 0 %: the engines can be skipped but the tail of an earlier setting should not
  jump [inferred].

## Measurement plan

1. **Parameter ranges and defaults.** ~~Send, Predelay, Feedback, Blend, algorithm Delay, Decay,
   Size, every algorithm control, EQ ranges, Stereo, Vintage, Dry/Wet~~ are answered by the dump
   [dump 12.4.6]. The defaults of the IR Attack, Decay, Size, time-shaping switch and the
   default IR come from plain values in the saved set [als 12.4.6]. Still to read: the ranges and
   tapers of the IR Attack, Decay and Size, the unit of the stored Decay, the on-screen order of the
   IR menus, what Sixth and Seventh do.
2. **Predelay and Delay.** Impulse in, convolution off (Routing: Algorithm), Dry/Wet 100 %,
   Algorithm Size minimum; measure the time of the first output sample versus Predelay and Delay
   settings (ms and sync steps). Also Feedback: ratio between successive repeats of the predelay
   at several settings, and the highest stable setting.
3. **Blend law.** Serial and Parallel: unit impulse; measure convolution and algorithm
   energy in the output at 100/0, 75/25, 50/50, 25/75, 0/100; decide linear, equal-power or
   other law. For Serial, check that 100/0 shows no algorithm tail.
4. **IR envelope.** Load a long IR made of a noise burst; render Attack and Decay at several
   settings and read the amplitude envelope. Also Size 0 %, 50 %, 100 %: length of the IR.
5. **IR handling.** Test mono, stereo, and different sample-rate IRs; impulse test of the
   category IRs' loudness (normalisation).
6. **Algorithm references.** For each of the five algorithms: impulse responses at default
   settings, then T60 versus Decay (check the wrapper's 0.1 – 60 s), and the effect of each
   algorithm control on the spectrum and the echo density. Sweep Bass X and Bass Mult (Dark Hall),
   X over, Low and High Mult (Prism) and fit crossover orders by checking decay time per band.
7. **Shimmer pitch.** Sine in; measure output frequencies at Pitch ±12, ±7 and 0 and Shimmer
   0 – 100 %; read the repeat intervals.
8. **Tides.** Noise in; measure the spectrogram at each Wave position, Tide, Phase, and the Rate
   list; record the tempo-sync list and its Hz values.
9. **Freeze.** Impulse, then toggle Freeze and Freeze In; check loop level over 60 s
   (infinite or slowly decaying), and fade times at toggling.
10. **EQ.** Sweep each band; record frequency, gain and Q ranges and the slope list for pass mode;
    check response shapes against a biquad cascade (6 dB/oct is a one-pole).
11. **Pre Algo.** Impulse with EQ extreme (low-pass at 500 Hz): compare spectra of the algorithm
    and convolution branches for Pre Algo on and off, for each routing.
12. **Output.** Stereo: correlation of L and R at 0, 100, max. Vintage: a sine sweep and a
    ramp; read the sample rate (aliasing frequency) and bit depth (step size) at each preset and
    on the slider. Bass Mono: low sine sweep with L/R out of phase; crossover frequency, slope.
13. **Dry/Wet.** Sine at 50 %: level versus 0 % and 100 %; check dry delay compensation by
    summing a pure-dry run with a wet run.
14. **Latency.** Impulse at Dry/Wet 0 % and 100 %; read the delay and the host's reported latency.

## Implementation notes

- **Building blocks:** biquad EQ bands and damping filters (6 dB/oct pass filters need a
  one-pole or first-order section), Linkwitz-Riley crossovers (Bass Mono at 180 Hz, the two-band
  decay splits), an LFO for modulation, parameter smoothing, dB conversions, a DC blocker. An
  open-source pitch shifter such as Signalsmith Stretch (MIT) could serve the Shimmer feedback.
- **Missing:** a delay-line primitive with modulated interpolated read; allpass and diffusion
  stages; a feedback delay network with orthogonal mixing matrix; a partitioned (FFT)
  convolver with a non-uniform scheme for low latency; IR loading (symphonia is in
  `suhina-media`), IR envelope and resampling; a pitch shifter in the loop; velvet-noise
  generator for Prism; a bit and sample-rate reducer for Vintage; a note-value table for
  Predelay and Tides Rate.
- **Hard to match:** the exact reverb algorithms (Dark Hall, Quartz, Shimmer, Tides, Prism)
  and the shipped IR library, which cannot be copied; only the controls and ranges can match.
  Choose own IRs (self-recorded or CC0) for the categories.
- **Plug-in boundary and threading:** convolution must be allocated off the audio thread
  (IR loading is a command with an async worker; swap the loaded convolver atomically).
- **Parameters to expose:** the wrapper ranges above fit as the normalised 0 – 1 values; the
  wrapper's own attribute names (`decay`, `size`, `damping`, `mod`, `shape`, `basscrossover`,
  `bassmult`, `diffusion`, `distance`, `low_damping`, `pitchshift`, `shimmer`, `tides`, `waveform`,
  `phase`, `rate`, `crossover`, `lowmult`, `highmult`) are useful as stable ids [source: 2].
- **`.als` element:** `Hybrid` [als 12.4.6] (not `HybridReverb`). Parameter elements in the set saved
  from Live 12.4.6 [als 12.4.6], each holding a `Manual` value: `On`; `PreDelay_Sync`, `PreDelay_Time`
  (stored in seconds: 0.01 = 10 ms), `PreDelay_Sixteenth`, `PreDelay_FeedbackTime`,
  `PreDelay_FeedbackSixteenth`; `Algorithm_Type` (index), `Algorithm_Delay`, `Algorithm_Freeze`,
  `Algorithm_FreezeIn`, `Algorithm_Decay` (s), `Algorithm_Size`, `Algorithm_Damping`,
  `Algorithm_Diffusion`, `Algorithm_Modulation`, `Algorithm_Shape` (0..1), `Algorithm_BassMultiplier`
  (1 = 100 %), `Algorithm_BassCrossover` (Hz), `Algorithm_Shimmer`, `Algorithm_PitchShift` (st),
  `Algorithm_TidesAmount`, `Algorithm_TidesRate` (index), `Algorithm_TidesWaveform`,
  `Algorithm_TidesPhaseOffset` (degrees), `Algorithm_Quartz_LowDamping`, `Algorithm_Quartz_Distance`,
  `Algorithm_Prism_HighMultiplier` and `_LowMultiplier` (1 = 100 %), `Algorithm_Prism_CrossoverFrequency`
  (Hz), `Algorithm_Prism_Sixth`, `Algorithm_Prism_Seventh`; `Eq_On`, `Eq_PreAlgo`, `Eq_LowBandType`
  (index), `Eq_LowBandFrequency` (Hz), `Eq_LowBandGain` (linear gain, 1 = 0 dB), `Eq_LowBandSlope`
  (index), `Eq_Peak1Frequency`, `Eq_Peak1Gain` (linear), `Eq_Peak1Q`, the same for Peak 2,
  `Eq_HighBandType`, `Eq_HighBandFrequency`, `Eq_HighBandGain`, `Eq_HighBandSlope`; `Send` (linear
  gain, 1 = 0 dB), `Routing` (index), `ConvoAlgoBlend` (0..1), `Vintage` (index), `StereoWidth`
  (1 = 100 %), `BassMono`, `DryWet` (fraction). Percent-type controls are stored as 0..1 fractions,
  times in seconds, frequencies in Hz. The IR controls are not parameters but plain values on the device element
  [als 12.4.6]: `Convolution_IrPostProcessingOn` (time shaping, true), `Convolution_IrAttackTime` (0),
  `Convolution_IrDecayTime` (20, probably seconds), `Convolution_IrSize` (1 = 100 %), and `CurrentTab` (0). The
  IR itself is under `ImpulseResponseHandler`: `SampleSlot` holds a reference (relative path, absolute path,
  length in samples, sample rate) to each of the two mono files of the default IR, L and R, and
  `SampleSlotTrueStereo` is a second slot, empty here, probably for four-channel true-stereo IRs [inferred].
  The category and file are not stored as indexes; they follow from the file path [inferred].
- **Max for Live relationship.** The five algorithms are available as Max objects named
  `abl.dsp.darkhall~`, `abl.dsp.quartz~`, `abl.dsp.shimmer~`, `abl.dsp.tides~` and
  `abl.dsp.prism~`, which Cycling '74 documents; their attribute lists are the best public
  parameter definition of each algorithm [source: 2]. The wrappers do not include the device's
  Delay, Predelay, Send, EQ, Stereo, Vintage or Bass Mono, which are outside the algorithms.
  Do not confuse them with `abl.device.reverb~`, which Cycling '74 describes as a plate reverb
  with `damping`, `decay`, `mix` and `predelay` (0 – 10 s) and which is not this device
  [source: 2].
