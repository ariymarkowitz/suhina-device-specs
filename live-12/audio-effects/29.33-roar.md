# Roar (manual 29.33)

**Family:** distortion
**Note status:** dump checked, partly measured (top100 and remaining runs, [Measured](#measured))

## Summary

Roar is a Live 12 Suite effect that saturates and colours sound in up to three stages. Each stage
has a shaper (a non-linear curve), a multimode filter and a few levels; seven routing modes decide
how the stages are connected. A feedback loop with its own band-pass filter, delay time and a
compressor, two LFOs, an envelope follower, a noise source and a modulation matrix make it a
sound-design device as much as a saturator [manual]. It arrived in Live 12.0 with five routings
[source: 8]; the Delay routing, the Dispersion filter, the external and MIDI sidechain, the Envelope
Hold toggle and LED modulation indicators came in 12.2 [source: 8]. This note describes the 12.2 and later device, because that is what
the manual chapter describes.

## Signal flow

Order of the stages as far as the manual and sources say. Where the exact tap of a connection is
unknown it is marked [measure].

```text
                               ┌────────────────── feedback path (Feedback Amount) ──────────────────┐
                               │   (inside it: band-pass filter → delay (Time/Sync/Note) → invert → gate)
                               ▼                                                                      │
input ─► Drive ─► Tone (low-shelf tilt) ─► [ ROUTING: stages 1..3 ] ─► Color Compensation ─► Compressor ─┴► Output Gain ─► hard clip ─► Dry/Wet ─► out
 │        [manual] [manual]                  each stage:                [manual] mirror      [manual]    [manual]        [manual]      [manual]
 │                                           filter(Pre) → shaper → shaper level → filter(Post)
 │                                           (filter before or after the shaper, per stage) [manual]
 └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────► dry ───────────────────────────────────┘
          envelope follower listens to the input (or to the external sidechain) and feeds the modulation matrix [manual]
```

Facts behind the picture:

- Drive and Tone come first and "the gain stages follow the Input section" [manual].
- Colour Compensation applies a mirrored Tone filter to the output of the distortion stages [manual].
- The compressor acts on the output signal, "and thereby" on the signal that is fed back [manual], so the
  feedback tap is at or after the compressor [inferred]. Whether the tap is before or after Output
  Gain, and where the feedback re-enters (before Drive, before Tone or at the stage input) is [measure].
- Output Gain comes after the stages and compressor, then a hard clipper, then Dry/Wet [manual]. The French and Spanish
  Ableton product pages say the same [source: 3].
- The dry signal for Dry/Wet is the device input; whether it is delayed to match latency is [measure].
- A design remark: each shaper is followed by a filter "to tame harmonics you don't want" [source: 3]; the filter's
  Pre toggle moves it before the shaper [manual].

### Routing modes (what is connected to what)

| Mode | Stages used | Blend | Connection | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Single | 1 | no | input → stage 1 | [manual] |
| Serial | 2 | yes | stage 1 → stage 2; Blend crossfades between "stage 1 alone" and "stages 1 and 2 together" | [manual] |
| Parallel | 2 | yes | both stages see the input; Blend crossfades stage 1 ↔ stage 2 | [manual] |
| Multi Band | 3 | no | input split in Low, Mid, High bands by two crossovers; one stage per band; bands summed | [manual] |
| Mid Side | 2 | yes | mid signal and side signal processed independently (one stage each, mid then side) | [manual], stage order [inferred] |
| Feedback | 2 (stage 1 on the input, stage 2 in the feedback loop) | yes | input and its feedback "processed independently"; the sources describe one shaper plus a feedback loop that contains its own shaper, in parallel | [manual], [source: 2] |
| Delay (12.2) | 2 | yes | stage 1 → delay → stage 2; the delayed signal enters stage 2 unscaled by the feedback gain (single distorted repeats, slapback); stage 2's output feeds back into stage 2 scaled by the feedback gain (long tails) | [manual] |

For Blend in Mid Side, Feedback and Delay the manual only says "blend between its two gain stages" [manual]; the exact law is [measure].
Cycling '74's description of the Max wrapper names Blend for "serial, parallel or mid/side" only [source: 4]. Whether Blend also
exists in Feedback and Delay depends on the version [measure].

## Behaviour

**Input stage.** Drive is a gain from -24 to +24 dB in dB steps [source: 4]. Tone is a first- or second-order low shelf at Tone Frequency whose gain is the Tone Amount mapped to some dB range [measure], with a complementary high-frequency change so that the whole behaves as a tilt: positive values cut lows and raise highs, negative the opposite [manual], [source: 2]. Probably one low-shelf filter (gain negative for positive Amount), with the highs relatively raised by normalisation [inferred]. Colour Compensation applies the inverse of this filter to the output of the stages [manual], so with the toggle on the net linear response is flat and only the shapers feel the tilt [inferred].

**Shapers.** Each shaper is a memoryless curve or a short non-linear system, applied to `Amount-scaled input + Bias` [inferred]. Amount sets the drive into the curve; higher Amount reaches the non-linear region sooner [manual]. The curve is drawn in the display with the signal level on it [manual]. The twelve curves, with what the manual says and what is most likely [inferred]:

| Curve | Manual says | Likely structure [inferred] |
|---|---|---|
| Soft Sine | smooth, warm, pleasant even when pushed hard | `sin(clamp(g·x, ±π/2))`, i.e. a quarter-sine that flattens at ±1 |
| Digital Clip | hard clipping; harsh high-order harmonics | `clamp(g·x, -1, 1)` (the Max enum calls it Hard clip [source: 4]) |
| Bit Crusher | quantised amplitude; Amount lowers the bit count and strengthens a built-in compander; works well with bias modulation; obvious on quiet signals | compand (A-law/µ-law-like) → round to 2^n levels → expand; n falls as Amount rises; the same family as Redux's Shape [inferred] |
| Diode Clipper | virtual analogue clipping circuit; as smooth as Soft Sine with less top end | antiparallel-diode clipper in a one-pole RC, solved per sample (Newton or a closed-form Lambert-W approximation); explains the lost highs [inferred] |
| Tube Preamp | soft analogue saturation that keeps transients and dynamics even at high Amount | asymmetric soft curve (tanh-like with a bias term) with slow gain behaviour so transients pass; possibly a one-stage triode model [inferred] |
| Half Wave Rectifier | completely asymmetric; adds crunch to drums | `max(x, 0)` with a smooth knee, plus the DC removal an offset curve needs [inferred] |
| Full Wave Rectifier | shifts the signal up by an octave | `|x|` (smoothed knee); output has twice the input frequency and no DC after a blocker [inferred] |
| Polynomial | moves between a sine-like curve and complex waveforms; modulates well; metallic | low-order polynomial or Chebyshev mix whose coefficients move with Amount [inferred] |
| Fractal | lots of high harmonics | iterated folding, a sum of the curve at several scales [inferred] |
| Tri Fold | triangle wavefolder; harsher than Fractal | `fold(x) = 1 - |((x+1) mod 4) - 2|` style triangle wave of the driven input [inferred] |
| Noise Injection | stereo noise followed by smooth distortion; saturation with dirt | independent L and R noise added before a soft curve [inferred]; noise level may follow Amount [measure] |
| Shards | the slope of its stereo curve segments changes continuously so the input breaks apart rhythmically | piecewise-linear curve with segment slopes driven by an internal LFO, left and right different [inferred] |

Bias: shifts the working point of the curve; an asymmetric curve then makes even harmonics; at extreme values the signal "goes completely quiet" [manual], which suggests the bias pushes the input into the flat part of the curve and the output is a constant [inferred]. After a bias the output has DC that a blocker removes [inferred]. SOS reports that with analogue models bias makes the sound break up, and with digital models it sounds like sweeping through a wavetable of curves [source: 2].

Shaper Level compensates level changes of the curve [manual]; range ±24 dB [source: 4]. It is not automatic, it is a user gain [inferred].

**Filters.** Eight in the Max reference, nine in the manual:

- LP, HP, BP, Notch: second-order (12 dB/octave) state-variable filters with Resonance [inferred]. The slope is [measure].
- Peak: bell filter with Filter Peak Gain ±12 dB and Resonance as bandwidth [inferred].
- Morph: a state-variable filter whose output moves with Filter Morph. The manual says LP to HP with BP in between [manual]; the Max reference says it cycles through low-pass, high-pass, band-pass and notch [source: 4]. At Morph 0.5 the render is bit-identical to the HP filter's [measured 12.4.6], so the midpoint is HP; the rest of the cycle is [measure].
- Comb: a feedback comb filter, a short delay (period from Frequency) with feedback from Resonance; modulating Frequency gives flanging [manual], [inferred].
- Resampling: sample-rate reduction (sample and hold with images) whose rate follows Filter Frequency; has no Resonance [manual], [inferred]; SOS says it models a low-sample-rate machine and pairs well with the Bit Crusher [source: 2].
- Dispersion: a chain of allpass filters that delays frequencies by different amounts without changing level; metallic, spring-like when modulated [manual]. Number of allpass sections and what Frequency and Resonance control are [measure] (likely a few dozen first-order or second-order sections; Frequency the centre of the delay change, Resonance its peakiness) [inferred].

Pre moves a stage's filter in front of its shaper [manual]. The filter is after the shaper by default [source: 3].

**Routing details.**

- Serial: output = `crossfade(stage1(x), stage2(stage1(x)), blend)` [manual]. The crossfade law (linear or equal-power) is [measure].
- Parallel: `crossfade(stage1(x), stage2(x), blend)` [manual].
- Multi Band: crossover filters at the Low and High frequencies split the input into three bands, processed by stages 1, 2 and 3, summed [manual]. Measured at 200 Hz and 2 kHz: 24 dB per octave skirts, -6 dB at the Low crossover (-6.2 dB at 200 Hz), flat magnitude sum, with the sum's all-pass tap 2 ms behind the first (Measured › The loaded routing mode is Multi Band) [measured 12.4.6]. Linkwitz-Riley 4th order fits [inferred]; the phase behaviour was not read.
- Mid Side: encode `M = (L+R)/2`, `S = (L-R)/2`, process each, decode [inferred].
- Feedback: stage 1 acts on the input, stage 2 on the feedback signal; Blend mixes them [manual], [source: 2].
- Delay: stage 1 → delay → stage 2, the first repeat is unscaled; stage 2's output feeds only itself, scaled by the feedback gain, which makes the tail [manual]. The delay is the same one as the Feedback Mode / Time controls [inferred].

**Feedback loop.** Output of the compressor goes into a band-pass (Frequency, Width), a delay with Time, Synced, Triplet, Dotted or Note length, optional inversion and a gate, and returns scaled by Feedback Amount [manual]. The compressor inside the loop lowers the feedback when the output is loud, so it grows again as the compressor releases; this "breathing" is intended [source: 3]. Its attack and release are fixed [source: 3]. The Max reference gives the loop delay range 1 – 500 ms [source: 4]; with a delay that short and a pitch-tuned Note mode, the loop is a comb filter or a Karplus-Strong-like resonator [source: 2], [inferred]. In Note mode the delay is `1 / note frequency` [inferred]; the note comes from the Feedback Time control, or from MIDI when MIDI > FB Note is on [manual]; note range and tuning reference are [measure]. The Feedback Gate follows the input level: it detects that the input has stopped and fades the loop out [manual]; threshold and fade time are [measure].

**Compressor.** One compression-amount control, a sidechain high-pass option, fixed times [manual], [source: 3]. Most likely a feed-forward peak or RMS compressor with automatic threshold and make-up where Amount sets ratio and threshold together [inferred]; all [measure].

**Output stage.** Output Gain → hard clip at 0 dBFS (digital full scale) → Dry/Wet [manual], [source: 3]. A 1 kHz tone driven 12 dB over full scale comes out at +1.4 dB (rms x sqrt 2) with 14.8 % THD, where a hard clip would give +2.5 dB and 35 %: the clip is a soft one, with a knee just below 0 dBFS [measured 12.4.6] (Measured › Reading the other Roar runs). Because of the clipper, Roar's wet output cannot exceed full scale, even with high Feedback [inferred].

**Modulation.** Sources produce values in a fixed range; the matrix multiplies each by its amount and the Global Modulation Amount, and adds the result to the target's normalised value [inferred]. The LFO is shapeable (Morph morphs among sine, ramp and square [source: 3]) and smoothed by a low-pass; Free rates 0.01 – 10 Hz [source: 4]; synced rates in four sync modes [manual]. The envelope follower: band-pass (Frequency, Width) on the detector input → rectify → attack/release smoother → threshold → gain [manual], [source: 4]. Hold completes attack before release starts [manual]. Noise: Simplex (interrate modulation, irregular), Wander (smoothed random), S & H, Brown [manual]; noise sources run at 0.01 – 10 Hz [source: 4]. LEDs in the Modulation column show which sources reach a control and with which source colour [manual].

## Edge cases

- **Feedback above the compressor's ability:** the compressor holds the loop from running away; with a high Feedback Amount, high shaper Amount and no gate the loop can howl or turn into noise [source: 2]. The output hard clipper bounds it [manual].
- **Feedback Gate off, silence in:** feedback continues indefinitely [manual]; with the gate on it fades [manual].
- **MIDI sidechain on:** Feedback Mode and Feedback Amount are inactive [manual].
- **Routing switch while playing:** stages not in the new mode are muted; whether state (delay lines, filters) is cleared or crossfaded is [measure].
- **Stage off:** the stage's tab is disabled; in Multi Band the band of an off stage is silent, not passed through [measured 12.4.6] (Measured › The loaded routing mode is Multi Band); behaviour in Serial/Parallel (pass-through versus silent) is [measure].
- **Bias extremes:** silence, as described [manual]. Check for DC and clicks.
- **Reactivating the device:** LFOs keep running in Free mode [source: 8]; the Noise modulator no longer resets to 0 [source: 8].
- **Tempo changes:** synced LFO, noise and feedback times follow tempo; whether the feedback delay time is smoothed or jumps is [measure].
- **Mono input:** Mid Side gives silence in Side [inferred]; Noise Injection and Shards use stereo-different curves, so mono in can give stereo out [manual].
- **Sample rate:** whether oversampling is used and whether the Resampling filter's rate is tied to the sample rate (as a fraction of it) is [measure].
- **Latency:** unknown; Live reports latency for devices with oversampling or lookahead [measure].

| all stages | -0.1 | -1.4 | -11.7 | -25.0 | -12.7 | -3.5 | -0.6 | -0.1 |
| stage 1 only | -0.1 | -1.4 | -11.4 | -35.6 | -68.8 | -105 | -120 | -120 |

- **Stage 1 sees only the low band, stage 2 the middle band, stage 3 the high band:** stage 1 alone passes the lows and nothing above about 400 Hz, stage 3 alone passes only the highs, and the sum of the three (the "all stages" row) is the sum of what each stage lets through. The unlikely-default doubt in Controls is settled: the device loads in Multi Band.
- **Crossover:** the low band is -6.2 dB at 200 Hz (-3 dB at 159 Hz) and falls 20 to 24 dB per octave above it (-26 dB at 400 Hz, -35 dB at 500 Hz; the 500 Hz low-pass adds its own 12 dB per octave to the slope, giving -68 dB at 1 kHz); this is the shape of a Linkwitz-Riley 4th-order low-pass at the Low crossover [measured 12.4.6] [inferred: Linkwitz-Riley]. The high band is -8.8 dB at 2 kHz, -6 dB at 2.3 kHz and -2.6 dB at 3 kHz, falling about 27 dB per octave below 2 kHz (stage 3's notch at 1 kHz is in the path and is probably the cause of the 2.8 dB offset from a -6 dB crossover at 2 kHz) [measured 12.4.6]. With all stages at pass-through (no filters, no shapers) the bands sum to a flat magnitude: sweep response within 0.1 dB of the bypass from 31.5 Hz to 16 kHz, with the impulse spread by an all-pass sum (a second tap 2.0 ms after the first and 36 dB lower in the default case of `roar-filter-types`) [measured 12.4.6].
- **A stage that is off is silent in Multi Band:** with stages 2 and 3 off the response above 500 Hz falls to the noise floor; the band is not passed through [measured 12.4.6]. (This is the Multi Band case of "Stage off" in Edge cases; it says nothing for the other routings.)
- **Blend does nothing in Multi Band:** Blend at 0 and 100 render bit-identically to the default render (same MD5), as the manual implies by leaving Blend out of this mode [measured 12.4.6].

### Reading the other Roar runs

Because the loaded mode is Multi Band, **stage 1 processes only the band below about 200 Hz**, and the probe's 1 kHz tone, noise at 200 Hz and above and most of the impulse go through stage 2 (shaper and filter off in these runs), not through the stage whose settings the runs change. Consequences [measured 12.4.6]:

- `roar-shaper-types-a` and `roar-shaper-amount-bias` could not read the shaper curves from the 1 kHz staircase: all 13 renders (six types, Amount 10, 25, 50, 100 %, Bias +/-0.5) show the same staircase to within 0.7 dB of gain at -60 dBFS and 0.1 dB at 0 dBFS in. Drive +12 dB gives a gain of +12.09 dB up to -18 dBFS in; the small differences (12.01 to 12.65 dB at -60 dBFS) come from 1 kHz energy that leaks into the low band.
- **What the staircase does show is Roar's output stage:** the gain stays at +12.09 dB up to -18 dBFS in, the output level (rms x sqrt 2, harmonics included) at -12 / -6 / 0 dBFS in is -0.05 / +1.10 / +1.44 dBFS, THD 0.01 / 9.9 / 14.8 %, with the 3rd harmonic at -85 / -20.7 / -16.8 dB and the 5th at -91 / -29 / -34 dB and the even ones below -97 dB. A plain hard clip at 0 dBFS of the same signal would read +1.94 dB, 23 % THD and a 3rd harmonic at -12.9 dB at -6 dBFS in (amplitude 2 before the clip), and +2.52 dB, 35 % and -10.3 dB at 0 dBFS in (amplitude 4); Saturator's Digital Clip renders exactly these values at those drives, Roar does not. So Roar's output stage is a symmetric soft clip, softer than the "hard clip" the manual names [measured 12.4.6] (the hard-clip figures computed for a clipped sine and checked against Saturator's Digital Clip). Its knee starts at about 0 dBFS (the gain is already 0.14 dB down at 0 dBFS out).
- **Low band only (sweep at -12 dBFS, so 0 dBFS into the shaper after Drive +12 dB):** the fundamental's gain in the low band tells a little about each shaper. At 63 Hz, Amount 50 %: Soft Sine +10.9 dB, Digital Clip +10.9, Diode Clipper +12.1, Tube Preamp +11.5, Polynomial +5.3, Tri Fold -5.4 dB, against +12.0 above 400 Hz where stage 1 is not involved (unprocessed). Bias +/-0.5 gives the same +10.9 dB as Bias 0 and is symmetric in sign (both bias values render within 0.1 dB of each other) [measured 12.4.6].
- **Noise through the low band (-18 dBFS rms, 4 s):** the low band's gain over its baseline of +11 dB grows with Amount: +4 dB at 10 %, +9 dB at 25 %, +16 dB at 50 %, +19 dB at 100 % (Soft Sine, 63 Hz), i.e. Amount adds small-signal gain ahead of the saturating curve; Tri Fold and Polynomial add less (+13 and +14 dB at 50 %) and Diode Clipper more (+18 dB). Bias +/-0.5 turns the added low-band gain into a loss of up to 6 dB (5.6 to 9.4 dB against a baseline of +11), the "goes quiet" side of Bias [measured 12.4.6]. These are noise-level readings that include distortion energy and so are not a transfer curve [inferred].

[measured 12.4.7; the reading as a bipolar offset is inferred from the Output cell and the pairs of plateaus].
- **The three null results are not findings about the targets.** (1) Blend (`ModulationMatrix_Blend`, the input Blend of the two-stage routings) has no control in Single, so nothing was to be heard. (2) `Stage1Morph` is the Stage 1 *filter's* Morph (filter type Morph), not the LFO's Morph (`Lfo1Morph`, a separate target); the run left the filter Morph at 0, where the cell's negative half clamps at 0 (Low Pass) and its positive half reaches 1.0, which is also Low Pass (Morph cycles LP, BP, HP, Notch, LP), so both halves were the same filter: the case needs the Morph knob at a middle value [inferred from the Morph cycle]. (3) Feedback Amount was stored at 0 % with the cell at 1, and the render did not change in either half, but a test in the Live window found Feedback Amount doing something in Single routing (user report), so the stored cell, the base or the reading missed it; the render shows no effect, which is only what it shows [measured 12.4.7; the cause is open].
- **The sources work on the same cells.** LFO 2 (a 1 Hz Square) into Input gives the render of LFO 1 into Input to the 0.1 dB (-1.3 and -20.1 dB), so the two LFOs run in step at start; Noise (Sample & Hold at 4 Hz) into Input gives random levels from -20 to -1.2 dB held for a quarter of a second each (16 distinct levels in 4 s); Env into Stage 1 Amount holds -2.9 dB with the distortion 27.7 dB under the fundamental (the default render has it 7.0 dB under), for the whole steady tone: the follower's constant output (the tone's 0.25 peak) lowers Amount [measured 12.4.7].
- **Global Mod Amt scales every cell linearly:** the Output cell with Global 50 % swings 30 dB (the low end at -31.2 dB instead of -49.1), with Global 100 and 200 % the same -49.1 dB (the 60 dB and 120 dB swings are both cut at the bottom of the range), and the high end is the same +2.1 dB in all three (the clip after Output) [measured 12.4.7].
- **LFO 2 into LFO 1's Rate stops the 1 Hz switching:** with LFO 2 a 0.25 Hz Square into the Rate cell and the Output cell on LFO 1, the gain no longer switches every half second; it holds -48 dB until 22.7 s, then changes only at 22.7, 24.6, 26.6 and 28.6 s, every 1.9 to 2.0 s, at LFO 2's half cycles (-3, +3, -3 dB between), so the Rate cell takes LFO 1 to a near standstill and moves it a little in each half [measured 12.4.7; the "near standstill" is read from the long plateaus].

- **Still open after the fifth set.** Feedback Amount and the filter Morph again (Morph in the middle, Feedback Amount at 50 %), Blend in a two-stage routing, matrix cells below 1, negative cells and the other stages' targets.

## Measurement plan

1. **Input ranges and Tone (Drive, Tone Amount, Tone Frequency, Colour Compensation).** Single mode, shaper off, filter off, compression 0 %, feedback 0 %. Sine sweep 20 Hz – 20 kHz at -30 dBFS (so nothing clips). Render with Tone Amount at -100, -50, 0, 50, 100 % and Tone Frequency at 80 Hz, 500 Hz, 2 kHz. From the frequency responses read the shelf gains, slopes and pivot; then turn Colour Compensation on and confirm the response goes flat. (Ranges, defaults and displays of these controls are in the dump [dump 12.4.6].)
   Done in the loaded routing: Tone Amount -100 to +100 % at Frequency 180 Hz, +100 % at 2 kHz, Color Compensation on (Measured › Remaining runs › Tone). Tone is a +-12 dB tilt at the top, a deeper cut than boost at the bottom, and Color Compensation cancels it exactly. The pivot law is not fitted.
2. **Shaper transfer curves.** Single mode, filter off, Tone 0. Send a slow ramp or a 20 Hz sine from -1 to +1 and plot output against input at Amount 25, 50, 100 % and Bias 0; repeat for each of the 12 types. For types with state (Diode Clipper, Tube Preamp, Noise Injection, Shards, Bit Crusher) also sweep frequency (50 Hz, 1 kHz, 8 kHz) and level. Fit formulas. Bias: repeat at Bias -100, -50, 50, 100 % and look at DC and when output goes to silence.
   Not answered: stage 1 sees only the band below 200 Hz in the loaded Multi Band routing, so the 1 kHz staircase does not reach the shaper.    The six other shapers could not be read from the 1 kHz staircase either; the low-band sweep tells the Full Wave Rectifier apart (it removes the fundamental) and little else (Measured › Remaining runs › Shapers in the low band).
   More (third set, routing stored in the set): all twelve types in Single at Drive +12 dB and Amount 50 %, Soft Sine at Amount 25 and 100 % and Bias ±50 % (Measured › Third set): gains, saturated levels, odd or even harmonics; the 20 Hz ramp curves and the state of the stateful types are still open.

## Implementation notes

- **Building blocks:** biquad low shelf (Tone), peak, low-pass, high-pass and band-pass (Peak, LP/HP/BP and the feedback band-pass; a band-pass Q from the width in octaves); Linkwitz-Riley crossovers for Multi Band (two in series with all-pass compensation); oversampling (every shaper should run oversampled; Roar's own rate is unknown); a DC blocker (after bias and half-wave); parameter smoothing; an LFO with the five basic waveforms; one-pole smoothing (LFO smooth, noise and envelope smoothing); dB conversions.
- **Also needed:** a state-variable filter with resonance and Morph (biquads cannot sweep and morph cheaply); a comb filter and a delay line with fractional interpolation (also for the feedback loop and Delay routing); an all-pass chain (Dispersion); sample-rate reduction (Resampling filter, shared with Redux); an envelope follower with attack, hold, release and a band-pass front end; the feedback compressor (a compressor block shared with Compressor, 29.9); noise generators (Simplex, Wander, S&H, Brown); LFO morph, smooth and free-run phase continuity, synced phases derived from the song position; a modulation matrix (per-parameter modulation amounts on top of automation; targets can be any parameter, including other sources); twelve shaper curves; a diode clipper solver; wavefolders; stereo shapers (Noise Injection, Shards).
- **Hard to match:** the exact shaper curves (twelve, several are stateful or random), the compressor's behaviour, the feedback loop's tap points, Note mode tuning, Dispersion, Morph. Start with: Single/Serial/Parallel, Soft Sine, Digital Clip, Diode Clipper (tanh-like first), Tube Preamp, LP/HP/BP/Notch/Peak, input section, output, Dry/Wet; then Multi Band, Mid Side; then feedback; then modulation; then the exotic curves.
- **Parameter design:** parameters per stage (three stage copies, each with 10+ parameters) plus the modulation matrix; use stable ids like `stage1.shaperAmount`. Stage count follows the routing mode: keep all three stages' parameters always present and ignore the unused ones.
- **Related:** Overdrive (29.27) is a single biased soft-curve stage with a band-pass in front, a tone filter behind and a Dynamics control; Roar's Single mode with Soft Sine and a band-pass filter Pre is the nearest to it, though Roar's Drive is ±24 dB and its Tone a tilt.
- **`.als` element:** `Roar` [dump 12.4.6]. Parameter elements [als 12.4.6] (stored units where they differ from the display), with N = 1, 2, 3 for the three stages (identical defaults): `On`; input: `Input_InputGain` (Drive, linear gain 1 = 0 dB), `Input_ToneAmount`, `Input_ToneFrequency` (Hz), `Input_ColorOn`, `Input_Blend` (0.5), `Input_LowMidCrossover`, `Input_MidHighCrossover` (Hz); per stage: `StageN_On`, `StageN_Shaper_On`, `StageN_Shaper_Type`, `StageN_Shaper_Amount`, `StageN_Shaper_Bias`, `StageN_Shaper_Trim` (Shaper Level, linear gain, 1 = 0 dB), `StageN_Filter_On`, `StageN_Filter_Type`, `StageN_Filter_Frequency` (Hz), `StageN_Filter_Resonance`, `StageN_Filter_Morph`, `StageN_Filter_PeakGain` (linear gain, 1 = 0 dB), `StageN_Filter_PreOn`; feedback: `Feedback_FeedbackAmount`, `Feedback_FeedbackTimeMode`, `Feedback_FeedbackTime` (seconds, 0.0182), `Feedback_FeedbackSyncedRate`, `Feedback_FeedbackNote`, `Feedback_FeedbackFrequency` (Hz), `Feedback_FeedbackBandwidth`, `Feedback_FeedbackInvert`, `Feedback_FeedbackGateOn`; modulation: `ModulationSources_Lfo1_` and `ModulationSources_Lfo2_` with `RateMode`, `Rate` (Hz), `SyncedRate`, `Sixteenth`, `Waveform`, `Morph`, `SmoothingAmount` (0.05 = 5 %); `ModulationSources_Envelope_Amount` (Env Gain, linear gain, 1 = 0 dB), `_Attack` and `_Release` (seconds), `_HoldOn`, `_Threshold` (linear, 0 for −inf), `_Frequency` (Hz), `_Width`; `ModulationSources_Noise_` with `RateMode`, `Rate`, `SyncedRate`, `Sixteenth`, `Type`, `SmoothingAmount`; `GlobalModulationAmount` (1 = 100 %); `Output_CompressionAmount` (0.25), `Output_CompressorHighpassFilterOn`, `Output_OutputGain` (linear gain, 1 = 0 dB), `Output_DryWet` (1); sidechain: `AudioSideChain/OnOff`, `AudioSideChain/DryWet` (S/C Mix; S/C Gain has no element in the saved set listing). Not parameters but plain values on the device element [als 12.4.6]: `Input_RoutingMode` (3, see Controls), `Feedback_FeedbackNoteSource` (0, probably MIDI > FB Note off), `HiQuality` (false), `SteppedDiscreteParameterModulation` (true, a setting no source describes: probably whether modulating a stepped parameter moves in steps [inferred]), the matrix as `ModulationMatrix_<target>_<source>` per target and source (`Lfo1`, `Lfo2`, `Env`, `Noise`), all 0, the sidechains as `AudioSideChain` and `MidiSideChain` routings (targets `AudioIn/None` and `MidiIn/None`), and view state (`ShowModulationFoldout`, `SelectedStageTab`, `SelectedModulationTab`, `SelectedModulationSource`, `SidechainIsExpanded`).
