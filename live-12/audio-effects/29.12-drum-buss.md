# Drum Buss (manual 29.12)

**Family:** dynamics
**Note status:** dump checked

## Summary

An all-in-one drum processor: an optional fixed compressor, three flavours of drive distortion, a mid-high "crunch" distortion, a low-pass "damp" filter, a transient shaper above 100 Hz, and a tunable low-end booster (Boom) with its own decay, a dry/wet mix and an output gain. Meant for a whole drum group, to thicken it and make the parts sit together.

## Signal flow

The manual describes the parts and gives only a partial order; the order below is [inferred] unless marked.

```text
input ─► Trim [manual: "before any processing"]
      ─► Comp (fixed compressor, optional) [manual: before the distortion]
      ─► Drive ─► distortion type Soft / Medium / Hard [manual]
      ─► Crunch (sine-shaped distortion on mid-high frequencies) [manual]
      ─► Damp (low-pass) [manual; one tutorial: after the processing, before the Boom section]
      ─► Transients (shaper for frequencies over 100 Hz) [manual]
      ─┬─► (direct) ───────────────────────────────────────────────┐
       └─► Boom resonant filter (Freq, Boom amount) ─► Decay ──────┼─► sum ─► Dry/Wet ─► Output Gain ─► output
            [Boom Audition solos this branch]                      │
```

- Decay acts on the incoming (after drive and distortion) signal when Boom is 0 %, and on both the incoming and the Boom-processed signal when Boom is above 0 % [manual].
- Dry/Wet blends processed and dry; Output Gain is the gain applied to the processed signal [manual]. Whether Output Gain also affects the dry part is [measure].

## Behaviour

**Comp.** A fixed-parameter compressor placed before the distortion [manual]. Its values are not published: "fast attack, medium release, moderate ratio, ample makeup" [manual]. Guesses for a first match: attack 1–5 ms, release 100–200 ms, ratio 3:1 to 4:1, makeup about +6 dB, threshold fixed [inferred] [measure]. The compression amount depends on the input level, and Trim reduces it [source: 3].

**Drive and distortion type.** Drive scales the signal before a nonlinearity [manual]. Soft: a smooth waveshaper (tanh-like); Medium: a limiting curve (a soft threshold followed by a stricter limit); Hard: a hard clipper with a bass boost [manual] [inferred for shapes]. The oversampling and any internal gain staging are [measure].

**Crunch.** A sine-shaped distortion on the mid-high band: a sine waveshaper applied to the high-passed (or mid-high) part and summed back [manual] [inferred]. The band's crossover is [measure].

**Damp.** A low-pass filter, order [measure] (one-pole or two-pole, [inferred]), range about 500 Hz to 20 kHz [source: 2].

**Transients.** A transient shaper acting above 100 Hz [manual]: probably a difference of a fast and a slow envelope follower that drives a gain, applied to the high-passed part only [inferred]. Positive values lift both the attack and the sustain; negative values lift the attack but lower the sustain [manual] — so the effect is not a simple attack/sustain balance: the sign changes the sustain behaviour while the attack goes up in both cases [manual]. Detailed curve [measure].

**Boom.** A resonant filter that "dramatically boosts bass" [manual]; one tutorial calls it a high-pass filter with a highly resonant peak [source: 4]. Probably a band-pass or peaking resonator tuned to Freq, excited by the input and added in parallel; its amount is Boom [inferred] [measure]. The Bass Meter shows its level [manual].

**Decay.** Changes the decay rate of low frequencies. With Boom at 0 % it acts on the signal coming from the drive stage; above 0 % it acts on that signal and on the Boom output together [manual]. Probably a low-band expander or a decay envelope applied to the low band [inferred] [measure].

**Force To Note.** Rounds Freq to the nearest MIDI note's frequency [manual]. The tuning reference (A = 440 Hz) is [inferred].

**Dry/Wet and Output Gain.** The mix of processed and dry signals and the gain on the processed signal [manual]. The dry signal's alignment with the processed one (any latency) is [measure].

**Latency and oversampling.** None documented for Drum Buss; the Live help centre's list does not cover it in the pages seen [source: 5] [measure].

## Edge cases

- Drive 0 %, Crunch 0 %, Boom 0 %, Transients 0 %, Comp off, Damp at its maximum: close to transparent, but the distortion type may still shape the signal [inferred] [measure].
- Hard type with a large Drive: strong clipping plus a bass boost; the Output Gain and Damp compensate [manual].
- Boom at 0 %: Decay still shapes the signal [manual].
- Freq at its minimum or maximum with Force To Note: the note is clamped to the range [inferred].
- Trim at its minimum: silence into the processing; Boom may still ring if it holds state [inferred].
- Mono input: a mono result [inferred].
- Silence in: Boom decays to zero; denormals [measure].
- Switching the distortion type while playing: a click or a crossfade [measure].
- Sample rates: filters, Damp and Boom should behave the same at 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz; aliasing from the distortion changes with the rate [measure].

## Measurement plan

1. ~~**Ranges and defaults.**~~ Answered by the dump of 12.4.6 (see Controls). Still open: how Freq reads when Force To Note is on (a button, not a parameter in the dump), and the panel display of Transients.
2. **Static curves (distortion).** Sine at 100 Hz, 1 kHz and 5 kHz from −40 to 0 dBFS, Drive 0, 50, 100 % for Soft, Medium and Hard with Comp off, Crunch 0, Boom 0, Transients 0, Damp at maximum, Dry/Wet 100 %; plot input versus output peak and measure the harmonic content (2nd, 3rd, 5th). Identify each type's curve (tanh, limiter, hard clip) and the bass boost of Hard (frequency response of the small-signal path in Hard versus Soft).
3. **Compressor.** With Comp on and Drive 0, a tone step (−40 → −10 dBFS) and a steady-state level sweep; read the gain reduction versus level (threshold, ratio), the attack and release times (fit exponentials), the makeup gain (output − input at low level); with Trim at 0, −6 and −12 dB, read how the effective compression changes.
4. **Crunch.** Sine sweeps and a noise burst with Crunch 0, 50, 100 %: the frequency range of the added distortion (spectrum of the difference signal), the waveshape (sine fold?), and the level.
5. **Damp.** Swept sine or noise through the device with everything else neutral: the low-pass response (corner, slope) at Damp 500 Hz, 2 kHz, 9.2 kHz, 20 kHz; check what the knob's default is.
6. **Transients.** A drum loop and a synthetic test (a decaying burst train: 1 ms attack, 100 ms decay) with Transients at −100, −50, 0, +50, +100 %: read the gain applied over time for signals above and under 100 Hz (high-pass the test) to check the 100 Hz split; measure the change in attack peak and in the sustain level for positive and for negative values.
7. **Boom and Freq.** An impulse and a kick-like tone burst with Boom 0, 50, 100 %: read the added resonance's frequency (the spectrum of the difference), its Q, its decay time and its level versus Boom; Freq at 30, 60, 90 Hz and every note; Force To Note's mapping of Freq to the nearest note frequency (A = 440 Hz?).
8. **Decay.** Boom 0 % then 50 %: decay values 0, 50, 100 %: read the effect on a short low burst (the time to decay by 20 dB); does Decay act on the direct signal only, on the Boom signal too (matching the manual), and what the filter does at Decay 0 and 100 %.
9. **Boom Audition and Bass Meter.** Output with Audition on versus off; the meter's value against the Boom branch's level.
10. **Dry/Wet and Output Gain.** Dry/Wet 0, 50, 100 % with a processed signal; check phase and level, and any latency; check whether Output Gain changes the dry level at 0 % and 50 %.
11. **Latency and aliasing.** Impulse delay (samples) at 44.1 and 48 kHz and a high-frequency sine near Nyquist for aliasing with Hard distortion at maximum Drive.
12. **Stereo.** A signal in one channel only and with different levels: is the processing linked (the compressor, the transient shaper) or per channel?

## Implementation notes

- **Also needed:** a compressor gain computer for Comp with fixed values (after measuring them; it can be shared with Compressor, 29.9); the three distortion curves (static waveshaping functions to start from, checked against test 2); a sine waveshaper; a transient shaper (fast and slow envelope followers); a resonant booster with its own decay (a resonant biquad peak or band-pass is a first guess, plus a decay shaper); the note-forcing helper; a bass meter; oversampling for the distortion.
- **Building blocks:** biquad low-pass for Damp and high-pass for the 100 Hz split, a Linkwitz-Riley crossover (for splitting at 100 Hz and the Crunch band), one-pole smoothing, dB conversions, oversampling.
- `.als` import: the element is `DrumBuss` [als 12.4.6]. Parameter elements [als 12.4.6]: `On`, `EnableCompression` (Comp), `DriveAmount` (stored 0.2 = 20 %, a fraction), `DriveType` (0 Soft, 1 Medium, 2 Hard), `CrunchAmount`, `DampingFrequency` (Hz, stored 9200), `TransientShaping` (-1 to 1), `BoomFrequency` (Hz, stored 50), `BoomAmount`, `BoomDecay` (stored 1 = 100 %), `BoomAudition`, `InputTrim` (stored as linear gain, 1 = 0 dB), `OutputGain` (linear gain, 1 = 0 dB), `DryWet` (stored 1 = 100 %). There is no element for Force To Note in the dump; whether it is stored is [measure].
