# Drum Buss (manual 29.12)

**Family:** dynamics
**Note status:** dump checked, partly measured (top100 and remaining runs, [Measured](#measured))

## Summary

An all-in-one drum processor: an optional fixed compressor, three flavours of drive distortion, a mid-high "crunch" distortion, a low-pass "damp" filter, a transient shaper above 100 Hz, and a tunable low-end booster (Boom) with its own decay, a dry/wet mix and an output gain. Meant for a whole drum group, to thicken it and make the parts sit together.

## Signal flow

The manual describes the parts and gives only a partial order; the order below is [inferred] unless marked.

```text
input ─► Trim [manual: "before any processing"]
      ─► Comp (fixed compressor, optional) [manual: before the distortion]
      ─► Drive ─► distortion type Soft / Medium / Hard [manual]
      ─► Crunch (sine-shaped distortion on mid-high frequencies) [manual]
      ─► Damp (low-pass) [manual; one tutorial: after the processing, before the Boom section]
      ─► Transients (shaper for frequencies over 100 Hz) [manual]
      ─┬─► (direct) ───────────────────────────────────────────────┐
       └─► Boom resonant filter (Freq, Boom amount) ─► Decay ──────┼─► sum ─► Dry/Wet ─► Output Gain ─► output
            [Boom Audition solos this branch]                      │
```

- Decay acts on the incoming (after drive and distortion) signal when Boom is 0 %, and on both the incoming and the Boom-processed signal when Boom is above 0 % [manual].
- Dry/Wet blends processed and dry; Output Gain is the gain applied to the processed signal [manual]. Whether Output Gain also affects the dry part is [measure].

## Behaviour

**Comp.** A fixed-parameter compressor placed before the distortion [manual]. Its values are not published: "fast attack, medium release, moderate ratio, ample makeup" [manual]. Measured: the threshold is about -20 dBFS (a 1 kHz sine's peak at the compressor's input, after Trim), the ratio about 3.5:1, the makeup +11.1 dB, the attack reaches 90 % in about 5 ms, and the release is an exponential in dB with a time constant of 0.40 s whatever the reduction (Measured › Comp) [measured 12.4.6]. An earlier guess of a 100–200 ms release and +6 dB of makeup was too low. Whether the detector is peak or RMS, and the exact attack law, are [measure]. The compression amount depends on the input level, and Trim reduces it [source: 3].

**Drive and distortion type.** Drive scales the signal before a nonlinearity [manual]. Soft: a smooth waveshaper (tanh-like); Medium: a limiting curve (a soft threshold followed by a stricter limit); Hard: a hard clipper with a bass boost [manual] [inferred for shapes]. Measured with a 1 kHz sine: all three types are odd-symmetric (no even harmonics), add gain with Drive (Soft: +1.99 dB at Drive 0, +8.91 at 50, +11.52 at 100 at small signals) and reach about 0 dBFS at a 0 dBFS input; Medium at Drive 100 is nearly a hard limiter and distorts more than Hard (Measured › Drive and Drive Type) [measured 12.4.6]. The oversampling is [measure].

**Crunch.** A sine-shaped distortion on the mid-high band: a sine waveshaper applied to the high-passed (or mid-high) part and summed back [manual] [inferred]. The band's crossover is [measure].

**Damp.** A low-pass filter, order [measure] (one-pole or two-pole, [inferred]), range about 500 Hz to 20 kHz [source: 2].

**Transients.** A transient shaper acting above 100 Hz [manual]: probably a difference of a fast and a slow envelope follower that drives a gain, applied to the high-passed part only [inferred]. Positive values lift both the attack and the sustain; negative values lift the attack but lower the sustain [manual] — so the effect is not a simple attack/sustain balance: the sign changes the sustain behaviour while the attack goes up in both cases [manual]. Detailed curve [measure].

**Boom.** A resonant filter that "dramatically boosts bass" [manual]; one tutorial calls it a high-pass filter with a highly resonant peak [source: 4]. Probably a band-pass or peaking resonator tuned to Freq, excited by the input and added in parallel; its amount is Boom [inferred] [measure]. The Bass Meter shows its level [manual].

**Decay.** Changes the decay rate of low frequencies. With Boom at 0 % it acts on the signal coming from the drive stage; above 0 % it acts on that signal and on the Boom output together [manual]. Probably a low-band expander or a decay envelope applied to the low band [inferred] [measure].

**Force To Note.** Rounds Freq to the nearest MIDI note's frequency [manual]. The tuning reference (A = 440 Hz) is [inferred].

**Dry/Wet and Output Gain.** The mix of processed and dry signals and the gain on the processed signal [manual]. The dry signal's alignment with the processed one (any latency) is [measure].

**Latency and oversampling.** None documented for Drum Buss; the Live help centre's list does not cover it in the pages seen [source: 5] [measure].

## Edge cases

- Drive 0 %, Crunch 0 %, Boom 0 %, Transients 0 %, Comp off, Damp at its maximum, Soft: not transparent. It has +1.98 dB of gain at small signals, a response with lifts of about 3 dB at 63 to 250 Hz and 2 to 8 kHz, and 3.9 % THD at -6 dBFS (Measured › Drive and Drive Type) [measured 12.4.6]. Medium and Hard at Drive 0 were not run.
- Hard type with a large Drive: strong clipping plus a bass boost; the Output Gain and Damp compensate [manual].
- Boom at 0 %: Decay still shapes the signal [manual].
- Freq at its minimum or maximum with Force To Note: the note is clamped to the range [inferred].
- Trim at its minimum: silence into the processing; Boom may still ring if it holds state [inferred].
- Mono input: a mono result [inferred].
- Silence in: Boom decays to zero; denormals [measure].
- Switching the distortion type while playing: a click or a crossfade [measure].
- Sample rates: filters, Damp and Boom should behave the same at 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz; aliasing from the distortion changes with the rate [measure].

## Measured

[measured 12.4.6] Behaviour runs at 44.1 kHz in Live 12.4.6 (`measurements/29.12-drum-buss/`:
`drumbuss-drive`, `drumbuss-comp` in the top100 set; `drumbuss-boom`, `-transients`, `-damp-crunch`, `-mix-output` in the remaining set) from a neutral state: Comp off, Crunch 0,
Transients 0, Boom 0, Decay 100 %, Damp 20 kHz, Trim and Output Gain 0 dB, Dry/Wet 100 %. The
Drive runs change only Drive Type and Drive; the Comp runs use Drive 0 (Soft). Gains are of the 1 kHz
staircase's fundamental against the step's peak level; the response is the -12 dBFS sweep; the
compressor's envelope is read from the 1 s bursts (-30, -6, -30 dBFS). These are observations, not
a model.

| Hard, Drive 100 | +14.23 | +10.75 | +6.07 | 14.8 / 32.1 | -0.08 |

- **Drive adds gain before it adds distortion.** At small signals every case is linear (THD under
  0.1 % up to about -30 dBFS) with a gain that rises with Drive: Soft +1.99 dB at Drive 0, +8.91 at 50,
  +11.52 at 100 [measured 12.4.6]. Above that the gain falls with level and the output approaches
  0 dBFS: with a 0 dBFS input the output peak is -0.08 to -0.67 dBFS at Drive 50 and 100, in all three
  types. All three curves behave as soft saturators with a ceiling at about 0 dBFS [inferred].
- **Odd harmonics only.** In every case the 2nd, 4th and 6th harmonics were below -95 dB; the 3rd was
  -17 dB (Soft, Drive 0, 0 dBFS), -10 to -9 dB (Hard and Medium, Drive 100, 0 dBFS) and the 5th 5 to 20 dB
  below the 3rd (closer at the high-drive settings). The curves are odd-symmetric: no DC and no even-order content [measured 12.4.6].
- **The order Soft < Medium < Hard in distortion does not hold in these numbers.** At Drive 50 the
  three are close and Hard has the lowest gain and THD (4.3 % at -12 dBFS against 5.5 % Soft and 5.4 %
  Medium); at Drive 100 Medium distorts most (25.7 % at -12 dBFS against 14.8 % Hard and 9.1 % Soft).
  Medium at Drive 100 is near hard limiting (43 % THD at 0 dBFS; an ideal square wave has 48 %) [measured
  12.4.6]. The manual says each type adds more distortion than the one before [manual]; for a 1 kHz sine
  that is true only of Soft against the other two.
- **Not tested here:** 100 Hz and 5 kHz sines, Drive values between the three, and the types at
  Drive 0 (only Soft at 0 was run). At a 0 dBFS input Soft Drive 100's THD figure (analysis) is an
  artefact of a waveform with several cycles per period; the peak above (-0.15 dBFS) is read from the
  samples.

- **The Drum Buss is not flat even at Drive 0 with every other control neutral.** Soft at Drive 0 has a
  broad lift of about 3 dB at 63 to 250 Hz and another of about 3 dB at 2 to 8 kHz over the 500 Hz to 1 kHz
  level, a fall below 63 Hz (-3.4 dB at 20 Hz from the impulse) and a fall above 8 kHz. The shape is
  about the same at Drive 50 and 100 (125 Hz is 3.0, 2.9 and 2.6 dB above 500 Hz at Drive 0, 50 and
  100), so it does not come from the drive amount [measured 12.4.6]. The bypassed device is flat to
  0.0 dB. Which part of the chain adds it (an input or output tilt, the Soft curve's own filtering, the
  100 Hz split of the transient shaper) is [measure]: it needs the same run with Transients and Crunch

- **Hard's bass boost shows at Drive 50.** At Drive 50, 31.5 Hz and 63 Hz are 5.6 and 6.1 dB above the

  sweep is already saturated [measured 12.4.6]. With the -18 dBFS noise the Hard Drive 100 spectrum
  is 27 dB at 31.5 and 63 Hz against 10.5 dB at 1 kHz; noise through a saturating device is not a linear
  gain, so this shows only that the low end is boosted more for loud broadband input than for a
  single sine [inferred from the measurement].

### Comp

Comp on, Drive 0 (Soft), Trim 0; levels are the sine's peak dBFS:

| Input (dBFS) | -60 to -30 | -24 | -21 | -18 | -15 | -12 | -9 | -6 | -3 | 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gain, Comp on (dB) | +13.08 to +13.06 | 12.92 | 12.75 | 11.30 | 9.19 | 7.05 | 4.86 | 2.63 | 0.34 | -2.00 |
| Gain, Comp off (dB) | +1.99 to +1.97 | 1.96 | 1.95 | 1.92 | 1.87 | 1.76 | 1.55 | 1.15 | 0.41 | -0.85 |

- **Makeup and level.** Below -27 dBFS the gain is +13.08 dB against +1.98 dB with Comp off: Comp adds
  a fixed +11.1 dB, and the output at 0 dBFS in is -2.0 dBFS [measured 12.4.6]. The earlier guess
  of about +6 dB was too low.
- **Threshold and ratio.** The gain starts to fall at about -24 dBFS and falls by 0.7 dB per dB of input
  from -18 to -6 dBFS (the output rises 0.29, 0.26 and 0.23 dB per dB over -18 to -12, -12 to -6 and
  -6 to 0 dBFS). Extending the straight part back gives a threshold of about -20 dBFS (the sine's peak,
  at the compressor's input) and a ratio of about 3.5:1; the last part includes the Soft stage's own
  saturation [inferred from the measurement]. The knee is short: 0.2 dB of reduction at -24 dBFS,
  0.35 dB at -21, 1.8 dB at -18 [measured 12.4.6].
- **Trim acts before the compressor.** Trim -6 and -12 dB shift the whole curve by exactly that much in
  input level: the reduction at Trim -6 for an input of -12 dBFS is 1.80 dB, as at Trim 0 for -18 dBFS;
  Trim -6 at -6 and 0 dBFS gives 6.05 and 10.47 dB, as Trim 0 at -12 and -6 (6.03, 10.45); Trim -12 at
  -6 and 0 dBFS gives 1.80 and 6.05 dB. Without Comp, Trim -6 gives a gain of -4.02 dB at small signals
  (the Soft stage's +1.98 dB less 6 dB) [measured 12.4.6]. So Trim lowers what the compressor and the
  drive stage see, and the makeup is not tied to it.
- **Attack.** For the step from -30 to -6 dBFS (10.4 dB of reduction, envelope resolution 0.25 ms) the
  gain change is half done at 1.2 ms, 80 % at 2.2 ms, 90 % at 4.7 ms, 95 % at 6 ms and 99 % at 15 ms;
  with Trim -6 (6.0 dB of reduction) 63 % at 1.5 ms, 90 % at 5.0 ms; with Trim -12 (1.8 dB) 63 % at
  1.5 ms and 90 % at 9.7 ms [measured 12.4.6]. There is no control; the attack is fast to begin with
  and has a slower tail of a few ms. The 1 ms guess for a "fast attack" was right in order of
  magnitude [inferred].
- **Release.** After the -6 dBFS burst the gain first recovers about 1.6 dB in 15 ms, then follows
  an exponential in dB: the remaining distance to the low-level gain shrinks by 0.78 for every 100 ms,
  a time constant of 0.397 s (0.397 s at Trim -6, 0.393 s at Trim -12), whether the reduction was
  10.4, 6.0 or 1.8 dB. The 63 % point is 0.34 s and the 90 % point 0.86 s (Trim 0); 0.34 s and 0.85 s
  (Trim -6); 0.26 s and 0.75 s (Trim -12); after a 1 s burst of -30 dBFS the gain is back to 12.3 of
  13.04 dB [measured 12.4.6]. t90/t63 is 2.5, close to 2.3 of an exponential in dB, unlike
  Compressor's Lin release. The release is four times longer than the 100 to 200 ms guessed
  earlier. (`analyse.py` reports a release t63 of 0.7 ms: it reads the burst edge, not the recovery.)

### Boom

|---|---|---|---|
| Boom 0 (default response) | +3.8 dB at 50 Hz (the device's low lift) | | |
| Boom 50 % | +20.7 dB at 51.4 Hz | 1.35 Hz | 38 |
| Boom 100 % | +37.1 dB at 49.98 Hz | 0.67 Hz | 74 |
| Boom 100 %, Freq 30 Hz | +39.8 dB at 30 Hz | | |

- **Boom adds one narrow resonance exactly at Boom Freq** (49.98 Hz for 50, 90.07 Hz for 90, 30 Hz for 30), a second-order peak whose height and sharpness grow with Boom: +17 dB (relative to the +3.8 dB without it) at 50 % and +33 dB at 100 %, with Q 38 and 74. The rest of the response is unchanged (1.5 dB at 1 to 2 kHz in every case) [measured 12.4.6].
- **Decay sets the resonator's Q**: at Boom 100 % it is 16, 33 and 74 for Decay 0, 50 and 100 % (the -3 dB width 3.2, 1.5 and 0.67 Hz); the ring time follows (about 1.0, 4.0 and 5.7 s by the impulse's envelope, the longest longer than the 2 s render, so these are rough) [measured 12.4.6].
- **Audition outputs the resonator alone**: the impulse response is a 50 Hz ring starting at -41 dB (0.009), alternating sign every 9.8 ms, with no direct part [measured 12.4.6].
- The direct tap of the impulse (1.009 at 0.05 ms) is the same in every Boom case: Boom adds without changing the path it rides on [measured 12.4.6].

### Transients

Comp off, Drive 0 (the default gain is +2.0 dB at small signals); the -30, -6 and -30 dBFS 1 s bursts of the 1 kHz tone; levels of the output in dB (`drumbuss-transients`) [measured 12.4.6]:

| Setting | tone at -30 dBFS, first second | first 5 ms after the step to -6 dBFS | settled at -6 dBFS | 20 to 40 ms after the step back down to -30 dBFS | tone after it (last 0.2 s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (default) | -28.0 | -4.96 | -4.86 | -26.0, -28.0 | -28.0 |
| -100 % | -37.6 | +0.04 | -4.95 | -27.7, -34.3, -37.5 ... -45.8 | -44.7 |
| -50 % | -32.8 | -0.59 | -4.90 | -26.9, -31.2, -32.8 ... -36.9 | -36.4 |
| +50 % | -24.7 | +0.84 | -4.82 | -25.3, -25.8, -24.7 ... -21.8 | -22.2 |
| +100 % | -21.3 | +2.05 | -4.79 | -24.6, -23.6, -21.4 ... -15.6 | -16.4 |

- **The shaper acts on level changes, and leaves a steady tone alone**: the settled output at -6 dBFS is within 0.15 dB of the default for every setting, and the staircase gain from -30 to 0 dBFS is within 0.1 dB of it for -100 to +100 % [measured 12.4.6].
- **A positive value lifts the onset and the decay**: at +100 % the step to -6 dBFS starts 6.9 dB above where it settles and falls back with t63 28 ms (t90 47 ms; +50 %: 27 / 46 ms); the tone after the step back down is held up, rising by 10 dB over 0.2 s as it settles (-24.6 to -15.6 dB against -28.0), and the staircase's first step reads +12 dB (14.3 against 2.0 dB at -60 dBFS) [measured 12.4.6].
- **A negative value pulls the sustain down**: at -100 % the tone after the loud burst drops 17 dB below its default level (-44.7 against -28.0 dB), falling with t63 60 ms (t90 103 ms; -50 %: 62 / 104 ms), and the first low tone is 9.6 dB down; the onset still overshoots by 5 dB (0.04 dB in the first 5 ms against a settled -4.95) [measured 12.4.6].
- So the control scales a gain driven by the difference of a fast and a slow envelope; the step sizes here (24 dB) are large, and the law against step size was not measured [measured 12.4.6; the structure inferred].

| Damp 2 kHz | +0.4 | +0.2 | -0.6 | -2.7 | -6.6 | -12.4 | -17.1 |
| Damp 9.2 kHz | 0.0 | +0.4 | +0.2 | -0.1 | -0.4 | -2.1 | -3.6 |
| Crunch 50 % | +5.6 | +6.1 | +6.0 | +6.0 | +6.0 | +2.6 | -0.7 |
| Crunch 100 % | +10.9 | +10.0 | +9.8 | +9.8 | +9.7 | +4.0 | -3.5 |

- **Damp is a first-order low-pass at the set frequency**: -3 dB at the frequency (-2.6 at 500 Hz for 500, -2.7 at 2 kHz for 2000) and 6 dB per octave beyond (-6.8, -12.0 and -17.8 dB at 1, 2 and 4 kHz for 500 Hz) [measured 12.4.6].
- **Crunch is a gain stage with a band**: it lifts 125 Hz to about 6 kHz by 6.0 dB (50 %) and 9.8 to 10.9 dB (100 %) and rolls off below 63 Hz and above 8 kHz; the small-signal gain is +8.5 and +13.3 dB (including the device's +2 dB), and it falls as the level rises (50 %: +0.7 dB at 0 dBFS; 100 %: +1.1 dB), with THD 15.6 and 33 % at -6 dBFS. The harmonics of a -12 dBFS tone are odd: the 3rd at -27 dB (50 %) and -18 dB (100 %), the 5th at -55 and -38 dB, the 2nd and 4th below -100 dB [measured 12.4.6].
- With Drive 0, the harmonics at Damp settings (3rd -40 to -52 dB for the -12 dBFS tone) are the device's own soft saturation; Damp lowers them in proportion to the level it removes [measured 12.4.6].

### Dry/Wet and Output

Hard, Drive 100 %, 1 kHz staircase and the impulse (`drumbuss-mix-output`) [measured 12.4.6]:

| Case | gain at -60 / -30 dBFS | THD at -12 / -6 dBFS | impulse peak |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dry/Wet 100 % | +14.2 dB | 14.8 / 32.1 % | +4.7 dB |
| Dry/Wet 50 % | +9.7 dB | 11.4 / 21.1 % | +1.9 dB |
| Dry/Wet 0 % | 0.0 dB | 0 % | -2.4 dB |
| Output -10 dB (100 %) | +4.2 dB | 14.8 / 32.1 % | -5.3 dB |
| Output +3 dB (100 %) | +17.2 dB | 14.8 / 32.1 % | +7.7 dB |
| Dry/Wet 50 %, Output -10 dB | +2.3 dB | 7.7 / 12.1 % | -3.7 dB |

- **Dry/Wet is a linear crossfade**: the 50 % gain of +9.7 dB is 0.5 + 0.5 x 5.1 (14.2 dB is 5.1) = 3.05, which is 9.7 dB; an equal-power law would give +12.7 dB. At 0 % the sweep is flat at 0.0 dB but the render is not bit-identical to the bypass (the impulse comes back at 0.76, 0.05 ms late with a small tail): the dry path is an all-pass-like path of the same group delay as the processing, not a bare wire [measured 12.4.6].
- **Output gain acts on the wet signal only**: -10 dB at 100 % gives exactly -10.0 dB and +3 dB gives +3.0 dB, but -10 dB at 50 % takes the gain from +9.7 to +2.3 dB, not to -0.3 dB: 0.5 + 0.5 x 5.1 x 0.316 = 1.31 (+2.3 dB) [measured 12.4.6].
