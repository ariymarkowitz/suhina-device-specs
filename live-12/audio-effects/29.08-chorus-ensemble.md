# Chorus-Ensemble (manual 29.8)

**Family:** modulation and pitch
**Note status:** dump checked

## Summary

Chorus-Ensemble is a modulated-delay effect with three modes shown as buttons in its display:
Chorus (two modulated delayed copies added to the input), Ensemble (three delayed copies with
evenly spaced modulation phases, a thicker and smoother sound inspired by a 1970s string-ensemble
pedal) and Vibrato (only the pitch-modulated signal, no dry) [manual]. Each mode can high-pass the
signal it modulates. The manual describes the 12.4 layout, where the earlier "Classic" mode was
renamed Chorus and gained Delay Time and Taps [manual], [source: 2].

## Signal flow

```text
                 ┌──────────────── dry ───────────────────────────────────────────┐
input ─► (HP?) ──┤                                                                 ├► Dry/Wet ─► Output ─► out
                 └► delay line(s) with LFO-modulated read position(s) ─► Warmth ───┘
                       ▲                        (Chorus: 1 or 2 taps; Ensemble: 3; Vibrato: 1 per channel)
                       └──── Feedback (± via Feedback Invert), per channel ◄── (not in Vibrato)
        wet ─► Width (mid/side balance; Chorus, Ensemble only)
```

- High-pass: the manual says it reduces the modulation effect on components below the
  frequency, i.e. low frequencies are left unmodulated, not simply removed from the output; whether
  it filters the wet send (so the low band is only dry) or splits the band is [measure] [manual],
  [inferred].
- Where Warmth sits (inside the feedback loop or after the wet sum), whether Output scales the
  dry as well, and where Width is applied are [measure].
- Dry/Wet is disabled in Vibrato: the output is the modulated signal only [manual], [inferred].
- A Max wrapper family exists for the building blocks: `abl.dsp.chorus~`, `abl.dsp.ensemble~` and
  `abl.dsp.vibrato~`, with matching attributes [source: 3]. They show the structure of the effect
  (three separate DSP objects), though the wrappers' bounds are not necessarily the device's.

## Controls

Screen order [manual]: display with the three mode buttons (Chorus, Ensemble, Vibrato); in the
display the delay-line waveforms; around them the mode-specific controls (High-Pass,
Width, Delay Time and Taps, or Offset and Shape); the modulation block (Rate, Amount,
Feedback and its invert toggle); the global block (Output, Warmth, Dry/Wet). The precise
arrangement is [measure].

## Behaviour

**Chorus.** The input is split into a dry path and wet paths, each wet path a delay line read at
a position that moves with an LFO. The pitch deviation is the time derivative of the delay
modulation, so a larger Amount or a higher Rate means a bigger pitch wobble [inferred]; a user
who watched Vibrato's pitch noticed its amplitude growing with Rate, consistent with that
[source: 5]. In Auto delay-time mode, each tap's centre delay is proportional to Amount, so
raising Amount gives both more depth and a longer base delay; the fixed Delay Time keeps the base
constant [manual]. Two taps (default in the older Classic mode [inferred]) are modulated by
LFOs of different phases (probably 180° apart, left and right swapped [inferred]). One tap
uses a single modulated line per channel [manual].

**Ensemble.** Three delay lines with modulation phases evenly split, i.e. 0°, 120°, 240° [manual,
"evenly split"], [inferred for 120°]. The output is a smoother, wider, more intense chorus than two
taps [manual]. The wrapper exposes an LFO "shaping" attribute (sine to triangle) on Ensemble as
well, but the device panel has no such control, so the shape is probably fixed internally
[source: 3], [inferred].

**Vibrato.** A single modulated delay per channel with no dry sound, so the output is a pitch
modulation [manual]. The shape morphs from sine to triangle. With a triangle the pitch
deviation is constant while the delay ramps, so it sounds like two alternating pitches (a
"police siren"); Ableton staff explained the effect as the read head moving at constant speed
[source: 5], [manual]. Offset sets the left/right LFO phase. The pitch deviation also depends
on Amount and Rate (as above).

**High-pass.** Applies in every mode with a frequency of 20 Hz – 2 kHz: low frequencies pass
without modulation [manual]. Structure probably a first- or second-order high-pass on the
signal going to the modulated delays, while the dry path keeps the full spectrum [inferred].
Order and slope are [measure].

**Width.** A mid/side balance of the wet signal only: wet M scaled by a factor, wet S by
another, so 100 % is the unchanged stereo image, 0 % is mono and 200 % has side level doubled
(by +6 dB relative to the middle) [manual], [inferred]. Whether the mid level also falls as Width
rises (a normalised law) is [measure].

**Feedback.** Each channel's output returns to its own input [manual], so repeats pile up and
the comb coloration deepens. At high values with Invert on, the sound hollows out [manual]. The
tip about "bursts of decaying oscillations" when Invert is automated above 90 % [manual 29.8.1]
says the loop's gain can approach or exceed 100 % effective gain at the top of the range
[inferred]; it is unknown whether the loop saturates (Warmth) or the range stops below 100 %
[measure].

**Warmth.** Slight saturation and filtering, probably a soft non-linearity and a gentle low-pass
inside the wet path (modelling the analogue pedal) [manual], [inferred]. Amount of each, the
cutoff and whether it is inside the feedback loop are [measure].

**Output and Dry/Wet.** Output scales the processed signal; Dry/Wet mixes dry against it
[manual]. Mix law [measure] (common.md assumes linear).

**Version history.** Chorus-Ensemble was already in Live 11 (users on 11.2.10 discuss its Vibrato
shape) [source: 5]; Live 10's chorus was simpler, and Dry/Wet and Warmth came with the new device
[source: 6]. 12.4 renamed Classic to Chorus and added Delay Time and Taps [source: 2]. A set saved
before 12.4 has neither parameter; it should load as Auto and (probably) two taps [inferred].

## Edge cases

- Amount 0 in Auto delay-time mode: the manual says the delay scales with the amount, so the
  base delay may shrink towards 0 as well; what the output is then (a pass-through, or a comb at
  a very short delay) is [measure]. In a fixed Delay Time the base delay stays [manual].
- Feedback near 100 % with Invert: very long decays, possible self-oscillation [manual, tips].
  Whether the loop is limited is [measure].
- Playback stopped: feedback tails continue (audible delays) [manual]; the effect keeps
  processing silence.
- Rate 0.1 Hz: slow phasing; Rate max: fast vibrato up to 15 Hz [source: 3, wrapper].
- Vibrato with Dry/Wet disabled: the dry control keeps its value but has no effect [manual].
- Mono input: Chorus and Ensemble produce a stereo output from mono through the different
  LFO phases; Width at 0 % gives a mono wet signal [manual], [inferred].
- Mode switch during playback: delay lines are reallocated or crossfaded; [measure] clicks and
  any latency change.
- Sample rates: time constants are in ms; delay buffers scale with the sample rate [inferred].
- Latency: any fixed base delay of the wet path, and whether the dry path is delayed to match
  it, is [measure] (step 10).

## Measurement plan

1. ~~**Ranges and defaults.**~~ Answered by the dump of 12.4.6 (see Controls), including the Delay
   Time entries and the Rate, Output and High-Pass Frequency limits.
2. **Delay times per mode:** an impulse through the device with Amount 0, Feedback 0 and Dry/Wet
   100 % (Vibrato: only modulated). Read the impulse's position: that gives the base delay of every
   tap in Chorus (1 and 2 taps, Auto and fixed values), Ensemble and Vibrato, per channel. Repeat
   at Amount 25 %, 50 %, 100 % to learn how Auto scales.
3. **Modulation depth in ms:** 1 kHz sine, Rate 0.1 Hz, Amount 100 %: track the instantaneous
   frequency (Hilbert or zero-crossing) to read the delay's peak-to-peak movement
   (Δf/f = −d(delay)/dt). Repeat at Amount 25 % and 50 % and at Rates 0.5 and 5 Hz to check the
   Rate dependence.
4. **LFO shape and phases:** the same with Vibrato Shape 0 %, 50 %, 100 %; plot the pitch curve.
   Offset 0, 90, 180: phase between channels. In Chorus and Ensemble find the per-tap phase
   (120° steps in Ensemble?).
5. **High-pass:** a sweep or a two-tone test (100 Hz and 2 kHz) with the filter at several
   frequencies; compare the modulation sidebands of the low tone against the high one, and read
   the filter's slope in the dry path.
6. **Width:** wet only (Dry/Wet 100 %), mono input; measure M and S levels at Width 0, 100,
   200 %.
7. **Feedback:** impulse with Feedback 50 %, 90 %, 100 %, Invert on and off; read repeat spacing,
   decay and any saturation; check the loop for runaway at 100 %.
8. **Warmth:** sine at several levels, FFT at Warmth 0 and 100 %; high-frequency roll-off.
9. **Dry/Wet and Output:** sine at 0 %, 50 %, 100 % and Output at −12, 0, +6 dB; mix law and
   whether Output affects the dry.
10. **Latency:** an impulse with Dry/Wet 0 % and 100 %; check the dry path for delay.
11. **Version:** open a pre-12.4 set and see what Delay Time and Taps load as.

## Implementation notes

- **Building blocks:** an LFO with Sine and Triangle (the Sine-to-Triangle morph is a linear
  blend of the two at the same phase [inferred], or a sine-shaped triangle; compare in step 4), a
  biquad high-pass for the High-Pass, parameter smoothing, a one-pole low-pass for Warmth, a
  static saturation curve for Warmth (oversampled if the curve is hard).
- **Also needed:** a fractional (interpolating) delay line with a modulated read position (cubic
  or allpass interpolation; Live's choice is [measure]), the feedback and Width (M/S) stages, a
  per-channel multi-tap structure. The delay line can be shared with Echo, Delay and Flanger.
- **Hard to match:** Auto delay-time scaling, Warmth's character, the pedal-like Ensemble
  smoothness (probably a specific LFO phase set and a slight delay-line low-pass), and the
  Vibrato triangle behaviour (a plain delay line with triangular modulation reproduces it
  [inferred]).
- **Stable ids and parameters:** per-mode parameters (High-Pass, High-Pass Frequency) are stored
  per mode [inferred]; decide whether the implementation keeps one set or three.
- **Import:** the `.als` element is `Chorus2` [als 12.4.6]; pre-12.4 sets lack Delay Time
  and Taps. Parameter elements [als 12.4.6]: `On`, `Mode` (0 = Chorus, 1 = Ensemble, 2 = Vibrato),
  `Shaping` (the Shape), `Rate` (Hz, stored 0.9), `Amount` (stored 0.5 = 50 %, a fraction), `Feedback`,
  `InvertFeedback` (boolean), `VibratoOffset`, `ChorusDelayTime` (stored 3 = 20 ms, a step index),
  `ChorusTapCount` (stored 1, which the API shows as "2": an index, 0 = 1 tap), `HighpassEnabled`,
  `HighpassFrequency` (Hz, stored 50), `Width` (stored 1 = 100 %, so a factor 0 to 2), `Warmth`,
  `OutputGain` (linear gain, 1 = 0 dB), `DryWet` (stored 0.5 = 50 %). There is one high-pass pair for
  all modes in the set, not one per mode.
