# Grain Delay (manual 29.22)

**Family:** delay and looping
**Note status:** dump checked, partly measured (top100 and remaining runs, [Measured](#measured))

## Summary

A granular delay: the input is cut into very short grains, each grain is delayed (with optional
random scatter in time) and pitch-shifted (with optional random variation), and the result can be
fed back. At low settings it is a crude pitch shifter, chorus or smear; at high settings it
destroys the structure of the source into textures and rhythmic noise [manual, source: 2].

## Signal flow

```text
in ─► [grain cutter: rate = Frequency] ─► per-grain delay (Delay Time + random Spray)
   ─► per-grain pitch (Pitch + random Random Pitch) ─► sum of overlapping grains ─► wet
       ▲                                                                           │
       └──────────────────── Feedback ◄────────────────────────────────────────────┘
dry ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── Dry/Wet ─► out
```

- A grain-based delay line: the buffer is written continuously; a grain player reads grains
  from it at a rate set by Frequency [manual, inferred].
- Feedback returns the output to the delay input [manual].
- The X-Y pad drives any two parameters of the row headings (horizontal from the bottom row,
  vertical from the left row) [manual].

## Behaviour

- **Grain rate and size.** Frequency is a grain rate in Hz, interpreted as grains per second;
  grain length in beats is `tempo / (60 × Frequency)`, so 4 Hz at 120 BPM is half a beat per
  grain [source: 2]. With a pitch shift the output has amplitude modulation at twice the grain rate and
  sidebands four grain rates away from the main line, and the grains overlap (the unshifted impulse is not
  windowed) [measured 12.4.6] (Measured › Grain rate, Pitch). The window shape and the exact overlap are [measure; inferred].
- **Delay Time.** Applies per grain as read offset from the write position: synced as button ×
  sixteenth with a percentage offset, or in ms [manual]. Measured at Pitch 0: the delay is exactly the time set
  (button n at 120 BPM gives n x 125 ms), the swing multiplies the whole delay by (1 + swing), the level is unity
  and the read is linear between two samples [measured 12.4.6] (Measured › Time).
- **Spray.** Randomises each grain's delay offset within a range; at the order of 1 ms it
  gives a piano-like detune, at larger values it breaks structure [manual, source: 2]. Distribution
  (uniform?) is [measure].
- **Pitch.** Each grain is read at a changed speed (resampling inside the grain), i.e. a
  time-domain granular pitch shifter, which is why artefacts appear and why grain size matters
  [inferred]. The sign and units are semitones [source: 2]. Measured: the mean pitch of a steady 1 kHz tone follows
  the setting (-1128 cents for -12, +1175 for +12), but at 60 Hz grains the output is a line spectrum with
  lines 60 Hz apart, the strongest one the nearest or second nearest to the target (Measured › Pitch).
- **Random Pitch.** Random per-grain pitch offset scaled by the knob; distribution [measure].
- **Interactions.** Lower Frequency (bigger grains) stays more stable with feedback, Pitch,
  Spray and Random Pitch; high Frequency (above about 4 Hz in the example) gets blurry and
  produces artefacts; around 15 Hz gives a phaser-like colour [source: 2].
- **Feedback.** Output returns to the delay input; pitch shifts accumulate in feedback, so a
  non-zero Pitch with feedback creates rising or falling repeats [inferred].
- **Dry/Wet.** Linear [inferred]; the device has no equal-loudness option in the manual
  [measure].
- **Stereo.** Not described. With Pitch 0 the channels are identical. With a pitch shift left and right differ:
  the correlation of the two outputs for a mono tone is between 0.77 (+12) and 0.996 (-5), and the amplitude
  modulation of the right channel is in antiphase with the left (envelope correlation -0.98 to -1.00 at 60 Hz and 12 Hz),
  as if the right channel's grains start a quarter of a grain period after the left [measured 12.4.6, the
  quarter period [inferred]] (Measured › Pitch). This agrees with the reported
  stereo-spread use with short delays below about 35 ms and low Spray, Frequency, Random Pitch [source: 3].

## Edge cases

- Frequency very low: grains very long; the device approaches a pitch-shifting delay with
  audible repeats [inferred].
- Frequency very high: grains of a few milliseconds, metallic, effectively a ring-modulation
  or chorus texture [inferred].
- Spray larger than the grain: random reordering; large delay times plus spray need the delay
  buffer to hold the sum [inferred].
- Feedback at maximum with Pitch ≠ 0: output grows or fades depending on gain; the
  limit is [measure].
- Tempo change in Sync: delay time changes continuously; whether grain-wise (smooth) or
  with glitches is [measure].
- Silence in: tail from feedback only.
- Delay Time 0 or minimum: grain player reads from the write position, a pitch-shift/chorus
  without echo [inferred]. At the 1.00 ms minimum the delay is 44 samples (0.998 ms): there is no extra
  latency [measured 12.4.6].

## Measured

[measured 12.4.6] Behaviour runs at 44.1 kHz in Live 12.4.6 (`devices/29.22-grain-delay/`:
`grain-delay-time`, `grain-delay-rate`, `grain-delay-pitch` in the top100 set; `grain-delay-feedback`, `-spray`, `-random`, `-stereo-mix` in the remaining set; `grain-delay-window`, `-buttons`, `-spray-train` in the third set, rendered by Live 12.4.7: Measured › Third set), free Delay Time 40 ms, Frequency
60 Hz, Pitch 0, Spray 0, Random Pitch 0, Feedback 0, Dry/Wet 100 % unless stated. Delay times come from
the impulse, pitch and sidebands from a long FFT of the steady 1 kHz tone (-12 dBFS, 8 s; spectral lines
are given to about 1 Hz), envelopes from the rectified tone. These are observations, not a model.

- The delay equals the set value to within 0.004 ms [measured 12.4.6]. Buttons give n x 125 ms at 120 BPM [measured 12.4.6]
  (only 1 and 4 tested). Swing multiplies the whole delay by (1 + swing): 500 ms x 1.333 = 666.5 ms
  [measured 12.4.6]. The 10 ms and 40 ms values in the set are the nearest the control stores [measured 12.4.6].
- At Pitch 0 the impulse comes out as a single sample, or as two neighbouring samples whose weights are
  the fractional parts of the delay (40 ms: 0.310 and 0.690; 125 ms: 0.4995 and 0.5005; 666.5 ms: 0.348 and
  0.652), summing to 1.000: a linear two-point interpolator at unity gain, with no grain windowing
  of the impulse [measured 12.4.6]. Left and right are the same.

### Grain rate

- At Pitch 0, Spray 0, Random Pitch 0, Frequency 1, 4, 12, 60 and 150 Hz give renders that differ by
  -166 dB (rounding) for the steady tone, with the same level (-12.02 dB) and a single line at 1000 Hz; for the noise segment the lag is
  40.000 ms and the residual against the delayed input is the same -7.7 dB (the linear interpolator at a
  fractional delay) at all five rates. The grain rate has no effect unless Pitch, Spray or Random Pitch is not 0
  [measured 12.4.6]. This can be taken as the device being a plain delay then (not run: Spray and Random Pitch alone).
- Pitch +12 at Frequency 12 Hz: one line at 2008 Hz (+14 cents), the next ones 48 Hz away are at -39 dB
  and -48 dB, level -14.06 dB, amplitude modulation at 24 Hz, left and right in antiphase (envelope correlation -0.99) [measured 12.4.6].
- Pitch +12 at Frequency 4 Hz: lines at 1992 Hz and 2008 Hz (-3 dB) and a strong 1000 Hz line (the unshifted tone,
  0 dB, the strongest), then 984 and 1016 Hz at -10 dB; level -13.02 dB, modulation at 8 Hz. The pitch
  is not a clean octave at the lowest grain rates [measured 12.4.6]. The modulation is at twice the grain rate in each case
  (8, 24 Hz, and 120 Hz at 60 Hz) [measured 12.4.6].
- Only Frequency 4 and 12 Hz were read at Pitch +12; 1 Hz, 150 Hz and the grain window were not measured with a shift.

| Feedback | ratio window 2 / 1 | 3 / 2 | 4 / 3 | 5 / 4 | 6 / 5 |

### Dry/Wet and stereo

Pitch +12, Frequency 60 Hz (the wet 1 kHz tone is near 2 kHz, the dry stays at 1 kHz); amplitude of the dry 1 kHz line against the tone, and the energy near 2 kHz (`grain-delay-stereo-mix`) [measured 12.4.6]:

| Dry/Wet | dry line | dry law (1 + cos pi x Dry/Wet) / 2 | energy near 2 kHz |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 % | 1.000 (0.0 dB) | 1.000 | none |
| 25 % | 0.854 (-1.4 dB) | 0.854 | -17.5 dB |
| 50 % | 0.500 (-6.0 dB) | 0.500 | -4.2 dB |
| 75 % | 0.146 (-16.7 dB) | 0.146 | -0.2 dB |

- **The dry part follows a raised cosine, (1 + cos(pi x Dry/Wet)) / 2 = cos²(pi x Dry/Wet / 2)** to 0.001 at the three points; it is the third law seen so far (after the S-shaped power-complementary one and the plain cosine). - With Spray 20 ms the tone's level falls 1.3 dB and the L/R envelope correlation is -0.40; with Random Pitch 40 it is -0.37: the channels' random streams are independent [measured 12.4.6].

6. **Random Pitch.** Sine in; histogram of per-grain frequencies at several Random Pitch values.
   Partly: Random Pitch 10, 40, 91, 161: a spread in cents, about the display wide (Measured › Random Pitch). Histograms are open.
   A saw at Random 40 and 161: spread of about the display in cents, extremes about 1.7 times it; unlike the tone-based figures (Measured › Third set).
7. **Feedback.** Impulse: repeat ratios; maximum.
   Done at 25, 50, 95 % and 50 % with Pitch +12: the ratio tends to the percentage (Measured › Feedback).
8. **Stereo.** Mono sine in; correlation between L and R outputs.
   Partly: Spray and Random Pitch are independent in left and right (Measured › Spray, › Random Pitch, › Dry/Wet and stereo).
9. **Mix.** Level at 50 % versus ends.
   Done at 25, 50, 75 % with Pitch +12: the dry part is cos²(pi x Dry/Wet / 2) (Measured › Dry/Wet and stereo).

## Implementation notes

- **Building blocks:** parameter smoothing, dB conversions.
- **Also needed:** a delay line, a grain scheduler with overlap, window tables, a pitch resampler
  (interpolated read), random generators (a hash of the position for repeatability), a
  note-value table shared with Delay and Filter Delay, an X-Y pad UI that rebinds parameters to
  axes.
- **Hard to match:** the grain window and overlap, the Spray and Random Pitch distributions, and
  default parameter values. Without them it will sound similar in kind but not identical.
- The X-Y pad parameter assignment is a UI preference, not a device parameter [manual]; decide
  whether to store the axis choice with the project.
- **.als element name:** `GrainDelay` [als 12.4.6]. Parameter elements in the set saved from Live
  12.4.6 [als 12.4.6], each holding a `Manual` value: `On` (bool), `Spray` (ms), `Freq` (Hz), `Pitch`
  (semitones), `RandomPitch` (0 – 161 scale), `Feedback` (fraction, 0 – 0.95), `NewDryWet`
  (fraction), `SyncMode` (bool), `BeatDelayEnum` (index 0 – 7 into 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 16),
  `BarDelayOffset` (the swing offset; 0), `MsDelay` (ms). Stored values are in the displayed units,
  percentages as 0..1 fractions. Not parameters but plain values [als 12.4.6]: `ParameterX` (3) and `ParameterY` (1),
  the X-Y pad's assignments as indexes into the selector rows (order [measure]), and `PitchScrollPosition` (view state).
