# Vinyl Distortion (manual 29.41)

**Family:** distortion
**Note status:** dump checked, partly measured (top100 and remaining runs, [Measured](#measured))

## Summary

Vinyl Distortion imitates the distortions of a stylus following a record groove, from the
geometry of stylus and groove rather than from the cutting process. Two models: Tracing, which
adds even harmonics, and Pinch, which adds odd harmonics out of phase between the channels. There is a
global Drive, a Soft or Hard mode, a stereo or mono choice for Pinch, and a Crackle generator with
Volume and Density [manual]. Robert Henke, who designed it, describes the physics behind it in an Ableton forum post [source: 2]. In the renders, Pinch makes even harmonics only, and Tracing a series dominated by the 2nd, not the even/odd split stated above (Measured) [measured 12.4.6].

## Signal flow

```text
input ─► analysis band-pass (Tracing: Freq, Q) ─► tracing distortion (even) ─┐
   │                                                                          ├─► + ─► Drive (wet/dry amount) ─► out
   ├────► analysis band-pass (Pinch: Freq, Q) ─► pinch distortion (odd, 180° out of phase L/R) ─┘              ▲
   └────────────────────────────────────────────────────── dry ─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
                       crackle generator (Volume, Density) ──────────────────────────────────────────────────┘ (added)
```

- Two band-pass filters sit in the "analysis stage" of each model and do not colour the output directly [source: 2]. They select which part of the signal is analysed (usually the highs, but the Pinch band can be low) [source: 2].
- The global Drive "increases or decreases the overall distortion created by both" [manual]. SOS reports that Drive acts as a wet/dry mix: at zero only the dry signal is heard [source: 3].
- Whether the Crackle passes through Drive is [measure].
- The Soft/Hard switch acts inside Pinch only; Tracing's render is bit-identical in both positions (Measured › Soft and Hard) [measured 12.4.6]. Both models take the sum of the two channels as their input and add the result to each channel (Pinch with opposite signs in Stereo) [measured 12.4.6], [inferred from the measurement].

## Behaviour

- **Tracing Model.** Physical basis: a spherical stylus rides on the groove walls, not the bottom, so it warps the waveform like a ball rolling over a sine; the effect depends on the signal's frequency and the stylus size [source: 2]. It makes even harmonic distortion [manual]. Measured with a 1 kHz sine: the 2nd harmonic is the strongest and the odd ones are present too (the 3rd 13 dB below the 2nd at -12 dBFS), so "even" holds for the leading term only; the added term acts like x + a x^2 with a = 4.5 x Drive at small levels and compresses the fundamental at high ones (Measured › Tracing) [measured 12.4.6]. A likely implementation is a band-limited analysis of the signal, a non-linear mapping creating a second-order component, and an added or mixed distortion term [inferred].
- **Pinch Effect.** The stylus moves vertically at twice the signal's frequency while it follows the sideways motion, so it adds harmonics 180° out of phase between the channels [source: 2]. It makes odd harmonics [manual]; the render of a 1 kHz sine has even harmonics only, 2nd, 4th, 6th and 8th, at 1.70 x Drive, -14.0, -21.4 and -26.5 dB relative to the 2nd, and no odd ones above -84 dB, which agrees with Henke's twice-the-frequency picture and contradicts the manual (Measured › Pinch) [measured 12.4.6]. In Stereo mode it creates a richer stereo image, even from mono sources [manual]; the added term has opposite signs in left and right, so the harmonics are 180° out of phase and a mono sum cancels them [measured 12.4.6]. In Mono mode the same distortion is applied to both channels in phase [manual], [measured 12.4.6].
- **Drive of each model.** Real-world strength depends on amplitude, stylus shape, playback speed and position on the record [source: 2]; in the device it is the amount [manual].
- **Analysis band-passes.** Two band-pass filters, one per model, define which band feeds the non-linearity [source: 2]. Q is the bandwidth of that filter [manual]. They do not colour the direct output [source: 2].
- **Soft and Hard.** Hard: pure geometric distortion; Soft: softened extremes (a smoother curve or a low-passed derivative) [source: 2], [inferred]. Measured in Pinch: Hard is a pure square of the signal (one harmonic, growing with the level squared), Soft a rectifier-like term proportional to the level (many even harmonics, constant relative level); the Tracing model does not respond to the switch (Measured › Soft and Hard) [measured 12.4.6].
- **Global Drive.** Scales both models' distortion [manual]; SOS: also behaves as a wet/dry amount [source: 3]. The scaling law is [measure].
- **Crackle.** "A random function with filters, tuned by ear" [source: 2]. Probably random impulses (a Poisson process, rate set by Density) shaped by a high-pass or band-pass filter, gain from Volume [source: 2], [inferred].
- **Stereo.** Stereo Pinch has left and right distortions of opposite sign [manual]. Measured: both models compute their distortion from the sum of the channels (a tone in the left channel only produces the same distortion in both outputs) and add it to each channel, Tracing with equal signs and Pinch with opposite ones in Stereo [measured 12.4.6]. Both add a DC offset that is not removed (Measured › Pinch) [measured 12.4.6].
- **No Dry/Wet control** in the manual's text [manual]; Drive takes this role [source: 3].

## Edge cases

- **Drive 0:** only the dry signal (and the crackle, if its Volume is up) [source: 3].
- **Pinch in Mono mode:** the same distortion on both sides: no stereo widening [manual].
- **Mono source with Pinch in Stereo mode:** the added harmonics (even, in the render) have opposite sign in left and right; the sum to mono cancels them to within 0.02 in peak, -21 dB against a -12 dBFS tone [source: 2], [measured 12.4.6].
- **DC offset:** Pinch and Tracing each add a DC offset (Pinch Soft: +0.32 left and -0.32 right for a -12 dBFS tone at Drive 0.5, Tracing +0.063) that follows the level, with no blocker in the device [measured 12.4.6].
- **Crackle with no input:** the crackle alone, a tail after the input ends; Live bounces an effect tail for generator-like effects such as Vinyl Distortion (a 12.2.5 fix kept muted clips from making a tail) [source: 4].
- **Q extremes:** a very narrow band gives little distortion; a very wide band approaches broadband [inferred].
- **Latency:** none documented [measure].
- **Tempo/transport:** no tempo sync; the crackle's random pattern is not locked to transport [inferred].

## Measured

[measured 12.4.6] Behaviour runs at 44.1 kHz in Live 12.4.6 (`measurements/29.41-vinyl-distortion/`: `vinyl-distortion-tracing`, `vinyl-distortion-pinch` in the top100 set; `vinyl-distortion-crackle`, `vinyl-distortion-global-drive` in the remaining set; `vinyl-distortion-crackle-repeat`, `vinyl-distortion-band` in the third set, rendered by Live 12.4.7: Measured › Third set): one model on at a time (the other off), its band at 1 kHz, Global Drive 1, Crackle Volume 0, Soft, Stereo, the model's Drive 0.5 and its Width at the default (Tracing 0.32, Pinch 3) unless the case says otherwise. The input is the probe's 1 kHz sine, identical in left and right; levels are of its peak. Harmonics were read from a Hann-windowed DFT at the harmonic frequencies of the 8 s steady tone (-12 dBFS) and of the last 0.2 s of each 0.25 s staircase step; "rel" is relative to the output fundamental. Nothing else than the 1 kHz tone was rendered, so frequency-dependence of the distortion is unmeasured. These are observations, not a model.

### Things that affect how to read the renders

- `vinyl-distortion-tracing default` and `hard` are bit-identical renders. The set stores `Pinch Soft On` 1 in `hard` and Live read it back. With Pinch off in these cases, the switch has nothing to act on: it belongs to Pinch only (consistent with its API name), so a Soft or Hard Tracing render is the same [inferred from the measurement]. The same switch changes the Pinch render completely (Measured › Soft and Hard), so the setting does reach the audio when Pinch is on [measured 12.4.6].
- `analyse.py`'s static "gain" and THD for `vinyl-distortion-pinch` are constant from -60 to 0 dBFS (default: +6.97 dB, THD 87 % at every level). The "gain" is the whole output's RMS (peak = RMS x sqrt 2) against the input's: it includes the DC offset and all harmonics. The fundamental itself is unchanged (-12.02 dBFS out for -12.00 in); without the DC the gain is +2.4 dB. The constancy over level is real: the Soft Pinch term is proportional to the level (Measured › Pinch) [measured 12.4.6].
- The outputs carry a DC offset, so a render must not be high-passed before reading it (Measured › Pinch, Tracing).

- The second harmonic is the strongest and the series falls steadily with order (about 13 dB per order at -12 dBFS, 8.5 dB at -6 dBFS): all orders are present, odd ones included. So Tracing is not the even-only distortion the note's [manual] line suggests. It is H2-dominated, with the 3rd harmonic about 13 dB (at -12 dBFS) below the 2nd [measured 12.4.6].
- Below about -24 dBFS the 2nd harmonic is proportional to the input amplitude: H2/H1 = 1.12 x A (+1.0 dB over the level) at Drive 0.5, so it doubles for 6 dB more input; the 3rd goes with the square of the amplitude. A quadratic term x + a x^2 with a = 4.5 x Drive describes the 2nd harmonic and the DC (a A^2 / 2: 0.071 predicted at -12 dBFS against 0.063) at small signals; the 3rd harmonic falls twice as fast with Drive (27.4 dB between Drive 0.5 and 0.1 at -12 dBFS, against 13.4 dB for the 2nd), as a repeated quadratic or a distortion of the time axis would do [inferred from the measurement].
- The fundamental falls with level above -18 dBFS (gain -0.6 dB at -12, -1.9 dB at -6, -3.05 dB at -3 dBFS), so the distortion compresses the peaks and adds a positive DC offset (0.36 at -3 dBFS) with no DC blocker; the offset goes with the square of the amplitude [measured 12.4.6].
- Left-only input: the same distortion appears in both outputs (H2 -35.3 dBFS in left and in right, against -24.1 dBFS for the same tone in both channels), while the fundamental is almost only in the left (-53 dBFS of it in the right output). So the distortion is computed from the sum of the channels (a mid signal) and added to each channel with the same sign, rather than channel by channel [measured 12.4.6], [inferred from the measurement].

### Pinch (Tracing off, band 1 kHz, Width 3, Drive 0.5)

- **Even harmonics only.** The 1 kHz tone comes out with the 2nd, 4th, 6th and 8th harmonics and no odd ones (the 3rd, 5th, 7th and 9th are below -84 dB rel). The note's [manual] statement that Pinch makes odd harmonics is therefore not what Live renders for this tone; Henke's description (the stylus moves at twice the signal frequency) agrees with even harmonics [measured 12.4.6].
- **Level independent.** Each harmonic keeps its level relative to the fundamental from -60 to -3 dBFS (H2 rel -1.4 dB at every step, from 10 ms after the step starts), because the added term is proportional to the input: the left output is x + 4 x Drive x |x| plus a small rest (a regression of the added term on |x| gives 1.91 for Drive 0.5 with the band at 1 kHz and 1.99 with the band at 5 kHz, against 2.0 = 4 x 0.5). The harmonics of a rectified sine fall in the ratio 3/(4k^2 - 1) relative to the 2nd (for harmonic 2k): 3/15, 3/35 and 3/63 are -14.0, -21.3 and -26.4 dB, and the measured H4, H6 and H8 against H2 are -14.0, -21.4 and -26.5 dB. So Soft Pinch adds a rectified copy of the signal [measured 12.4.6], [inferred from the measurement].
- **Drive is linear in the amount.** H2 rel -15.4, -1.4 and +4.6 dB at Drive 0.1, 0.5 and 1: H2/H1 = 1.70 x Drive; every harmonic scales by the same factor, and the DC offset too (0.064, 0.319, 0.638 at the -12 dBFS tone) [measured 12.4.6]. At Drive 1 the 2nd harmonic is louder than the fundamental.
- **Stereo:** the left output has the term added and the right output has it subtracted. The even harmonics have R/L phase 180° (4th, 6th, 8th as well) and the DC offset is +0.319 in the left and -0.319 in the right. The sum L + R equals twice the input to within 0.02 in peak value (-21 dB against the tone's 0.25 peak), so the distortion cancels in a mono sum [measured 12.4.6].
- **Mono:** both outputs carry exactly the Stereo render's left output (bit-identical samples), so the distortion is added to both channels with the same sign, with the same DC offset of +0.319 [measured 12.4.6].
- **No DC removal:** the offset equals 4 x Drive x 2/pi x the amplitude (0.319 for Drive 0.5 at 0.251), appears at once and stays for the whole tone, and goes with the level [measured 12.4.6].
- **Left-only input:** the distortion appears in both outputs with half the amplitude (H2 -19.5 dBFS in both outputs against -13.4 dBFS for the same tone in both channels, with the right output opposite in sign). So it is computed from the sum of the channels, as for Tracing [measured 12.4.6], [inferred from the measurement].
- **Band:** with a tone at 1 kHz, moving the band to 200 Hz or 5 kHz at Width 3 changes the harmonics by 0.1 dB at most; narrowing Width to 0.1 lowers every harmonic and the DC by 22.6 dB (0.074 times), with the same spectral shape (H4/H2 and the others unchanged). So Width scales the amount while the shape stays; it does not filter the harmonics (a filter after the distortion would not cut all harmonics equally) [measured 12.4.6], [inferred from the measurement]. Only Width 0.1 and 3 were rendered.

### Soft and Hard

- **Soft** (the default of the switch, used in the Pinch cases above) adds a term proportional to |x|: relative harmonics independent of level [measured 12.4.6].
- **Hard** (Pinch only) gives a pure square: the left output is x + 8 x^2 at Drive 0.5 and the right one x - 8 x^2 (Drive was not varied in Hard; H2 rel +0.0 dB at -12 dBFS, rising 1 dB per dB of input, so H2/H1 = A/0.251 = 3.98 A; no harmonic above the 2nd above -150 dB), with the DC offset 4 A^2 (0.251 at -12 dBFS), plus in the left and minus in the right. THD at -60, -24, -12, -6, 0 dBFS: 0.4, 25, 100, 200, 398 %. The Soft and Hard Pinch renders therefore differ in kind: Hard is quadratic and grows with the square of the level; Soft is linear in the level [measured 12.4.6].
- With a tone in the left channel only, Hard's distortion is also the same in both outputs (H2 -24.0 dBFS, 12 dB below the both-channels case, as a square of the halved sum would be) but a fundamental of -25.5 dBFS leaks into the silent right output, which Soft does not do (below -108 dBFS); the left fundamental is -12.2 dBFS [measured 12.4.6]. The cause is not clear.
- Tracing: Soft and Hard are the same (Measured › Things that affect how to read the renders) [measured 12.4.6].

Tracing and Pinch both on at Drive 0.5 with the bands at 1 kHz, Crackle off, the 1 kHz staircase (`vinyl-distortion-global-drive`); "gain" is the whole output's rms times the square root of 2 against the input's, so it includes the harmonics and the DC offset [measured 12.4.6]:

- **Global Drive scales the added distortion linearly and leaves the dry signal at unity**: at low level the THD is 21.6, 43.3 and 86.6 % for 0.25, 0.5 and 1 (proportional to the setting), and at 0 the output has no harmonics at all: it is the dry path. The amount is therefore a multiplier on the sum of both models' distortion terms, not a dry/wet mix (the fundamental stays at the input's level) [measured 12.4.6]. The impulse gives the same split: left = 0.700 + 1.043 x Global, right = 0.700 - 1.043 x Global (the opposite signs of the Pinch term in the two channels) [measured 12.4.6].
- **The dry path is not transparent**: with Global 0, or with both Drives at 0, the sweep reads -0.3 dB at 4 kHz, -0.7 at 6 kHz, -1.2 at 8 kHz, -1.9 at 10, -2.8 at 12, -3.9 at 14, -5.1 at 16, -6.4 at 18 and -7.5 dB at 20 kHz, flat below 2 kHz. The impulse is one tap of 0.700 at 0 ms with a short tail, with a group delay of 0.3 samples at 1 to 5 kHz and none above 10 kHz: a gentle high-frequency roll-off with no latency in the export (the same in both channels) [measured 12.4.6].

| Density 50 | -45.1 dB | -17.1 dBFS | 11 | 28 ms | 0.60 |

- **Volume is about linear in amplitude**: the rms rises 12.5 dB for 5 times the Volume (14.0 dB if linear) and 5.5 dB for 2 times (6.0 dB), the peak 13.8 dB and 7.7 dB; the click rate does not depend on Volume (147 to 155 per second) [measured 12.4.6].
- **Density sets how many clicks there are and also how big they grow**: from Density 1 to 10 the rms rises 13.4 dB and the click rate from about 39 to 154 per second; above 10 the clicks merge into longer bursts (4 ms wide at 25, 28 ms at 50) and the rms rises only 4.6 and then 1.8 dB for 10 to 25 to 50, while the peak keeps climbing (-22.6, -21.8, -17.1 dBFS); the spectrum of the sparse Density 1 clicks is brighter (+9 and +11 dB at 4 to 10 and 10 to 20 kHz relative to 200 Hz to 1 kHz against +3 to +5 dB at the other densities) [measured 12.4.6]. A clean click count against Density needs a longer recording than the 2 s of silence in these runs.
- **The two channels share part of the pattern**: the left and right crackle have a correlation of 0.5 to 0.7 at every Density except 1, where the two are independent (0.00); the clicks come at the same times in both channels at the higher densities [measured 12.4.6].

### Third set (Live 12.4.7)

[measured 12.4.7] Behaviour runs at 44.1 kHz in Live 12.4.7 (`measurements/third/`: `vinyl-distortion-crackle-repeat`, `vinyl-distortion-band`). These are observations, not a model.

**Crackle between instances** (`vinyl-distortion-crackle-repeat`: silence in, Crackle Density 10, Volume 0.5, Tracing and Pinch off; track `a` and track `b`, which differs only in the unused Tracing Freq., and a track at Global Drive 0; click times in the 2 s silent segment):

| Track | peak | clicks (6 % of the peak, 2 ms apart) | first click times (s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| a | 0.0750 | 362 | 0.0011, 0.0055, 0.0076, 0.0200, 0.0238 |
| b | 0.0743 | 362 | 0.0038, 0.0066, 0.0140, 0.0163, 0.0219 |
| Global Drive 0 | 0.0761 | 368 | 0.0001, 0.0048, 0.0083, 0.0106, 0.0147 |

- **The click pattern is not repeatable between two instances with the same audible settings**: the click times differ and the renders are not equal (largest difference 0.69), the correlation between a and the Global Drive 0 render is -0.001; only the statistics agree (peak 0.074 to 0.076, 362 to 368 clicks in 2 s, which is 181 to 184 per second at this threshold against 154 per second at 12.4.6's threshold). The crackle is random per instance or per render, not a fixed sequence; whether re-rendering the same track repeats it was not tested [measured 12.4.7; inferred].
- **Global Drive does not change the crackle**: at Global Drive 0 the level and click count are those of Global Drive 1 (peak 0.0761 against 0.0750, 368 against 362 clicks): the crackle bypasses the Global Drive [measured 12.4.7].

**The band, Drive 0 and distortion against tone frequency** (`vinyl-distortion-band`: Tracing on, Pinch off, Crackle 0, Global Drive 1; the band Freq. and Drive per case):

| Case | tone | result |
|---|---|---|
| Drive 0.5, band 1 kHz | 3 kHz at -12 dBFS | 6 kHz -15.1 dB, 9 kHz -31.2 dB re the fundamental; DC +0.041 |
| Drive 1, band 1 kHz | 3 kHz at -12 dBFS | 6 kHz -9.6, 9 kHz -19.7, 12 kHz -30.2 dB |
| Drive 0.5, band 1 kHz | 10 kHz at -6 dBFS | the 3rd to 5th harmonics aliased to 14.1, 4.1 and 5.9 kHz at -33 to -34 dB, the 2nd at 20 kHz -19.4 dB, DC +0.049 |
| Drive 1, band 100 Hz | 100 Hz staircase | 2nd harmonic -42.4, -30.5, -24.5 dB at -24, -12, -6 dBFS (band 1 kHz, Drive 0.5: -58, -47, -41) |
| Drive 1, band 3 kHz | 3 kHz at -12 dBFS | fundamental -19.9 dBFS (gain -7.9 dB), 6 kHz -18.9, 9 kHz -23.8, 12 kHz -24.7, 15 kHz -28.8 dBFS: the 2nd harmonic stronger than the fundamental; DC +0.158 |
| Drive 1, band 3 kHz | 10 kHz at -6 dBFS | no 10 kHz line (under -53 dBFS): broadband noise-like output at -10.5 dB rms, correlation with the input 0.003 |
| Drive 0 | 100 Hz staircase, 3 and 10 kHz | no harmonics (2nd harmonic -64 dB, the noise floor), gain 0.0 dB; the impulse is one tap of 0.700 (-3.1 dB) at 0 ms in every case with Drive 0.5 or lower |
| Drive 1, band 1 kHz | impulse | peak -0.08 dB at 0.14 ms (the response depends on the Drive) |

- **The distortion is largest when the band is on the tone and grows steeply with Drive**: the 2nd harmonic of the 100 Hz tone is 17 dB higher with the band at 100 Hz and Drive 1 than with the band at 1 kHz and Drive 0.5 (11 dB of that from the Drive step of 12.4.6's linear law, 6 dB from the band); at 3 kHz the 2nd harmonic is 5.5 dB higher at Drive 1 than at 0.5, in the 12.4.6 proportion; and with the band at the tone at Drive 1 the model is driven past the small-signal law (the 2nd harmonic exceeds the fundamental) [measured 12.4.7].
- **The 3 kHz tone is distorted nearly as much with the band at 1 kHz as the 1 kHz tone**: the 2nd harmonic is -15.1 dB at 3 kHz against -11.5 dB at 1 kHz for the same Drive and level (12.4.6), so the band (Width 0.32) cuts the tone only 3.5 dB at a distance of 1.6 octaves above it [measured 12.4.7].
- **The Tracing model is not a memoryless quadratic**: the 10 kHz tone with the band at 3 kHz and Drive 1 comes out as broadband noise with no line of the tone, which a memoryless curve in series with a band-pass cannot do; the model has a state that goes unstable or chaotic at this setting (the stylus model of the manual) [measured 12.4.7; the reading is inferred] [measure: the same case with Drive 0.75 and 0.9, and at 8 kHz].
- **The distortion aliases**: with the 10 kHz tone the 3rd, 4th and 5th harmonics (30, 40, 50 kHz) land at 14.1, 4.1 and 5.9 kHz at about -33 dB, with no sign of oversampling [measured 12.4.7; inferred].

## Measurement plan

1. **Ranges and defaults.** Settled by the dump [dump 12.4.6]. Still to read: the units behind the plain numbers (Q or octaves for Width, clicks per second for Density, gain law for Crackle Volume and the drives) and what the XY pad's Alt-drag edits.
2. **Tracing harmonics.** Sine at 100 Hz, 1 kHz, 5 kHz and -20, -10, 0 dBFS through Tracing only (Pinch off, Crackle 0), Freq at the sine's frequency, Drive 0, 25, 50, 75, 100 %, Hard and Soft: FFT for 2nd, 4th, 3rd harmonics (even vs odd), the distortion level against Drive, level and band; fit the non-linearity; check that the analysis band-pass does not colour the output (impulse at Drive 0).
   Partly: 1 kHz only, Drive 0.1, 0.5, 1 and one band at a time, levels -60 to 0 dBFS in 3 dB steps (Measured › Tracing). Harmonics of every order present, the 2nd strongest and linear in Drive and level at small signal; Soft and Hard identical for Tracing. Not answered: 100 Hz and 5 kHz tones, Drive 0 (needs the `vinyl-distortion-global-drive` run), the impulse to check the band-pass colouring.
   More: a 100 Hz staircase with the band at 100 Hz, 3 and 10 kHz tones, Drive 0 and Drive 1 (Measured › Third set): the band-pass is wide above the band, the model is unstable at Drive 1 with the band at 3 kHz and a 10 kHz tone, the distortion aliases. The Drive 0 impulse is one tap of 0.700.
3. **Pinch.** Same with Pinch only, in Stereo and Mono: harmonics, L/R phase relationship (mono sine into both channels), the sum.
   Done for the 1 kHz tone: even harmonics only, in opposite phase in L and R in Stereo, the same in Mono, the sum cancels; the term is proportional to the level in Soft; a DC offset (Measured › Pinch). 100 Hz and 5 kHz tones not rendered.
4. **Q and Freq.** Sweep the sine frequency at fixed Freq and Q (Alt-drag range) for each model: the analysis band-pass shape through the amount of distortion; Q range and units.
   Partly: one tone at 1 kHz against the band at 200 Hz, 1 kHz and 5 kHz and Width 0.1, default, 3 (Measured › Tracing, Pinch). Tracing's distortion falls 20 dB with the band at 200 Hz and 11.5 dB at 5 kHz, and moves by 14 dB over Width 0.1 to 3; in Pinch (Width 3) the band moves nothing and Width 0.1 lowers the amount by 22.6 dB. Not answered: the band-pass shape against tone frequency (only 1 kHz in the probe), the units of the Width display, what the XY pad's Alt-drag edits (a UI action).
   More: tones at 100 Hz, 3 kHz and 10 kHz against bands at 100 Hz, 1 kHz and 3 kHz (Measured › Third set). A frequency sweep through a fixed band is still open.
5. **Global Drive.** Both models at a fixed setting, global Drive at 0, 25, 50, 75, 100 %: dry level and distortion level (wet/dry law).
   Done at Global Drive 0, 0.25, 0.5 and 1 with both models at Drive 0.5: a linear multiplier on the added distortion, the dry stays at unity (Measured › Global Drive and the path at Drive 0).
6. **Soft and Hard.** Compare the harmonic spectra and the curve (a slow sine ramp) in both modes.
   Done for the spectra at Drive 0.5 and a 1 kHz tone (Measured › Soft and Hard): the switch does nothing to Tracing; in Pinch Hard is a pure square and Soft a rectifier-like term. No slow ramp was rendered.
7. **Crackle.** Silence in: record 60 s at Density min, mid, max and Volume min, mid, max: click rate (counts per second), click shape and spectrum, amplitude distribution, stereo correlation; check repeatability of the random pattern between renders; check whether Drive affects it.
   Partly, from the 2 s silent segment rather than 60 s: the click rate, size and width against Density, the level against Volume, the channel correlation (Measured › Crackle). The repeatability of the pattern, the spectrum of a single click and Drive's effect were not read.
   More: the pattern is not repeatable between two instances and Global Drive does not change it (Measured › Third set).
8. **Latency.** Impulse at Drive 0 and 100 %.
   Answered at Drive 0: no latency (the impulse tap is at 0 ms), but the path is not transparent: -1.2 dB at 8 kHz and -5.1 dB at 16 kHz (Measured › Global Drive and the path at Drive 0).

## Implementation notes

- **Likely structure [inferred]:** three parts. Tracing as a band multiplied by its own shaped copy (even harmonics); Pinch as a symmetric curve with the opposite sign on the right side in stereo mode; each processed oversampled with only the difference added, so with the drives at 0 the output is the input; Crackle as random clicks at a density.
- **Open points for the design:** the global Drive scales both models and acts like a wet/dry amount; Live's Soft/Hard mode is about the dub-plate sound and probably softer extremes of motion, not only a softer curve; the units of Crackle Volume and Density and of the Q display are unknown [measure]; whether the device reports latency (perhaps none) [measure].
- **Building blocks:** a biquad band-pass with the bandwidth in octaves, static curves, oversampling, a DC blocker, parameter smoothing, a seeded noise source (shared with Erosion, 29.17).
- **Also needed:** a global Drive stage (mix and scale), the Hard and Soft modes, an XY display, double-click value entry for Freq, Alt-drag for Q (UI).
- **`.als` element:** `Vinyl` [dump 12.4.6] (the summary header; the display name is Vinyl Distortion). Parameter elements [als 12.4.6]: `On`; Tracing is the `Band2` set (the one with 684 Hz) and Pinch the `Band1` set (7500 Hz), matched by their stored values [inferred]: `Band2On`, `Gain2` (Tracing Drive, 0), `BandFreq2` (Hz, 684), `BandQ2` (0.32); `Band1On`, `Gain1` (Pinch Drive, 0), `BandFreq1` (Hz, 7500), `BandQ1` (3); `Drive` (global, 1); `SoftHard` (0 = Soft); `NormalMono` (0 = Stereo); `CracleDensity` (10) and `CracleVolume` (0), both spelled so in the file.
