# Reverb (manual 29.32)

**Family:** reverb and resonance
**Note status:** dump checked, measured (Reverb runs and remaining runs, [Measured](#measured))

## Summary

A classic algorithmic room reverb split into the stages a designer thinks in: filters on the
input, a set of early reflections, a diffusion network that builds the late tail (with
frequency-dependent decay), a little chorus on the tail, and global controls for size, decay,
predelay, stereo width and freezing. It runs from tight rooms to very long, almost-echo spaces
and can hold a frozen tail indefinitely [manual].

## Signal flow

```text
in -> mono sum -> low roll-off -> [Lo Cut] -> [Hi Cut] -> Predelay -> early reflections (Spin, Shape) --Reflect--+
                                     \                                              +-> sum -> Stereo -> Dry/Wet -> out
                                      -> diffusion network (Size, Decay, Diffusion, Scale,
                                         High and Low filters inside, Chorus) ----Diffuse--+
in ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ dry ---^
```

- The input is summed to mono before anything else: a left-only and a right-only input give the
  same output [measured 12.4.6] (Measured › Mix and levels).
- The input filters come first in the chain [manual]. Before them, with both cuts off, the
  lowest two octaves are already rolled off (Measured › Input filter) [measured 12.4.6]. The
  predelay sits before the first early reflection [manual].
- The early reflections and the diffused tail are separate outputs that Reflect and Diffuse
  scale before they are summed [manual]. The diffusion network is fed in parallel with the
  reflections, not from them: the tail is identical with Reflect at its minimum and at 0 dB, and
  the reflections identical with Diffuse at either end [measured 12.4.6]. Shape implies an
  overlap in time between the two [manual]; it moves the tail's onset (Measured).
- Chorus acts on the diffusion (the delays of the network) [manual], not on the early
  reflections (those have Spin) [manual].
- Stereo is applied at the output of the wet signal [inferred]; Dry/Wet is last [manual].
- Freeze acts on the diffusion network: it holds the diffused response of the input [manual].

## Behaviour

- **Structure.** A feedback delay network (FDN) late reverb with allpass diffusers and
  modulated delay lines, preceded by a multitap (or modulated) early-reflection stage, is the
  most likely design [inferred]. The manual itself calls the digital approach "a combination of
  filters and modulated delay lines" [manual].
- **Predelay.** A delay of up to the maximum before both reflections and tail [manual]. The
  reported silence at long predelay (fixed in 11.2) suggests a dedicated buffer sized to the
  maximum [source: 2, inferred].
- **Input filter.** Two filters in series: a high-pass (Lo Cut) and a low-pass (Hi Cut), the
  pad's X and Y positioning them symmetrically around a centre with a bandwidth [manual,
  inferred]; the Hi Cut is a 12 dB/octave low-pass; the Lo Cut is a gentle high-pass of 3 to 8 dB/octave, not a clean 6 or 12 dB/octave section [measured 12.4.6]. With both off, a fixed roll-off still takes 4 to 9 dB off 63 Hz [measured 12.4.6].
- **Spin.** A slow modulation of the early reflections' delay times; the Ableton forum
  reports about 2 ms swing at 0.07 Hz up to about 55 ms swing at about 1.3 Hz in the
  older X-Y design, and a good starting point of about 10 ms at about 1 Hz [source: 4]. A
  Doppler pitch shift at high rates follows from modulated delay [manual, inferred].
- **Shape.** Changes the time envelope of the reflections and when the diffusion's onset is
  [manual]; measured: a higher Shape fades the reflections faster and starts the diffuse tail later [measured 12.4.6] (Measured › Early reflections). A shorter Decay fades them faster too, roughly in inverse proportion [measured 12.4.6].
- **Diffusion network.** The High filter and Low shelf are inside the feedback loop, so they
  set frequency-dependent decay time (decay shortens in the bands they cut) [manual, inferred];
  the High filter is a one-pole low-pass or a shelf [source: 2].
- **Size and Decay.** Size scales the delay lengths; Decay sets the loop gain per delay length
  so that T60 does not depend on Size [inferred; confirmed, measured 12.4.6], though T60 reads 0.87 to 0.9 of the setting, not the setting itself (Measured › Decay). The tail starts at the same level whatever the Decay and decays from there, so a short Decay is quieter overall [measured 12.4.6]. Size is stated as the room's
  volume; very large sizes give a delay-like "shifting diffused" sound because the delay
  lines become long compared to the echo density [manual, inferred].
- **Smooth.** None: delay times jump. Slow or Fast: the delay times glide to the new value over
  a period [manual, source: 2]; the two durations are [measure].
- **Diffusion and Scale.** Echo density and the coarseness of the diffuser delay times;
  effect strongest in small rooms [manual].
- **Chorus.** Low-frequency modulation of the network delay lines (one LFO per channel with
  offset phase is likely) [inferred].
- **Freeze.** The loop gain goes to 1 (with the shelving filters in the loop unless Flat is
  on); Cut mutes the input to the network; with Cut off the input keeps adding to the
  frozen tail, which therefore builds up [manual].
- **Stereo.** The 120-degree maximum is a microphone-angle style label: it controls how much of
  the two decorrelated tail channels are cross-mixed; 0 gives mono [manual, inferred].
- **Density.** Sparse, Low, Mid, High change the number of delay lines or diffuser stages
  [manual, inferred].
- **Latency.** Not documented. In the export the dry impulse sits at sample 0 and the wet path starts at the Predelay setting, so there is no latency the export did not remove [measured 12.4.6]; Live's own latency readout was not read.
- **Max wrapper.** Cycling '74 has `abl.device.reverb~`, described as a plate reverb with
  `damping` (20 – 20000 Hz), `decay` (0 – 1), `mix` (0 – 1) and `predelay` (0 – 10 s); it
  is not this device and has none of its stages [source: 5, inferred].

## Edge cases

- Predelay at the maximum with a short Decay: nothing is audible until the predelay ends;
  must not produce silence permanently (Live 11.2 fixed such a bug) [source: 2].
- Freeze on with Cut off, Flat off: the tail gradually changes in colour as the shelves act
  [manual].
- Freeze on, then Freeze off: the tail decays with the current Decay value from the held level
  [inferred; measure].
- Size changed while playing with Smooth None: clicks and pitch glitches [manual]. With Slow or
  Fast: a pitch glide in the tail [inferred].
- Stereo at 0 degrees with a mono input: mono output [manual].
- Both input filters off: full-band input, no filter CPU [manual].
- Both Spin and Chorus off: static tail, lowest CPU, metallic ringing at very small sizes
  [inferred].
- Mono input to a stereo reverb: the tail's decorrelation comes from the network, not from the
  input [inferred]; as the input is summed to mono, a stereo input's width is not kept in the wet
  signal either [measured 12.4.6].
- Silence in: the tail decays to zero; denormals must be flushed [inferred].

## Measured

[measured 12.4.6] Behaviour runs at 44.1 kHz in Live 12.4.6 with the harness's probe signal (long-gap versions, 10 s and 30 s between signals), Dry/Wet 100 %, Chorus and Spin off unless the case says otherwise. Impulse, sweep, noise and tone renders, 98 cases in five sets (`measurements/29.32-reverb/`: `reverb-structure`, `-decay`, `-filters`, `-texture`, `-size`), then 28 cases in six runs of the remaining set (`reverb-locut-law`, `-network-freq`, `-freeze-static`, `-smooth-slow`, `-spin-grid`, `-diffusion-size10`; the last section of this chapter), then `reverb-smooth-glide` and `reverb-freeze-toggle` of the third set, rendered by Live 12.4.7 (Measured › Third set). Figures are what the renders show; where a number comes from a fit, the fit is named. These are observations, not a model.

### Things that affect how to read the renders

- **Size does nothing in an export while Smooth is Fast (or Slow).** In the first runs (`reverb-structure`, `reverb-decay`), with the loaded preset's Smooth = Fast, every Room Size from 0.22 to 500 gave a bit-identical render. With Smooth = None the same sizes give different renders, and a Fast case at Size 1.53 is bit-identical to the untouched default. So the Size values in the first runs never took effect (their Size cases are void; the Diffusion and Scale cases of `reverb-texture` ran at Size 100, not 1.53: their energy in the first 0.5 s matches Size 100, not the Size 1.53 renders of `reverb-size`). The `reverb-size` run repeats them with Smooth None. This is how the API-driven offline render behaves; it says nothing about what Smooth does live (the glide times stay [measure]).
- **Three of the five exports (`reverb-filters`, `-texture`, `-size`) came out 1764 samples (40.00 ms) early** against the probe, bypass included; the first two exports were exact. Unexplained. Those three were analysed with `--align -1764`, which makes the bypass match the probe (impulse at 0 ms, 0 dB).
- **Two runs of the same settings do not render identically.** `reverb-decay decay-3.46s` and `reverb-size decay-3.46s-size-100` hold the same values (Decay 3.46 s, Reflect at its minimum, network filters off, Size 100); the first ran with the preset's Smooth Fast and the second with Smooth None. The second's impulse response is 0.3, 0.2, 0.5, 1.0 and 1.6 dB louder in the windows 0-0.15, 0.15-0.5, 0.5-1, 1-2 and 2-4 s, and its T60 reads 3.13 s against 3.01 s [measured 12.4.6]. Whether Smooth, or something else about the run, makes the difference is not known; differences of this size between runs are not a property of the controls.
- **The remaining set does not reproduce the void Size under Smooth Fast or Slow.** In `reverb-smooth-slow` the untouched template (stored Smooth = 2, Fast) at Size 10.54 renders bit for bit like Smooth Slow at 10.54, and both have the tap spacing of Smooth None at 10.54 and at 1.53 (below). So a Size stored in the set does reach the audio under Fast and Slow there; the identical renders of the first runs are not a general property of Smooth. What differed in those runs is not known [measured 12.4.6].
- `impulse.rt60_s` from `analyse.py` is not usable for a reverb with early reflections: it fits the Schroeder curve's first stretch and read 0.05 s for a 1.2 s tail in one run. The decays below are refits of the energy envelope of the impulse response (50 ms or 20 ms bins, both channels summed).

### Mix and levels

- **Dry/Wet** is an equal-power crossfade: at 55 % the dry impulse arrives at sample 0 with gain 0.6494 = cos(0.55 x pi/2) to six digits, and the wet energy is 2.6 dB below the 100 % render (sin(0.55 x pi/2) is 2.4 dB below; the preset also differs in Spin, Chorus and filters) [measured 12.4.6]. Not a linear 1 - x.
- **Reflect and Diffuse** are independent gains in the displayed dB: with Diffuse at its minimum the tail is 30.46 dB below the 0 dB render, and with Reflect at its minimum the reflections are 30.46 dB below [measured 12.4.6]. So the displayed -30 dB is -30.46 dB in practice (or the 0.0 dB reading is +0.46 dB; the render cannot tell which).
- The dry path adds no delay in the export: the dry impulse is at sample 0 for bypass, the preset and the template [measured 12.4.6].
- **The input is summed to mono.** The harness's stereo matrix (a 1 kHz tone into the left channel only, then the right only) gives the same output levels for both inputs: identical to 0.0001 dB in all 78 cases that are fully wet and static, within 0.12 dB in the nine Chorus and Spin cases (the tone moves while it is measured) and 0.013 dB at Decay 60 s (the first tone's tail still sounds under the second), and different only in the five preset cases, where 45 % of dry stereo signal is mixed in [measured 12.4.6]. So both inputs reach the reflections and the tail the same way; a single input channel, or (L + R) / 2, feeds them.

### Predelay

The first reflection arrives at the Predelay setting plus about 0.06 to 0.09 ms (three or four samples): 0.57, 2.56, 10.09, 50.07 and 249.82 ms for 0.50, 2.5, 10, 50 and 250 ms (the 250 ms case was built at 249.9 ms: 0.9998 of the travel). The whole response, reflections and tail, shifts with it, and nothing goes silent at the maximum [measured 12.4.6]. The reflections' level does not follow the predelay: the first tap's peak is -29.9, -30.0, -30.4, -32.7 and -44.0 dB at 0.5, 2.5, 10, 50 and 250 ms, a fall of about 0.057 dB per ms of Predelay (-14 dB at 250 ms), as if the reflections' fade runs from the input and not from the first reflection [measured 12.4.6].

### Early reflections

- **Pattern.** A fixed pattern of taps, each a short cluster (the tap, then about -17.6 dB at +0.09 ms and -26 dB at +0.25 ms: a few samples of filter smear), alternating between a dominant left and right channel [measured 12.4.6]. At Size 100, Predelay 2.5 ms, Shape 0.5, Stereo 100, times after the first tap and dominant-channel levels: 0 ms -29.9 dB (R), 7.37 -30.3 (R), 8.98 -31.3 (R), 11.43 -30.5 (L), 16.35 -31.9 (R), 17.91 -30.9 (L), 20.41 -36.6 (R), 26.55 -31.4 (L), 28.44 -31.4 (L), 30.29 -37.0 (L), 34.17 -31.8 (R). About 50 taps above -62 dB in 130 ms; the reflections are gone by about 135 ms. The first tap is 0.032 linear (-29.9 dB) with an impulse of -6 dB in.
- **Size.** The tap times scale with the cube root of Size, as a room volume would: tap k's time divided by Size^(1/3) is constant to 1 % for Size 1.53, 10.54, 100 and 500 (tap 1: 1.59 ms, tap 3: 2.46, tap 5: 3.86, tap 7: 5.72). At Size 0.22 the early taps are too close to separate (about 1 ms apart) and the ratio drifts. The first tap always arrives at the predelay, whatever the Size. The first tap's level moves by under 3 dB over the whole range (-30.5 dB at 1.53 and 10.54, -29.9 at 100, -27.6 at 500; the -26.3 at 0.22 is several taps overlapping) [measured 12.4.6].
- **Shape** sets how fast the reflections fade, without moving any tap. Fitting a straight line (in dB) through the 11 strongest taps in the first 57 ms, the slope is -0.020, -0.034, -0.048, -0.062 and -0.075 dB/ms for Shape 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 1: about -0.020 dB/ms - 0.055 dB/ms x Shape, with the first tap (-30.2 dB on this fit) unchanged [measured 12.4.6]. At 41 ms after the first tap Shape 0 is 1.2 dB above and Shape 1 is 1.1 dB below the default.
- **The diffuse tail's onset** (first 2 ms bin within 20 dB of the tail's plateau, tail alone) moves with Shape: about 54, 62, 72, 80 and 88 ms for Shape 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 1 at Size 100, so roughly 54 ms + 34 ms x Shape, measured from the input and including the 2.5 ms predelay [measured 12.4.6]. It also moves with Size: 32, 44, 72 and 108 ms for Size 1.53, 10.54, 100 and 500, which fits 19.5 ms + 11.1 ms x Size^(1/3) to 1 ms [measured 12.4.6, Shape 0.5]. The tail then builds to its plateau over a further 40 to 100 ms.
- **Decay** also sets how fast the reflections fade. The energy envelope of the reflections (10 ms bins, a straight line in dB through the first 120 ms after the first tap, Diffuse at its minimum, Shape 0.5) falls 0.355 and 0.373 dB/ms at Decay 200 ms (`reverb-structure predelay-2.5ms`, `predelay-10ms`) against 0.072 dB/ms at the default 1.2 s (`er-only`): about five times faster for a Decay six times shorter [measured 12.4.6]. With this fit's numbers, the fade is roughly the Shape slope above times 1.2 s / Decay; the strongest-taps fit of Shape and this envelope fit give different slopes for the same render (0.048 against 0.072 dB/ms at 1.2 s), so compare like with like.
- **Spin.** The reflections' delays are modulated; measured as the FM of a 1 kHz sine through the reflections (the harness's convention, half the spread between the 1st and 99th percentile of the zero-crossing frequency): the LFO rate matches the Rate display (0.305 Hz at 0.30, 1.31 Hz at 1.30), the deviation is proportional to Rate, and Amount sets a peak delay deviation of about 0.7 ms per unit (Amount 2: 1.2 ms, 17.5: 12.3 ms, 55: 37.9 ms, taken as peak deviation = cents / (1731 x 2 pi x rate); 17.5 gives 12.4 ms at 0.30 Hz and 12.2 ms at 1.30 Hz). That figure is superseded: the tone's FM through the sum of many taps is inflated by the interference between them. Tracking single taps by correlation (Measured › Remaining runs › Spin) gives a swing proportional to each tap's own delay, about 0.97 % per unit of Amount, 4.9 ms peak to peak on a 14 ms tap at the default [measured 12.4.6]. The forum's "2 ms to about 55 ms" fits the Amount range, not the delay swing.

### Decay

- **T60 against the Decay setting** (energy envelope of the impulse response, network filters off, Reflect at its minimum, fit -5 to -35 dB below the peak; Size 100): 0.2 s reads 0.20 s, 0.832 s reads 0.75 s, 1.2 s reads 1.06 s, 3.46 s reads 3.01 s, 14.4 s reads 12.0 s, 60 s reads about 47 s [measured 12.4.6]. That is 0.87 to 0.9 of the setting from 0.8 to 3.5 s and lower above. The 60 s case has only a 30 s window: -5 to -15 dB gives 43 s, -35 to -45 dB gives 51 s, so the true figure is somewhere near 45 to 50 s (not measured with a window past 60 s).
- **The curve is slightly concave**: the local T60 rises by 5 to 7 % over each 10 dB (3.46 s: 2.97 s at -5 to -15 dB, 3.13 s at -45 to -55 dB; 14.4 s: 11.7 to 12.5 s). That is the frequency dependence below, not a drift: even with both network filters off the decay is longer at low frequencies. Octave-band T60 at Decay 3.46 s: 3.11 s at 63 Hz, 3.12 at 125, 3.30 at 250, 3.15 at 500, 3.16 at 1 kHz, 3.09 at 2 kHz, 3.00 at 4 kHz, 2.71 at 8 kHz, 2.39 at 16 kHz [measured 12.4.6]. The mid bands are therefore at about 0.91 x the setting.
- **T60 does not depend on Size**: at Decay 3.46 s, 3.09 to 3.21 s for Size 1.53, 10.54, 100 and 500 (20 ms bins, three fit ranges); the build-up to the peak takes longer at larger Size (peak of the envelope at 0.11 s for 1.53 and 10.54, 0.17 s at 100, 0.23 s at 500) [measured 12.4.6].
- **The tail starts at the same level whatever the Decay.** Its energy from 0.15 to 0.3 s, against the 60 s render, is -15.2, -10.7, -3.7 and -0.7 dB at Decay 0.832, 1.2, 3.46 and 14.4 s (`reverb-decay`, tail alone, filters off). The 0.2 s render (-54.7 dB) is still building up in that window and does not fit this reading.

### Input filter

Measured as the response of the whole reverb (sweep, checked against the noise cross-spectrum), relative to the same reverb with both cuts off. A time-invariant case, so the sweep is valid [measured 12.4.6].

- **Hi Cut** is a low-pass, about 12 dB/octave (a second-order section fits to 0.7 dB at Input Freq 100 Hz, Q about 0.7, and to 0.45 dB at 1 kHz). Its -3 dB point is at Input Freq x 2^(Width/2): 762 Hz at 100 Hz and Width 5.85 (nominal 758), 7.58 kHz at 1 kHz (nominal 7.58 kHz); with both cuts on at 1 kHz and Width 4.75 the upper -3 dB point is 5.3 kHz (nominal 5.2). At Width 9 the Hi Cut is above the range and the response is flat to 0.05 dB. So the pad's Y axis is the band's width in octaves between the two cut frequencies, and the Hi Cut sits half a width above the centre.
- **Lo Cut** is a high-pass, but not a clean one: at Input Freq 1 kHz and Width 5.85 the response is -0.5, -1.6, -3.9, -7.1, -10.0, -13.2 dB at 905, 453, 226, 113, 57 and 28 Hz (slope 3 to 8 dB/octave, flattening slowly at the bottom), with -3 dB at 279 Hz and -6 dB at 143 Hz; at 5 kHz -3 dB is at 1.65 kHz and -6 dB at 980 Hz; at 100 Hz it has no effect above 20 Hz. The nominal corner would be Input Freq / 2^(Width/2) (132 Hz at 1 kHz), but the measured -3 dB points sit 2.1 to 2.5 times above it and rise slightly faster than Input Freq. A single biquad or one-pole does not fit (1.3 to 1.6 dB error); the shape is not understood [measured 12.4.6; the Lo Cut's law uncertain].
- **A fixed low roll-off below both cuts.** With Lo Cut and Hi Cut off, the noise response of the whole reverb (cross-spectrum against the input, both channels, octave bands, against the 1 kHz octave) is -8.9 dB at 63 Hz and -3.7 dB at 125 Hz with reflections and tail (`reverb-filters input-off`), -9.4 and -3.8 dB for the reflections alone (`reverb-structure er-only`) and -5.1 and -2.3 dB for the tail alone (`reverb-decay decay-3.46s`), while 250 Hz to 2 kHz stay within 2 dB; the bypass is flat to 0.01 dB at 63 Hz [measured 12.4.6]. So the input loses the lowest two octaves whatever the Lo Cut does: about a first-order high-pass near 90 Hz for the tail, steeper for the reflections. The Lo Cut figures above are relative to this response, not to the input.
- With both cuts on at 1 kHz and Width 0.5 the pair acts as a band-pass whose peak is 9.3 dB below the unfiltered response (at 1.14 kHz), -3 dB at 608 and 1896 Hz.

- The filters are in the feedback loop: they set the decay per band, and the effect fits "extra loss per trip round the loop". If a band loses L dB per trip and the loop's mean length is tau, its decay rate is 60/T60,off + L/tau (dB/s). With tau about 0.15 to 0.19 s every row fits: the High shelf's high band loses 20 log10(gain): -14 dB at 0.2 and -4.4 dB at 0.6 (T60 0.6 and 1.2 s: tau 0.19 and 0.15 s), the Lowpass loses 1.3, 2.9 and 6.7 dB at 500 Hz, 1 kHz and 2 kHz, which is a one-pole low-pass with its corner at the setting (1, 3 and 7 dB), and the Low shelf's low band loses about 12 dB at 0.2 and 3.6 dB at 0.6 (against 14 and 4.4 dB, so the loop looks about 0.14 s long at low frequencies). The preset's High filter (4.5 kHz, 0.70) predicts 1.56 s at the top band and reads 1.4 to 1.6 s.
- **So: the gain control is a linear gain per loop pass applied above (High shelf) or below (Low shelf) the frequency; the Lowpass type ignores Gain and is a one-pole low-pass with its corner at Hi Freq.** The shelf transition is about one octave wide; at its set frequency a first-order shelf would be half way (7 dB of 14), the render shows 5.2 dB at 1 kHz for the High shelf (37 %) and about 3.9 dB at 500 Hz for the Low shelf (28 %), so the shelf's own midpoint sits above the High frequency and below the Low frequency, not at it. A first-order shelf is the simplest fit but the order was not fitted.
- Even with both filters off the decay shortens above 2 kHz (2.39 s at 16 kHz against 3.1 s at 1 kHz): a fixed high-frequency loss is built into the network.

### Stereo

- The output is a rotation of the two decorrelated wet channels. With the crosstalk as the ratio of the weaker to the dominant channel at a reflection tap, and the correlation measured over the reflections and over 0.3 to 1.5 s of the tail: **Stereo 0** gives L = R exactly (correlation 1.000, every tap at the same level in both); **60** gives crosstalk -7.7 dB, correlation 0.71; **100** (the default) -17.6 dB, 0.26; **120** at least -35 dB (95 dB at the first taps), correlation 0.00 [measured 12.4.6].
- These fit a mixing angle beta = 45 degrees x (1 - Stereo / 120): L' = cos(beta) L + sin(beta) R, R' = sin(beta) L + cos(beta) R. Crosstalk = tan(beta): tan 22.5 = 0.414 = -7.66 dB (measured -7.7), tan 7.5 = 0.132 = -17.6 dB (measured -17.6). Correlation = sin(2 beta) = cos(0.75 x Stereo in degrees): 0.707, 0.259 and 0 (measured 0.71, 0.26 and 0.00). At Stereo 0 both channels carry (L + R) / sqrt(2): the first tap is 0.0229 against 0.0323 at 120 [measured 12.4.6].

- **Fast has finished its glide 50 ms after the jump, Slow has not.** Fast is 0.09 to 0.20 ms short of None's tap times, the offset being the 0.09 ms shift that Slow and Fast already show for a stored Size (12.4.6) and no more; Slow is 1.0 to 2.1 ms short in the left channel (11 %, 9.6 %, 7 %, 6.5 %, 2.3 % of the tap's delay) and 1.1 to 2.2 ms in the right (the first right tap, 2.6 ms, does not depend on Size). The later taps are closer because they leave the network later, when more of the glide is done [measured 12.4.7]. The Size change of 10 to 100 moves the right early taps by 3.7 to 8 ms (the 12.4.6 taps at Size 10.54, 6.19, 8.09 and 11.16 ms, against these), so 50 ms after the jump about 2 to 5 % of the change is left under Fast and about 30 to 40 % under Slow, which for an exponential glide is a time constant near 15 ms (Fast) and 40 to 50 ms (Slow) [measured 12.4.7; the exponential form and the time constants are inferred from the one time point].

**Freeze toggled during the render** (`reverb-freeze-toggle`: Decay 3.46 s, the impulse at 0.5 s, Freeze on at beat 2 (1.0 s), off at beat 40 (20 s); the probe's 4 s sweep at 10.5 s enters while the reverb is frozen; levels are rms over 0.5 s windows of the stereo output):

| Case | level at 2 / 5 / 8 s | drain while frozen | with the sweep entering (14 to 19 s) | after release |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cut on, Flat on (the template's) | -64.5 / -65.3 / -66.0 dB | 0.25 dB/s | not entered: -66.3 dB at 10 s, -67.5 at 19 s | decays 19.65 dB/s |
| Cut on, Flat off | -66.2 / -67.7 / -68.6 | 0.40 dB/s | not entered | decays 19.4 dB/s |
| Cut off, Flat on | -64.5 / -65.3 / -66.0 | 0.25 dB/s | enters: -5.95 dB at 14 s, -6.6 at 19 s | decays 19.55 dB/s |
| Cut off, Flat off | -66.2 / -67.7 / -68.6 | 0.40 dB/s | enters: -6.6 dB at 14 s, -7.6 at 19 s | decays 19.5 dB/s |

- **Freeze on stops the tail's fall within about 0.2 s**: the level falls about 20 dB/s up to 1.0 s, is -62.8 at 1.0 s, -64.3 at 1.2 s and -64.1 at 1.5 s; after that it holds with the slow drain of the table [measured 12.4.7]. With Flat on the drain is 0.25 dB/s, with Flat off 0.40 dB/s, in the same order as the 12.4.6 renders frozen from the first sample (0.4 and 1 dB/s per second of energy, an impulse response without the half second of tail built up first) [measured 12.4.7].
- **Cut on keeps the new input out entirely**: while frozen with Cut on the sweep does nothing (the level goes on draining from -66.3 to -67.5 dB through the sweep at 10.5 to 14.5 s) [measured 12.4.7]. With Cut off the sweep enters and fills the frozen loop: about 60 dB louder within 3 s (-20.6 dB at 11 s, -11 at 12, -6.9 at 13 s) to a plateau of about -6 dB (Flat on) or -6.6 dB (Flat off), which then drains slowly (0.13 and 0.2 dB/s) [measured 12.4.7].
- **Freeze off returns to the normal decay within about 0.3 s**: from 20.0 s the level falls at -14 dB/s at first and at 19.4 to 19.65 dB/s from 21 s on, which is a T60 of 3.05 to 3.09 s, the 0.87 to 0.9 of the Decay setting that 12.4.6 found for 3.46 s [measured 12.4.7].
- **The freeze leaves no trace**: from 25 s on, with the staircase entering, the four renders are identical (-62.1, -50.1, -38.1 dB, ...), so the reverb state after the released tail has decayed (-150 dB by 24 s) is the same in all [measured 12.4.7].

## Measurement plan

1. **Ranges and defaults.** Settled by the dump [dump 12.4.6]. What the plain-number controls mean, as measured: Input Width is the width in octaves between the cuts, Size is a volume (reflection times go with its cube root), Shape is the reflections' fade slope (0.020 + 0.055 x Shape dB/ms), Spin Amount about 0.97 % of each tap's delay (not milliseconds) and Chorus Amount about 1.8 ms of peak delay deviation per unit, the shelf gains a linear loss per trip round the loop, Stereo a mixing angle (see Measured). Spin Amount's unit, Chorus Amount's unit and the Lo Cut's law stay uncertain.
2. **Predelay.** Impulse in, Dry/Wet 100 %, Reflect 0 dB, Diffuse minimum; find the first
   reflection's arrival versus Predelay across its range; read the maximum.
   Done: arrival = Predelay + 0.06 to 0.09 ms up to the 250 ms maximum; level falls 0.057 dB/ms (Measured › Predelay).
3. **Input filter.** Sweep a sine or use white noise with Reflect and Diffuse both on; measure
   the response of Lo Cut and Hi Cut at several settings: corner, slope, Q. Confirm what the
   pad's Y axis maps to by driving the pad and reading the two sliders.
   Done for the cut shapes (Measured › Input filter); the Lo Cut's law is not understood. The pad's Y was read from Input Width: the Hi Cut sits at centre x 2^(width/2).
   More data (Measured › Remaining runs): the Lo Cut's response at five settings in a table, the Lo Cut's corner is clamped at 44 Hz or above, the law is still not understood.
4. **Early reflections.** Impulse with Diffuse minimum: record the tap pattern (times and
   levels) at Size minimum, mid and maximum; Shape at 0, 50, 100 %; compare Spin on and off,
   and read the modulation depth and rate at several Amount and Rate values.
   Done for tap pattern, Size and Shape, Spin depth and rate (Measured › Early reflections); Spin at one Rate and Amount pair per axis only.
   Spin re-read with a cross-correlation tracker: the swing is a fraction of each tap's delay (about 0.97 % per unit of Amount, 17 % at the default), independent of Rate (Measured › Remaining runs). The earlier 0.7 ms per unit of Amount was too large.
5. **Decay.** Impulse at several Decay and Size settings; fit T60 from the energy decay curve
   (Schroeder integration). Check that it is independent of Size.
   Done (Measured › Decay): T60 is independent of Size, 0.87 to 0.9 of the setting; the 60 s end needs a window past 60 s.
6. **Frequency-dependent decay.** With High and Low filters on, measure T60 per octave band
   at several gains and frequencies; determine filter orders and whether the High filter is a
   one-pole low-pass or a high shelf; compare with the switch in each position.
   Done (Measured › Diffusion network filters): per-loop-pass gain above (High shelf) or below (Low shelf) the frequency; Lowpass is a gain-independent one-pole; the shelf is a high shelf.
   Added: High shelf at 250 Hz, 4 kHz and 12 kHz, Lowpass at 250 Hz and 4 kHz, Low shelf at 200 Hz and 2 kHz: the Lowpass is a one-pole at its setting, the shelves' half-loss point is at 1.2 to 1.25 times Hi Freq and 0.75 to 0.85 times Lo Freq (Measured › Remaining runs).
7. **Diffusion and Scale.** Impulse at small Size; read echo density over time and the
   spectral colouring (comb spacing) at Diffusion 0, 50, 100 % and Scale min and max.
   Partly: five cases at Size 1.53 (Measured › Density, Diffusion and Scale); comb spacing was not read.
   Added: Diffusion 0.1 and 96 % and Scale 5 and 100 % at Size 10.54: Diffusion is slowest at both ends, Scale low builds sooner, the colour hardly changes (Measured › Remaining runs).
8. **Chorus.** Sine in; read side-band spacing and depth at several Amount and Rate values.
   Done with a 1 kHz tone through the tail (Measured › Chorus); side-band spacing is the Rate.
9. **Freeze, Flat, Cut.** Burst in, toggle Freeze, then compare level over 60 s for Flat on
   and off, Cut on and off; send a second burst while frozen.
   Not done: needs automation during the render.
   Partly done without automation: Freeze on from the first sample, Cut and Flat on and off. With Cut on the impulse never enters; the frozen tail holds, draining about 1 dB per second with Flat off and 0.4 dB per second with Flat on (Measured › Remaining runs). Toggling during the render is still open.
   Done in the third set (Measured › Third set): Freeze toggled during the render, with Flat and Cut on and off, and a sweep entering while frozen; the freeze holds the tail within about 0.2 s, drains 0.25 dB/s (Flat on) or 0.4 dB/s (Flat off), Cut on keeps the sweep out, release returns to the Decay setting's own T60 within about 0.3 s. A second burst while frozen with Cut off is the sweep.
10. **Smooth.** Automate Size with a jump while a sine is fed; read the glide time for None, Slow
    and Fast.
    Not done (the render ignores Size while Smooth is Fast or Slow, Measured › Things that affect how to read the renders).
    Partly: Smooth Slow and None give the same Size taps in a set with Size stored (Measured › Remaining runs); the glide time is still open. The remark that the render ignores Size under Smooth Fast or Slow does not hold for this run.
   Done with a lane: Size 10 to 100, 50 ms before the impulse (Measured › Third set): Fast has finished, Slow has 30 to 40 % of the change left at 50 ms; the glide times are bounded by one time point (about 15 ms and 40 to 50 ms if exponential).
11. **Stereo.** Mono sine or noise; inter-channel correlation of the wet output over 0 – 120
    degrees.
    Done (Measured › Stereo): a rotation by 45 degrees x (1 - Stereo/120).
12. **Density.** CPU time per setting and the echo density of impulse responses.
    Echo density done, CPU not (Measured › Density).
13. **Output and mix.** Reflect and Diffuse level steps; Dry/Wet at 50 % versus the ends;
    delay compensation of the dry path.
    Done: equal-power Dry/Wet, Reflect and Diffuse as displayed dB, dry path at sample 0 (Measured › Mix and levels).

## Implementation notes

- **Building blocks:** biquad filters (input filters, the High and Low shelves; the one-pole
  low-pass can be a first-order section), one-pole parameter smoothing (and the Smooth ramps), an
  LFO for Spin and Chorus, dB conversions, a DC blocker.
- **Missing:** an interpolating delay line, allpass diffusers, a feedback delay network with a
  Hadamard or Householder mixing matrix, an early-reflection multitap generator, a loop-gain
  calculator for T60 with frequency-dependent shelves, noise-free size changes (crossfade or
  glide), a Freeze path that cuts the input and holds loop gain at 1.
- **Hard to match:** the shipped sound (delay lengths, diffuser coefficients, modulation
  design) cannot be copied; use a published FDN design with the implementation's own numbers.
  Dragonfly Reverb (GPL-3.0) is a candidate base for an algorithmic reverb; whether it fits
  Live's controls is a design question.
- **Parameter set for import:** Predelay, Smooth, Size, Decay, Freeze, Flat, Cut, Stereo,
  Density, Reflect, Diffuse, Dry/Wet, input filter on/off and frequencies, Spin on/off,
  amount, rate, Shape, High and Low filter switches, frequencies and gains, High filter type,
  Diffusion, Scale, Chorus on/off, amount and rate.
- **.als element:** `Reverb` [dump 12.4.6]; Live 11.2 renamed parameters, so sets from before and after may differ in their parameter names and Density's list (Eco, Mid, High versus Sparse, Low, Mid, High) [source: 2, inferred]. Parameter elements [als 12.4.6] (stored units where they differ): `On`, `PreDelay` (ms), `BandHighOn` (false) and `BandLowOn` (true), which by their values are Lo Cut On (Off) and Hi Cut On (On) [inferred from the values], `BandFreq` (Hz), `BandWidth`, `SpinOn`, `EarlyReflectModFreq` (Hz), `EarlyReflectModDepth`, `DiffuseDelay` (Shape, 0.5), `ShelfHighOn`, `HighFilterType` (0 = Shelf), `ShelfHiFreq` (Hz), `ShelfHiGain`, `ShelfLowOn`, `ShelfLoFreq` (Hz), `ShelfLoGain`, `ChorusOn`, `SizeModFreq` (Chorus Rate, Hz), `SizeModDepth` (Chorus Amount), `DecayTime` (ms, 1200), `AllPassGain` (Diffusion, 0.6 = 60 %), `AllPassSize` (Scale, 0.4 = 40 %), `FreezeOn`, `FlatOn`, `CutOn`, `RoomSize`, `SizeSmoothing` (2 = Fast), `StereoSeparation` (Stereo, 100), `RoomType` (Density, 1 = Low), `MixReflect` and `MixDiffuse` (linear gain, 1 = 0 dB), `MixDirect` (Dry/Wet, 0.55).
  before and after may differ in their parameter names and Density's list (Eco, Mid, High
  versus Sparse, Low, Mid, High) [source: 2, inferred].
