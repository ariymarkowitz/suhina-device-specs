# Reverb (manual 29.32)

**Family:** reverb and resonance
**Note status:** dump checked

## Summary

A classic algorithmic room reverb split into the stages a designer thinks in: filters on the
input, a set of early reflections, a diffusion network that builds the late tail (with
frequency-dependent decay), a little chorus on the tail, and global controls for size, decay,
predelay, stereo width and freezing. It runs from tight rooms to very long, almost-echo spaces
and can hold a frozen tail indefinitely [manual].

## Signal flow

```text
in -> [Lo Cut] -> [Hi Cut] -> Predelay -> early reflections (Spin, Shape) --Reflect--+
                                     \                                              +-> sum -> Stereo -> Dry/Wet -> out
                                      -> diffusion network (Size, Decay, Diffusion, Scale,
                                         High and Low filters inside, Chorus) ----Diffuse--+
in ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ dry ---^
```

- The input filters come first in the chain [manual]. The predelay sits before the first
  early reflection [manual].
- The early reflections and the diffused tail are separate outputs that Reflect and Diffuse
  scale before they are summed [manual]. Whether the diffusion network is fed from the
  reflections or in parallel with them is [measure]. Shape implies an overlap in time between
  the two, which works either way [manual].
- Chorus acts on the diffusion (the delays of the network) [manual], not on the early
  reflections (those have Spin) [manual].
- Stereo is applied at the output of the wet signal [inferred]; Dry/Wet is last [manual].
- Freeze acts on the diffusion network: it holds the diffused response of the input [manual].

## Controls

Section names follow the manual. Live 11.2 renamed two older controls: the old Density
became Diffusion, and the old Quality became Density (Eco, Mid, High became Sparse, Low, Mid,
High) [source: 2].

## Behaviour

- **Structure.** A feedback delay network (FDN) late reverb with allpass diffusers and
  modulated delay lines, preceded by a multitap (or modulated) early-reflection stage, is the
  most likely design [inferred]. The manual itself calls the digital approach "a combination of
  filters and modulated delay lines" [manual].
- **Predelay.** A delay of up to the maximum before both reflections and tail [manual]. The
  reported silence at long predelay (fixed in 11.2) suggests a dedicated buffer sized to the
  maximum [source: 2, inferred].
- **Input filter.** Two filters in series: a high-pass (Lo Cut) and a low-pass (Hi Cut), the
  pad's X and Y positioning them symmetrically around a centre with a bandwidth [manual,
  inferred]; slopes (probably 12 dB/octave) are [measure].
- **Spin.** A slow modulation of the early reflections' delay times; the Ableton forum
  reports about 2 ms swing at 0.07 Hz up to about 55 ms swing at about 1.3 Hz in the
  older X-Y design, and a good starting point of about 10 ms at about 1 Hz [source: 4]. A
  Doppler pitch shift at high rates follows from modulated delay [manual, inferred].
- **Shape.** Changes the time envelope of the reflections and when the diffusion's onset is
  [manual]; the curve is [measure].
- **Diffusion network.** The High filter and Low shelf are inside the feedback loop, so they
  set frequency-dependent decay time (decay shortens in the bands they cut) [manual, inferred];
  the High filter is a one-pole low-pass or a shelf [source: 2].
- **Size and Decay.** Size scales the delay lengths; Decay sets the loop gain per delay length
  so that T60 matches the Decay setting at any Size [inferred]. Size is stated as the room's
  volume; very large sizes give a delay-like "shifting diffused" sound because the delay
  lines become long compared to the echo density [manual, inferred].
- **Smooth.** None: delay times jump. Slow or Fast: the delay times glide to the new value over
  a period [manual, source: 2]; the two durations are [measure].
- **Diffusion and Scale.** Echo density and the coarseness of the diffuser delay times;
  effect strongest in small rooms [manual].
- **Chorus.** Low-frequency modulation of the network delay lines (one LFO per channel with
  offset phase is likely) [inferred].
- **Freeze.** The loop gain goes to 1 (with the shelving filters in the loop unless Flat is
  on); Cut mutes the input to the network; with Cut off the input keeps adding to the
  frozen tail, which therefore builds up [manual].
- **Stereo.** The 120-degree maximum is a microphone-angle style label: it controls how much of
  the two decorrelated tail channels are cross-mixed; 0 gives mono [manual, inferred].
- **Density.** Sparse, Low, Mid, High change the number of delay lines or diffuser stages
  [manual, inferred].
- **Latency.** Not documented; an algorithmic reverb should report none [inferred; measure].
- **Max wrapper.** Cycling '74 has `abl.device.reverb~`, described as a plate reverb with
  `damping` (20 – 20000 Hz), `decay` (0 – 1), `mix` (0 – 1) and `predelay` (0 – 10 s); it
  is not this device and has none of its stages [source: 5, inferred].

## Edge cases

- Predelay at the maximum with a short Decay: nothing is audible until the predelay ends;
  must not produce silence permanently (Live 11.2 fixed such a bug) [source: 2].
- Freeze on with Cut off, Flat off: the tail gradually changes in colour as the shelves act
  [manual].
- Freeze on, then Freeze off: the tail decays with the current Decay value from the held level
  [inferred; measure].
- Size changed while playing with Smooth None: clicks and pitch glitches [manual]. With Slow or
  Fast: a pitch glide in the tail [inferred].
- Stereo at 0 degrees with a mono input: mono output [manual].
- Both input filters off: full-band input, no filter CPU [manual].
- Both Spin and Chorus off: static tail, lowest CPU, metallic ringing at very small sizes
  [inferred].
- Mono input to a stereo reverb: the tail's decorrelation comes from the network, not from the
  input [inferred].
- Silence in: the tail decays to zero; denormals must be flushed [inferred].

## Measurement plan

1. **Ranges and defaults.** Settled by the dump [dump 12.4.6]. Still to read: what the plain-number controls mean physically (Spin Amount, Shape, Size, Chorus Amount, the two shelf gains, Input Width).
2. **Predelay.** Impulse in, Dry/Wet 100 %, Reflect 0 dB, Diffuse minimum; find the first
   reflection's arrival versus Predelay across its range; read the maximum.
3. **Input filter.** Sweep a sine or use white noise with Reflect and Diffuse both on; measure
   the response of Lo Cut and Hi Cut at several settings: corner, slope, Q. Confirm what the
   pad's Y axis maps to by driving the pad and reading the two sliders.
4. **Early reflections.** Impulse with Diffuse minimum: record the tap pattern (times and
   levels) at Size minimum, mid and maximum; Shape at 0, 50, 100 %; compare Spin on and off,
   and read the modulation depth and rate at several Amount and Rate values.
5. **Decay.** Impulse at several Decay and Size settings; fit T60 from the energy decay curve
   (Schroeder integration). Check that it is independent of Size.
6. **Frequency-dependent decay.** With High and Low filters on, measure T60 per octave band
   at several gains and frequencies; determine filter orders and whether the High filter is a
   one-pole low-pass or a high shelf; compare with the switch in each position.
7. **Diffusion and Scale.** Impulse at small Size; read echo density over time and the
   spectral colouring (comb spacing) at Diffusion 0, 50, 100 % and Scale min and max.
8. **Chorus.** Sine in; read side-band spacing and depth at several Amount and Rate values.
9. **Freeze, Flat, Cut.** Burst in, toggle Freeze, then compare level over 60 s for Flat on
   and off, Cut on and off; send a second burst while frozen.
10. **Smooth.** Automate Size with a jump while a sine is fed; read the glide time for None, Slow
    and Fast.
11. **Stereo.** Mono sine or noise; inter-channel correlation of the wet output over 0 – 120
    degrees.
12. **Density.** CPU time per setting and the echo density of impulse responses.
13. **Output and mix.** Reflect and Diffuse level steps; Dry/Wet at 50 % versus the ends;
    delay compensation of the dry path.

## Implementation notes

- **Building blocks:** biquad filters (input filters, the High and Low shelves; the one-pole
  low-pass can be a first-order section), one-pole parameter smoothing (and the Smooth ramps), an
  LFO for Spin and Chorus, dB conversions, a DC blocker.
- **Missing:** an interpolating delay line, allpass diffusers, a feedback delay network with a
  Hadamard or Householder mixing matrix, an early-reflection multitap generator, a loop-gain
  calculator for T60 with frequency-dependent shelves, noise-free size changes (crossfade or
  glide), a Freeze path that cuts the input and holds loop gain at 1.
- **Hard to match:** the shipped sound (delay lengths, diffuser coefficients, modulation
  design) cannot be copied; use a published FDN design with the implementation's own numbers.
  Dragonfly Reverb (GPL-3.0) is a candidate base for an algorithmic reverb; whether it fits
  Live's controls is a design question.
- **Parameter set for import:** Predelay, Smooth, Size, Decay, Freeze, Flat, Cut, Stereo,
  Density, Reflect, Diffuse, Dry/Wet, input filter on/off and frequencies, Spin on/off,
  amount, rate, Shape, High and Low filter switches, frequencies and gains, High filter type,
  Diffusion, Scale, Chorus on/off, amount and rate.
- **.als element:** `Reverb` [dump 12.4.6]; Live 11.2 renamed parameters, so sets from before and after may differ in their parameter names and Density's list (Eco, Mid, High versus Sparse, Low, Mid, High) [source: 2, inferred].
