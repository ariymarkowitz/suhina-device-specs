# Resonators (manual 29.31)

**Family:** reverb and resonance
**Note status:** dump checked, partly measured (top100 and remaining runs, [Measured](#measured))

## Summary

Five tuned resonators in parallel that ring when the input excites them. Each is tuned in
semitones (or scale degrees, or tuning-system steps) relative to one root note, so the device
turns drums, noise or speech into plucked-string, chord or vocoder-like tones that follow the
input's level. An input filter, a decay, a brightness (Color) control and two resonation modes
shape the character [manual, source: 2].

## Signal flow

```text
in L ----+--> [Filter: LP / BP / HP / Notch, Frequency] ---+--> Resonator I   (L + R input) ---Gain I--+
in R ----+                                                  +--> Resonator II  (L) ---Gain II--+      |
                                                            +--> Resonator III (R) ---Gain III-+      |
                                                            +--> Resonator IV  (L) ---Gain IV--+      +--> Width --> Gain --> Dry/Wet --> out
                                                            +--> Resonator V   (R) ---Gain V---+      |
dry ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

- The filter comes first, then the resonators; the first resonator receives both channels,
  II and IV the left, III and V the right [manual].
- Each resonator has its own On/Off and Gain; one that is off costs no CPU; turning off I does
  not affect the others [manual].
- Resonator I's Note sets the root for all five, whether or not I is switched on [manual,
  source: 2].
- Width, the output Gain and Dry/Wet are at the output; Width acts on the wet signal only
  [manual]. Gain sets the level of the resonated signal, which is inaudible at Dry/Wet 0 % [manual]; the Gain is on the wet path [inferred].

## Behaviour

- **Structure.** Five tuned feedback delay lines (comb filters) in parallel, each with a
  damping filter in the loop, is the most likely design; the Karplus-Strong plucked-string
  model is the same structure [inferred; the manual's "plucked-string timbres", the
  pitch-dependent decay and the Const switch all fit it].
- **Tuning.** A resonator at frequency `f` uses a loop delay of about `1 / f` seconds (minus the
  loop filter's delay), with interpolation for fine tuning; the five frequencies are
  `f_root * 2^(pitch/12)` plus the fine tuning in cents [inferred]. Note is C-1 to C5 by
  default [manual]. Live's note names put C2 at MIDI 48 (the dump's I Note default, 48 -> C2 [dump 12.4.6]), so C-1 is MIDI 12 (about 16.35 Hz at A4 = 440 Hz) and C5 is MIDI 84 (about 1046.5 Hz) [inferred from the dump's internal 12 to 84].
  Measured: the internal Note value n gives a comb whose peaks are multiples of 44100 / (44100 / f + 1) Hz with
  f = 440 × 2^((n − 69)/12), i.e. the delay line is exactly one sample longer than the period of f: the
  pitch is 0.6 cents flat at C-1, 5 at C2, 17 at A3 and 42 at C5; Fine Tuning is ±50 cents in the equal-tempered
  ratio (Measured › Tuning) [measured 12.4.6]. Resonator I outputs nothing before one period has passed.
- **Decay and Const.** If feedback gain `g` is fixed, decay time scales with the delay length,
  so low notes ring longer (the default behaviour), as with a real string [manual, inferred].
  With Const on, feedback is rescaled per delay length so T60 is constant at every pitch
  [manual, inferred]. Measured for Resonator I at C2 with Const on: Decay 0 gives one echo and no repeat; T30
  is 0.093 s at Decay 15, 0.76 s at 50 and 2.83 s at 100, close to 0.00066 × Decay^1.81 s; Decay 100 still
  decays (it stops, below -160 dB, after about 6 s). Const on is only roughly constant (0.72 to 0.96 s from C-1
  to A3, 0.51 s at C5 at Decay 50) and Const off is 1.9 times longer at C0 than with Const on, but only 2.1 times
  longer than at C2, not 4 times as a fixed feedback gain would give (Measured › Decay, Const)
  [measured 12.4.6].
- **Mode A and Mode B.** B lowers the sound by an octave (compared at the same Note) [source: 2].
  That fits a comb filter with negated feedback (peaks at odd multiples of half the loop
  frequency, so the lowest peak is an octave below) [inferred]. The manual says B is
  especially interesting when Resonator I's Note is low [manual]. Measured: both modes have the same period,
  the same echo size and the same T30; in Mode B the echoes alternate in sign, so the peaks sit at odd multiples
  of half the comb spacing and the lowest one is exactly an octave below Mode A (65.2 Hz against 130.4 Hz at
  C2) (Measured › Modes) [measured 12.4.6]. B is therefore A with the feedback sign inverted [inferred from the measurement].
- **Color.** A low-pass in the loop (or a tilt); at the minimum it reduces twang by rolling off
  highs [manual, source: 2]. The corner frequency range and the order are [measure].
- **Filter.** A multimode filter on the input with four types; slope (likely 12 dB per octave
  state-variable or biquad) and Q are [measure] [inferred]. With Decay at 0 the filter alone can
  be heard at 100 % wet [source: 2].
- **Input routing.** I takes the stereo input as a mix (L plus R) [manual]; II and IV take left;
  III and V take right [manual]. Their outputs are panned by Width: 0 % puts all of II to V in
  the centre [manual]; the pan law between centre and full width is [measure]; where
  resonator I is placed (centre) [inferred].
- **Excitation.** Any input excites the resonators: the output follows the input's level and
  envelope, like a vocoder carrier [manual, source: 2]. For sustained resonance with a
  non-percussive input the resonators act as a pitched comb filter [inferred].
- **Scale awareness (12.2).** With Use Current Scale, Note and Pitch are degrees of the clip's
  scale (I in octave 2; pitches ±21 degrees); with no active scale the behaviour (chromatic) is
  [measure] [manual, source: 3]. Extreme pitch values in scale mode once caused glitches or
  crashes, fixed in 12.2: clamp indices [source: 3].
- **Tuning systems (12.2).** With a tuning loaded, resonator pitches follow its set of pitches;
  Note is an index and I is at an absolute pitch of the tuning [manual, source: 3].
- **CPU.** A resonator that is off consumes no CPU [manual].
- **Latency.** None expected; [measure]. At Dry/Wet 100 % the first wet output is one period after the
  impulse (for example 7.6 ms at C2), so no latency can be separated from the loop delay in these renders
  (Measured › Tuning).

## Edge cases

- Note at C-1 (about 16 Hz, loop about 61 ms): long delay lines; the delay buffer must hold the lowest
  pitch, and with Mode B an octave lower again [inferred].
- Pitch +24 on top of Note C5: about 4.2 kHz (C5 is about 1046.5 Hz); the highest resonator frequency must stay under
  Nyquist, also at 44.1 kHz [inferred].
- Decay at maximum: the loop gain approaching 1 must stay stable; Measured: Decay 100 does not sustain: T30 is
  2.83 s (3.07 s from -20 to -50 dB) at C2 with Const on, and the tail ends in digital silence after about
  6 s (Measured › Decay) [measured 12.4.6].
- Input DC or very loud input: the resonators can ring and clip; whether the device limits is
  [measure].
- Mono input: all five resonators get the same signal; the stereo effect comes only from
  Width and the pitch differences [inferred].
- All resonators off: the wet signal is silent [inferred].
- Filter frequency at its limits with Notch: little change [inferred].
- Changing Note or Mode during ringing: delays retune with a click or a pitch glide [measure].
- Scale mode with no scale set: see Behaviour [measure].
- Silence in: rings out and decays; denormals must be flushed [inferred].

## Measured

[measured 12.4.6] Behaviour runs at 44.1 kHz in Live 12.4.6 (`devices/29.31-resonators/`:
`resonators-tuning`, `resonators-decay`, `resonators-modes` in the top100 set; `resonators-color`, `resonators-filter`, `resonators-routing` in the remaining set; `resonators-others`, `resonators-gain-mix`, `resonators-notes-const` in the third set, rendered by Live 12.4.7: Measured › Third set), Dry/Wet 100 %, input
Filter off, Resonator I alone (II to V off), and the other controls at the values of a freshly loaded device
(Mode A, Decay 50, Const on, Color 90, Note C2 = internal 48, Fine Tuning 0, Gain 0 dB, Width 100 %) except
the one under test. Read from the -6 dBFS impulse (2 s after it in the tuning run, 10 s in the other two).
Peak frequencies come from a regression of the comb's spectral peaks on harmonic number (10 harmonics, or
those under Nyquist); T30 is the time to fall 60 dB extrapolated from the Schroeder curve between -5 and
-35 dB. These are observations, not a model.

### Things that affect how to read the renders

- Resonator I is centred: the left and right outputs are identical sample for sample in every case
  [measured 12.4.6].
- The response is a train of echoes one period apart, with nothing before the first (no direct sound). The
  first echo is a smoothed pulse about 3 samples wide, so it begins 2 to 3 samples before one period
  [measured 12.4.6].
- The default case of the modes run is the same render as that of the decay run (both Mode A, C2, Decay 50);
  no two other renders are bit-identical (apart from the bypass references), and every setting asked for reached the audio [measured 12.4.6].

### Input filter

Frequency 1 kHz, Decay 0 (the resonator hardly rings), Resonator I alone, sweep response (`resonators-filter`). Decay 0 does not bypass the resonator: it adds the same high-pass-like roll-off to every case (-31.6 dB at 32 Hz, -25.7 at 63 Hz, -20.2 at 127 Hz, -16.4 at 254 Hz against -13.6 dB at 0.5 to 1.4 kHz, seen at the low end of the Low-pass and Notch renders), which is the resonator's, not the filter's. Dividing it out [measured 12.4.6]:

| Type | Passband level | Shape against the 2nd-order response with Q = 0.8, corner at the Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Low pass | -13.6 dB | -2.0 dB at 1 kHz (model -1.9), -6.0 dB at 1.4 kHz (-6.4), -11.5 dB at 2 kHz (-12.1), -24.5 dB at 4 kHz (-24.2) |
| High pass | -12.7 dB | -2.7 dB at 1 kHz, -6.8 dB at 0.7 kHz (-5.9), -26.9 dB at 254 Hz (-23.5 after the resonator's -3.4 dB) |
| Band pass | -12.5 dB peak at 1 kHz | -1.1 dB at 0.72 kHz (-1.2), -0.8 dB at 1.4 kHz (-1.0) |
| Notch | -13.9 dB | at 1 kHz below -80 dB; -6 dB at 0.7 and 1.4 kHz, -12 to -13 dB at 0.85 and 1.15 kHz: a 2nd-order notch with Q about 0.8 |

- All four types fit one second-order response with Q about 0.8 (a Butterworth would be 0.71: it would give -3.0 dB at the corner, the render shows -2.0 for the low pass and -2.7 for the high pass; the resonator's own roll-off adds to the high pass); the notch is centred on the setting within a few Hz and its depth is beyond what the sweep resolves (-38.6 dB at the sweep's bin, -95 dB in the impulse response) [measured 12.4.6]. The slope is 12 dB per octave for the low pass from 2 to 4 kHz and steepens above 8 kHz (-87 dB at 16 kHz against -73 for the model), which the second-order model does not reach; this may be the resonator's own response there [measured 12.4.6], [inferred].
- The Frequency scales the filter: the low pass at 200 Hz is at -40.7 dB at 1 kHz and -66.1 dB at 4 kHz, 12.7 dB per octave; the band pass at 4 kHz peaks at -13.3 dB at 4 kHz and is down 7 dB at 1 kHz and at 8 kHz [measured 12.4.6].

### Routing and Width

One resonator on at a time, Note C5, 1 kHz tone in the left and in the right channel alone (`resonators-routing`) [measured 12.4.6]:

| Case | left to left | left to right | right to left | right to right |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I, Width 100 % | -1.1 dB | -1.1 | -1.1 | -1.1 |
| II, Width 100 % | +5.0 | none | none (-212) | none |
| III, Width 100 % | none | none (-217) | none | +5.0 |
| II, Width 50 % | +2.5 | -7.1 | none | none |
| II, Width 0 % | -1.1 | -1.1 | none | none |
| III, Width 0 % | none | none | -1.1 | -1.1 |

- **Resonator I hears the mean of both channels and sits in the centre**: a one-sided tone gives -1.1 dB in both outputs, 6.1 dB under the +5.0 dB it gives when both channels carry it, so its input is (L + R) / 2 and its output is the same signal in both channels [measured 12.4.6]. **II hears only the left channel and III only the right** (the unused channel is at -212 dB or below); II's output goes to the left, III's to the right at Width 100 % [measured 12.4.6]. IV and V, which the manual puts with II and III, were not rendered.
- **Width is a mid/side scale**: with s the resonator's signal, the left output of II is s (1 + w) / 2 and its right output s (1 - w) / 2 for the Width fraction w: at 50 % the outputs are 0.75 s (-2.5 dB) and 0.25 s (-12.0 dB against -12.1 measured), at 0 % both 0.5 s (-6.0 dB below the +5.0 dB, measured -1.1 dB). III is the mirror. The law is amplitude-linear, not equal-power: the total power changes with Width, and the sum L + R is constant [measured 12.4.6]. Resonator I's Width was not rendered; as it is centred it probably does not change [inferred].
- The impulse response is the same in every case (first echo at 0.95 ms, -18.7 dB at Width 100 %, -24.7 dB at Width 0 %).

### Third set (Live 12.4.7)

[measured 12.4.7] Behaviour runs at 44.1 kHz in Live 12.4.7 (`resonators-others`, `resonators-gain-mix`, `resonators-notes-const`), read like the 12.4.6 runs above: the -6 dBFS impulse, Dry/Wet 100 %, input Filter off, Decay 50, Mode A, Const on, Color 90, Note C2, Fine Tuning 0, Gain 0 dB, Width 100 % unless a case says otherwise; one resonator on at a time. The period of the echo train is the slope of the positions of the first 12 echoes (each located to a fraction of a sample from its 3-sample pulse) against their number; the first-echo level is the 12.4.6 tap figure (the sum of the pulse's samples, which is twice the peak sample); T30 is the same Schroeder figure as above. Renders of the default Resonator I at C2, Note C3 and C5 agree with the 12.4.6 ones (first echo -19.2, -20.5, -18.7 dB). These are observations, not a model.

- **II and IV answer on the left, III and V on the right, and all four are the same resonator**: with the same settings the II and IV renders are bit-identical on the left (and silent on the right), the III and V renders bit-identical on the right (silent on the left), and the left of II equals the right of III sample for sample [measured 12.4.7].

Tune is in cents: +50 gives 328.46 samples, 44 100 / (130.81 x 2^(50/1200)) + 1 = 328.5 [measured 12.4.7].
- **Const off acts on II as on I**: at Pitch +12 T30 falls from 0.734 to 0.552 s, as I at C3 does (0.737 to 0.552) [measured 12.4.7].

- **The Note law holds over the whole range**: the period is 44 100 / f + 1 samples with f = 440 x 2^((note - 69)/12), to 0.08 samples, from Note 30 to Note 84 and at Const off, so the extra sample of 12.4.6 is a constant, not a function of the pitch [measured 12.4.7].
- **T30 with Const on drifts down slowly over the range**: 0.955 (C-1), 0.841 (C0), 0.811 (F#1), 0.757 (C2), 0.737 (C3), 0.718 (A3), 0.707 (C4), 0.511 s (C5), where the last octave is much shorter [measured 12.4.6 and 12.4.7].

It is not proportional to the period, which a constant loss per echo would give, and not constant [measured 12.4.6 and 12.4.7]. The first-echo level does not depend on Const (-20.5 against -20.5 dB at C3, -19.4 against -19.4 at C4) [measured 12.4.7].
- **Const on and off cross near C2**: Const off is 1.94 times longer than Const on at C0, 1.05 times at C2, 0.75 at C3 and 0.54 at C4; so Const keeps the decay in a narrow band around the C2 value while the other law falls with pitch, which suggests that the decay of a resonator with Const off is set at a reference pitch near C2 [measured 12.4.7; the reference pitch is inferred].

The device adds no latency (the dry sample sits at the input's position; the wet starts with its first echo one period later) [measured 12.4.7].

## Measurement plan

1. **Ranges and defaults.** Settled by the dump [dump 12.4.6] for all controls. Still to read: the Note-name to frequency mapping (see Tuning) and what Decay and Color mean physically.
2. **Tuning.** Impulse in, Dry/Wet 100 %; read each resonator's peak frequency with FFT versus
   Note (the whole range) and Pitch (±24) and Fine Tuning; settle the Note-name mapping and
   the maximum cents.
   Partly: Resonator I at Note C-1, C0, C2, C3, A3, C5 and Fine Tuning ±50: Note n is 440 × 2^((n − 69)/12) Hz
   with the loop one sample long, Fine Tuning exactly ±50 cents (Measured › Tuning). Still open: Resonators II to V,
   Pitch ±24 and the Fine Tuning of the other resonators, all other Note values, the scale and tuning forms.
   More: Resonators II to V at Pitch +12, -24, +24 and Tune +50, and Resonator I at Notes 30, 36, 60, 72, 84 (Measured › Third set): Pitch is in semitones from I's Note, the period 44 100 / f + 1 samples holds across the range. Pitch values between and Tune on the other resonators are still open.
3. **Decay.** Fit T60 versus Decay for each resonator at several pitches, with Const on and off;
   find the Decay unit and the maximum; check the stability at maximum.
   Partly: Resonator I at C2, Const on, Decay 0 to 100 (T30 0.09 to 2.8 s, stable, no sustain at 100), Const on at six
   pitches and Const off at C2 and C0 (Measured › Decay, Const). Still open: Const off at other pitches (two points do
   not give a law), the other resonators, Decay at other pitches.
   More: Const off at Notes 36, 60 and 72 and on II (Measured › Third set): T30 with Const off is proportional to the period to the power 0.52 over four octaves, Const on drifts from 0.96 to 0.71 s; Decay at other pitches and the other resonators' Decay are open.
4. **Modes.** Impulse and noise into Mode A and Mode B at the same Note: partial series (odd or
   all harmonics, spacing, level) and the pitch difference; the comb pattern of A and B from
   white noise.
   Done: from the impulse response at C2 and C0: A has all multiples of the comb spacing, B the odd multiples of half
   of it, the lowest peak exactly an octave lower (Measured › Modes). Noise was not rendered; the comb is the transfer
   function of the echo train, so noise would show the same peaks if the device is linear (not tested).
5. **Color.** White noise through the resonators at several Color values: tilt, corner and
   slope of the loop filter.
   Done at Color 0, 25, 50, 75 and 100 % at Decay 20 and Note C2: a low-pass tilt of the peaks whose slope goes from 8 dB per octave at Color 0 to flat at 100 % (Measured › Color). Still open: other Decay values and notes, noise as input.
6. **Filter.** Sweep a sine at Decay 0 and 100 % wet for each of the four types: corner,
   slope, Q, notch depth; confirm whether the filter can be bypassed.
   Done at 1 kHz for the four types and for the low pass at 200 Hz and the band pass at 4 kHz: second order, Q about 0.8 (Measured › Input filter). Whether the filter can be bypassed at Decay 0 is answered: it cannot, the resonator stays in the path.
7. **Routing.** Left-only and right-only impulses: which resonators respond (I stereo; II, IV
   left; III, V right). Width at 0, 50, 100 %: L/R balance of each resonator output; pan law.
   Done for I, II and III and Width 0, 50, 100 % on II: I hears (L + R) / 2 and is centred, II is left only, III right only, Width a mid/side amplitude scale (Measured › Routing and Width). IV and V, and Width on I, were not rendered.
8. **Gains and mix.** Gain steps of each resonator and the output Gain; Dry/Wet at 50 % versus
   the ends; whether the dry is delayed.
   Done for Resonator I Gain (-12 dB), the output Gain (+6 dB) and Dry/Wet at 50 and 0 %: exact in dB, a linear crossfade (Measured › Third set). The other resonators' Gain is open.
9. **Scale and tuning.** With a clip scale set (for example C major, D Dorian) and with a loaded
   tuning file, read Note and Pitch ranges, each resonator's frequency versus index, and
   the behaviour with no scale.
10. **Latency.** Impulse at Dry/Wet 0 %.
   Done: no latency, the dry is at the input's position and the wet begins with the first echo (Measured › Third set).

## Implementation notes

- **Building blocks:** biquad filters (the input filter; the loop's Color low-pass), parameter
  smoothing (tuning and gain), dB conversions, a DC blocker to keep DC out of the loops.
- **Missing:** an interpolating delay line (fractional delay with allpass or Lagrange read), a
  tuned comb or Karplus-Strong loop with per-pitch feedback compensation for Const, a negative
  feedback variant for Mode B (if confirmed), pitch-to-delay mapping with fine cents, a
  scale-degree or tuning-system pitch mapper (also needed for Spectral Resonator, Meld and
  the MIDI tools), a stereo Width matrix.
- **Hard to match:** Mode B's exact structure, the Color filter, the decay law and the default
  settings; an implementation from the comb-filter model should be close in kind but not
  identical.
- **Parameter set for import:** Filter type and Frequency, Mode, Decay, Const, Color, five On
  and Gain pairs, Note, five Pitches and five Fine Tunings, Width, Gain, Dry/Wet, Use
  Current Scale.
- **.als element:** `Resonator` [dump 12.4.6]. Parameter elements [als 12.4.6] (stored units where they differ): `On`, `InFilterOn`, `InFilterFreq` (Hz, 500), `InFilterMode` (index, 2 = band pass), `ResMode` (true = Mode A), `ResDecay` (50), `ResConst`, `ResColor` (90), `Width` (1 = 100 %), `DryWet` (0.5), `GlobalGain` (dB, 0), `UseCurrentScale`, `ResOn1` … `ResOn5`, `ResNote` (Resonator I's Note, 48), `ResNoteScaleDegrees`, `ResPitch2` … `ResPitch5` (semitones), `ResPitchScaleDegrees2` … `ResPitchScaleDegrees5`, `ResTune1` … `ResTune5`, `ResGain1` … `ResGain5` (linear gain, 1 = 0 dB, the loaded 0.85 internal value).
