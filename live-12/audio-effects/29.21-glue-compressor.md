# Glue Compressor (manual 29.21)

**Family:** dynamics
**Note status:** dump checked, partly measured (top100 and remaining runs, [Measured](#measured))

## Summary

An analog-style bus compressor made with Cytomic and modelled on the compressor of a well-known 1980s mixing console (Sound On Sound names it as the SSL G-series bus compressor) [manual] [source: 2]. It has stepped Ratio, Attack and Release values, no knee control (the knee gets sharper as the ratio rises), an Auto release made of two times, a Range limit on the gain reduction, a switchable soft clipper at the output and a needle meter. It is meant for the Main track or a group, to make several sources sound like one.

## Signal flow

Shared parts: [common.md](common.md) (side-chain section, dry/wet).

```text
input ─┬─► [oversampling 2x, context menu] ─► gain element ─► Makeup ─► soft clip (Soft clip on) ─► dry/wet ─► output
       │                                           ▲
       └─► detector input = own input or external side-chain × Gain, blended by side-chain Dry/Wet [manual]
             └─► side-chain EQ (optional) [manual]
                   └─► level detector (type: [measure])
                         └─► gain computer: Threshold, Ratio, ratio-dependent knee [manual]
                               └─► Range clamp (limits the reduction) [manual]
                                     └─► attack / release smoothing, Release "A" = two times [manual]
                                           └─► needle meter (dB of gain reduction) [manual]
```

- Order of the gain stage, Makeup, Soft clip and Dry/Wet: Soft clip is a "fixed waveshaper" with a −0.5 dB maximum output, which places it last in the wet path [manual]; the dry/wet position relative to Makeup and the clipper is [measure]. In Cytomic's stand-alone plug-in the peak clip comes after the compressor [source: 3].
- Oversampling runs the device at twice the sample rate [manual]. The Live help centre says it costs 32 samples of latency (0.73 ms at 44.1 kHz) and the device has no lookahead [source: 5, 6].

## Behaviour

**Structure.** A feed-forward VCA-style bus compressor [inferred from the SSL-style description]. The original console circuit has a fixed set of values for these controls, which explains the stepped knobs [inferred].

**Ratio and knee.** The knob has three positions. There is no knee control; the knee gets sharper as the ratio rises [manual]. A Cytomic-plug-in review says 2:1 is a soft knee and 10:1 behaves hard, peak-limiter-like [source: 3] (about the stand-alone plug-in, assumed the same here [inferred]). Measured: Ratio 10 has a hard knee starting about 3 dB over Threshold, Ratio 4 starts about 1 dB under it, and Ratio 2 is a wide, smooth knee (about -4.3 dB of reduction at Threshold itself); the output slope keeps falling with level (at 20 dB over Threshold 0.35, 0.231 and 0.093, i.e. 2.9:1, 4.3:1 and 10.7:1), and Ratio 2 reduces more than Ratio 10 at 0 dBFS (Measured › Static curve and knee) [measured 12.4.6].

**Attack and release.** Attack is the time to reach maximum compression after the signal passes the threshold; in Cytomic's plug-in it is described as a 10 %–90 % time and does not depend on how much compression there is [source: 8] (assumed the same, [inferred] [measure]). Measured: Attack is close to a 63 % time constant from 10 ms (9.7 ms for 10 ms), longer than the knob at 1 to 3 ms, and a slow Attack also lowers the reduction a steady sine receives (9.77 dB at 0.01 ms, 5.76 dB at 30 ms) (Measured › Attack) [measured 12.4.6]. Release is the time to return to normal [manual]; measured: the reduction in dB decays exponentially with a time constant of 80 ms (0.1 s) to 413 ms (1.2 s), so the knob is neither the time constant nor the full recovery time (Measured › Release) [measured 12.4.6]. Attack knob values are in milliseconds, release values in seconds [manual].

**Auto release (A).** Two times are used: a slow one as a base value and a fast one that reacts to transients [manual]. Auto release is "gentle", and may be too slow for sudden level changes [manual]. Measured after a 1 s, 9.9 dB reduction: about 1.5 dB of it recovers with a time constant of 42 ms and about 8.4 dB with one of about 5 s (Measured › Release) [measured 12.4.6]; how they depend on the amount and duration of the reduction is [measure]. A plausible structure: gain reduction follows a fast and a slow release, and the output takes the larger of the two reductions or a weighted blend [inferred] [measure].

**Range.** The gain reduction is limited so the compression never exceeds the value: at −60 to −70 dB it hardly acts as a limit (hardware-like), at −15 dB at most 15 dB of reduction is applied, at 0 dB nothing happens [manual]. Whether it is a hard clamp of the reduction in dB, a soft one, or applied to the threshold or the ratio is [measure].

**Makeup.** A plain gain, not automatic; the needle position gives a hint of what to set [manual].

**Soft clip.** A fixed waveshaper, with a maximum output near −0.5 dB, that is not transparent and distorts when on [manual]. Its curve is [measure]; common shapes are `tanh`-like or a cubic or arctangent soft saturator scaled to −0.5 dB [inferred]. The Clip LED goes yellow when it works [manual].

**Oversampling.** 2 × [manual]; the filters used and their latency (32 samples reported) are [source: 6] [measure].

**Meter.** The needle shows gain reduction in dB [manual]; its scale is [measure] (about 0 to −20 dB).

**Side-chain.** As in [common.md](common.md). Cytomic's stand-alone plug-in has a side-chain high-pass of 6 dB/oct up to 2 kHz [source: 3]; Live replaced it by the side-chain EQ section [manual] [inferred].

**Stereo.** The two channels are most likely linked, so the image does not move (the stand-alone plug-in has a stereo link from 0 % to 100 % in 25 % steps [source: 3], but Live shows none) [inferred] [measure].

## Edge cases

- Threshold at its maximum (0 dB), with Range at 0 dB: no gain change (manual, Range 0 dB = no compression).
- Release at A with a very short sound: the fast stage should give recovery; with continuous sound the slow stage dominates [inferred] [measure].
- Soft clip with a very hot input: output limited near −0.5 dB, hard distortion [manual]; with oversampling peaks may pass 0 dB [manual].
- Attack at 0.01 ms with low-frequency content: gain modulation at the signal period (distortion) [inferred] [measure].
- Switching the oversampling while playing: changes latency by 32 samples and so shifts delay compensation [source: 5, 6] [inferred]; clicks are [measure].
- Ratio, Attack, Release are stepped, so automation passes through discrete values [inferred]; whether Live smooths between steps is [measure].
- Mono input, silence, denormals: [measure].

## Measured

[measured 12.4.6] Behaviour runs at 44.1 kHz in Live 12.4.6 (`measurements/29.21-glue-compressor/`:
`glue-ratio`, `glue-attack`, `glue-release` in the top100 set; `glue-range`, `-attack-amount`, `-softclip`, `-makeup-mix` in the remaining set; `glue-sidechain`, `-detector-release`, `-knee-ramp`, `-stereo-oversample` in the third set, rendered by Live 12.4.7: Measured › Third set). Base settings: Threshold -20 dB, Ratio 4,
Attack 0.01 ms, Release 0.1 s, Range 70 dB, Makeup 0, Soft clip off, Dry/Wet 100 %, side-chain off.
The static curve is the 1 kHz staircase (gain of the fundamental against the step's peak level, 3 dB
steps); the times come from the bursts (-30, -6, -30 dBFS, 1 s each, so 14 dB over the threshold in the
middle) through the 0.25 ms envelope of the gain. These are observations, not a model.

### Static curve and knee

Gain (dB) at Threshold -20 dB, by the step's peak level (dBFS). The reduction depends only on the level
relative to Threshold: Ratio 4 at Threshold -30 and -10 dB gave the same curve shifted by 10 dB
(at 10 dB over Threshold the gain, interpolated between the 3 dB steps, is -6.84 dB in all three cases).

| Level (dBFS) | -60 | -42 | -30 | -24 | -21 | -18 | -15 | -12 | -9 | -6 | -3 | 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ratio 2 | -0.06 | -0.18 | -1.29 | -2.83 | -3.88 | -5.11 | -6.50 | -8.02 | -9.68 | -11.44 | -13.31 | -15.26 |
| Ratio 4 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | -0.02 | -1.39 | -3.34 | -5.41 | -7.56 | -9.77 | -12.04 | -14.35 |
| Ratio 10 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | -1.22 | -3.88 | -6.56 | -9.25 | -11.96 | -14.68 |

Local slope of the output (dB per dB), over the last 3 dB step below 0 dBFS and over -12 to -9 dBFS:
Ratio 2: 0.350 and 0.449; Ratio 4: 0.231 and 0.283; Ratio 10: 0.093 and 0.107. THD stayed under 0.5 %.

- **The knee depends on the ratio, as the manual says.** Ratio 10 has a hard knee that starts about
  3 dB above Threshold (no reduction at 2 dB over, 1.22 dB at 5 dB over); Ratio 4 starts about 1 dB
  under Threshold and has taken 1.4 dB at 2 dB over; Ratio 2 is a very wide, smooth knee: the
  gain is already -0.06 dB 40 dB under Threshold, -1.3 dB at 10 dB under, and about -4.3 dB at
  Threshold itself [measured 12.4.6].
- **The slope never settles at the ratio.** The output slope keeps falling with level: at 20 dB over
  Threshold it is 0.35 (2.9:1), 0.231 (4.3:1) and 0.093 (10.7:1) for the three ratios, and between 8 and
  11 dB over 0.449, 0.283 and 0.107 (2.2:1, 3.5:1, 9.3:1). So the labelled ratio is reached around the
  middle of the range, not as a corner followed by a straight line [measured 12.4.6].
- **Ratio 2 compresses more than Ratio 10 at the top** because of its wide knee: the reduction at -6 and 0
  dBFS is 11.44 and 15.26 dB for Ratio 2, 9.25 and 14.68 dB for Ratio 10 [measured 12.4.6].
- **No static offset.** Below the knee the gain is 0.00 dB (Makeup 0, Range 70 dB) [measured 12.4.6].

### Attack

Release 0.1 s; the -30 to -6 dBFS step. "Final" is the reduction reached by the end of the burst.

| Attack | Final reduction | Time to 63 % | Time to 90 % | Time to 99 % |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.01 ms | 9.77 dB | under 0.25 ms | 1.0 ms | 3.0 ms |
| 0.1 ms | 9.70 | 0.7 ms | 1.7 ms | 4.7 ms |
| 0.3 ms | 9.59 | 1.0 ms | 2.5 ms | 7.2 ms |
| 1 ms | 9.32 | 1.7 ms | 5.0 ms | 12.7 ms |
| 3 ms | 8.70 | 4.2 ms | 11.7 ms | 27.7 ms |
| 10 ms | 7.61 | 9.7 ms | 25.4 ms | 55.9 ms |
| 30 ms | 5.76 | 21.4 ms | 53.4 ms | 108.7 ms |

- **Attack is close to a 63 % time constant** from 10 ms up (63 % at 9.7 ms for 10 ms; 21 ms for 30 ms,
  with a tail slower than an exponential: the 90 % time is 2.5 times the 63 % time, not 2.3). At 1 to 3 ms the
  63 % time is 1.4 to 1.7 times the knob and at 0.1 to 0.3 ms the fixed lag of the detector (about 0.5
  ms) dominates [measured 12.4.6].
- **A slower Attack also gives less reduction from a steady sine.** The reduction at the end of the same
  14 dB burst falls from 9.77 dB (0.01 ms) to 9.32 (1 ms), 7.61 (10 ms) and 5.76 dB (30 ms). The gain
  starts to fall at a little under 9.8 dB per Attack time (0.27 dB per ms at 30 ms) but the
  level it settles at is set by the balance of Attack and the 0.1 s Release against the 1 kHz cycle
  [measured 12.4.6] [inferred for the cause]. The same was seen in Compressor.

### Release

Attack 0.01 ms; the -6 to -30 dBFS step (reduction of 9.8 to 10.0 dB). The gain reduction in dB follows
GR(t) = GR0 x exp(-t / tau) from 10 ms after the burst ends until it is gone, within 0.02 dB:

| Release knob | tau | Time to fall to 10 % of the reduction (2.3 tau) | Reduction left 950 ms after the burst |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1 s | 80 ms | 185 ms | 0.00 dB |
| 0.2 s | 117 ms | 270 ms | 0.00 dB |
| 0.4 s | 160 ms | 370 ms | 0.02 dB |
| 0.6 s | 225 ms | 520 ms | 0.14 dB |
| 0.8 s | 302 ms | 695 ms | 0.42 dB |
| 1.2 s | 413 ms | 950 ms | 0.99 dB |

- **The release is an exponential in dB**: the log of the reduction in dB is a straight line against time.
  Its time depends on the Release knob but not on the amount of reduction [inferred, one amount
  measured]. The knob value is not the time constant: tau is 0.8 of it at 0.1 s and 0.34 of it at 1.2 s,
  and the time to 10 % of the reduction is 1.85, 1.35, 0.92, 0.87, 0.87 and 0.79 times the knob
  [measured 12.4.6]. The knob is therefore close to the 90 % recovery time at 0.4 to 0.8 s.
- **Auto (A).** After a 1 s burst the reduction of 9.9 dB falls in two parts: about 1.5 dB with a time
  constant of 42 ms, and about 8.4 dB with a time constant of about 5 s (fit within 0.02 dB; the slow
  time is poorly fixed by 1 s of data, 4 to 7 s). 7.6 dB is still left 500 ms after the burst and 7.0 dB
  at 950 ms [measured 12.4.6]. Auto is therefore fast for only the first 1.5 dB of the reduction [inferred]. How the split depends on the amount and duration of the reduction was not measured here
  (the manual says a fast and a slow stage react to the signal).
  The slow stage also holds over from earlier material: the staircase that ends 2 s before the bursts
  (up to 0 dBFS, 5.25 s long) left 6.5 dB of reduction at the start of the first -30 dBFS burst, which
  recovered only to 5.6 dB by its end, so the -6 dBFS burst started from about 6 dB of reduction, not
  from 0 [measured 12.4.6].
- **Cross-check, Compressor.** Compressor's Release in Lin mode recovered 7.4 dB per Release time in a
  nearly straight line; Glue's does not: it slows as the reduction goes (an exponential in dB).

| Soft clip on, Makeup 20 dB | -24 in -> -4.0; -18 in -> +0.7; -12 in -> +1.7; -6 in -> +2.1; 0 in -> +2.3 | 41.6 % |

- **Makeup is an exact gain (+10 and +20 dB, 0.0 % THD, the impulse 3.162 and 10.0) and it sits before the soft clip**: the clipper bends the signal that has had the makeup, so it starts to act where the level after makeup reaches 0 dBFS [measured 12.4.6].
- **The soft clip is linear up to about -6 dBFS and rounds off to a ceiling of about +2.5 dBFS** (the effective output of the clipped tone is -0.2 dBFS at an input of 0 dBFS, +1.5 at +6, +2.0 at +12 and +2.3 dBFS at +20 dB over): a gradual saturating curve with odd harmonics only (3rd and 5th at -34 and -38 dB for a tone just reaching 0 dBFS, -12.5 and -25 dB for one 6 dB over) rather than a hard limit at 0 dBFS or at the manual's -0.5 dB [measured 12.4.6; the curve was read from the effective level of the clipped tone, which includes the harmonics].

### Dry/Wet

Ratio 10, Threshold -30 dB, Range 70 dB (`glue-makeup-mix`) [measured 12.4.6]:

- **Dry/Wet is a linear mix in phase with no extra delay**: Dry/Wet 0 % is the bypass (0.0 dB, the impulse 1.000 at 0 ms), and at 50 % the gain is 0.5 + 0.5 x the compressed gain: -1.0 dB at -24 dBFS, -4.2 dB at -12 and -5.5 dB at 0 dBFS (0.531 = 0.5 + 0.5 x 0.062, and 0.062 is the -24.2 dB of the fully compressed curve), the impulse 0.951 at 0 ms [measured 12.4.6].

### Third set (Live 12.4.7)

Four runs rendered by Live 12.4.7 (`glue-sidechain`, `-detector-release`, `-knee-ramp`, `-stereo-oversample`) at 44.1 kHz [measured 12.4.7].

**The knee on a 2 dB ramp** (`glue-knee-ramp`; Threshold -20 dB, Attack 0.01 ms, Release 0.1 s; the 1 kHz ramp rising at 10 dB per second; gain in dB, the falling half agrees to 0.05 dB at the knee):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ratio 2 | -0.23 | -1.26 | -2.81 | -3.49 | -4.26 | -5.09 | -6.00 | -6.98 | -8.01 | -10.25 | -12.67 | -15.16 |
| Ratio 4 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | -0.19 | -1.35 | -2.64 | -4.00 | -5.40 | -8.29 | -11.27 | -14.24 |
| Ratio 10 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | -0.32 | -2.08 | -3.86 | -7.45 | -11.06 | -14.56 |

- **Ratio 10 has a knee that opens 2 dB above the Threshold:** no reduction until -18 dBFS, then 0.32 dB at -16, and from -14 dBFS up the output rises 0.1 dB per dB (a 10:1 line: 1.8 dB of reduction per 2 dB of level), so the 10:1 slope is reached 6 dB over the Threshold and then holds [measured 12.4.7].
- **Ratio 4 starts at the Threshold** (0.19 dB at -20 dBFS, 1.35 dB at -18) and reaches its final slope near -12 dBFS: 1.4 dB of reduction per 2 dB from -14 up, which is a 3.5:1 line (output slope 0.28), not 4:1 [measured 12.4.7].
- **Ratio 2 has no sharp knee at all:** it reduces by 0.23 dB at -40 dBFS (20 dB under the Threshold), 1.26 dB at -30 and 4.26 dB at the Threshold itself, and its last 2 dB step (-2 to 0 dBFS) takes 1.2 dB of reduction (an output slope near 0.4, 2.5:1). The ramp adds a finer resolution to the staircase's reading [measured 12.4.7].
- **The gain follows the level with no memory** (rising and falling halves differ by under 0.05 dB) [measured 12.4.7].

**Detector** (`glue-detector-release`; Threshold -16 dB, Ratio 10 (API 2); the 1 kHz bursts at -6 dBFS; deepest 1 ms gain during each burst): -4.54 dB for 1 ms, -5.68 dB for 5 ms, then -5.70 dB for 10 to 300 ms. **The full reduction (5.7 dB, the settled value) is reached by a 5 ms burst and 80 % of it by a 1 ms burst:** the detector is faster than the Compressor's RMS reading and about as fast as its Peak, with no lag over 1 ms to speak of [measured 12.4.7].

- **Auto release has a fast part and a slow part, and the slow part gets slower the longer the compression lasted:** after a click or 100 ms the gain recovers about 4.7 of 11.5 dB in 100 ms and the rest slowly (0.9 dB from 0.4 to 1 s); after 3 s of compression it recovers 1.2 of 12.7 dB in 100 ms and about 2 dB per second after that (-12.7 to -7.1 dB in 2.5 s). A click and a 100 ms burst release alike [measured 12.4.7].

- **The channels are linked and the detector does not read the sum:** with Threshold -24 dB and Ratio 10 the -6 dBFS left and -18 dBFS right tone both lose 12.78 dB, and mid-only and side-only noise both lose 9.71 dB (the defaults: 9.18 dB and 6.4 dB) [measured 12.4.7].

A test that clips the signal (a threshold above the tone with Makeup) is open.

## Implementation notes

- **How it differs from Compressor (29.9):** the gain computer idea is shared, but Glue has stepped values, a ratio-dependent knee, Auto release (two times), Range, Soft clip, a needle meter and oversampling.
- **Building blocks:** oversampling for the 2× mode (a half-band FIR; Live reports 32 samples of latency, which a 63-tap filter's 31 frames come close to), biquad filters for the side-chain EQ, one-pole envelopes and ramps, a static waveshaper for Soft clip (check against test 9), dB conversions.
- **Also needed:** a two-stage (fast and slow) auto release, a ratio-dependent knee, a Range clamp and the soft-clip curve, a needle meter model.
- The stepped controls should be exposed as enumerated parameters (Ratio three values, Attack seven, Release seven including A) so a Live set's values map one to one.
- Airwindows (MIT) has bus-compressor designs that can serve as a reference for the DSP; the numbers in this note come from Live's public documentation and observation only.
- `.als` import: the element is `GlueCompressor` [als 12.4.6]. Parameter elements in the set saved from Live 12.4.6 [als 12.4.6], each holding a `Manual` value: `On` (bool), `Threshold` (dB, 0), `Range` (dB, 70), `Makeup` (dB, 0), `Attack` (index 0 – 6, loaded 3 = 1 ms), `Ratio` (index 0 – 2, loaded 1 = 4), `Release` (index 0 – 6, loaded 3 = 0.6 s; 6 = A), `DryWet` (fraction), `PeakClipIn` (bool, the Soft clip button), `SideChain/OnOff` (bool), `SideChain/RoutedInput/Volume` (linear gain, 1 = 0 dB), `SideChain/DryWet` (fraction), `SideChainEq/On` (bool), `SideChainEq/Mode` (index 0 – 5), `SideChainEq/Freq` (Hz), `SideChainEq/Q` and `SideChainEq/Gain` (dB). Stepped controls are stored as indices, not as the displayed values; the others in dB or Hz, except the side-chain Gain (linear). Not parameters but plain values [als 12.4.6]: `Oversample` (false), `SideChainListen` (false), `ParamBlockSize` (128, a constant with no control: probably the block length in samples over which parameter changes are applied [inferred]), and the side-chain source as `SideChain/RoutedInput` (target `AudioIn/None`).
