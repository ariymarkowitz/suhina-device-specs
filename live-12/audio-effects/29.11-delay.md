# Delay (manual 29.11)

**Family:** delay and looping
**Note status:** dump checked, partly measured (top100 and remaining runs, [Measured](#measured))

## Summary

A stereo delay with one delay line per channel, a band-pass filter in front of the lines, a
modulation oscillator that moves the delay time and the filter, and a choice of what happens to the
sound when the delay time changes (tape-style pitch change, crossfade, or an instant jump). It
covers plain slap and echo delays, ping-pong, chorus-like short modulated delays, and glitchy
chopped repeats [manual]. It is the device that stands in for the older simple and ping-pong delays
[source: 5].

## Signal flow

```text
in L ──► [band-pass filter (Filter toggle)] ──► (+) ──► delay line L ─┬─► (smoothing mode) ─► wet L
                                                 ▲                    │
                                                 └── Feedback ◄───────┘      [manual: independent per channel]
in R ──► same, independent                                                 
```

- The band-pass filter sits before the delay line and filters only what enters the line; the dry
  signal is not filtered [manual]. The filter is inside the loop: every repeat passes through it
  once more, so the n-th repeat carries the filter's response n times and the repeats get narrower
  [measured 12.4.6] (Measured › Feedback). The filter is two 2nd-order Butterworth sections, a high-pass and a low-pass,
  not a resonant band-pass [measured 12.4.6] (Measured › Filter).
- One LFO modulates the delay time and the filter centre frequency, each with its own depth
  [manual, source: 2].
- Feedback loops are independent per channel; left is never fed into right, and the other way
  round [manual]. Ping Pong changes this: the signal alternates between the channels [manual]. A
  cross-fed pair of lines is the likely structure [inferred].
- Freeze stops writing new input into the lines and recirculates what the buffer holds
  [manual].
- Dry/Wet crossfades dry input and wet output [manual]; linear by default, equal-loudness option
  in the context menu [manual].

### Context menu and hidden options

- **Hi-Quality:** better delay-line interpolation in Repitch and Fade modes, and in Repitch an
  analog-modelled tape-style smoothing of delay-time changes [manual]. The Max wrapper has an
  `eco` switch, which is probably the same setting seen from the other side (eco on = Hi-Quality
  off) [inferred from source: 2].
- **Dry/Wet - Equal-Loudness:** switches the mix from linear to an equal-loudness law; a 50/50 mix
  is meant to sound as loud as 0 % or 100 % for most signals [manual]. Reachable from the
  context menu and from the Show Options menu in the title bar [manual].

## Behaviour

- **Delay time.** Sync: delay = number × one sixteenth note, `15000 / BPM` ms per sixteenth, so at
  150 BPM "2" is 200 ms [source: 3]. On the legacy devices the offset scales the whole selected delay by
  `1 + offset` (at 150 BPM, "2" is 200 ms and +33.3 % gives 266.6 ms) [source: 3]. The manual
  describes the new device's Offset as adjusting "the sixteenth notes" by fractional amounts
  [manual], so whether it scales the whole delay or one sixteenth step is [measure]. Time mode: 1.00 ms – 5.00 s [manual].
- **Repitch.** A change of delay time moves the read position gradually, so the audio is
  resampled while it moves: the pitch rises when the time is shortened and falls when it is
  lengthened, like tape machines [manual]. A fixed slew limit or a one-pole glide of the delay
  time is the likely implementation [inferred]; the glide time is [measure]. Echo's Max wrapper
  exposes such a time (0.01 – 1 s, `repitch_smoothing_time`) [source: 2b]; Delay's wrapper does
  not list one [source: 2]. Default mode [manual].
- **Fade.** Two read positions are kept; on a change the old one fades out as the new one fades in
  [manual]. A change made gradually produces a granular-like texture [manual]. Fade time is
  [measure] (probably tens of milliseconds [inferred]).
- **Jump.** The read position changes at once; clicks are possible when the old content is still
  sounding [manual].
- **Modulation and modes.** The LFO drives the same delay-time change that a manual change
  would, so Repitch makes pitch vibrato, Fade makes a grainy shimmer, Jump makes stepped glitches
  [manual]. How the "Delay" depth maps to milliseconds (absolute or relative to the delay
  time) and how the "Filter" depth maps to octaves are [measure]. One LFO modulates both, so
  both move with the same waveform [source: 2].
- **Hi-Quality.** Raises interpolation order for Repitch and Fade (for example linear → cubic
  Hermite or longer) [inferred]; tape-style smoothing is added in Repitch [manual].
- **Band-pass.** A high-pass and a low-pass in series, each a 2nd-order Butterworth (12 dB per
  octave) with the corner at Freq / 2^(Width/2) and Freq x 2^(Width/2) respectively, each corner
  held between 50 Hz and 18 kHz; the response matches this model to 0.03 dB in all seven
  cases measured [measured 12.4.6] (Measured › Filter). It is not a resonant band-pass: there is no
  peak, and the passband loses level as Width narrows (-1.9 dB at Width 1). Width is therefore the
  bandwidth in octaves as guessed [inferred from source: 2, 3, confirmed by the measurement].
- **Ping Pong.** Signal bounces left, right, left. With Stereo Link and equal times the
  period of the full bounce is twice the delay time [inferred]. Whether it is one mono line with
  alternating output or two cross-fed lines is [measure].
- **Freeze.** Input to the lines is ignored; the content that was in the buffer at the moment of
  pressing circulates until Freeze is released [manual]. Feedback during freeze is effectively
  100 % [inferred]. A report from the 10.1 beta says Repitch interpolation with Freeze on first
  sounded different from the legacy delays and was fixed [source: 7].
- **Stereo handling.** Fully independent channels; the only links are Stereo Link (time) and Ping
  Pong [manual].
- **Dry/Wet.** Linear crossfade by default; equal-loudness law optional [manual]. The exact law
  (for example equal-power with a −3 dB centre) is [measure]. Whether the dry signal is
  delayed to match latency is unlikely to matter here since the device reports no latency
  [inferred].

## Edge cases

- Delay time at its minimum (1 ms) with feedback and a band-pass acts as a resonator/comb
  [inferred]. The chorus tip uses 12 ms and 30 ms with 0 % feedback [manual].
- Changing Delay Time Mode from Sync to Time while playing is a delay-time change and goes
  through the transition mode [inferred].
- Tempo change while in Sync mode changes the delay time continuously and is also handled by
  the transition mode (Repitch gives a tape-like glide) [inferred].
- Freeze engaged while the delay time is modulated: modulation continues to move the read
  position in the frozen buffer [inferred; measure].
- Mono input: both lines receive the same signal; Ping Pong then still alternates the output
  [inferred].
- Maximum 5 s at 192 kHz needs a 960 000 sample buffer per channel; allocate once at
  prepare time, never in the audio callback [inferred: real-time safety].
- Feedback 100 % with no Freeze: nothing decays; gain staging in the loop is [measure]. At the
  95 % maximum the repeats fall 0.45 dB each, still -8 dB after 19 repeats [measured 12.4.6]
  (Measured › Feedback).
- The filter corners cannot go below 50 Hz or above 18 kHz: at Width 9 the filter is a fixed
  50 Hz high-pass and 18 kHz low-pass whatever Freq is, and at Freq 50 Hz Width 4 the high-pass
  stays at 50 Hz [measured 12.4.6] (Measured › Filter).
- Denormals in a feedback loop with a filter: flush to zero [inferred].

## Measured

[measured 12.4.6] Behaviour runs at 44.1 kHz in Live 12.4.6 (`measurements/29.11-delay/`:
`delay-feedback`, `delay-filter` in the top100 set; `delay-offset`, `-time`, `-pingpong`, `-drywet`, `-lfo-delay`, `-lfo-filter`, `-lfo-waves` in the remaining set), Time mode, Repitch, Stereo Link off, no
LFO, Dry/Wet 100 %. `delay-feedback`: both channels 100 ms, Freq 1 kHz, Width 8 where the filter
is on. `delay-filter`: left 100 ms, right 200 ms, filter on, Feedback 0. Repeat levels are the
peak of each 100 ms window of the impulse response; the filter response is the spectrum of the
65536-sample impulse response (the two channels are identical). These are observations, not a model.

### Feedback

| Feedback | Level change per repeat | As a linear gain | Repeat 19 vs repeat 1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 % | -11.96 dB | 0.252 | -215 dB |
| 50 % | -5.94 dB | 0.505 | -107 dB |
| 75 % | -2.50 dB | 0.750 | -45 dB |
| 95 % | -0.45 dB | 0.950 | -8.0 dB |

- Filter off: the first repeat is at the delay time (100.00 ms) with the input's level (0 dB
  against the dry impulse), and every later repeat is the same fixed step below the one before:
  a straight geometric decay, the same in left and right [measured 12.4.6]. The step is the displayed percentage as a
  linear gain, to within 1 % (25 % gives 0.252, 50 % gives 0.505) [measured 12.4.6].
- The filter sits inside the loop. With the filter at 1 kHz, Width 1 (about -1.9 dB at 1 kHz) the
  drop from one repeat to the next at 1 kHz is -7.9 dB, which is the -6.0 dB of 50 % Feedback plus the
  filter's own -1.9 dB; at 500 Hz and 2 kHz it is -13.0 dB, at 250 Hz -24 dB. The n-th repeat
  therefore carries the filter's response n times [measured 12.4.6]. With a Width 8 filter the step is -5.9 to
  -6.0 dB across 250 Hz to 8 kHz but -6.6 dB at 100 Hz, where the filter's high-pass already bites
  [measured 12.4.6].
- With the filter on, the first repeat arrives 0.02 ms (1 sample) late at Width 8 and about
  0.1 ms later at Width 1 (100.09 ms), the filter's group delay [measured 12.4.6].

### Filter

The table gives the -3 dB points of the measured response against unity (wet path only,
Feedback 0). For Width 1 the two sections overlap, so the -3 dB points sit inside the model's
corners.

| Freq, Width | Low corner | High corner | Level at the peak |
|---|---|---|---|
| 200 Hz, 4 | 49.9 Hz | 802 Hz | -0.03 dB |
| 1 kHz, 4 | 249 Hz | 4010 Hz | -0.03 dB |
| 5 kHz, 4 | 1250 Hz | 18.0 kHz | -0.01 dB |
| 1 kHz, 1 | 734 Hz (model corner 707 Hz) | 1363 Hz (model corner 1414 Hz) | -1.93 dB |
| 1 kHz, 9 | 50.1 Hz | 18.0 kHz | 0 dB |
| 50 Hz, 4 | 50.2 Hz | 199 Hz | -0.53 dB at 100 Hz |
| 1 kHz, 8 (loaded values) | 62.6 Hz | 15.99 kHz | 0.00 dB |

- The response equals a high-pass and a low-pass in series, both 2nd-order Butterworth (bilinear
  transform at 44.1 kHz), with corners at `Freq / 2^(Width/2)` and `Freq x 2^(Width/2)`, each
  corner clamped to the range 50 Hz to 18 kHz. The model differs from the measured response by at
  most 0.03 dB at 13 test frequencies in each of the seven cases (200 Hz, 1 kHz, 5 kHz at Width 4;
  1 kHz at Widths 1, 8, 9; 50 Hz at Width 4) [measured 12.4.6].
- Slopes: 12 dB per octave below the low corner (-24 dB at 63 Hz and -36 dB at 31.5 Hz for the
  250 Hz corner) and, above the high corner, 12 dB per octave
  turning steeper near Nyquist (-13.8 dB at 8 kHz, -34.8 dB at 16 kHz for the 4 kHz corner), as a
  bilinear-transform low-pass does [measured 12.4.6].
- Width is the distance in octaves between the two -3 dB corners: Width 4 gives corners 2 octaves
  either side of Freq (250 Hz and 4 kHz at 1 kHz) [measured 12.4.6].
- The clamp: at Width 9 and Freq 1 kHz the corners would be 44 Hz and 22 kHz, and the response is
  instead -3 dB at 50 Hz and 18 kHz (-8.7 dB at 31.5 Hz, -12.5 dB at 20 kHz). At Freq 50 Hz and
  Width 4 the high-pass stays at 50 Hz while the low-pass is at 200 Hz [measured 12.4.6].
- The passband is not unity when the band is narrow: the peak is -1.9 dB for Width 1, -0.5 dB for
  50 Hz at Width 4 (two overlapping 2nd-order slopes), and about 0 dB from Width 4 upward
  [measured 12.4.6]. No resonance peak at any setting: the peak never exceeds 0 dB [measured 12.4.6].
- Filter off is flat to 0.00 dB at 31.5 Hz to 20 kHz, so the 50 Hz and 18 kHz limits belong to the
  filter [measured 12.4.6].

### Delay time and Offset

Impulse taps at 120 BPM, Feedback 0, Dry/Wet 100 % (`delay-time`, `delay-offset`) [measured 12.4.6]:

| Setting | left tap | right tap |
|---|---|---|
| Sync 1 and 2 sixteenths | 125.01 ms | 250.00 ms |
| Sync 3 and 4 | 375.01 ms | 500.00 ms |
| Sync 5 and 6 | 625.01 ms | 750.00 ms |
| Sync 8 and 3 | 1000.00 ms | 375.01 ms |
| Time 1 and 3 ms | 1.00 ms | 2.99 ms |
| Time 20 and 50 ms | 20.00 ms | 50.00 ms |
| Time 250 and 500 ms | 250.00 ms | 500.00 ms |
| Time 750 and 1000 ms | 750.00 ms | 1000.00 ms |

- **Synced times are N x one sixteenth (125 ms at 120 BPM) and Time is exact**, from 1 ms to 1 s, with no minimum and no fixed offset (the taps are at the set time to 0.01 ms; the 3 ms right tap reads 2.99 ms because it falls between samples) [measured 12.4.6].
- **Offset scales the whole synced delay**, by (1 + Offset): +33 % / -33 % at Sync 4 (500 ms) gives 665.01 and 335.01 ms; +17 / -17 % gives 585.01 and 415.01 ms; at Sync 8 (1 s) 1330.02 and 670.00 ms; at Sync 1 (125 ms) 166.26 and 83.74 ms (+/-33 % of 125 is 41.25 ms, not a fraction of one sixteenth fixed in ms). It is a percentage of the delay time, not of a sixteenth [measured 12.4.6]. The tap levels of fractional delays are 0.57 to 0.77 (the delay line interpolates between samples, spreading an impulse), the exact ones 0.98 to 1.00.

### Ping Pong

250 ms on both, Feedback 50 %, an impulse in both channels, taps by channel (`delay-pingpong`) [measured 12.4.6]:

| Case | taps (ms: side, level) |
|---|---|
| Ping Pong off | 250: L and R, 1.00 each; 500: 0.50; 750: 0.25; 1000: 0.125 ... (-6.0 dB per repeat, both sides together) |
| Ping Pong on | 250: L 1.413 (+3.0 dB); 500: R 1.413; 750: L 0.706; 1000: R 0.705; 1250: L 0.352; 1500: R 0.352; 1750: L 0.176 |
| Ping Pong on, L 200 ms and R 300 ms | 200: L 1.414; 500: R 1.414; 700: L 0.707; 1000: R 0.707; 1200: L 0.354; 1500: R 0.354 |
| Ping Pong on, Feedback 0 | 250: L 1.413; 500: R 1.413 and nothing more |

- **Ping Pong makes one chain: the input goes to the left delay, the left output feeds the right delay, and the right output feeds back to the left**: each side uses its own time (200 then 300 ms alternate; the taps are at the sums 200, 500, 700, 1000, 1200 ...) [measured 12.4.6].
- **Feedback applies once per round trip**, not once per tap: the right tap after the left has the same level (1.413 and 1.413), and the left tap after the right is half (0.706), so a pair of taps halves at 50 % [measured 12.4.6].
- **Even at Feedback 0 the signal goes left then right** (the second tap at the sum of the times, level 1.413), and a centred impulse is **1.414 times the input** in the first tap: the left and right inputs are summed (0.707 each) into the chain [measured 12.4.6].

### Dry/Wet

100 ms, Feedback 0, an impulse in both channels, taps (`delay-drywet`) [measured 12.4.6]:

| Dry/Wet | dry tap (0 ms) | wet tap (100 ms) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 % | 1.000 | |
| 25 % | 0.974 | 0.228 |
| 50 % | 0.707 | 0.707 |
| 75 % | 0.228 | 0.974 |
| 100 % | 0 | 1 |

- **Dry/Wet is a power-complementary S-shaped law, not a linear crossfade**: wet = sin(theta), dry = cos(theta) with theta = 90 degrees x (1 - cos(pi x Dry/Wet)) / 2. At 25 % the dry signal is still at 0.974 (-0.2 dB) and the wet at only -12.8 dB; the two cross at 50 % at -3 dB each. It is the same law as Auto Shift's and Chorus-Ensemble's [measured 12.4.6]. The manual says the default is linear and the Equal-Loudness option is a different law; the option itself is not an API parameter, so it was not set in these runs.
- The dry path is not delayed (its tap is at 0 ms) [measured 12.4.6].

### LFO > Delay

Time mode, Repitch, LFO Sine 1 Hz; the swing of the delay is the integral of the tone's Doppler shift (`delay-lfo-delay`) [measured 12.4.6]:

| LFO > Delay | Delay Time | swing (peak to peak) | pitch deviation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 % | 300 ms | 5.6 ms (+/-2.8 ms) | +/-30 cents |
| 50 % | 300 ms | 44.5 ms (+/-22.3 ms) | +/-243 cents |
| 100 % | 100 ms (left) | about 122 ms (+/-61 ms) | -876 to +651 cents (reaches the analysis' limits) |
| 100 % | 300 ms | at least 136 ms (the pitch moves out of the analysis band, -1650 to +1000 cents) | |

- **The depth falls with the cube of the percentage and is relative to the delay time**: 25 % to 50 % multiplies the swing by 8 (2.8 to 22.3 ms), and 0.594 x Delay Time x (LFO > Delay / 100)³ gives +/-2.8 ms, +/-22.3 ms and +/-178 ms at 300 ms, and +/-59 ms at 100 ms and 100 % (measured +/-61 ms). At 100 % the delay of 300 ms therefore runs between about 120 ms and 480 ms [measured 12.4.6; the cubic law fitted to three points, the 100 % at 300 ms not read].
- **Fade and Jump do not modulate**: with the LFO at 100 % the steady tone's level stays at -12.0 dB (Jump) or -13.8 dB with 1 dB jitter (Fade), and its pitch constant (Jump: 0 cents, Fade: +/-20 cents), so in these smoothing modes the LFO does not move the delay (the first 0.6 s are the delay line filling) [measured 12.4.6].

### LFO > Filter and the LFO waves

Delay filter on, 2 kHz, Width 4, the 1 kHz steady tone read through the band (`delay-lfo-filter`) [measured 12.4.6]:

| LFO > Filter | lowest level of the tone | filter frequency range |
|---|---|---|
| 25 % | -1.0 dB | 2 kHz x 2^(+/-0.52) = 1.4 to 2.9 kHz |
| 50 % | -3.0 dB | 2 kHz x 2^(+/-1.0) = 1 to 4 kHz |
| 100 % | -12.4 dB | 2 kHz x 2^(+/-2.0) = 0.5 to 8 kHz |

- **LFO > Filter moves the centre by +/-2 octaves at 100 %, linear in the percentage** (+/-0.5, +/-1.0, +/-2.0 octaves at 25, 50, 100 %). The level of the 1 kHz tone gives the position of the Butterworth pair (corners Freq / 4 and Freq x 4); each level matches the symmetric excursion (the alternative, one-sided downward excursions of 2.5, 3.0 and 4.0 octaves, is not linear) [measured 12.4.6; the symmetry inferred].
- The LFO waves of `delay-lfo-waves` (300 ms, 100 %) put the tone's pitch far outside the 1 kHz analysis band (-2400 to +1100 cents), so their shapes were not resolved. What shows: Sine and Triangle give a continuous pitch swing, Square gives long stable stretches with steps, Ramp Up and Ramp Down give a two-level pitch (a constant slope in delay), and S&H and Wander give stretches of steady and wandering pitch [measured 12.4.6].

## Implementation notes

- **Building blocks:** a biquad band-pass with the bandwidth in octaves for Width, one-pole
  parameter smoothing (and the repitch glide), an LFO with Sine, Triangle, Saw up and down,
  Square and Random, dB/linear conversions.
- **Also needed:** a fractional delay line (ring buffer with linear, Hermite and higher-order
  interpolation); a two-tap crossfading reader for Fade; a note-value table with sixteenth
  counts, triplet and dotted variants and a tempo/offset calculator; Wander, a smoothed S&H LFO
  and a Morph control; an equal-loudness mix law.
- **Hard to match:** the exact tape-style smoothing in Hi-Quality Repitch, the feedback/filter
  topology, the exact Offset scaling. A random shape that holds one value per cycle differs from
  S&H at its own rate; they need separate shapes.
- The delay times are set in sixteenths by their own buttons, unlike an LFO's synced-rate list
  in note lengths.
- **.als element name:** `Delay` [als 12.4.6]. Parameter elements [als 12.4.6]: `On`,
  `DelayLine_SmoothingMode` (0 Repitch, 1 Fade, 2 Jump), `DelayLine_Link`, `DelayLine_PingPong`,
  `DelayLine_SyncL`, `DelayLine_SyncR`, `DelayLine_TimeL`, `DelayLine_TimeR` (stored in seconds: 0.375 = 375 ms),
  `DelayLine_SimpleDelayTimeL`, `DelayLine_SimpleDelayTimeR` (stored 100) and
  `DelayLine_PingPongDelayTimeL`, `DelayLine_PingPongDelayTimeR` (stored 1): the last four are not
  panel controls in the dump, probably leftovers of the legacy Simple and Ping Pong delays,
  `DelayLine_SyncedSixteenthL`, `DelayLine_SyncedSixteenthR` (step indices: stored 2 and 3, which the API shows as "3" and "4"),
  `DelayLine_OffsetL`, `DelayLine_OffsetR`, `Feedback` (stored 0.5 = 50 %), `Freeze`, `Filter_On`,
  `Filter_Frequency` (Hz, stored 1000), `Filter_Bandwidth` (stored 8), `Modulation_TimeMode`, `Modulation_Frequency` (Hz),
  `Modulation_Time` (seconds), `Modulation_SyncedRate`, `Modulation_Sixteenth`, `Modulation_Waveform`, `Modulation_Morph`,
  `Modulation_AmountTime` (the Delay depth), `Modulation_AmountFilter` (the Filter depth), `DryWet` (stored 0.5).
- Reference ranges for the Max wrapper (`abl.device.delay~`) are a good first set of parameter
  bounds but are wrapper limits, not necessarily the device's [source: 2].
