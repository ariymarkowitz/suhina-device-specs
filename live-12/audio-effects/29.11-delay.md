# Delay (manual 29.11)

**Family:** delay and looping
**Note status:** dump checked

## Summary

A stereo delay with one delay line per channel, a band-pass filter in front of the lines, a
modulation oscillator that moves the delay time and the filter, and a choice of what happens to the
sound when the delay time changes (tape-style pitch change, crossfade, or an instant jump). It
covers plain slap and echo delays, ping-pong, chorus-like short modulated delays, and glitchy
chopped repeats [manual]. It is the device that stands in for the older simple and ping-pong delays
[source: 5].

## Signal flow

```text
in L ──► [band-pass filter (Filter toggle)] ──► (+) ──► delay line L ─┬─► (smoothing mode) ─► wet L
                                                 ▲                    │
                                                 └── Feedback ◄───────┘      [manual: independent per channel]
in R ──► same, independent                                                 
```

- The band-pass filter sits before the delay line and filters only what enters the line; the dry
  signal is not filtered [manual]. Whether the feedback signal is also filtered before it re-enters
  (inside the loop, so repeats get narrower) or only fresh input is filtered is not stated
  [measure]. With a filter in front of a line that also receives feedback, the natural structure is
  filter before the summing point of the loop, so repeats are filtered on each pass [inferred].
- One LFO modulates the delay time and the filter centre frequency, each with its own depth
  [manual, source: 2].
- Feedback loops are independent per channel; left is never fed into right, and the other way
  round [manual]. Ping Pong changes this: the signal alternates between the channels [manual]. A
  cross-fed pair of lines is the likely structure [inferred].
- Freeze stops writing new input into the lines and recirculates what the buffer holds
  [manual].
- Dry/Wet crossfades dry input and wet output [manual]; linear by default, equal-loudness option
  in the context menu [manual].

### Context menu and hidden options

- **Hi-Quality:** better delay-line interpolation in Repitch and Fade modes, and in Repitch an
  analog-modelled tape-style smoothing of delay-time changes [manual]. The Max wrapper has an
  `eco` switch, which is probably the same setting seen from the other side (eco on = Hi-Quality
  off) [inferred from source: 2].
- **Dry/Wet - Equal-Loudness:** switches the mix from linear to an equal-loudness law; a 50/50 mix
  is meant to sound as loud as 0 % or 100 % for most signals [manual]. Reachable from the
  context menu and from the Show Options menu in the title bar [manual].

## Behaviour

- **Delay time.** Sync: delay = number × one sixteenth note, `15000 / BPM` ms per sixteenth, so at
  150 BPM "2" is 200 ms [source: 3]. On the legacy devices the offset scales the whole selected delay by
  `1 + offset` (at 150 BPM, "2" is 200 ms and +33.3 % gives 266.6 ms) [source: 3]. The manual
  describes the new device's Offset as adjusting "the sixteenth notes" by fractional amounts
  [manual], so whether it scales the whole delay or one sixteenth step is [measure]. Time mode: 1.00 ms – 5.00 s [manual].
- **Repitch.** A change of delay time moves the read position gradually, so the audio is
  resampled while it moves: the pitch rises when the time is shortened and falls when it is
  lengthened, like tape machines [manual]. A fixed slew limit or a one-pole glide of the delay
  time is the likely implementation [inferred]; the glide time is [measure]. Echo's Max wrapper
  exposes such a time (0.01 – 1 s, `repitch_smoothing_time`) [source: 2b]; Delay's wrapper does
  not list one [source: 2]. Default mode [manual].
- **Fade.** Two read positions are kept; on a change the old one fades out as the new one fades in
  [manual]. A change made gradually produces a granular-like texture [manual]. Fade time is
  [measure] (probably tens of milliseconds [inferred]).
- **Jump.** The read position changes at once; clicks are possible when the old content is still
  sounding [manual].
- **Modulation and modes.** The LFO drives the same delay-time change that a manual change
  would, so Repitch makes pitch vibrato, Fade makes a grainy shimmer, Jump makes stepped glitches
  [manual]. How the "Delay" depth maps to milliseconds (absolute or relative to the delay
  time) and how the "Filter" depth maps to octaves are [measure]. One LFO modulates both, so
  both move with the same waveform [source: 2].
- **Hi-Quality.** Raises interpolation order for Repitch and Fade (for example linear → cubic
  Hermite or longer) [inferred]; tape-style smoothing is added in Repitch [manual].
- **Band-pass.** A resonant 2-pole band-pass; Width is likely a bandwidth in octaves (0.5 – 9),
  since 9 octaves is effectively wide open, matching the note that the Filter Delay's top width
  acts like a low-pass or high-pass [inferred from source: 2, 3]. Slope [measure].
- **Ping Pong.** Signal bounces left, right, left. With Stereo Link and equal times the
  period of the full bounce is twice the delay time [inferred]. Whether it is one mono line with
  alternating output or two cross-fed lines is [measure].
- **Freeze.** Input to the lines is ignored; the content that was in the buffer at the moment of
  pressing circulates until Freeze is released [manual]. Feedback during freeze is effectively
  100 % [inferred]. A report from the 10.1 beta says Repitch interpolation with Freeze on first
  sounded different from the legacy delays and was fixed [source: 7].
- **Stereo handling.** Fully independent channels; the only links are Stereo Link (time) and Ping
  Pong [manual].
- **Dry/Wet.** Linear crossfade by default; equal-loudness law optional [manual]. The exact law
  (for example equal-power with a −3 dB centre) is [measure]. Whether the dry signal is
  delayed to match latency is unlikely to matter here since the device reports no latency
  [inferred].

## Edge cases

- Delay time at its minimum (1 ms) with feedback and a band-pass acts as a resonator/comb
  [inferred]. The chorus tip uses 12 ms and 30 ms with 0 % feedback [manual].
- Changing Delay Time Mode from Sync to Time while playing is a delay-time change and goes
  through the transition mode [inferred].
- Tempo change while in Sync mode changes the delay time continuously and is also handled by
  the transition mode (Repitch gives a tape-like glide) [inferred].
- Freeze engaged while the delay time is modulated: modulation continues to move the read
  position in the frozen buffer [inferred; measure].
- Mono input: both lines receive the same signal; Ping Pong then still alternates the output
  [inferred].
- Maximum 5 s at 192 kHz needs a 960 000 sample buffer per channel; allocate once at
  prepare time, never in the audio callback [inferred: real-time safety].
- Feedback 100 % with no Freeze: nothing decays; gain staging in the loop is [measure].
- Denormals in a feedback loop with a filter: flush to zero [inferred].

## Implementation notes

- **Building blocks:** a biquad band-pass with the bandwidth in octaves for Width, one-pole
  parameter smoothing (and the repitch glide), an LFO with Sine, Triangle, Saw up and down,
  Square and Random, dB/linear conversions.
- **Also needed:** a fractional delay line (ring buffer with linear, Hermite and higher-order
  interpolation); a two-tap crossfading reader for Fade; a note-value table with sixteenth
  counts, triplet and dotted variants and a tempo/offset calculator; Wander, a smoothed S&H LFO
  and a Morph control; an equal-loudness mix law.
- **Hard to match:** the exact tape-style smoothing in Hi-Quality Repitch, the feedback/filter
  topology, the exact Offset scaling. A random shape that holds one value per cycle differs from
  S&H at its own rate; they need separate shapes.
- The delay times are set in sixteenths by their own buttons, unlike an LFO's synced-rate list
  in note lengths.
- **.als element name:** `Delay` [als 12.4.6]. Parameter elements [als 12.4.6]: `On`,
  `DelayLine_SmoothingMode` (0 Repitch, 1 Fade, 2 Jump), `DelayLine_Link`, `DelayLine_PingPong`,
  `DelayLine_SyncL`, `DelayLine_SyncR`, `DelayLine_TimeL`, `DelayLine_TimeR` (stored in seconds: 0.375 = 375 ms),
  `DelayLine_SimpleDelayTimeL`, `DelayLine_SimpleDelayTimeR` (stored 100) and
  `DelayLine_PingPongDelayTimeL`, `DelayLine_PingPongDelayTimeR` (stored 1): the last four are not
  panel controls in the dump, probably leftovers of the legacy Simple and Ping Pong delays,
  `DelayLine_SyncedSixteenthL`, `DelayLine_SyncedSixteenthR` (step indices: stored 2 and 3, which the API shows as "3" and "4"),
  `DelayLine_OffsetL`, `DelayLine_OffsetR`, `Feedback` (stored 0.5 = 50 %), `Freeze`, `Filter_On`,
  `Filter_Frequency` (Hz, stored 1000), `Filter_Bandwidth` (stored 8), `Modulation_TimeMode`, `Modulation_Frequency` (Hz),
  `Modulation_Time` (seconds), `Modulation_SyncedRate`, `Modulation_Sixteenth`, `Modulation_Waveform`, `Modulation_Morph`,
  `Modulation_AmountTime` (the Delay depth), `Modulation_AmountFilter` (the Filter depth), `DryWet` (stored 0.5).
- Reference ranges for the Max wrapper (`abl.device.delay~`) are a good first set of parameter
  bounds but are wrapper limits, not necessarily the device's [source: 2].
