# EQ Three (manual 29.16)

**Family:** EQ and filter
**Note status:** dump checked

## Summary

A three-band DJ-mixer style EQ. Two crossover frequencies split the signal into low, mid and high
bands; each band has a gain control that goes from silence up to +6 dB and an on/off button that
kills the band. A 24 dB / 48 dB switch sets how steep the crossovers are. Three LEDs show whether
there is signal in each band [manual]. The manual says the filters are voiced to sound like a
powerful analogue filter cascade, not perfectly clean: the 48 dB setting in particular colours the
sound even with all gains at 0 dB [manual].

## Signal flow

Shared parts: [common.md](common.md) (title bar only; no side-chain and no dry/wet).

```text
input ─► crossover at FreqLo ─► low band  ─► GainLo  ─► on/off ─┐
          │                                                      ├─► sum ─► output
          └► crossover at FreqHi ─► mid band  ─► GainMid ─► on/off ─┤
                                  └► high band ─► GainHi  ─► on/off ─┘
LEDs: a level indicator per band, taken before or after the band's gain/kill?  [measure]
```

- Low band is everything below FreqLo, mid between FreqLo and FreqHi, high above FreqHi; the
  manual's example is 500 Hz and 2000 Hz [manual].
- The LED threshold is -24 dB [manual]. The LEDs light "even if a band is turned off", so they read
  the band signal before the on/off kill [inferred].
- Whether the mid band is made as a low-pass at FreqHi followed by a high-pass at FreqLo,
  or as the remainder (input minus low minus high) is [measure]; a KVR poster says the mids
  behave as a shelf, not a band-pass, since turning all three gains down gives almost silence
  [source: 2], which does not exclude either form.
- The 24 dB / 48 dB switch changes the order of the crossover filters [manual].

A forum poster reports the Live crossover frequencies as around 1 kHz and 1.4 kHz,
unsure [source: 3]; the dump's defaults are 250 Hz and 2.5 kHz, which wins for the factory preset
(the poster may have been describing an old build or a changed setting). The slope switch loads
as 48, not 24, though it resets to 24 on double-click or Delete, so a device from the browser is
in 48 dB mode (the coloured one [manual]). A search summary of an educational article said EQ Three's slope switch offered "48 dB
per octave or 12" [source: 4]; the manual says 24 dB and 48 dB, which wins [manual].

## Behaviour

- **Gain.** Each band's gain runs from -inf to +6 dB [manual]; at the lowest value the band is
  removed, "for example the bass drum", while other frequencies stay [manual]. The knob's taper
  and the dB at its mid position are [measure].
- **Kill.** The on/off button silences the band (equivalent to -inf) [manual]. Whether it ramps
  (click-free) or switches is [measure].
- **Crossover.** Two crossover frequencies: FreqLo and FreqHi [manual]. A Linkwitz-Riley
  structure (the low and high outputs of each crossover sum to an all-pass) is a good fit for a
  band-split EQ whose bands add back up flat [inferred]; the manual says the filters are voiced
  more like an analogue cascade and that 48 dB mode "does not provide a perfect linear transfer
  quality", with slight coloration even at 0 dB on all bands [manual]. So a textbook Linkwitz-Riley
  split is only a starting point: a forum poster hears resonance even when the device is flat and an
  audible change when the crossovers move [source: 2]. The extra colour (resonant peaks, a non-flat
  sum, phase) is [measure].
- **Slopes.** 24 dB is a 4th-order band split; 48 dB is 8th-order [manual: "sharpness at the
  crossover"; orders are [inferred]].
- **LEDs.** Three LEDs with an internal threshold of -24 dB, so they light when the band's level
  rises above -24 dB, whatever the on/off state [manual]. The level measure (peak or RMS), hold
  time and whether the LED is before or after the gain are [measure].
- **Sum at 0 dB.** With all gains at 0 dB the sum is almost flat in 24 dB mode and slightly coloured in
  48 dB mode [manual].
- **Naming.** The gain controls are GainLo, GainMid and GainHi, the frequencies FreqLo and
  FreqHi [manual].

## Edge cases

- **FreqLo above FreqHi:** whether the controls cross, clamp or swap is [measure].
- **All three bands off:** silence [inferred]; a forum poster notes that all three gains turned down
  gives almost total silence [source: 2].
- **Gain at +6 dB on all bands:** about +6 dB overall (bands add) with a ripple near the crossovers
  [inferred]; the level at the crossover points is [measure].
- **Switching 24 / 48 dB while playing:** a click or a crossfade is [measure].
- **Crossovers close together:** the mid band becomes very narrow; its level is [measure].
- **Mono input:** both channels identical [inferred].
- **Sample rate:** a crossover set near Nyquist [measure].
- **Silence:** LEDs off [inferred].

## Measurement plan

1. ~~**Ranges and defaults.**~~ Answered by the dump (ranges, defaults, gain taper, frequency
   tapers, slope default) [dump 12.4.6].
2. **Slopes and crossover shape.** Swept sine at -30 dBFS: with GainLo at -inf and the others at
   0 dB, read the high-pass response at FreqLo = 500 Hz in both slope settings; likewise GainHi at
   -inf and GainMid at -inf for the other crossings: slopes, -3 or -6 dB points, any peaking or
   notch, phase. Compare with Linkwitz-Riley 24 and 48 dB.
3. **Flatness.** All gains 0 dB: swept sine and impulse for 24 and 48 dB: the magnitude ripple and
   phase; compare against an all-pass.
4. **Mid band.** FreqLo 500, FreqHi 2000, kill low and high: read the mid band's response; is it
   the remainder or a cascade? Check the sum of kill-isolated bands against the full response.
5. **Gain and kill.** Steps to +6 dB, 0, -6, -12, -inf; kill on/off clicks (a 100 Hz sine, look
   for discontinuities).
6. **LEDs.** A 100 Hz tone in 3 dB steps: the level at which the low LED lights; same for the
   other bands; with the band killed; time to light and to turn off.
7. **Latency.** Impulse alignment against the dry.
8. **Order of FreqLo > FreqHi.** Set FreqLo above FreqHi and read what the display shows.

## Implementation notes

- **Structure:** three bands split by Linkwitz-Riley crossovers (24 or 48 dB/octave) with kills,
  an all-pass on the low band so the sum is flat, and smoothed gains and frequencies, is the
  likely structure [inferred]; the coloration the manual describes (a non-linear 48 dB mode,
  resonance) and the LEDs come on top. Gains go from -inf to +6 dB [dump 12.4.6].
- **Building blocks:** Linkwitz-Riley crossovers (24 and 48 dB/octave), parameter smoothing, dB
  conversions with -inf as silence.
- **Also needed:** the LED level measurement (a band level detector at -24 dB), the analogue-style
  coloration (`[measure]`), and a UI with the 24/48 switch.
- **`.als` element:** `FilterEQ3` [als 12.4.6]. Parameter elements in the set saved from Live 12.4.6
  [als 12.4.6], each holding a `Manual` value: `On` (bool), `GainLo`, `GainMid`, `GainHi` (stored as
  linear gain, 1 = 0 dB; the loaded value is 1), `FreqLo` (Hz, 250), `FreqHi` (Hz, 2500), `LowOn`,
  `MidOn`, `HighOn` (bool) and `Slope` (index, 1 = 48 dB; 0 = 24 dB). Whether a stored gain of 0
  means −inf and how gains above 1 (up to 6 dB, i.e. about 2.0) are stored is a conversion to check
  when importing [measure].
