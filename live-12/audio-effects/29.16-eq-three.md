# EQ Three (manual 29.16)

**Family:** EQ and filter
**Note status:** dump checked, partly measured (top100 and remaining runs, [Measured](#measured))

## Summary

A three-band DJ-mixer style EQ. Two crossover frequencies split the signal into low, mid and high
bands; each band has a gain control that goes from silence up to +6 dB and an on/off button that
kills the band. A 24 dB / 48 dB switch sets how steep the crossovers are. Three LEDs show whether
there is signal in each band [manual]. The manual says the filters are voiced to sound like a
powerful analogue filter cascade, not perfectly clean: the 48 dB setting in particular colours the
sound even with all gains at 0 dB [manual].

## Signal flow

Shared parts: [common.md](common.md) (title bar only; no side-chain and no dry/wet).

```text
input ─► crossover at FreqLo ─► low band  ─► GainLo  ─► on/off ─┐
          │                                                      ├─► sum ─► output
          └► crossover at FreqHi ─► mid band  ─► GainMid ─► on/off ─┤
                                  └► high band ─► GainHi  ─► on/off ─┘
LEDs: a level indicator per band, taken before or after the band's gain/kill?  [measure]
```

- Low band is everything below FreqLo, mid between FreqLo and FreqHi, high above FreqHi; the
  manual's example is 500 Hz and 2000 Hz [manual].
- The LED threshold is -24 dB [manual]. The LEDs light "even if a band is turned off", so they read
  the band signal before the on/off kill [inferred].
- The mid band is a high-pass at FreqLo followed by a low-pass at FreqHi (a cascade, not the
  remainder), and the high band is a high-pass at FreqLo followed by a high-pass at FreqHi; the low
  band is a low-pass at FreqLo alone, so the three bands sum without any all-pass on the low band
  [measured 12.4.6] (Measured › Band shapes). A KVR poster says the mids behave as a shelf, not a
  band-pass, since turning all three gains down gives almost silence [source: 2]; the mid band
  alone is a band-pass, and removing it from a flat sum leaves a notch (-21.6 dB at 1 kHz for
  500 Hz and 2 kHz, 24 dB mode) [measured 12.4.6].
- The 24 dB / 48 dB switch changes the order of the crossover filters [manual].

as 48, not 24, though it resets to 24 on double-click or Delete, so a device from the browser is
in 48 dB mode (the coloured one [manual]). A search summary of an educational article said EQ Three's slope switch offered "48 dB
per octave or 12" [source: 4]; the manual says 24 dB and 48 dB, which wins [manual].

## Behaviour

- **Gain.** Each band's gain runs from -inf to +6 dB [manual]; at the lowest value the band is
  removed, "for example the bass drum", while other frequencies stay [manual]. The knob's taper is
  in the dump; the displayed dB is the real gain: a band set to +6, -6 and -20 dB is scaled by
  1.995, 0.501 and 0.100 (the band's own response multiplied by the gain, nothing else changes)
  [measured 12.4.6] (Measured › Gain and kill).
- **Kill.** The on/off button silences the band (equivalent to -inf) [manual]. In the steady state
  the render with the Mid button off is bit-identical to the one with GainMid at -inf
  [measured 12.4.6]. Whether it ramps (click-free) or switches is [measure].
- **Crossover.** Two crossover frequencies: FreqLo and FreqHi [manual]. A Linkwitz-Riley
  structure (the low and high outputs of each crossover sum to an all-pass) is a good fit for a
  band-split EQ whose bands add back up flat [inferred]; the manual says the filters are voiced
  more like an analogue cascade and that 48 dB mode "does not provide a perfect linear transfer
  quality", with slight coloration even at 0 dB on all bands [manual]. So a textbook Linkwitz-Riley
  split is only a starting point: a forum poster hears resonance even when the device is flat and an
  audible change when the crossovers move [source: 2]. Measured: each crossover is a Linkwitz-Riley
  pair, -6.02 dB at the set frequency in both slope settings, and the band responses equal the
  Linkwitz-Riley 4th (24 dB) and 8th order (48 dB) Butterworth-squared responses to 0.01 dB. The
  colour is the sum: without an all-pass on the low band the three bands do not add to a flat
  response (-0.75 dB at most in 24 dB mode, -2.1 dB in 48 dB mode, at 500 Hz and 2 kHz crossovers)
  [measured 12.4.6] (Measured › Band shapes, Sum at 0 dB). No resonant peak was seen.
- **Slopes.** 24 dB is a 4th-order band split; 48 dB is 8th-order [manual: "sharpness at the
  crossover"; orders are [inferred]]. Measured: the bands fall at 24 and 48 dB per octave, and the
  fits are 4th-order and 8th-order Linkwitz-Riley (two cascaded 2nd-order and 4th-order
  Butterworth filters) [measured 12.4.6].
- **LEDs.** Three LEDs with an internal threshold of -24 dB, so they light when the band's level
  rises above -24 dB, whatever the on/off state [manual]. The level measure (peak or RMS), hold
  time and whether the LED is before or after the gain are [measure].
- **Sum at 0 dB.** With all gains at 0 dB the sum is almost flat in 24 dB mode and slightly coloured in
  48 dB mode [manual]. Measured with crossovers at 500 Hz and 2 kHz: a dip of 0.75 dB at 653 Hz in
  24 dB mode and 2.14 dB at 528 Hz in 48 dB mode, both around the low crossover only; the dip at the
  high crossover is smaller (-0.07 dB at 2 kHz in 24 dB mode, 0 in 48 dB) [measured 12.4.6]
  (Measured › Sum at 0 dB). So 48 dB mode is the more coloured one in magnitude and in phase.
- **Naming.** The gain controls are GainLo, GainMid and GainHi, the frequencies FreqLo and
  FreqHi [manual].

## Edge cases

- **FreqLo above FreqHi:** whether the controls cross, clamp or swap is [measure].
- **All three bands off:** silence [inferred]; a forum poster notes that all three gains turned down
  gives almost total silence [source: 2].
- **Gain at +6 dB on all bands:** about +6 dB overall (bands add) with a ripple near the crossovers
  [inferred]; the level at the crossover points is [measure].
- **Switching 24 / 48 dB while playing:** a click or a crossfade is [measure].
- **Crossovers close together:** the mid band becomes very narrow; its level is [measure].
- **Mono input:** both channels identical [inferred].
- **Sample rate:** a crossover set near Nyquist [measure].
- **Silence:** LEDs off [inferred].

## Measured

[measured 12.4.6] Behaviour runs at 44.1 kHz in Live 12.4.6 (`devices/29.16-eq-three/`:
`eq-three-bands`, `eq-three-gain` in the top100 set; `eq-three-freq` in the remaining set; `eq-three-kill-lanes`, `-flat-element` in the third set, rendered by Live 12.4.7: Measured › Third set), FreqLo 500 Hz and FreqHi 2 kHz, Slope 48 dB
(`eq-three-bands`) or 24 dB (`eq-three-gain`), a band switched off by setting its Gain to -inf. Complex
responses from the -6 dBFS impulse (65536-point FFT, the sweep agrees), compared with
Linkwitz-Riley filters built from cookbook biquads at 44.1 kHz. These are observations, not a model.

### Band shapes

Each band alone (the other two at -inf), dB:

| Band | 250 Hz | 500 Hz | 1 kHz | 2 kHz | 4 kHz |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low, 24 dB | -0.53 | -6.02 | -24.7 | -48.4 | -73.2 |
| Mid, 24 dB | -24.6 | -6.05 | -1.04 | -6.05 | -25.3 |
| High, 24 dB | -97.1 | -54.4 | -25.3 | -6.05 | -0.49 |
| Low, 48 dB | -0.03 | -6.02 | -48.3 | -96.7 | -146 |
| Mid, 48 dB | -48.2 | -6.02 | -0.07 | -6.02 | -49.6 |
| High, 48 dB | below -110 (floor) | -103 | -48.6 | -6.02 | -0.03 |

- Every crossover point is -6.02 dB exactly at the set frequency (500 and 2000 Hz) in both slopes
  [measured 12.4.6].
- The low band equals a low-pass at FreqLo, the mid band a high-pass at FreqLo followed by a
  low-pass at FreqHi, the high band a high-pass at FreqLo followed by a high-pass at FreqHi, each
  of them the Linkwitz-Riley filter of 4th (24 dB) or 8th (48 dB) order (two Butterworth filters of
  half that order in series). The fits are within 0.01 dB in magnitude and 0.003 rad in phase
  wherever the band response is above -40 dB, and within 0.1 dB above -60 dB; below that the render
  has a floor near -95 dB, where mid and high bands differ by up to 0.9 dB [measured 12.4.6].
- The mid band is therefore a cascade, not the input minus the other two. The high band also passes
  through the low crossover's high-pass: at 500 Hz (24 dB mode) it is -54.4 dB, against -48.2 dB for
  a 2 kHz high-pass alone, the extra 6.02 dB being the FreqLo high-pass at its corner
  [measured 12.4.6]. The slope far from a corner is 24 or 48 dB per octave [measured 12.4.6].
- The band responses are the same in left and right and do not depend on level (the static test
  gives the same gain from -60 to 0 dBFS) [measured 12.4.6].

### Sum at 0 dB

| Sum | 250 Hz | 500 Hz | 650 Hz | 1 kHz | 2 kHz | worst dip |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 24 dB | -0.03 | -0.57 | -0.75 | -0.47 | -0.07 | -0.75 dB at 653 Hz; below -0.5 dB from 473 to 968 Hz |
| 48 dB | -0.01 | -2.01 | -1.09 | -0.06 | 0.00 | -2.14 dB at 528 Hz; below -1 dB from 432 to 660 Hz |

- Both sums equal low-pass(FreqLo) + high-pass(FreqLo) x [low-pass(FreqHi) + high-pass(FreqHi)] with
  Linkwitz-Riley filters, exactly (0.000 dB, 0.000 rad): no all-pass on the low band and nothing
  else added [measured 12.4.6].
- The phase turns through 715 degrees by 20 kHz in 24 dB mode and 1432 degrees in 48 dB mode; group
  delay at 100 Hz, 500 Hz, 2 kHz and 10 kHz is 0.94, 1.26, 0.29 and 0.014 ms (24 dB) and 1.7, 3.5, 0.70 and
  0.025 ms (48 dB). The impulse's largest tap is at the first sample (-4.4 dB in 24 dB mode, -2.7 dB
  in 48 dB mode) so there is no latency beyond that group delay [measured 12.4.6].

  zero for a steady signal. Click behaviour is not read.

| Case | at the set frequency | 1 octave further | remark |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low band, FreqLo 50 Hz | -6.1 dB at 50 Hz | -24.6 dB at 100 Hz | |
| Low band, FreqLo 5 kHz | -6.0 dB at 5 kHz | | -19.8 dB at 8 kHz |
| High band, FreqHi 200 Hz | -6.2 dB at 200 Hz | -38.1 dB at 100 Hz | |
| High band, FreqHi 18 kHz | -6.0 dB at 18 kHz | -37.5 dB at 12 kHz | |

## Implementation notes

- **Structure:** three bands split by Linkwitz-Riley crossovers (24 or 48 dB/octave) with kills,
  and smoothed gains and frequencies; the low band is the low-pass at FreqLo, the mid and high
  bands are taken after a high-pass at FreqLo, and there is no all-pass on the low band, which is why
  the sum is not flat: this is how the measured responses and the flat sum are reproduced
  to 0.01 dB, so the earlier guess of an all-pass was wrong [measured 12.4.6]. The LEDs come on
  top. Gains go from -inf to +6 dB [dump 12.4.6].
- **Building blocks:** Linkwitz-Riley crossovers (24 and 48 dB/octave), parameter smoothing, dB
  conversions with -inf as silence.
- **Also needed:** the LED level measurement (a band level detector at -24 dB), and a UI with the
  24/48 switch. The coloration the manual mentions is the uncompensated sum (Measured › Sum at 0 dB);
  whether anything non-linear remains is [measure] (the response is level-independent from -60 to
  0 dBFS in the tests).
- **`.als` element:** `FilterEQ3` [als 12.4.6]. Parameter elements in the set saved from Live 12.4.6
  [als 12.4.6], each holding a `Manual` value: `On` (bool), `GainLo`, `GainMid`, `GainHi` (stored as
  linear gain, 1 = 0 dB; the loaded value is 1), `FreqLo` (Hz, 250), `FreqHi` (Hz, 2500), `LowOn`,
  `MidOn`, `HighOn` (bool) and `Slope` (index, 1 = 48 dB; 0 = 24 dB). Whether a stored gain of 0
  means −inf and how gains above 1 (up to 6 dB, i.e. about 2.0) are stored is a conversion to check
  when importing [measure].
