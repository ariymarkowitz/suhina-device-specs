# Phaser-Flanger (manual 29.29)

**Family:** modulation and pitch
**Note status:** dump checked

## Summary

Phaser-Flanger is three modulated-comb or modulated-notch effects in one device, selected in the
display: Phaser (a feedback chain of modulated all-pass filters that makes moving notches),
Flanger (a short modulated delay with feedback that makes a moving comb filter) and Doubler (longer
modulated delays that imitate a second take) [manual]. The unfolded view adds a second LFO, an
envelope follower and a Safe Bass high-pass [manual]. It arrived in Live 11 and a new default
preset came in 12.4 [source: 2], [source: 6].

## Signal flow

```text
                      ┌─ Phaser: N modulated all-pass stages (centre, spread) ──┐
input ─► Safe Bass? ──┤  Flanger: modulated delay, time 'Time'                  ├─► Warmth ─► Output ─► Dry/Wet ─► out
                      └─ Doubler: modulated delays, time 'Time' (bipolar)       ┘      ▲
                              ▲  feedback (Ø = invert) ◄── wet out, per channel        │
        LFO1 (waveform, duty, stereo Phase/Spin) ─┐                                    │ [manual]
        LFO2 (always triangle) ── LFO2 Mix ───────┼─► modulation × Amount ─► (stage)   │
        envelope follower (Attack, Release) ── Envelope Amount ┘                       │
dry ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- Mix of LFO1 and LFO2: the two are crossfaded (0 % only LFO1, 100 % only LFO2) and Amount
  scales the result [manual]. The envelope follower adds to this [manual], [inferred].
- Position of Safe Bass (before the effect, or splitting a low band that bypasses the modulated
  stage), of Warmth, Output and Dry/Wet is [measure]. The manual says Safe Bass reduces the
  effect below its frequency, i.e. keeps the low end out of the modulated path [manual],
  [inferred].
- Stereo: each channel has its own modulated stage and LFO phase [manual].

## Behaviour

**Modulation sum.** The modulation signal per channel is m = (1 − LFO2 Mix)·LFO1 + LFO2 Mix·LFO2 +
Envelope Amount·env, scaled by Amount [manual], [inferred] (the manual says Amount scales both
LFOs; whether it also scales the envelope is [measure]). LFO1 and LFO2 have independent
sync settings [manual].

**Phaser.** A cascade of all-pass stages with a feedback path from the output to the input; a
notch appears where the summed phase shift of the chain reaches an odd multiple of 180°, and the
dry or fed-back sum at that point cancels [manual], [inferred]. "Notches" counts the all-passes
used [manual], so the maximum of 42 stages (wrapper) may give fewer actual notches (a first-order
all-pass gives 90° at its corner and 180° at high frequency; each notch needs two of them, or one
second-order section) [source: 3], [inferred]. The all-pass Q is set by Spread, so higher Spread
makes the notches more spread apart in frequency; the notch positions are arranged around Center
[manual], [inferred]. The structure of the chain (cascaded second-order all-pass sections with
frequencies spaced geometrically around Center, which moves with the LFO) is [inferred]; the exact
frequency law is [measure]. **Blend** mixes where the modulation goes: at 0.0 the LFO moves Center,
at 1.0 it modulates Spread; the display shows the moving notches [manual]. The modulation range
(octaves per 100 % Amount) is [measure].

**Flanger.** A delay line of short length with the time modulated and feedback; the output adds
the delayed signal to the input and creates a comb [manual]. The time moves from `Time` towards
shorter values (unipolar): the left-most position in the display equals Time [manual]. Modulation
depth in ms or in proportion to Time is [measure]. Feedback polarity is switchable.

**Doubler.** Like the Flanger with a longer base delay and a bipolar modulation around Time:
right in the display means longer, left shorter [manual]. Feedback gives audible delays after
playback stops [manual]. The delay range (about 20 – 150 ms in the wrapper) is much longer than
the Flanger's (about 0.1 – 20 ms) [source: 4], [source: 5], which would make the Doubler a
chorus-like comb; the manual compares it to doubled recordings [manual].

**LFO shapes.** Sine, Triangle (default), Saw Up, Saw Down are standard. Rectangle is called
Square in the wrapper [source: 3]. Random and Random S&H are noise-based: Random is probably a
smoothly interpolated random value and S&H a stepped one [inferred]. Triangle 8 and 16 are
quantised triangles [manual]. Triangle Analog is a rectangular wave passed through a low-pass
filter (so a shape that depends on rate: rounded at low rates, small and sine-like at high rates)
[manual]; the cutoff (fixed in Hz, or relative to the rate) is [measure]. The stereo LFO of
Max has the same ten waveforms [source: 7].

**Duty Cycle.** Warps the time axis: the waveform's period is compressed towards the front
(positive) or back (negative) of the cycle, like pulse width for a rectangle [manual]. A
monotonic phase-warp function that maps one cycle onto itself, moving the midpoint of the
waveform towards the start (positive) or end (negative), is the likely structure [inferred];
the exact warp curve is [measure].

**Phase and Spin.** Phase shifts the right LFO by the stated angle; at 180° the channels are
inverted [manual]. Spin runs the right LFO at a different rate than the left, so the stereo image
changes continuously [manual]. In the Max LFO the right-channel frequency is the left's times
(1 + spin) [source: 7]; whether this device uses the same scale [measure].

**Envelope follower.** A level detector with its own attack and release controls whose output is
scaled by Envelope Amount (negative flips the sign) and added to the modulation [manual].
Detector type (peak or RMS) and the scaling of its output relative to the LFO's range are
[measure]. Per-channel or summed is [measure].

**Safe Bass.** A high-pass: the effect on components below the frequency is reduced
[manual]. Probably the input of the modulated stage is high-passed and the low band is routed
around it [inferred]. Order and slope [measure].

**Warmth.** Mild saturation with low-pass filtering of the wet signal [manual], [inferred].
Same as in Chorus-Ensemble (the same control with the same wrapper range) [source: 3].

**Output and Dry/Wet.** [manual]; mix law [measure].

## Edge cases

- Amount 0: no modulation, static comb or notch (not bypass) with feedback and Time/Center
  still audible [inferred]. The flanger at fixed Time is a static comb.
- Feedback near 100 %: high gain at resonant peaks, possible runaway; Warmth may limit it
  [manual caution], [measure].
- Doubler with feedback: tails after playback stops [manual]; the others also ring on
  with feedback, but the manual notes it only for the Doubler [manual].
- Notches 1: a single notch pair; 42: dense comb-like pattern; CPU grows with stage count
  [inferred].
- Center at the top of the range (18.5 kHz) near Nyquist at 44.1 kHz: all-pass frequencies need
  clamping [inferred].
- Spread at 0 or 100 %: Q limits [measure].
- Envelope Amount negative with the follower low: modulation is subtracted [manual].
- LFO synced and transport stopped: free-running or frozen [measure].
- Duty Cycle at ±100 %: the waveform shrinks to a short pulse/ramp at the start or end of the
  cycle [manual], [inferred].
- Mono input: stereo output via LFO Phase or Spin [inferred].
- Silent input: the 12.2 notes say the device uses less CPU when silent [source: 2]; tails of
  feedback must have died first [inferred].
- Parameter jumps: Time, Center and Notches changes while playing need smoothing; Notches changes
  re-route stages [measure] (clicks or crossfade).
- Latency: none expected; dry path is not delayed [inferred], [measure].

## Measurement plan

1. **Ranges and defaults** (answered by the dump [dump 12.4.6], including the full Rate and LFO2 Rate lists). Compare the stored values with the `.als` [als 12.4.6].
2. **Flanger and Doubler delay:** impulse, Amount 0, Feedback 0, Dry/Wet 100 %, Warmth 0:
   position of the delayed impulse against Time over the range; check unipolar vs. bipolar by
   Amount 100 %, LFO Sine, 0.1 Hz and recording the delay over a cycle (impulse train or sine
   sweep with a measured comb spacing).
3. **Flanger/Doubler modulation depth:** sine tone, track instantaneous frequency to compute
   d(delay)/dt; or an impulse train at 1 kHz and extract the delay; at Amount 25, 50, 100 %.
4. **Phaser notch layout:** white noise, Dry/Wet 100 %, Feedback 0 and 50 %, Amount 0; FFT at
   Notches 1, 2, 4, 8, 42; read the number and the spacing of the notches, versus Center and
   Spread (0, 50, 100 %). Fit an all-pass chain model (orders, frequencies, Q).
5. **Blend:** Amount 100 %, slow LFO: record the notch pattern over a cycle at Blend 0, 0.5, 1;
   note the Center excursion and the Spread excursion.
6. **LFO shapes and Duty Cycle:** with a Flanger at a slow rate and a tone, plot the modulation
   (instantaneous frequency) for each of ten waveforms and Duty Cycle −100, 0, 100 %. Fit
   Triangle Analog at several rates (0.1, 1, 5 Hz).
7. **Phase/Spin:** read channel phase difference at Phase 0, 90, 180, 360; Spin at 25, 50, 100 %:
   measure each channel's LFO frequency.
8. **LFO2:** Mix 0, 50, 100 %; frequencies range; sync lists (read each rate entry and measure
   at 120 BPM).
9. **Envelope follower:** tone bursts at known levels; read the modulation depth (Envelope
   Amount ±100 %) against level; Attack/Release time constants (10 – 90 % times) for the
   range's endpoints.
10. **Safe Bass:** two-tone input (60 Hz, 2 kHz), strong Amount, Safe Bass 5 Hz, 200 Hz, 3 kHz;
    compare modulation sidebands of the low tone; filter slope.
11. **Warmth and feedback:** sine at several levels, FFT at Warmth 0 and 100 %; impulse with
    Feedback 50, 90, 100 %, Ø on and off; find any limiting.
12. **Dry/Wet, Output:** 0, 50, 100 % mix law; Output dB range.
13. **Version:** the 12.4 default preset's settings were read from a freshly loaded device [dump 12.4.6]; the loaded and reset values differ for several controls (see the tables).

## Implementation notes

- **Building blocks:** an LFO with Sine, Triangle, Saw up and down, Square (Rectangle) and
  Random; a biquad high-pass for Safe Bass; a tunable first-order or cookbook all-pass stage for
  the phaser (a crossover's phase-matching all-pass is not a tunable stage); an envelope follower
  with separate attack and release; a static curve for Warmth.
- **Missing:** an interpolating modulated delay line (shared with Chorus-Ensemble, Delay, Echo);
  a bank of all-pass sections with per-section frequency/Q and a Notches count up to 42;
  LFO extras: Random S&H, Triangle Analog, Triangle 8 and 16, Duty Cycle warp; a Spin
  stereo LFO pair; an envelope follower with attack and release; the sync lists.
- **Hard to match:** the notch layout law (Center and Spread to frequencies), the modulation
  ranges, Warmth, Triangle Analog.
- **Order of work:** the Flanger is the smallest slice (delay line + feedback + LFO); Doubler is
  the same with other limits; Phaser adds the all-pass chain.
- **Import:** `.als` element `PhaserNew` [dump 12.4.6]; the 12.4 default preset is a reference, not a mapping. Parameter elements [als 12.4.6] (stored units in brackets where not the display's): `On`, `Modulation_Amount` (0.75), `Modulation_Waveform` (index, 1 = Triangle), `Modulation_Frequency` (Hz, 0.1486), `Modulation_Frequency2` (0.5 Hz), `Modulation_Sync`, `Modulation_Sync2`, `Modulation_SyncedRate`, `Modulation_SyncedRate2` (steps, 4), `Modulation_PhaseOffset` (0), `Modulation_SpinEnabled`, `Modulation_Spin`, `Modulation_DutyCycle` (0.1 for the displayed 10 %, so ±1), `Modulation_LfoBlend`, `Modulation_EnvelopeEnabled`, `Modulation_EnvelopeAmount` (0), `Modulation_EnvelopeAttack` (seconds, 0.001), `Modulation_EnvelopeRelease` (seconds, 0.12), `Mode` (0), `Notches` (2), `FlangerDelayTime` (seconds, 0.0025), `DoublerDelayTime` (seconds, 0.08), `ModulationBlend`, `CenterFrequency` (Hz, 400), `Spread` (0.3516 for 35 %), `Feedback`, `Warmth`, `SafeBassFrequency` (Hz, 80), `InvertWet`, `OutputGain` (linear gain, 1 = 0 dB), `DryWet` (1 = 100 %).
  a reference, not a mapping.
