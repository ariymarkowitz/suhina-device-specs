# Phaser-Flanger (manual 29.29)

**Family:** modulation and pitch
**Note status:** dump checked, partly measured (top100 and remaining runs, [Measured](#measured))

## Summary

Phaser-Flanger is three modulated-comb or modulated-notch effects in one device, selected in the
display: Phaser (a feedback chain of modulated all-pass filters that makes moving notches),
Flanger (a short modulated delay with feedback that makes a moving comb filter) and Doubler (longer
modulated delays that imitate a second take) [manual]. The unfolded view adds a second LFO, an
envelope follower and a Safe Bass high-pass [manual]. It arrived in Live 11 and a new default
preset came in 12.4 [source: 2], [source: 6].

## Signal flow

```text
                      ┌─ Phaser: N modulated all-pass stages (centre, spread) ──┐
input ─► Safe Bass? ──┤  Flanger: modulated delay, time 'Time'                  ├─► Warmth ─► Output ─► Dry/Wet ─► out
                      └─ Doubler: modulated delays, time 'Time' (bipolar)       ┘      ▲
                              ▲  feedback (Ø = invert) ◄── wet out, per channel        │
        LFO1 (waveform, duty, stereo Phase/Spin) ─┐                                    │ [manual]
        LFO2 (always triangle) ── LFO2 Mix ───────┼─► modulation × Amount ─► (stage)   │
        envelope follower (Attack, Release) ── Envelope Amount ┘                       │
dry ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- Mix of LFO1 and LFO2: the two are crossfaded (0 % only LFO1, 100 % only LFO2) and Amount
  scales the result [manual]. The envelope follower adds to this [manual], [inferred].
- Position of Safe Bass (before the effect, or splitting a low band that bypasses the modulated
  stage), of Warmth, Output and Dry/Wet is [measure]. The manual says Safe Bass reduces the
  effect below its frequency, i.e. keeps the low end out of the modulated path [manual],
  [inferred].
- Stereo: each channel has its own modulated stage and LFO phase [manual].

## Behaviour

**Modulation sum.** The modulation signal per channel is m = (1 − LFO2 Mix)·LFO1 + LFO2 Mix·LFO2 +
Envelope Amount·env, scaled by Amount [manual], [inferred] (the manual says Amount scales both
LFOs; whether it also scales the envelope is [measure]). LFO1 and LFO2 have independent
sync settings [manual]. With only the sine LFO, the delay (Flanger, Doubler) and Center or Q (Phaser) follow it on a logarithmic scale: the modulation is in octaves, not in milliseconds or hertz (Measured › Modulation depth) [measured 12.4.6].

**Phaser.** A cascade of all-pass stages with a feedback path from the output to the input; a
notch appears where the summed phase shift of the chain reaches an odd multiple of 180°, and the
dry or fed-back sum at that point cancels [manual], [inferred]. "Notches" counts the all-passes
used [manual]. Measured: the chain is N identical second-order all-pass sections, all at the same frequency Center, with Q set by
Spread (2.0 at 0 %, 0.50 at 50 %, 0.10 at 100 %); the output is 0.5 x (dry + chain) and Notches N gives N notches, one
at each frequency where one section's phase lag is an odd multiple of 180°/N (Measured › Phaser notch layout). The frequency law is therefore not a
geometric spacing of section frequencies: a higher Spread (lower Q) pushes the notches apart because one section's phase lag then changes slowly over a wide range [measured 12.4.6]. **Blend** mixes where the modulation goes: at 0.0 the LFO moves Center,
at 1.0 it modulates Spread; the display shows the moving notches [manual]. The modulation range is +-4 octaves of Center per 100 % Amount at Blend 0, and +-4 octaves of the section Q at Blend 1 (Measured › Modulation depth) [measured 12.4.6].

**Flanger.** A delay line of short length with the time modulated and feedback; the output adds
the delayed signal to the input and creates a comb [manual]. The time moves from `Time` towards
shorter values (unipolar): the left-most position in the display equals Time [manual]. Modulation
depth is proportional to Time on a logarithmic scale: at 100 % Amount the delay sweeps down by 6 octaves, to Time/64, in a sine of octaves (Measured › Modulation depth) [measured 12.4.6]. Feedback polarity is switchable. At Amount 0 and Feedback 0 the output is 0.5 x dry + 0.5 x the signal delayed by Time + 0.022 ms [measured 12.4.6].

**Doubler.** Like the Flanger with a longer base delay and a bipolar modulation around Time:
right in the display means longer, left shorter [manual]. Measured: the delay is Time x 2^(Amount/2 x sine), i.e. at 100 % it swings from Time/sqrt 2 to Time x sqrt 2 (56.6 to 113.1 ms at 80 ms) [measured 12.4.6]. Feedback gives audible delays after
playback stops [manual]. The delay range (about 20 – 150 ms in the wrapper) is much longer than
the Flanger's (about 0.1 – 20 ms) [source: 4], [source: 5], which would make the Doubler a
chorus-like comb; the manual compares it to doubled recordings [manual].

**LFO shapes.** Sine, Triangle (default), Saw Up, Saw Down are standard. Rectangle is called
Square in the wrapper [source: 3]. Random and Random S&H are noise-based: Random is probably a
smoothly interpolated random value and S&H a stepped one [inferred]. Triangle 8 and 16 are
quantised triangles [manual]. Triangle Analog is a rectangular wave passed through a low-pass
filter (so a shape that depends on rate: rounded at low rates, small and sine-like at high rates)
[manual]; the cutoff (fixed in Hz, or relative to the rate) is [measure]. The stereo LFO of
Max has the same ten waveforms [source: 7].

**Duty Cycle.** Warps the time axis: the waveform's period is compressed towards the front
(positive) or back (negative) of the cycle, like pulse width for a rectangle [manual]. A
monotonic phase-warp function that maps one cycle onto itself, moving the midpoint of the
waveform towards the start (positive) or end (negative), is the likely structure [inferred];
the exact warp curve is [measure].

**Phase and Spin.** Phase shifts the right LFO by the stated angle; at 180° the channels are
inverted [manual]. Spin runs the right LFO at a different rate than the left, so the stereo image
changes continuously [manual]. In the Max LFO the right-channel frequency is the left's times
(1 + spin) [source: 7]; whether this device uses the same scale [measure].

**Envelope follower.** A level detector with its own attack and release controls whose output is
scaled by Envelope Amount (negative flips the sign) and added to the modulation [manual].
Detector type (peak or RMS) and the scaling of its output relative to the LFO's range are
[measure]. Per-channel or summed is [measure].

**Safe Bass.** A high-pass: the effect on components below the frequency is reduced
[manual]. Probably the input of the modulated stage is high-passed and the low band is routed
around it [inferred]. Order and slope [measure].

**Warmth.** Mild saturation with low-pass filtering of the wet signal [manual], [inferred].
Same as in Chorus-Ensemble (the same control with the same wrapper range) [source: 3].

**Output and Dry/Wet.** [manual]; mix law [measure].

## Edge cases

- Amount 0: no modulation, static comb or notch (not bypass) with feedback and Time/Center
  still audible [inferred]. The flanger at fixed Time is a static comb: the delayed tap at gain 0.50 beside a dry tap of 0.50, notches at odd multiples of 1/(2 x delay) [measured 12.4.6].
- Feedback near 100 %: high gain at resonant peaks, possible runaway; Warmth may limit it
  [manual caution]. At the panel's top, 99 %, a 5 ms Flanger rings down with T60 3.1 s and does not run away; comb peak gain follows 1/(1 - g) (6 dB at 50 %, 19.8 dB at 90 %) (Measured › Feedback) [measured 12.4.6].
- Doubler with feedback: tails after playback stops [manual]; the others also ring on
  with feedback, but the manual notes it only for the Doubler [manual].
- Notches 1: a single notch pair; 42: dense comb-like pattern; CPU grows with stage count
  [inferred].
- Center at the top of the range (18.5 kHz) near Nyquist at 44.1 kHz: all-pass frequencies need
  clamping [inferred].
- Spread at 0 or 100 %: Q limits [measure].
- Envelope Amount negative with the follower low: modulation is subtracted [manual].
- LFO synced and transport stopped: free-running or frozen [measure].
- Duty Cycle at ±100 %: the waveform shrinks to a short pulse/ramp at the start or end of the
  cycle [manual], [inferred].
- Mono input: stereo output via LFO Phase or Spin [inferred].
- Silent input: the 12.2 notes say the device uses less CPU when silent [source: 2]; tails of
  feedback must have died first [inferred].
- Parameter jumps: Time, Center and Notches changes while playing need smoothing; Notches changes
  re-route stages [measure] (clicks or crossfade).
- Latency: none expected; dry path is not delayed [inferred], [measure].

## Measured

[measured 12.4.6] Behaviour runs at 44.1 kHz in Live 12.4.6 (`measurements/29.29-phaser-flanger/`: `phaser-flanger-notches`, `phaser-flanger-delay`, `phaser-flanger-feedback`, `phaser-flanger-depth` in the top100 set; `phaser-flanger-lfo-shape`, `-lfo-stereo`, `-envelope`, `-warmth-output` in the remaining set): Amount 0 (except in the depth run), Feedback 0, Warmth 0, Dry/Wet 100 %, Output 0 dB, Notches 4, Center 1 kHz, Spread 50 %, Flanger Time 2.5 ms, Doubler Time 80 ms, a 1 Hz sine LFO in Hz mode, Phase 0 and the envelope follower off unless the case says otherwise. With Amount 0 the device is time-invariant, so the impulse segment (a 65536-point FFT) is its exact response. With the LFO running, delays were read by correlating 3 to 10 ms windows of the output (dry tap removed) against the probe's noise, and by the phase of the 1 kHz tone; the Phaser's Center and Q were fitted window by window to the output spectrum divided by the input's. Left and right outputs were identical in every case (Phase 0). These are observations, not a model.

### Things that affect how to read the renders

- `phaser-flanger-delay doubler-20ms` and `flanger-20ms` are bit-identical renders. The set stores Mode 1 and 2 and a Time of 0.02 s in both and Live read them back, so the setting reached the audio. The two coincide because 20 ms is the top of the Flanger's range and the bottom of the Doubler's, and at Amount 0 and Feedback 0 both are the same plain delay [inferred from the measurement]. This pair therefore says nothing about how the modes differ; they differ under modulation (Measured › Modulation depth) [measured 12.4.6].
- `analyse.py`'s FM figures in `phaser-flanger-depth` are not usable: the Flanger and Doubler outputs hold a dry tap and a delayed tap, and the Phaser's `ph-a100-blend1` shows no FM (0.01 cents) only because a 1 kHz tone at Center 1 kHz passes every 4-section chain unchanged whatever the Q (each section is at 180° there, 720° in all). The delays and ranges below come from correlation against the noise and from the tone's phase instead [measured 12.4.6].

### Phaser notch layout (Amount 0, Feedback 0)

With H the measured response, A = 2H - 1 has magnitude 1 to within -0.3 to +1.1 dB for 1 to 8 notches at Spread 50 % (-0.7 to +2.0 dB at Spread 100 %, -1.9 to +2.7 dB at 42 notches), a phase of 0° at DC and -360° x Notches at Nyquist, and a phase of -180° x Notches at Center. So the output is half the dry plus half an all-pass chain of Notches second-order sections [measured 12.4.6]. Fitting identical second-order all-passes (RBJ form) at one frequency to A gave, with errors below 0.0002 in mean squared complex error:

| Case | Notches | Center, Spread | Fitted section frequency, Q | Notch frequencies (Hz) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `n1` | 1 | 1 kHz, 50 % | 1005 Hz, 0.50 | 1005 |
| `n2` | 2 | 1 kHz, 50 % | 1002 Hz, 0.50 | 418, 2398 |
| `default` | 4 | 1 kHz, 50 % | 1001 Hz, 0.50 | 202, 670, 1495, 4838 |
| `n8` | 8 | 1 kHz, 50 % | 1001 Hz, 0.50 | 102, 305, 536, 822, 1218, 1864, 3244, 8804 |
| `n42` | 42 | 1 kHz, 50 % | 1000 Hz, 0.50 | 21.5, 57.3, 94.6, 132, 171, 209, ... 963, 1038, 1119, ... 9132, 12691, 18452 |
| `spread-0` | 4 | 1 kHz, 0 % | 1000 Hz, 2.00 | 567, 902, 1109, 1767 |
| `spread-100` | 4 | 1 kHz, 100 % | 1006 Hz, 0.10 | 44.7, 232, 4247, 14679 |
| `center-300` | 4 | 300 Hz, 50 % | 301 Hz, 0.50 | 62.8, 202, 450, 1506 |

- Notches N gives exactly N notches (counted from the phase crossings of -180°, -540°, ...); the stages are not spread in frequency: all N sit at Center [measured 12.4.6]. The notches are where one section's phase lag, going 0° to 360° through Center, reaches 180°/N, 540°/N, ...: for Q 0.5 and N = 4 these are 0.20, 0.67, 1.50 and 5.0 times Center, which the analytic prediction from the analog prototype (199, 668, 1497, 5028 Hz) matches to within 4 %. The highest notches come out lower than the analog values where those are near or above Nyquist (4838 against 5028 Hz for 4 notches, 8804 against 10154 Hz for 8, 14679 against 24184 Hz for Spread 100 %), as a digital all-pass does [inferred from the measurement].
- Spread sets the section Q: 2.0, 0.50 and 0.10 at 0, 50 and 100 % (fitted to 1 %). In terms of octaves of Q these are +1.0, -1.0 and -3.3, so the law is a little steeper above 50 % than below. Only these three points were rendered, so the curve between them is unmeasured [measured 12.4.6].
- Center moves all notches together by the same factor: the 300 Hz case has the notches of the 1 kHz case at about 0.3 times the frequency (the lowest, 62.8 against 60.8 Hz, differ by the warp near the bottom) [measured 12.4.6].
- The chain is 1 at DC and at Nyquist (phase 0 and -360° x Notches), so the response is 0 dB at both ends; with many notches the phase has already advanced at 20 Hz: the first of 42 notches is at 21.5 Hz and 20 Hz is -17 dB [measured 12.4.6].
- With Feedback 50 %, the notches stay where they are (204, 671, 1495, 4840 Hz) and the response fits H = 0.5 (1 + A) / (1 - 0.5 A) to 0.05 dB on average (Measured › Feedback) [measured 12.4.6].

- Left and right equal at Phase 0 [measured 12.4.6].

### Feedback

Flanger at 5 ms, summed value of each echo around the multiples of the delay (a window of 13 samples); the dry tap is 0.50 in every case:

| Case | Echo 1 | Echo 2 | Echo 3 | Echo 4 | Ratio between echoes | T60 (analyse.py) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Feedback 50 % | 0.748 | 0.366 | 0.177 | 0.084 | 0.49 | 0.052 s |
| Feedback 90 % | 0.948 | 0.843 | 0.750 | 0.667 | 0.89 | 0.305 s |
| Feedback 99 % | 0.994 | 0.976 | 0.959 | 0.943 | 0.983 | 3.13 s |
| Feedback 50 %, Ø | -0.740 | +0.378 | -0.177 | +0.096 | -0.49 | 0.051 s |
| Doubler 20 ms, 50 % | 0.747 | 0.366 | 0.179 | 0.088 | 0.49 | 0.205 s |

- The first echo is 0.5 (1 + g) for a Feedback fraction g (0.75, 0.95, 0.995), and each later echo is g times the one before (0.49 against 0.50, 0.89 against 0.90, 0.983 against 0.99: a little less because the interpolating delay loses top end each trip). This is the response H = 0.5 (1 + W) / (1 - g W), W the wet branch (delay or all-pass chain). The Doubler (whole-sample delay) fits it with g = 0.50 to 0.10 dB on average; the Flanger fits it with the measured half-sample kernel as W to 0.07 dB on average (median 0.02 dB); the Phaser fits it with W = the chain A of the 4-notch case to 0.05 dB (median 0.02 dB) [measured 12.4.6].
- So g is the displayed fraction and the loop carries the sum of dry and wet, the output before its factor 0.5 [inferred from the measurement]. The comb peak gain is 1/(1 - g): +5.9 dB at 50 % and +19.8 dB at 90 % (the model gives 6.0 and 20.0) [measured 12.4.6]; 40 dB would follow at 99 %, but the decay of 3 s is longer than the 1.5 s window of the response, so that peak was not read.
- At 99 % the echoes decay with 0.983 per trip and T60 3.13 s; nothing runs away, nothing clips (impulse peak -4.0 dB) and there is no sign of a limiter in the loop at the panel's maximum [measured 12.4.6].
- Ø turns over the wet branch: the first echo is negative (-0.740) and the later ones alternate; the dry tap stays +0.502. The model is H = 0.5 (1 - W) / (1 + g W), which fits the Flanger (0.06 dB) and the Phaser (0.08 dB); with Ø on, the Phaser's notches move to the frequencies where A = +1: at DC, at Nyquist and, for the 4-notch chain, at 417, 1002 and 2398 Hz (N - 1 inside the band instead of N); the Flanger's become notches at DC and at multiples of 1/delay [measured 12.4.6].

### Modulation depth (Amount, mode, rate, Blend)

1 Hz sine LFO, Phase 0, Flanger Time 2.5 ms, Doubler Time 80 ms. Delays exclude the 0.022 ms offset. The log2 of the delay (Flanger, Doubler) or of Center or Q (Phaser) follows a sine of the LFO, with the residuals given:

| Case | Delay or parameter range | Fitted log2 swing | Residual (octaves) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flanger, Amount 25 % | 0.885 to 2.50 ms | from 0 to -1.50 (mean -0.75, amplitude 0.75) | 0.0005 |
| Flanger, Amount 50 % | 0.313 to 2.50 ms | from 0 to -3.00 | 0.0007 |
| Flanger, Amount 100 % | 0.041 to 2.50 ms | from 0 to -5.96 | 0.009 |
| Flanger, Amount 100 %, 5 Hz | 0.041 to 2.50 ms | the same, frequency 5.00 Hz | 0.009 |
| Doubler, Amount 50 % | 67.3 to 95.1 ms | +-0.25 around 80 ms | below 0.001 |
| Doubler, Amount 100 % | 56.6 to 113.1 ms | +-0.50 around 80 ms | below 0.001 |
| Phaser, Amount 100 %, Blend 0 | Center 63 Hz to about 15 kHz or more | +-3.99 around 1 kHz | 0.025 |
| Phaser, Amount 100 %, Blend 1 | section Q 0.031 to about 8 | +-4.00 around Q 0.50 | 0.022 |

- Flanger: delay = Time x 2^(-3 A (1 - sine)), A the Amount as a fraction. The delay moves only from Time towards shorter values (unipolar), reaching Time at one extreme and Time/64 at 100 % (6 octaves) [measured 12.4.6]. The sweep depth does not depend on the LFO rate (5 Hz and 1 Hz give the same range) [measured 12.4.6]. At 100 % and the lowest Time of 0.10 ms the sweep would reach below one sample; not rendered, so any clamp is [measure].
- Doubler: delay = Time x 2^(A/2 x sine), bipolar around Time, a factor sqrt 2 either way at 100 % [measured 12.4.6]. The pitch swing is large: at 100 % the delay changes by up to 0.17 s per second, a speed change of about +-17 % (roughly +-300 cents). A 3 ms window of noise stays correlated with its source only near the LFO's extremes, where the delay stands still [measured 12.4.6], [inferred].
- Phaser: Center moves +-4 octaves around the set value at 100 % Amount and Blend 0 (the Q stays 0.50); at Blend 1 Center stays at 1 kHz and the section Q swings +-4 octaves around the Q the Spread sets (0.50 at 50 %), which takes it outside the Q range of the Spread knob (0.1 to 2.0) [measured 12.4.6]. Blends between 0 and 1 and Amounts below 100 % were not rendered; the Flanger and Doubler swing grow linearly in Amount (25, 50, 100 %), so the same is probable for the Phaser [inferred].
- The LFO period equals 1/Freq (1.000 Hz and 5.000 Hz fitted) [measured 12.4.6]. The tone's amplitude stays constant (0.125 to 0.126 for the wet part), so modulation changes no gain [measured 12.4.6].

### LFO shapes, Duty Cycle, Phase, Spin and LFO2

Flanger Time 2.5 ms, Amount 100 %, 1 Hz LFO (`phaser-flanger-lfo-shape`, `phaser-flanger-lfo-stereo`). The wet tap's delay was tracked every 5 to 20 ms by correlating the output, with the dry tap (0.5 of the input) taken off, against the probe's noise; the LFO value u is read as u = 1 + log2((delay - 0.022 ms) / 2.5 ms) / 3, which is 1 at the longest delay and -1 at the shortest (Time / 64). With the sine, the Flanger law of Measured › Modulation depth makes the exponent of the delay linear in the LFO, and every shape below follows it [measured 12.4.6]. The LFO's phase at the start of the noise segment is not pinned to the render, so the traces show shapes, not starting phases.

| Mod Wave | What the delay exponent u does over one 1 s cycle |
|---|---|
| Triangle | falls and rises linearly between +1 and -1 (slope 4 per second); full depth |
| Triangle Analog | a rounded triangle of about +-0.14 (+0.13 to -0.14): the delay stays between 0.25 and 0.45 ms; one seventh of the triangle's depth at 1 Hz. The rate dependence was not rendered |
| Triangle 8 | 8 steps per cycle on the levels 1, 0.5, 0, -0.5, -1, -0.5, 0, 0.5 (each 0.125 s long) |
| Triangle 16 | 16 steps per cycle on the levels in quarters from 1 to -1 |
| Saw Up | u falls linearly from +1 to -1 over the whole cycle and jumps back: the delay shortens by 6 octaves over the cycle (0.6 octave per 0.1 s) |
| Saw Down | the mirror: u rises from -1 to +1, then jumps down; the same slope |
| Rectangle | two values only, +1 (2.52 ms) for half the cycle and -1 (0.065 ms) for the other half, with no slew visible at 20 ms resolution |
| Random S&H | one new value per cycle (held 1.0 s at 1 Hz): the held delays were 0.12, 0.85, 0.61, 0.23, 0.79 ms in a row, u = -0.57, 0.47, 0.30, -0.20, 0.43 |

- In the delay exponent u as defined here, Saw Up falls and Saw Down rises [measured 12.4.6]. Whether the exponent follows the LFO's value or its inverse is not settled by these runs, because the LFO's phase at the start of the render is unknown [measure].
- **Duty Cycle** warps the time axis of the sine: at +100 % the cycle holds at the middle value (u = 0) for about 0.45 to 0.5 s of each second, then the first half-wave takes about 0.1 s and the second about 0.45 s; at -100 % the order is mirrored: a half-wave of about 0.55 s, one of about 0.12 s and a hold of about 0.4 s. The warp is not a simple stretch of one half-wave, and it was not fitted; only two settings were rendered [measured 12.4.6].
- **Phase** offsets the right channel's LFO by the displayed angle, the left being the reference: at 90° the right peak comes 0.25 s before the left (the right leads), at 180° the right is the left inverted (the minimum where the left has the maximum). The left channel is the same render in every case [measured 12.4.6]. Output gain matches: left -1.9 dB, right -2.0 dB (90°), -2.9 dB (180°) for the 1 kHz tone, which the comb sets, not the stereo mode.
- **Spin** detunes only the right LFO: the left stays at 1.000 Hz; the right runs at 1.25 Hz at Spin 25 % and at 1.50 Hz at Spin 50 % (3 cycles per 2 s), so the right rate is Freq x (1 + Spin) [measured 12.4.6]. A Spin of 100 % does not exist (the panel stops at 50 %) [dump 12.4.6].
- **LFO2** is a 4 Hz triangle of the same size as the main LFO (u from -0.94 to +1.0 as a sine at 1 Hz does). **Mix** crossfades the two LFO values linearly: u = (1 - m) u1 + m u2. A least-squares fit of the 50 % render to the 0 % and 100 % ones gave the weights 0.506 and 0.499 with an rms residual of 0.036 in u, so the blend happens on the LFO values before the delay law, not between two delays [measured 12.4.6].

### Envelope follower

Flanger, Amount 100 %, Freq 0.01 Hz (the LFO stays at the bottom of the sweep: delay 0.04 ms plus the 0.022 ms offset) with the 1 kHz tone bursts of -30, -6 and -30 dBFS (`phaser-flanger-envelope`). The level of the tone, a comb at 1 kHz, tells the delay: gain = |cos(pi x delay x 1 kHz)| [measured 12.4.6].

| Case | gain at -30 / -6 / -30 dBFS | delay at the -6 dBFS burst |
|---|---|---|
| Env Fol on, Amount 0 | -0.2 / -0.2 / -0.2 dB | 0.06 ms (the LFO's minimum) |
| Amount +1, +0.5, and +1 with Attack 30 ms or Release 400 ms | the same, within 0.1 dB | unchanged |
| Amount -1 | -0.3 / -1.1 / -0.3 dB | about 0.16 ms (+1.8 octaves) |

- The envelope follower moves the delay opposite to the sign of the Amount for the Flanger: Amount -1 lengthens it with the level, +1 asks for shorter than the LFO's minimum and so shows nothing here [measured 12.4.6], [inferred] (a clamp at the shortest delay).
- At Amount -1 the quiet bursts moved by about +0.6 octave (gain -0.3 dB) and the loud one by about +1.8 octaves (-1.1 dB): a 24 dB higher input moves it about three times as far, so the follower is not linear in amplitude (that would be 16 times) [measured 12.4.6]. Only three levels were read.
- Attack and Release were not seen: with Amount +1 the delay is clamped, so the three variations are bit-for-bit alike in the tone's level. The time constants need a rerun with the LFO at the top of its sweep [measure].

### Warmth, Output and Dry/Wet

Phaser, Center 4 kHz, Notches 4, Amount 0, Feedback 0; the staircase and the impulse (`phaser-flanger-warmth-output`). The 1 kHz tone sits near the lowest notch (800 Hz), so its gain is -9.6 dB at the default and the levels below are relative to that [measured 12.4.6].

| Case | gain at -60 / -30 / -12 / 0 dBFS | THD at -30 / -12 / 0 dBFS | impulse peak |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warmth 0 | -9.6 / -9.6 / -9.6 / -9.6 dB | 0 % | -5.9 dB |
| Warmth 50 % | -7.5 / -7.5 / -7.7 / -10.1 | 0.2 / 1.5 / 10.2 % | -10.3 dB |
| Warmth 100 % | -5.8 / -5.8 / -6.1 / -10.4 | 0.3 / 2.4 / 21.5 % | -11.5 dB |
| Output +6 dB | -3.6 at every level | 0 % | +0.1 dB |
| Output -12 dB | -21.6 at every level | 0 % | -17.9 dB |
| Dry/Wet 0 % | 0.0 at every level (bit-for-bit input) | 0 % | -0.0 dB |
| Dry/Wet 50 % | -4.8 at every level | 0 % | -2.5 dB |

- **Warmth is a level-dependent saturation in the wet path**: below about -30 dBFS the gain is up by 2.1 dB (50 %) and 3.8 dB (100 %), then falls: at 0 dBFS it is 0.5 and 0.8 dB below the unsaturated case. The harmonics at -24 dBFS are the 2nd at -49.8 and -45.5 dB and the 3rd at -66.9 and -62.6 dB (Warmth 50 and 100 %); at -6 dBFS the 2nd and 3rd are nearly equal (-31.9 and -30.8 dB at 50 %, -27.1 and -26.0 dB at 100 %) with the 4th and 5th at -60 to -68 dB. Even and odd harmonics are both present, with the even order the stronger at low level, so the curve is not odd-symmetric [measured 12.4.6].
- Warmth also takes top end from the wet path: relative to Warmth 0 the response is down 7.9 and 7.4 dB at 11.5 kHz and 6.6 and 20.6 dB at 16 kHz (50 and 100 %), while at 1 kHz it is up 1.9 to 3.5 dB. The impulse peak falls from 0.504 to 0.304 and 0.266 and its T60 grows from 2 ms to 36 and 88 ms: the saturation adds ringing, so the path holds a filter besides the curve [measured 12.4.6].
- **Output is exact**: +6 dB and -12 dB shift every level by 6.00 and 12.0 dB; the fit of the output's spectrum against the default's has a spread of 0.0002 dB across 20 Hz to 20 kHz. Output has no effect on THD [measured 12.4.6].
- **Dry/Wet is a linear amplitude crossfade of the dry and the whole device output, with the dry not delayed**: the 50 % response equals 0.5 + 0.5 H to 5e-7 dB across 20 Hz to 20 kHz (H being the Dry/Wet 100 % response), and the impulse's first tap is 0.752 = 0.5 + 0.5 x 0.504. Dry/Wet 0 % returns the input bit for bit [measured 12.4.6]. The Phaser's own dry share (0.5) sits inside H.

## Implementation notes

- **Building blocks:** an LFO with Sine, Triangle, Saw up and down, Square (Rectangle) and
  Random; a biquad high-pass for Safe Bass; a tunable first-order or cookbook all-pass stage for
  the phaser (a crossover's phase-matching all-pass is not a tunable stage); an envelope follower
  with separate attack and release; a static curve for Warmth.
- **Missing:** an interpolating modulated delay line (shared with Chorus-Ensemble, Delay, Echo);
  a bank of all-pass sections with per-section frequency/Q and a Notches count up to 42;
  LFO extras: Random S&H, Triangle Analog, Triangle 8 and 16, Duty Cycle warp; a Spin
  stereo LFO pair; an envelope follower with attack and release; the sync lists.
- **Hard to match:** the notch layout law (Center and Spread to frequencies), the modulation
  ranges, Warmth, Triangle Analog.
- **Order of work:** the Flanger is the smallest slice (delay line + feedback + LFO); Doubler is
  the same with other limits; Phaser adds the all-pass chain.
- **Import:** `.als` element `PhaserNew` [dump 12.4.6]; the 12.4 default preset is a reference, not a mapping. Parameter elements [als 12.4.6] (stored units in brackets where not the display's): `On`, `Modulation_Amount` (0.75), `Modulation_Waveform` (index, 1 = Triangle), `Modulation_Frequency` (Hz, 0.1486), `Modulation_Frequency2` (0.5 Hz), `Modulation_Sync`, `Modulation_Sync2`, `Modulation_SyncedRate`, `Modulation_SyncedRate2` (steps, 4), `Modulation_PhaseOffset` (0), `Modulation_SpinEnabled`, `Modulation_Spin`, `Modulation_DutyCycle` (0.1 for the displayed 10 %, so ±1), `Modulation_LfoBlend`, `Modulation_EnvelopeEnabled`, `Modulation_EnvelopeAmount` (0), `Modulation_EnvelopeAttack` (seconds, 0.001), `Modulation_EnvelopeRelease` (seconds, 0.12), `Mode` (0), `Notches` (2), `FlangerDelayTime` (seconds, 0.0025), `DoublerDelayTime` (seconds, 0.08), `ModulationBlend`, `CenterFrequency` (Hz, 400), `Spread` (0.3516 for 35 %), `Feedback`, `Warmth`, `SafeBassFrequency` (Hz, 80), `InvertWet`, `OutputGain` (linear gain, 1 = 0 dB), `DryWet` (1 = 100 %).
  a reference, not a mapping.
