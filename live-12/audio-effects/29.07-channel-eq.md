# Channel EQ (manual 29.7)

**Family:** EQ and filter
**Note status:** dump checked, partly measured (top100 and remaining runs, [Measured](#measured))

## Summary

A simple three-band mixing-desk style EQ: a switchable 80 Hz high-pass, a low shelf at 100 Hz, a
sweepable mid peak (120 Hz – 7.5 kHz) and a high shelf that turns into a low-pass when cut, plus an
Output level and a spectrum display. It has a handful of controls and a musical, slightly
non-textbook curve shape ("adaptive" curves that change with the gain) [manual].

## Signal flow

Shared parts: [common.md](common.md) (title bar only; no side-chain, no dry/wet).

```text
input ─► HP 80 Hz (switch) ─► Low shelf 100 Hz ─► Mid peak (Mid Freq) ─► High shelf ─┬─► Output gain ─► out
          [manual]             [manual]            [manual]                [manual]   │   [manual]
                                                       when High < 0 dB: shelf + low-pass 20 kHz → 8 kHz [manual]
spectrum display: real-time spectrum of the processed signal with the combined filter curve [manual]
```

- The order of the stages is [inferred] from the panel order; the manual lists high-pass, Low, Mid,
  High, then Output. Linear filters in series commute except for the adaptive parts, so order only
  matters if any part is non-linear [inferred].
- Whether the spectrum is taken before or after Output is [measure].

## Behaviour

- **High-pass (HP 80 Hz).** A high-pass at 80 Hz [manual]. Slope and Q are not given; a 12 dB per
  octave Butterworth or a steeper filter are both plausible [measure].
- **Low.** A low shelf at 100 Hz with range ±15 dB [manual] [source: 2]. The "curve is adaptive": its
  shape (width of the transition, and so effective Q or the transition frequency) varies with the gain
  amount [manual]. This is probably a shelf whose Q or corner is a function of the gain, as in
  Adaptive Q in EQ Eight [inferred]. Measured: only boosts adapt. A cut keeps one shape (half the
  gain at 195 to 200 Hz for -6 and -15 dB); a boost narrows as it grows (half the gain at 168 Hz for
  +6 dB, 101 Hz for +15 dB), so +15 dB is only +7.6 dB at 100 Hz while -15 dB is -12.8 dB there
  [measured 12.4.6] (Measured › Low).
- **Mid.** A peak filter, sweepable from 120 Hz to 7.5 kHz, ±12 dB [manual]. The bandwidth does not
  depend on the gain: 2.51 octaves between the half-gain points at ±6 and ±12 dB, cut and boost
  mirror images, the centre exactly at Mid Freq [measured 12.4.6]. That is a constant-Q peak of
  Q about 0.5 in the cookbook's bandwidth definition [inferred]. Near Nyquist the bell narrows (2.35
  octaves at 4 kHz, 2.0 at 7.5 kHz) [measured 12.4.6].
- **High.** For boosts, a high shelf up to +15 dB. For cuts, the shelf is combined with a low-pass:
  turning High from 0 dB to -15 dB lowers the low-pass cutoff from 20 kHz to 8 kHz [manual]. The
  shelf's half-gain point is 2.9 kHz for every gain, boost or cut [measured 12.4.6]. With a cut the
  response falls 3 dB below the shelf's level at 17.0, 13.6, 11.3, 9.8 and 8.9 kHz for High -3, -6, -9,
  -12 and -15 dB, and the slope between 14 and 20 kHz is -29 to -49 dB/octave [measured 12.4.6]
  (Measured › High). The low-pass order is [measure].
- **Output.** A plain gain, ±12 dB [source: 2].
- **Display.** Shows the resulting filter curves and the spectrum of the processed signal [manual].
- **Structure.** Console-style EQ sections are usually minimum-phase IIR filters; no latency is
  stated [inferred]. Most likely implementation: biquads (high-pass, low shelf, peak, high shelf,
  low-pass) with gain-dependent coefficients [inferred]. Whether it oversamples is [measure].

## Edge cases

- **All gains at 0 dB, HP off:** a flat response is expected [inferred]; with High at 0 dB the low-pass
  is at 20 kHz so near-transparent, but 20 kHz at 44.1 kHz is close to Nyquist. Measured at 44.1 kHz:
  flat within 0.01 dB up to 21 kHz [measured 12.4.6].
- **High at -15 dB:** the low-pass at 8 kHz as well as the shelf's cut [manual].
- **Mid Freq at the ends:** 120 Hz and 7.5 kHz, with interaction with the Low shelf (100 Hz) and
  the high shelf [measure].
- **HP and Low together:** the high-pass removes energy the low shelf then boosts [inferred].
- **Parameter jumps:** smoothing [measure].
- **Silence:** silent [inferred].

## Measured

[measured 12.4.6] Behaviour runs at 44.1 kHz in Live 12.4.6 (`devices/29.07-channel-eq/`:
`channeleq-low`, `-high`, `-mid-gain`, `-mid-freq` in the top100 set; `channeleq-highpass`, `-combined` in the remaining set; `channeleq-lanes`, `-alias-boost` in the third set, rendered by Live 12.4.7: Measured › Third set), HP off, Output 0 dB, the other
bands at 0 dB. Responses from the -6 dBFS impulse (65536-point FFT); the sweep agrees. These are
observations, not a model.

| -15 dB | -14.8 | -14.5 | -12.8 | -7.5 | -1.3 | 200 Hz |
| -6 dB | -6.0 | -5.8 | -5.2 | -2.9 | -0.4 | 195 Hz |

No overshoot either side of the shelf. Cuts keep their shape; boosts move their transition down as
they grow, which is the "adaptive" curve [measured 12.4.6].

### Mid

At every gain (±6, ±12 dB at 1.5 kHz) the half-gain points are 623 and 3557 Hz (2.51 octaves) and
the peak is the set gain at 1500 Hz exactly. At +12 dB the centre is exact at every Mid Freq: 119.8,
399.7, 1499.9, 3999.8 and 7500.3 Hz for 120 Hz to 7.5 kHz; the width is 2.54, 2.54, 2.51, 2.35 and
2.00 octaves [measured 12.4.6].

### High-pass

`channeleq-highpass`; response from the -6 dBFS impulse (65536-point FFT) [measured 12.4.6]:

| Case | 10 Hz | 20 | 40 | 80 | 120 | 200 | 500 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| HP on, everything else flat | -18.0 dB | -12.4 | -7.0 | -3.0 | -1.6 | -0.6 | -0.1 |
| HP on, Low +12 dB | -6.1 | -0.5 | +4.5 | +6.5 | +5.1 | +2.3 | +0.3 |
| HP on, Mid +12 dB at 120 Hz | -17.6 | -10.8 | -2.4 | +6.9 | +10.4 | +8.4 | +2.7 |

- **The high-pass is a first-order filter, -3.0 dB at 80 Hz**: 6 dB per octave (-18.0, -12.4 and -7.0 dB at 10, 20 and 40 Hz are 20 log10 of 1 / sqrt(1 + (80 / f)²) to 0.1 dB), no peak at the corner, the response is above -0.1 dB from 500 Hz [measured 12.4.6].
- **It multiplies with the bands**: Low +12 dB at 20 Hz reads 12 - 12.4 = -0.4 dB (measured -0.5), and Mid +12 dB at 120 Hz reads 12 - 1.6 = 10.4 dB (measured 10.4); the HP is a separate filter in series with the shelves and the bell, not a change to the Low band [measured 12.4.6].
- The impulse has its tap at 0 ms (0.988) with a small negative tail (-36 dB at 1 ms): no latency [measured 12.4.6].

### Output and all bands together

`channeleq-combined` [measured 12.4.6]:

| Case | gain of the 1 kHz staircase | response |
|---|---|---|
| Output +12 dB | +12.0 dB at every level from -60 to 0 dBFS | flat +12.0 dB from 20 Hz to 20 kHz |
| Output -12 dB | -12.0 dB | flat -12.0 dB |
| Low +15, Mid +12 (1.5 kHz), High +15 | +10.8 dB | +14.7 dB at 20 Hz, +3.2 dB at 400 Hz (the trough between the shelves), +14.3 dB at 1.5 kHz, +15.9 dB at 5 kHz, +15.0 dB at 20 kHz |
| HP, Low -15, Mid -12, High -15 | -11.1 dB | -27.2 dB at 20 Hz (the HP's -12.4 plus the shelf's -14.8), -5.7 dB at 400 Hz, -14.4 dB at 1.5 kHz, -24.4 dB at 8 kHz, -54.0 dB at 20 kHz |

- **Output is a plain linear gain**: exact to 0.1 dB in dB at every level, 0.0 % THD, the same at all frequencies, and its impulse tap is 11.9 and -12.1 dB [measured 12.4.6].
- **The sections multiply (add in dB)**: at 1.5 kHz the all-boost response is the bell's +12 dB plus about +2.3 dB from the shelves' skirts (the Low shelf +0.3 dB, the High shelf +2.0 dB from the High table above); at 20 Hz the all-cut response is the sum of the HP and the Low shelf. No section changes another's shape: the HP, the Low shelf, the Mid bell and the High shelf are in series, and no limiting or non-linearity shows at +10.8 dB net gain, 0 dBFS in [measured 12.4.6].
- The impulse of the all-boost case has a main tap of 5.5 (+14.8 dB) and a second at 1.16 ms (-23.5 dB, 0.067): the response is a minimum-phase filter with no latency [measured 12.4.6].

### Third set (Live 12.4.7)

`channeleq-lanes` and `channeleq-alias-boost`, rendered by Live 12.4.7 [measured 12.4.7].

**Parameter changes during playback** (`channeleq-lanes`; automation lanes, the 1 kHz steady tone and the noise):

- **Mid Gain is smoothed over 20 ms.** A jump from 0 to +12 dB at 23.000 s (beat 46) reaches 12 dB in an S-shaped rise (read as the tone's level every millisecond) from 22.990 s to 23.010 s: 10 % at -5 ms, 50 % at 0 ms, 90 % at +6 ms, 99 % at +9 ms. The rise begins 10 ms before the lane's step and is complete 10 ms after, with no overshoot [measured 12.4.7].
- **Mid Freq glides cleanly.** With Mid Gain +12 dB and the frequency moved from 120 Hz to 7.5 kHz over beats 62 to 69 (31.0 to 34.5 s), the bell's centre, read from the noise's gain per third-octave band every 25 ms, is a straight line on the log-frequency scale: 125 Hz at 31.0 s, 392 Hz at 32.0 s, 1.31 kHz at 33.0 s, 4.39 kHz at 34.0 s and 7.5 kHz at 34.5 s. Its peak reads +11.9 to +12.1 dB from 400 Hz up (10.7 to 13.1 dB below that, where a 50 ms window is too coarse), so the bell does not change height as it moves. No steps in the centre or the gain show at this resolution [measured 12.4.7]. A finer look for zipper noise (sample-level discontinuities) was not made.

**High, and all bands, at full boost** (`channeleq-alias-boost`; the extended probe's -6 dBFS 10 and 15 kHz sines):

| Case | 10 kHz gain | 15 kHz gain | other lines | impulse |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| High +15 dB | +14.6 dB | +14.9 dB | none above -130 dBFS | one tap at 0 ms, +12.5 dB |

- The impulse offset is 0 and the second tap of the all-boost case is the one seen before: no latency [measured 12.4.7].

## Measurement plan

1. ~~**Ranges and defaults.**~~ Answered by the dump of 12.4.6 (see Controls); the wrapper bounds
   [source: 2] equal the panel's.
2. **High-pass.** Swept sine at -30 dBFS with everything else at 0 dB, HP on: -3 dB point, slope
   in dB/octave, Q or peaking at the corner, phase.
   Done: first order, -3.0 dB at 80 Hz, no peak (Measured › High-pass); the phase was not read.
3. **Low shelf.** Low at -15, -6, +6, +15 dB: shelf frequency (the half-gain point), slope, overshoot,
   how the corner or Q shifts with gain; fit Q (gain).
   Done: cuts fixed (half-gain 200 Hz), boosts narrowing (168 Hz at +6, 101 Hz at +15), no overshoot (Measured › Low). No Q fitted yet.
4. **Mid peak.** Mid at ±6 and ±12 dB at Mid Freq 120 Hz, 1 kHz, 7.5 kHz: centre, width (-3 dB points
   of the bell) against gain and against frequency; Q fixed or not.
   Done: constant 2.51-octave width at 1.5 kHz for every gain, exact centre from 120 Hz to 7.5 kHz, narrowing near Nyquist (Measured › Mid).
5. **High.** High at +15, +6, -6, -15 dB: the shelf's corner, the low-pass cutoff against High
   (read at -1, -3, -6, -9, -12, -15 dB), the low-pass order and Q.
   Done for the magnitudes at +15, +6 and -3 to -15 dB (Measured › High). The low-pass order and Q are still to be fitted.
6. **Output.** Gain steps; check for a non-flat response.
   Done at +12 and -12 dB: exact and flat (Measured › Output and all bands together).
7. **Combined.** All controls at extremes: the combined response against the display.
   Done for all boosts at maximum and for all cuts with the HP (Measured › Output and all bands together); the display was not compared.
8. **Latency and oversampling.** Impulse alignment; aliasing of a 15 kHz tone at full boost.
   Done: no latency; no added line above -130 dBFS at 10 and 15 kHz with High +15 dB or all bands at full boost (Measured › Third set). Parameter changes: Mid Gain steps are smoothed over 20 ms, a Mid Freq glide is clean at 25 ms resolution (Measured › Third set).
9. **Display.** Spectrum block size, averaging, dB scale (compare with Spectrum).

## Implementation notes

- **Building blocks:** biquad high-pass, low shelf, peak, high shelf and low-pass (RBJ cookbook),
  parameter smoothing, dB conversions. All five stages are biquads, so a first version needs no
  other filter; the adaptive curve shapes and the High-to-low-pass mapping need the
  measurements.
- **Also needed:** the adaptive Q/shape functions, the High cut's low-pass mapping, the spectrum
  display (an FFT), a UI with the combined curve.
- **`.als` element:** `ChannelEq` [als 12.4.6]. Parameter elements [als 12.4.6]: `On`, `HighpassOn`,
  `LowShelfGain`, `MidGain`, `MidFrequency` (Hz, stored 1500), `HighShelfGain`, `Gain` (the Output).
  The three gains are stored as linear gain, 1 = 0 dB (the saved set holds 1, 0.99999994, 1).
- **Oddity [dump 12.4.6]:** Low and High load at internal 0.498841, which reads 0.0 dB, so the internal
  scale is not exactly symmetric about its middle; Mid and Output load at exactly 0.5.
