# Vocoder (manual 29.42)

**Family:** modulation and pitch
**Note status:** dump checked, partly measured (top100 and remaining runs, [Measured](#measured))

## Summary

The Vocoder analyses the level of a modulator signal (the track it sits on, usually speech or drums)
in a bank of band-pass filters and uses each band's level to set the level of the matching band of a
carrier signal (a synthesizer, noise, the modulator itself, or an oscillator that follows the
modulator's pitch) [manual]. The carrier is chosen from four types, an extra noise generator
re-creates unpitched sounds like "s" and "f", and a formant control shifts the carrier's filter bank
[manual]. Not available in the Intro and Lite editions [manual].

## Signal flow

```text
modulator (the track's input) ─► analysis filter bank (Bands, Range, BW, Precise|Retro)
      ─► per-band envelope followers (Attack, Release) ─► Depth ─► Gate ─► band gains ─┐
                                                                                        ▼
carrier (Noise | External | Modulator | Pitch Tracking) ─► (Enhance) ─► synthesis filter bank
      (frequencies shifted by Formant) ─► × band gains ─► sum ─► Level ─► Dry/Wet ─► out
unvoiced detector (Sens., Fast|Slow) ─► noise generator ─► Unvoiced level ─► sum ─────────────┘
dry = the modulator track's own signal ────────────────────────────────────► Dry/Wet
```

- Insert the device on the modulator's track; External carriers come from two Audio From choosers
  (source track and tap point), like a side-chain [manual]; see [`common.md`](common.md) for how
  tap points are named there. Unlike Compressor's side-chain, here the external signal is the
  carrier and is heard [manual].
- Order of Enhance, Level, Unvoiced, Dry/Wet and where Formant sits (shifting the synthesis bank
  or the analysis bank) are [measure]; the manual says the carrier's filter bank frequencies
  are the ones shifted [manual].
- No Max DSP wrapper for the vocoder is published (abl.dsp.vocoder~ does not exist) [source: 6].

## Behaviour

**Analysis and synthesis filter banks.** N band-pass filters (4 – 40; 20 as loaded) spaced between the two
Range limits at equal ratios, the first at the lower limit and the last at the upper limit [measured 12.4.6] (Measured › Band layout), the same set for analysis
(modulator) and synthesis (carrier), except for the Formant shift, which moves the carrier's
bank [manual]. Likely structure: second-order band-pass biquads, one set for the modulator and
one (per channel) for the carrier, perhaps with higher-order cascades for steeper skirts [inferred].
BW: low values give narrow, near-sinusoidal bands, up to 100 % where neighbours just meet
("most accurate"), higher values overlap more [manual]. Measured at 20 bands: the -3 dB width is about 0.10 octave at 25 % (Q about 14) and the bands tile into a flat sum at 100 %, so the width follows the spacing and the Q depends on the number of bands and the Range [measured 12.4.6] (Measured › Band layout).

**Precise and Retro.** Precise: bands equal in gain and relative bandwidth, so the bank is flat
[manual], [inferred]. Retro: modelled on analogue vocoders, where the filter bandwidth grows less
than the centre frequency, so higher bands are narrower (relative to their frequency) and, by the
manual, louder [manual]. A fixed-Hz-bandwidth bank (constant absolute bandwidth) has this
property [inferred]. Measured: the centres are the same as in Precise and the carrier's level tilts upward with frequency, about 3 dB per octave from 250 Hz to 1 kHz and flat above 5 kHz (Measured › Band layout) [measured 12.4.6].

**Envelope followers.** One per analysis band: rectify (or square) then smooth with separate
Attack and Release; the output is the band's envelope [manual], [inferred]. The smoothing is a one-pole in amplitude (rise about 0.27 x Attack, fall about 0.22 x Release as time constants) [measured 12.4.6] (Measured › Envelope times); the detector type (peak, RMS, average of rectified) is [measure].

**Depth.** The envelope used on the carrier band is a function of the detected level and Depth:
0 % constant (the envelope is discarded), 100 % linear (classic), 200 % expansion so that only
high peaks pass [manual]. Measured with one 1 kHz tone: the output level of the band against the input level has slope 1.01 at 100 %, 0.39 at 50 %, 0.24 at 0 %, 2.6 at 150 % and 3.4 at 200 %; so it is not gain = env^(Depth/100) (that would give slopes of 0 at 0 %, 0.5 at 50 %, 1.5 at 150 % and 2 at 200 %); the slope is lower than that below 100 % and higher above, and at 0 % a band's level still depends on its input (Measured › Depth and Gate) [measured 12.4.6].

**Gate.** Each band's envelope below the threshold produces silence in that band [manual]. Measured: the transition is soft by a few dB (about 4 to 5 dB of attenuation at the first level that passes, none from 3 dB higher) and a closed gate gives digital silence [measured 12.4.6] (Measured › Depth and Gate). Whether the gate has hysteresis or release is [measure].

**Carrier types.**
- *Noise:* an internal noise generator. X: sample-rate reduction (a sample-and-hold or decimation
  of the noise, giving a band-limited, lower-frequency noise); Y: density (a sparser noise; at
  low densities probably random impulses, a crackle-like noise) [manual], [inferred]. At full density the noise is white and Gaussian-like, L and R independent; X is a sample-and-hold (sinc-shaped spectrum with nulls at multiples of the Rate); Y sets the density of random impulses, and at Y of 0 % there are still about 80 per second [measured 12.4.6] (Measured › Noise carrier).
- *External:* the carrier comes from the routing choosers (Audio From), for example a synthesizer
  track [manual]. Latency alignment between the carrier and modulator and the tap points are as
  for the side-chain in [`common.md`](common.md); the manual recommends Post FX for the carrier.
- *Modulator:* the modulator is also the carrier: a resynthesis of itself; with Depth 100 %, Enhance
  on and the Formant control it works as a formant shifter [manual 29.42.1.2].
- *Pitch Tracking:* a monophonic oscillator (saw or one of three pulse waves) follows the
  modulator's pitch. It updates only when a clear pitch is detected and keeps its last pitch
  otherwise. High and Low limit the tracking range; Pitch tunes it [manual]. A pitch
  detector (autocorrelation-type) is needed [inferred]; Ableton ships a "frequency detector"
  block in its Max DSP package [source: 5]. Resetting (such as grouping the track) can
  change the sound, since the oscillator has state [manual].

**Enhance.** Evens out the carrier's spectrum and dynamics [manual]: the carrier's band levels are
flattened (an automatic gain per band, so that the carrier has a constant level in every band
regardless of its own spectrum) [manual], [inferred]. Probably an AGC with a slow time constant
per band, or a division by the carrier's per-band envelope [inferred]; time constant [measure].

**Unvoiced.** A noise generator (probably high-passed, as sibilants are high-frequency) whose
level is the Unvoiced knob times a detection signal; the detector decides whether the modulator
is pitchless at that moment, for example from high-to-low band energy ratio or zero-crossing rate
[manual], [inferred]. Sens. at 100 % forces the noise on; 0 % turns the detection output off.
Fast and Slow set the speed of the detector's switching, probably its smoothing time [manual],
[inferred]. A beta tester wrote that it lets plosives and "ess" sounds through [source: 2].

**Stereo modes.** Mono: both inputs summed to mono and output mono (or dual-mono) [manual],
[inferred]. Stereo: one analysis from the mono modulator, two synthesis banks for the carrier's
left and right. L/R: two complete channels [manual].

**Formant.** The carrier's band centre frequencies are multiplied by a ratio (shift in
semitones or by a unit [measure]) while the analysis bank stays; the envelopes of the original
bands thus modulate bands at shifted frequencies, which moves the spectral envelope of the
result [manual], [inferred]. At the range ends the shifted bank may leave the Range; behaviour
(clipping at the Range limit, or dropping bands) is [measure].

**Level and Dry/Wet.** Level scales the vocoded output (and the Unvoiced noise?) [manual],
[measure]. Dry/Wet mixes the unprocessed modulator track signal [manual]; mix law [measure].

**Band display.** Shows each band's level, as bars; clicking in it attenuates individual bands,
which are weighted in the carrier gain [manual].

## Edge cases

- Bands 4: a few broad bands, robotic or "vowel-like"; 40: finer detail; CPU grows with bands
  [manual], [source: 4].
- BW 0: each filter approaches a sine line; very quiet output [manual], [inferred]. BW above 100 %:
  overlapping bands, brighter and louder [inferred].
- Depth 0 %: constant carrier gain [manual]; measured: not constant, a band's level still follows its analysis level with a slope of 0.24 dB per dB, and a tone opens only the bands near it at low levels; Depth 200 %: only strong peaks pass [manual], measured: essentially nothing (below -140 dBFS) under -33 dBFS for a 1 kHz tone and a slope of 3.4 dB per dB above [measured 12.4.6] (Measured › Depth and Gate).
- Gate high: only the loudest bands sound [manual]; measured: Gate -10 dB passes a 1 kHz tone only above -21 dBFS [measured 12.4.6].
- Range collapsed (low = high): [measure] (single band?); Range limits crossing.
- Pitch Tracking with unpitched or polyphonic material: unpredictable [manual]; the oscillator
  holds the last pitch through silence [manual].
- External with no source chosen: silent or the modulator [measure].
- Carrier = External: the carrier track must be running; a carrier track's own audio is also heard
  there unless muted (the manual solos the voice track) [manual 29.42.1.1].
- Mono modulator with Stereo mode: single analysis [manual].
- Attack and Release at the minimum: distortion artifacts at fast times [manual].
- Formant beyond the Range limit: [measure].
- Latency: analysis and synthesis filters (IIR): none beyond filter group delay, if the
  external carrier is aligned [inferred], [measure]; Pitch Tracking adds the pitch detector's
  delay [inferred].
- Denormals: IIR bands and envelope followers decay to subnormal numbers on silence [inferred].
- Sample rates: band count above 40 not offered; range limit at Nyquist.

## Measured

[measured 12.4.6] Behaviour runs at 44.1 kHz in Live 12.4.6 (`measurements/29.42-vocoder/`: `vocoder-bank`, `vocoder-noise-carrier`, `vocoder-envelope`, `vocoder-depth-gate` in the top100 set; `vocoder-formant`, `vocoder-unvoiced`, `vocoder-output-modes` in the remaining set), Carrier Noise (the loaded state, so the carrier is the device's own noise at Noise Rate 20 kHz and Crackle 100 %), the probe on the track as modulator, Bands as loaded (20, see Band layout), Range 80 Hz to 12 kHz, BW 100 %, Precise, Enhance off, Unvoiced at its lowest, Mono/Stereo Stereo, Dry/Wet 100 %. The bank and noise runs have Depth 0 % (the manual says that discards the envelope), the envelope and depth runs Depth 100 % unless a case says otherwise. Band layouts were read from the power spectrum of the 4 s noise segment (8192- to 65536-point FFTs, both channels, smoothed over 1/12 to 1/96 octave), envelope times from the 4 to 10 ms RMS envelope of the burst segment (both channels), the Depth and Gate runs from the staircase (1 kHz tone, -60 to 0 dBFS). Because the carrier is noise, every level here scatters by about ±1 dB between channels and windows. These are observations, not a model.

### Things that affect how to read the renders

- **The carrier is a different random noise in every render, independent in L and R** (correlation 0.005 between the channels, none between segments), so only spectra and levels can be compared between cases, not waveforms. [measured 12.4.6]
- **Depth 0 % is not a constant-gain carrier** (Measured › Depth and Gate), so the bank cases, which were set to Depth 0 % to see the carrier through the bank, show the whole bank only with a broadband modulator (the noise segment). With a 1 kHz tone only the bands near it are open at low levels. The band layouts below are read from the noise segment. [measured 12.4.6]
- **Shared renders.** The Depth cases `depth-0` and `default` of `vocoder-depth-gate` are the renders of `vocoder-bank` `default` and `vocoder-envelope` `default` (same settings); `vocoder-noise-carrier` `default` is `vocoder-bank` `default`. No two other cases of these runs are bit-identical (md5). [measured 12.4.6]
- **`analyse.py` attack and release times are useless here:** its 63 % and 90 % times read 0.7 ms for every case, because they follow a 1 ms envelope of a noise output (some cases return no time at all). The times below were computed again with longer windows. [measured 12.4.6]

- **Twenty bands as loaded.** Counting the peaks of the BW 25 % case gives 20, at 80, 104, 137, 174, 230, 298, 392, 508, 668, 854, 1124, 1458, 1890, 2451, 3225, 4183, 5424, 7035, 9255 and 12003 Hz [measured 12.4.6]. The first sits on the lower Range limit, the last on the upper, and the ratio between neighbours is constant, (12000/80)^(1/19) = 1.301 (0.38 octave), within 2 % at every band [measured 12.4.6]. The stored `BandCount` = 4 is therefore likely an index into a list in which 20 comes fifth [inferred]. The other counts (4 to 40) are not API parameters and were not rendered.
- **BW is the width relative to the spacing.** At 25 % each band is about a quarter of the spacing wide (0.10 octave, constant in octaves from 176 Hz to 9 kHz); at 100 % the bands meet and the sum is flat; 200 % overlaps them [measured 12.4.6]. The Q follows the spacing, so a different Bands count or Range changes it [inferred]. At 200 % the level falls about 6 dB, so the bands are not scaled to constant total power at the wider setting.
- **Range changes the spacing, not the width relative to it:** the same 20 bands are laid between the limits; the spectrum of the noise in the bank goes up 3.7 dB when they are packed into 3 octaves, about the 3.8 dB of the factor 2.4 in band density, so the bands are not rescaled for the spacing [measured 12.4.6]. The skirts just outside the end bands are steep (about 90 to 100 dB per octave, 29 dB down a third of an octave below 500 Hz and 33 dB down a third of an octave above 4 kHz), then fall 15 to 18 dB per octave over four octaves below 400 Hz [measured 12.4.6].
- **Retro keeps the centres and tilts the level.** Against the flat Precise level, Retro is lower by 16 dB at 113 Hz and 9 dB at 160 Hz, and higher by 2 dB at 2.5 kHz and 4 to 6 dB from 4 to 9 kHz: per octave about +3 dB from 250 Hz to 1 kHz (-26.8 to -20.8 dB), then +1.3 dB to 4 kHz, then flat; above 4 kHz the bands are separate, with 9 dB dips between 4.2 and 5.4 kHz [measured 12.4.6]. That fits narrower and louder high bands.
- **Level of the stereo carrier:** the left and right outputs have the same rms within 0.6 dB in every case (within 0.05 dB for the bank cases) [measured 12.4.6].

| Rate 1.41 kHz | +12 below 300 Hz, -6 at 1.3 kHz, -3 to -14 above | -25.2 dBFS (-0.1) | nulls at 1.40 and 2.85 kHz (and about 4.3 and 5.7) |

- **X is a sample-and-hold at the displayed Rate:** the output spectrum has nulls at the Rate and at its multiples (98, 199, 304 Hz for 100 Hz; 1.40 and 2.85 kHz for 1.41 kHz; 5.5 and 10.6 kHz for 5.32 kHz, each within the resolution of 1/16 octave) and the energy is concentrated below the Rate: the nulls and the concentration below the Rate are measured, and a zero-order hold of white noise without interpolation is what they point to [measured 12.4.6; inferred]. The total level is kept (within 0.2 dB) when the bank still covers the spectrum, and falls 8.5 dB at 100 Hz where most of it is under the 80 Hz Range limit. 
- **Y sets the density of the impulses:** at 100 % the noise is white and Gaussian (kurtosis 2.98); at 50 % -2.6 dB and still near-Gaussian; at 10 % and 0 % it is made of sparse random impulses (kurtosis 17 and 43) with the same flat spectrum, 6.0 and 6.8 dB lower in rms. Y at 0 % is not silence: about 80 isolated impulses per second remain [measured 12.4.6]. The difference in level between 10 and 0 % is only 0.8 dB.
- **Rate does nothing at Crackle 10 %:** Rate 100 Hz at Crackle 10 % has the same flat spectrum (within 0.5 dB) as Rate 20 kHz at Crackle 10 %, so the hold applies to the continuous part of the noise, not to the impulses [measured 12.4.6]. A low Rate together with a low Crackle therefore gives only sparse impulses, not a slowed-down sparse noise.
- Both axes are in the displayed units: the nulls are at the Rate shown, and Crackle 0 to 100 % maps to impulse density from about 80 per second to white noise [measured 12.4.6].

### Envelope times

- **Release:** the fall of the output envelope matches a one-pole in amplitude with a time constant of 0.20 to 0.24 x the setting (18, 32, 112, 922 ms for 74, 150, 548, 4050 ms) to within 4 to 6 % of the step (fit error 0.005 to 0.008 of an amplitude of 0.13; 0.02 at 4.05 s, where the 1 s burst phase ends before the fall does). The setting is therefore about 4.5 time constants: the time in which the envelope falls about 40 dB [inferred from the measurement]. The 63 % and 90 % times of the step are 0.20 to 0.26 and 0.41 to 0.44 x the setting [measured 12.4.6].
- **Attack:** the rise matches a one-pole in amplitude with a time constant of 0.27 x the setting for the two slow settings (8.4 ms at 31.6 ms, 48.6 ms at 178 ms); at 5.6 ms and the default 1 ms the windows (4 to 8 ms) set the limit, the fits being 4.8 and below 3 ms. The fit errors are larger than for the release (0.15 of the step) because of the carrier noise [measured 12.4.6].
- Whether the envelope is formed from the rectified or the squared band signal is not visible here.

### Depth and Gate

| In (dBFS) | -54 | -42 | -30 | -24 | -18 | -12 | -6 | 0 | slope (dB per dB) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Depth 0 % | -35.7 | -30.8 | -28.1 | -26.2 | -24.7 | -23.7 | -22.3 | -22.3 | 0.24 (-60 to 0 dBFS) |
| Depth 50 % | -41.3 | -35.9 | -31.4 | -28.8 | -26.4 | -24.3 | -22.2 | -21.0 | 0.39 (-60 to -12) |
| Depth 100 % | -60.0 | -48.6 | -36.5 | -30.6 | -24.6 | -18.9 | -14.5 | -13.3 | 1.01 (-60 to -18) |
| Depth 150 % | silent | -99.6 | -52.8 | -38.7 | -26.2 | -16.1 | -10.1 | -9.5 | 2.6 (-39 to -18) |
| Depth 200 % | silent | silent | -88.3 | -48.7 | -29.6 | -14.1 | -5.0 | -5.1 | 3.4 (-27 to -12) |
| Depth 100 %, Gate -30 dB | silent | silent | -36.9 | -32.0 | -24.6 | -18.9 | -14.0 | -13.9 | |
| Depth 100 %, Gate -10 dB | silent | silent | silent | silent | -28.6 | -19.7 | -13.5 | -13.8 | |

("silent" is digital silence, below -200 dBFS; Depth 150 % still reads -127 dBFS at -45 dBFS and 200 % -145 dBFS at -33 dBFS.)

- **Depth 100 % is linear** (slope 1.01 from -60 to -18 dBFS, gain -6.8 dB with a scatter of ±1 dB) and compresses above -18 dBFS: the output reaches -13 dBFS at 0 dBFS in. The ceiling rises with Depth: -22.3, -21.0, -13.3, -9.5 and -5.1 dBFS at 0 dBFS in for 0, 50, 100, 150 and 200 % [measured 12.4.6].
- **Depth below 100 % compresses the range:** a 60 dB range of the tone gives 11.8 dB of output at 0 % and 21 dB at 50 %. **Above 100 % it expands:** 150 % gives slopes of 9 dB per dB at the turn-on (-45 to -42 dBFS) falling to 1.5 at -12 dBFS; 200 % starts at -33 dBFS at a slope of 19, 5.5 at -27 to -24 dBFS and about 3 above. Neither is a power law of the envelope with the exponent Depth/100, which would give slopes 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5 and 2 [measured 12.4.6].
- **Depth 0 % still follows the analysis level.** Its spectrum widens with the input level: a 1 kHz tone at -60 dBFS gives output near 1 kHz only (640 Hz 17 dB down, 2 kHz 35 dB down); at -36 dBFS the output spans 640 Hz to 2 kHz within 4 dB; at -12 dBFS 160 Hz to 6 kHz within 4 dB, and 80 Hz is 22 dB down; at 0 dBFS the whole bank from 80 Hz to 12 kHz is open within 8 dB. So the output level rises with input mostly because more bands open: each band seems to have a floor on its analysis level below which it closes progressively, whatever the Depth [inferred from the spectra]. Digital silence in gives digital silence out at every Depth [measured 12.4.6].
- **Gate** (Depth 100 %): a closed gate gives digital silence, with no leak. Gate -30 dB passes the tone from -39 dBFS (5.0 dB below the ungated output) and fully from -36 dBFS; Gate -10 dB passes it from -18 dBFS (4.0 dB below) and fully from -15 dBFS. So the tone must be about 10 dB (8 to 12 dB, the steps are 3 dB) below the displayed threshold to pass it, and the knee is a few dB wide [measured 12.4.6]. The default Gate (-inf, stored -60 dB) shows no gating down to the tone at -60 dBFS. Hysteresis and per-band behaviour were not read.

### Formant

The 1 kHz tone as modulator (8 s steady segment), Depth 100 %, BW 50 %, the noise carrier; the spectrum of the output in 1/10-octave bands (`vocoder-formant`) [measured 12.4.6]:

| Formant | Log-centroid of the output | Ratio to the unshifted (1060 Hz) | 2^(st/12) | Level (rms) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| -12 | 532 Hz | 0.502 | 0.500 | -31.6 dB |
| -5 | 804 Hz | 0.759 | 0.749 | -29.6 |
| 0 | 1060 Hz | 1 | 1 | -28.6 |
| +5 | 1421 Hz | 1.341 | 1.335 | -27.2 |
| +12 | 2135 Hz | 2.014 | 2.000 | -25.5 |

- **Formant is in semitones and shifts the whole synthesis bank**: the band the tone excites in the carrier moves by 2^(Formant / 12) to within 1.5 % over -12 to +24, so the modulator's energy at 1 kHz lands at 532 Hz, 2135 Hz and 4252 Hz (the 90 % energy ranges are 409 to 605 Hz, 1608 to 2378 Hz and 3188 to 4714 Hz, each about 0.6 octave wide). The edge of the Range is not a limit here: +24 still produces a band at 4.25 kHz [measured 12.4.6].
- The output level rises 3 dB per octave of the shift (-31.6 dB at -12 to -23.1 dB at +24, 8.5 dB over 3 octaves = 2.8 dB per octave), as the white carrier gives more power to the wider bands of the high part of a log-spaced bank [measured 12.4.6], [inferred] for the reason.

### Unvoiced noise

Depth 100 %, Unvoiced at 0, -10 and -20 dB, Sens. 100 % unless said, the carrier as loaded, Fast (`vocoder-unvoiced`); the modulator is the probe's silence, then its noise segment (white, -18 dBFS rms) [measured 12.4.6]:

| Case, noise segment as modulator | rms | noise density 100 to 400 / 0.4 to 1.5 / 1.5 to 4 / 4 to 8 / 8 to 14 / 14 to 20 kHz |
|---|---|---|
| Unvoiced 0 dB, Sens. 100 % (the run's base) | -3.4 dBFS | 32.5 / 38.8 / 43.3 / 46.4 / 47.8 / 29.8 dB |
| Unvoiced -10 dB | -12.7 | 23.3 / 29.9 / 34.5 / 37.2 / 38.4 / 20.1 |
| Unvoiced -20 dB | -18.8 | 18.5 / 25.2 / 29.6 / 31.6 / 31.6 / 11.7 |
| Sens. 0 %, Unvoiced 0 dB | -20.6 | 17.3 / 24.2 / 28.6 / 30.3 / 29.1 / 7.4 |
| Sens. 50 %, Fast | -3.4 | the same as 100 % within 0.2 dB |
| Sens. 50 %, Slow | -5.2 | 2.1 dB lower in every band |

- **In silence the Unvoiced noise is silent too**: with the modulator at zero, the output is digital zero at every Unvoiced setting and Sens. including 100 %, so the noise is not an always-on source; it follows the modulator (it is gated or multiplied by the input's energy) [measured 12.4.6].
- **The noise is tilted and band-limited**: its density rises 3.7 dB per octave from 250 Hz to 10 kHz (the 100 to 400 Hz band at 32.5 dB, 8 to 14 kHz at 47.8 dB) and falls 18 dB above 14 kHz, as a high-pass-weighted noise would; the vocoded carrier (Sens. 0 %, bands 17 to 30 dB) is 15 to 17 dB under it in the audio band [measured 12.4.6], [inferred] for the weighting.
- **The Unvoiced level is a gain in dB**: -10 dB lowers the bands by 9.2 to 9.5 dB, -20 dB lowers the low bands by 14 dB and the highs by 15 to 16 dB; below about -12 dB the noise falls under the vocoded carrier part (Sens. 0 % level), so only the first step is a clean 10 dB [measured 12.4.6].
- **Sens. 0 % switches the noise off**: it leaves only the vocoded carrier (-20.6 dBFS rms, 17 dB under the Unvoiced-on level); 50 % is already fully on for white noise as modulator (a signal with much high-frequency content) [measured 12.4.6]. What Sens. does for a modulator with little high-frequency content was not tested.
- **Fast and Slow**: with Fast the output reaches its level within 10 ms of the noise starting; with Slow it takes about 30 ms to settle and sits 1.9 dB lower in level [measured 12.4.6].

### Mono, Stereo, L/R, Level, Dry/Wet and Enhance

The 2x2 matrix of a 1 kHz tone in the left channel alone and the right alone, Depth 100 %, the carrier noise (`vocoder-output-modes`) [measured 12.4.6]:

| Mode | L to L | L to R | R to L | R to R | Correlation of the two outputs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stereo (default) | -12.7 dB | -12.4 | -12.6 | -12.5 | 0.00 (independent carriers) |
| Mono | -12.4 | -12.4 | -11.9 | -11.9 | 1.00 (the same signal in both) |
| L/R | -6.3 | none | none | -6.2 | 0.00 |

- **Stereo and Mono take the mean of the two input channels as the modulator**: a tone in one channel gives -12.5 dB in both outputs, 6.2 dB under what L/R gives in its own channel (-6.3 dB), and the same level for left and right input. **Stereo** uses an independent carrier noise in each output (correlation 0.00), so the two outputs have the same envelope but different noise; **Mono** sends one signal to both outputs; **L/R** drives each output from its own input, with nothing crossing [measured 12.4.6].
- **Level is an exact gain in dB on the output**: +12 and -12 dB shift the steady tone's rms by +12.0 and -12.1 dB and the noise segment's by +12.0 and -12.0 dB, so the Output range includes +12 and does not alter the Unvoiced or the envelope; the staircase compresses near full scale as the default does (-1.4 dBFS out at 0 dBFS in at +12 dB) [measured 12.4.6].
- **Dry/Wet is a linear crossfade**: at 50 % a left-only tone gives -5.8 dB left and -18.2 dB in the right output: 0.5 of the dry tone (-6.0 dB) plus 0.5 of the wet (the wet at -12.7 / -12.4 dB gives -18.4 dB alone), the dry unprocessed and not delayed [measured 12.4.6].
- **Enhance** (white noise as carrier): the 1 kHz tone through it comes out 0.6 dB louder (-20.8 against -21.4 dBFS rms) with the centroid 1.2 % lower, and the noise segment 1.7 dB quieter (-22.3 against -20.6 dBFS) with the centroid 6 % lower (5975 against 6364 Hz): the switch changes the level and tilt of the carrier through the bank slightly. A flat carrier gives it little to even out; a dull and a bright carrier were not available [measured 12.4.6].

## Implementation notes

- **Building blocks:** biquad band-passes (0 dB peak, the Q from a bandwidth in octaves) for both
  banks, one-pole smoothing, dB conversions; no crossovers are needed.
- **Also needed:** an envelope follower with separate attack and release (an asymmetric
  one-pole); a noise generator and a sample-rate reducer (Noise carrier with X and Y); a sawtooth
  and pulse oscillator with band-limiting (PolyBLEP or wavetable) and a monophonic pitch detector
  (Pitch Tracking); the voiced/unvoiced detector; an input for an external carrier (an audio
  side-chain that must be audible, [common.md](common.md) › Side-chain section); a per-band gain
  display.
- **Cost:** 40 bands × (analysis + synthesis) × 2 channels × biquad (possibly cascaded) is well
  within a block budget; per-sample envelope follower per band.
- **Hard to match:** Retro's gain and bandwidth law, the Depth curve, Enhance, Unvoiced
  detection, Formant's exact units.
- **Import:** `.als` element `Vocoder` [dump 12.4.6]; carrier routing (Audio From) uses the same routing fields as other side-chain devices. Parameter elements [als 12.4.6] (stored units where they differ from the display): `On`, `LowFrequency` (Hz, 80), `HighFrequency` (Hz, 12000), `FormantShift` (0), `FilterBandWidth` (1 = 100 %), `Retro` (false), `LevelGate` (dB, −60 = the −inf end), `OutputGain` (dB, 0), `EnvelopeRate` (Attack, ms, 1), `EnvelopeRelease` (ms, 150), `UvdThreshold` (Sens., 0.5), `UvdSlow` (false), `UvdLevel` (Unvoiced, linear gain, 0.000316), `CarrierSource/NoiseRate` (Hz, 20000), `CarrierSource/NoiseCrackle` (1 = 100 %), `CarrierSource/DetectionLowerBound` and `DetectionUpperBound` (Hz, 80 and 500), `CarrierSource/OscillatorPitch` (0), `CarrierSource/OscillatorWaveform` (0 = Saw), `CarrierFlatten` (Enhance), `MonoStereo` (1 = Stereo), `DryWet` (1), `ModulatorAmount` (Depth, 1 = 100 %). Not parameters but plain values [als 12.4.6]: `CarrierSource/Type` (0 = Noise [inferred]), `CarrierSource/RoutedInput` (the Audio From routing, target `AudioIn/None`), `FilterBank/BandCount` (4, see Controls) and `FilterBank/BandLevel.0` to `.39` (one level per possible band, 1 = full). No element for Ext. In Gain was found.
  the same routing fields as other side-chain devices.
