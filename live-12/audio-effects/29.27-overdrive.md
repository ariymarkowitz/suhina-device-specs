# Overdrive (manual 29.27)

**Family:** distortion
**Note status:** dump checked

## Summary

Overdrive is a classic pedal-style distortion that keeps dynamic range even when driven hard. A
band-pass filter (set on an X-Y pad) selects what is distorted, Drive sets the amount, Tone shapes
the result, Dynamics sets how much compression and make-up the drive brings with it, and Dry/Wet
mixes in the original [manual].

## Signal flow

```text
input ─► band-pass (Frequency, Bandwidth) ─► distortion (Drive) with built-in compression (Dynamics) ─► Tone ─► Dry/Wet ─► out
          [manual]                           [manual]                                                 [manual]  [manual]
 └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────► dry ───────┘
```

- The band-pass comes before the distortion [manual].
- Whether the band-pass also affects what remains of the dry signal (the dry is the unfiltered input) is [inferred]: dry is the device input.
- SOS: with Dry/Wet at 0 % Tone still affects the signal [source: 2]. That suggests Tone (or part of it) sits after the mix, or applies to the dry path too [measure].
- Where Dynamics' compression sits (before, inside or after the distorting stage) is [measure].

## Behaviour

- **Band-pass and drive.** The signal outside the band is attenuated before the curve, so the saturation only sees the selected range [manual]. The bandwidth is a width in octaves or a Q [measure]. The band-pass is probably a pair of high- and low-pass filters, or a band-pass biquad [inferred].
- **Distortion.** A soft, asymmetric-capable curve in the style of a pedal; the manual says heavy drive still keeps the dynamics intact [manual]. This is only possible with some level control that stops the output rising with drive [inferred].
- **Dynamics.** With Dynamics low, rising Drive increases the internal compression and make-up gain; with Dynamics high, less compression is applied [manual]. SOS reads the same: high settings give almost no compression as Drive rises, low settings heavy compression [source: 2]. The implementation may be a gain computer that follows the input level and a make-up gain linked to Drive [inferred].
- **Tone.** A tilt, shelving or low-pass EQ after the distortion [manual]. SOS describes it as post-distortion high-pass filtering and says it runs from deep bass to harsh noise at extremes, especially with Drive at maximum [source: 2], which conflicts with the manual's "more high-frequency content at higher values" only in wording; it is probably a shelf or tilt with its centre at mid frequencies [inferred]; type and corner are [measure].
- **Dry/Wet.** Linear [inferred]. SOS notes that Tone still acts at Dry/Wet 0 % [source: 2].

## Edge cases

- **Drive 0 %:** still distorts [manual].
- **Dynamics at either end with Drive high:** at 0 % heavy compression, at 100 % none [manual], [source: 2]. Output level changes with Drive; the amount is [measure].
- **Narrow band:** the distorted signal is only the narrow band; at Dry/Wet 100 % the rest of the spectrum is attenuated [inferred].
- **Silence:** silent; a DC blocker may remove offset from asymmetry [inferred].
- **Latency:** none documented; oversampling inside would add latency [measure].

## Measurement plan

1. **Band-pass.** Sine sweep at -40 dBFS with Drive at 0 %, Dynamics 100 %, Dry/Wet 100 %, Tone minimal: read the band's centre, slopes and bandwidth for X positions (Frequency at 100 Hz, 1 kHz, 8 kHz) and Y positions (min, mid, max). The ranges and defaults of both parameters are answered by the dump [dump 12.4.6]; check the bandwidth unit (octaves or Q).
2. **Drive curve and level.** 200 Hz and 2 kHz sine from -60 to 0 dBFS in 6 dB steps, Drive at 0, 25, 50, 75, 100 %, Dynamics 0 and 100 %: output level, harmonic spectrum (odd and even), small-signal gain; gives the curve and the compression behaviour.
3. **Dynamics.** Tone bursts alternating between -30 and -6 dBFS, Drive 100 %, Dynamics at 0, 50, 100 %: read the level difference between bursts (compression) and the make-up gain; detector attack and release from the settling times.
4. **Tone.** Sine sweep at -40 dBFS, Drive 0 %, Tone at 0, 25, 50, 75, 100 %: read the filter shape, corner and slope; repeat at Dry/Wet 0 % to see the SOS observation.
5. **Dry/Wet.** 1 kHz sine at 50 %; impulse for the dry path delay.
6. ~~**Defaults.**~~ Answered by the dump and the saved set [dump 12.4.6] [als 12.4.6].

## Implementation notes

- **Likely structure [inferred]:** a band (high-pass and low-pass pair) → Drive into a biased asymmetric tanh at 2x oversampling → DC blocker → Tone → Dynamics → Dry/Wet, with the dry path delayed by the oversampler's latency if there is any. Live's ranges and defaults [dump 12.4.6]: Frequency up to 20 kHz; Drive 50 %, Tone 50 %, Dynamics 50 %, Dry/Wet 50 %, 1.25 kHz, width 6.5.
- **Open points for the design:** whether Tone is a low-pass, a tilt or a shelf [measure]; Dynamics is described as a compressor link, not a level follower [manual]; whether the device reports latency (probably none) [measure]; the X-Y display for the band; the device has no Output control.
- **Building blocks:** biquad high-pass, low-pass and band-pass; an asymmetric tanh curve with a bias; oversampling; a DC blocker; one-pole smoothing.
- **Missing:** a compressor-style Dynamics stage, a Tone filter matched to the measurement, the XY display (UI).
- **`.als` element:** `Overdrive` [als 12.4.6]. Parameter elements in the set saved from Live 12.4.6 [als 12.4.6], each holding a `Manual` value: `On` (bool), `MidFreq` (Hz, 1250), `BandWidth` (6.5), `Drive` (0 – 100, stored as the percentage number: 50), `DryWet` (0 – 100: 50), `Tone` (0 – 100: 50) and `PreserveDynamics` (fraction: 0.5 = 50 %). Note that Drive, Dry/Wet and Tone are stored on a 0 – 100 scale while Dynamics is a 0..1 fraction.
